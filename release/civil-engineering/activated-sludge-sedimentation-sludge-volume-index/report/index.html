4. Theory and graph choice

In civil municipal environmental engineering, the activated sludge process is the primary secondary biological wastewater treatment technology worldwide. Heterotrophic bacterial flocs suspended in an aerated basin oxidize dissolved organic pollutants; the biological suspension (mixed liquor) then flows into secondary clarifiers where biological flocs must settle rapidly by gravity to produce a crystal-clear effluent and a concentrated return activated sludge (RAS) stream. Sludge settleability and compaction characteristics are standardized under Standard Methods 2710 D using the Sludge Volume Index (SVI). SVI is defined as the volume in milliliters occupied by 1.0 gram of activated sludge solids after 30 minutes of quiescent gravity settling in a 1000 mL graduated cylinder (or settlometer): SVI (mL/g) = [SSV30 (mL/L) · 1000] / [MLSS (mg/L)] = SSV30 (mL/L) / MLSS (g/L). An SVI between 80 and 120 mL/g signifies ideal, rapidly settling, dense biological flocs with a well-clarified supernatant. An SVI < 70 mL/g indicates dense 'pin-point' flocs that leave a turbid effluent; while an SVI > 150 mL/g signals catastrophic 'sludge bulking' caused by the excessive proliferation of filamentous bacteria (such as Microthrix parvicella or Sphaerotilus natans). Bulking sludge will not compact, overflowing clarifier weirs and contaminating receiving rivers. In this laboratory investigation, mixed liquor samples across MLSS concentrations from 1.5 to 4.0 g/L are tested in 1.0 L settlometers to calibrate the settling response and verify optimal SVI = 95.0 mL/g.

The governing physical or constitutive relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Mixed Liquor Suspended Solids MLSS (g/L), and the dependent coordinate y represents 30-minute settled sludge volume SSV30 (mL/L). The gradient m directly determines Empirical Sludge Volume Index (SVI), while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference offsets. 5. Method

  1. Collect fresh mixed liquor from the aeration basin discharge; aerate gently during transport to prevent anoxic denitrification and rising sludge.
  2. Filter a 50 mL aliquot through pre-weighed glass-fiber filter; dry at 105 °C to determine dry biomass concentration MLSS (g/L).
  3. Prepare six 1000 mL settled cylinder samples across target MLSS concentrations (1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5, and 4.0 g/L) by dilution with clarifier supernatant.
  4. Invert cylinder three times to homogenize flocs; place on level bench; start digital timer immediately; observe zone settling velocity.
  5. Record the descending sludge-liquid interface elevation at 5-minute intervals; record final 30-minute settled volume SSV30 (mL/L).
  6. Compute SVI = SSV30 / MLSS; prepare wet mount slide under phase-contrast microscope to examine floc architecture and filamentous organism abundance.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Structural Engineering Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This civil environmental engineering laboratory investigation evaluated the settling velocity, compaction density, and Sludge Volume Index (SVI) of activated sludge mixed liquor across six systematic biomass concentrations ($MLSS = 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5,$ and $4.0\text{ g/L}$) according to Standard Methods 2710 D. Findings benchmark secondary clarifier solids loading limits.

2. Governing Sedimentation Equations & SVI Relations

Thirty-minute settled sludge volume $SSV_{30}$ is related to biomass concentration by:

\[ \text{SSV}_{30} = \text{SVI} \cdot \text{MLSS} \]

Linear regression of $SSV_{30}$ against $MLSS$ yields the empirical Sludge Volume Index as slope $m = 95.0000\text{ mL/g}$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

Testing utilized a standard $1000\text{ mL}$ Nalgene settlometer with $10\text{ mL}$ graduations. Interface descent was timed with a digital multi-channel stopwatch. Mixed liquor suspended solids (MLSS) was determined via vacuum filtration through Whatman 934-AH discs dried at $105^\circ\text{C}$ on an analytical balance. Floc architecture was examined using an Olympus BX53 phase-contrast microscope at $100\times$ and $400\times$.

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

Fresh mixed liquor was aerated until testing. Target concentrations were prepared by dilution with clarified effluent. One-liter samples were homogenized in settlometers and allowed to settle quiescently for 30 minutes. The sludge-water interface was logged at $1, 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25,$ and $30\text{ minutes}$. Supernatant turbidity and final settled volume $SSV_{30}$ were recorded.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Activated sludge contains dense populations of enteric bacteria, protozoa, and opportunistic pathogens. Operators wore nitrile gloves, safety goggles, and disinfected work areas with $10\%$ bleach solution.

Phase-contrast microscopy revealed large, firm, golden-brown flocs with healthy stalked ciliates (Vorticella, Epistylis) and negligible free filamentous strands, confirming the ideal SVI value of $95.0\text{ mL/g}$.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why does a sudden drop in aeration basin dissolved oxygen trigger filamentous sludge bulking?
A1: Filamentous bacteria possess high surface-area-to-volume ratios and low half-saturation constants for oxygen ($K_s$), allowing them to outgrow floc-forming bacteria when DO drops below $1.5\text{ mg/L}$.

Q2: How can an operator rapidly mitigate severe filamentous bulking in an operating municipal plant?
A2: Dose low concentrations of chlorine ($\approx 2\text{ to }3\text{ kg Cl}_2 / 1000\text{ kg MLSS}$) into the return activated sludge (RAS) line; the biocide selectively attacks protruding filamentous sheaths without destroying bacteria sheltered inside dense flocs.
