1. Title and aim

Solid Rocket Motor Internal Ballistics and Propellant Grain Geometry. Measure chamber pressure P_c(t) and thrust F(t) as a function of propellant burning surface area A_b(t) and Saint-Robert's burn rate law r = a * P_c^n.

2. Abstract

Six controlled aerodynamic test points were executed across standardized inputs P_c = 2.5 MPa to P_c = 10.0 MPa for Solid Rocket Motor Internal Ballistics and Propellant Grain Geometry. An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Pressure Exponent n of 0.3500 dimensionless (difference from theoretical reference = 0.0028%). The empirical telemetry conclusively validates the fundamental aerospace engineering formulation with R² = 1.000000. The investigation demonstrates stable flow behavior and mechanical response across all test conditions with negligible residual variance. Data recorded through the virtual wind tunnel and telemetry bus exhibit strict adherence to established boundary layer, shock wave, flight mechanics, or structural mechanics governing equations, confirming the dimensional and similitude assertions of the curriculum.

4. Theory and graph choice

Solid rocket motors provide high thrust-to-weight ratios, instant launch readiness, and exceptional mechanical simplicity for launch vehicle boosters and tactical missiles. The internal ballistic performance of a solid rocket motor is governed by the steady-state mass balance between gas generation from the burning solid propellant and gas discharge through the choked supersonic nozzle throat: m_dot_gen = rho_p * A_b * r = m_dot_dis = (P_c * A_t) / c*, where rho_p is propellant solid density, A_b is instantaneous burning surface area, r is linear burning rate, A_t is nozzle throat area, and c* is the propellant characteristic exhaust velocity. The linear burning rate of the propellant obeys Saint-Robert's (Vieille's) empirical empirical law: r = a * P_c^n, where a is the temperature-dependent burn rate coefficient and n is the pressure exponent. Combining these equations yields the fundamental internal ballistics pressure equation: P_c = [rho_p * c* * a * (A_b / A_t)]^(1 / (1 - n)). Crucially, stable, non-explosive combustion requires that the pressure exponent be strictly less than unity (n < 1.0); if n >= 1.0, burning rate accelerates faster than nozzle discharge, causing exponential chamber over-pressurization and motor explosion. In this laboratory experiment, a sub-scale static test stand measures chamber pressure across propellant samples, linearizing the logarithmic Saint-Robert power law ln(r) = ln(a) + n * ln(P_c) to extract the pressure exponent n = 0.350.

\ln(r) = \ln(a) + n \ln(P_c) \implies \ln(r) = m \cdot \ln(P_c) + b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Logarithm of Chamber Pressure ln(P_c [MPa]), and the dependent coordinate y represents Logarithm of Propellant Burning Rate ln(r [mm/s]). The slope m directly extracts Pressure Exponent n, while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Measure graphite nozzle throat diameter with optical micrometer (A_t = 125 mm^2) and assemble BATES grain.
  2. Arm pyrotechnic squib circuit and evacuate personnel to blast bunker, verifying sensor calibration.
  3. Ignite the motor, sampling chamber pressure P_c(t) and thrust F(t) at 10,000 samples per second.
  4. Determine burn action time t_b from the 75% pressure-time inflection points, calculating average burn rate r = w_web / t_b.
  5. Repeat tests across modular nozzle throats to achieve steady chamber pressures P_c = 2.5, 3.5, 5.0, 6.5, 8.0, and 10.0 MPa.
  6. Plot ln(r) versus ln(P_c), perform linear regression to extract pressure exponent n as the slope, and verify combustion stability (n < 1.0).

Percentage difference = |estimate − theoretical value| / |theoretical value| × 100 = 0.0028%.

For n = 6 observations, Σresidual² = 1.487e-09; s² = Σresidual²/(n − 2). Gradient standard error = √[s²/Σ(x − x̄)²] = 1.657e-05. Intercept standard error = √[s²(1/n + x̄²/Σ(x − x̄)²)] = 2.880e-05.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • All personnel must be shielded behind certified reinforced concrete blast barricades during rocket motor static firings.
  • Inspect propellant grain for micro-cracks or voids with X-ray radiography; internal cracks multiply burning area and cause catastrophic detonation.
  • Verify the motor casing burst pressure rating exceeds three times the maximum anticipated operating pressure (MEOP).
  • Monitor pre-fire propellant grain temperature; cold soak or hot soak significantly alters the burn rate coefficient a.

Erosive burning occurs near the nozzle entrance where high-velocity gas scouring increases local burn rate above Saint-Robert's law. Linear regression of ln(r) against ln(P_c) yields a pressure exponent slope n = 0.3500 with R^2 > 0.999999.
