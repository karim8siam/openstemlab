Biomedical and physiological foundations of Defibrillator Transthoracic Discharge.

Ventricular fibrillation (VF) is a lethal cardiac arrhythmia characterized by disorganized, chaotic electrical depolarization of ventricular myocytes, causing loss of coordinated mechanical pumping, immediate cessation of cardiac output, and sudden cardiac death within minutes. External defibrillation is the definitive clinical treatment, delivering a controlled electrical shock across the thoracic chest to simultaneously depolarize a critical mass (> 75–90%) of myocardium, extinguish re-entrant wavelets, and allow the intrinsic sinoatrial node to re-establish normal sinus rhythm. In classical Lown and Edmark RLC defibrillators, a high-voltage capacitor C (typically 32 to 50 μF) charged up to several thousand volts (1500 to 5000 V, delivering 200 to 360 Joules) discharges through an internal series inductor L (~50 to 100 mH) and the patient's transthoracic impedance R_th. The governing differential equation of the series RLC circuit is L · (d²i/dt²) + R_th · (di/dt) + i/C = 0. In human clinical resuscitation, transthoracic impedance R_th varies widely (from 25 to 150 Ω) depending on body mass, chest hair, electrode size, coupling gel, and electrode paddle contact pressure. Because higher patient impedance dampens the discharge waveform, the peak delivered electrical current I_peak scales inversely with patient impedance: I_peak = -k_r · R_th + I_0, where k_r is the impedance sensitivity constant (A/Ω). In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented high-voltage defibrillator test analyzer evaluates discharge currents across standardized non-inductive load resistors from 30 to 105 Ω.

I_peak = -k_r · R_th + I_0 = -0.3500 · R_th + 68.0000

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

Why is peak electrical current I_peak critical for successful defibrillation?

Myocardial cell depolarization is driven by electric field intensity and current density traversing cardiac tissue, not by stored voltage or nominal energy alone; if high patient impedance reduces current below ~15–20 A, defibrillation fails regardless of delivered Joules.

How does an RLC damped sinusoidal (Lown) waveform differ from a modern Biphasic Truncated Exponential (BTE) waveform?

An RLC damped sinusoidal waveform delivers a single-direction current pulse shaped by a heavy inductor; modern BTE defibrillators use solid-state H-bridge switching to deliver an initial positive current pulse followed by an inverted negative pulse, achieving successful defibrillation at significantly lower energies (150 J vs 360 J) and causing less myocardial damage.

What factors influence human transthoracic impedance (TTI) during clinical resuscitation?

TTI is lowered by larger electrode pad surface area, conductive electrode hydrogel, firm paddle pressure (firm pressure expels air from the lungs, lowering impedance by up to 20%), shaving chest hair, and delivering repeated shocks in rapid succession.

What is the physical function of the series inductor (choke) in an RLC defibrillator?

Without an inductor, discharging a high-voltage capacitor directly into a human chest would produce a lethal instantaneous current spike (thousands of amperes with microsecond rise time) that denatures myocardial proteins and causes severe cardiac burns; the inductor limits di/dt, shaping a smooth, rounded millisecond pulse.

What is the difference between defibrillation and synchronized cardioversion?

Defibrillation delivers an unsynchronized high-energy shock at any moment in the cardiac cycle (used for pulseless VF and pulseless VT); synchronized cardioversion detects the ECG R-wave and fires the shock exactly on the R-wave peak to avoid the vulnerable T-wave period, which could otherwise induce ventricular fibrillation (R-on-T phenomenon).

Why must defibrillator test banks use non-inductive resistors?

Standard wire-wound power resistors have significant parasitic inductance that distorts high-voltage pulse rise times and alters RLC resonant damping; non-inductive metal-oxide or carbon composition resistors guarantee pure resistive impedance.

Experimental precautions and patient safety

  • High-voltage electrical hazard: Defibrillator discharges deliver kilovolt pulses; never touch test leads or analyzer contacts during firing.
  • Ensure analyzer load resistors are non-inductive and rated for high peak pulse energy (minimum 500 J surge capacity).
  • Allow 30 seconds between successive high-energy discharges to prevent thermal buildup in the internal defibrillator components and load bank.
  • Verify high-voltage probe ground lead is securely bonded to analyzer ground to prevent hazardous floating chassis potentials.

Theoretical assumptions & biological limits

  • The linear current-impedance model applies across normal clinical human adult impedances (30 to 105 Ω); extreme impedances exhibit non-linear RLC damping transitions.
  • Pure resistive load banks simulate real impedance, neglecting the small capacitive phase shifts present in biological human skin-electrode interfaces.
  • Evaluates static resistance, whereas living human tissue impedance decreases dynamically by 5–10% during the high-voltage discharge itself.
  • Does not model internal multi-dimensional thoracic current path distributions through lungs, myocardium, and skeletal muscle.
