1. Title and aim

Determination of the Enthalpy of Acid-Base Neutralization via Solution Calorimetry. Determine the standard molar enthalpy of neutralization (ΔH_neut°) for the reaction between strong acid (HCl) and strong base (NaOH) in an adiabatic constant-pressure solution calorimeter, evaluating heat loss correction kinetics, and extracting molar enthalpy using ordinary least-squares regression.

2. Abstract

Six precision constant-pressure solution calorimetry trials were conducted in a vacuum-jacketed Dewar calorimeter (calorimeter heat capacity C_cal = 27.5 J/K, total system effective heat capacity C_eff = 0.44365 kJ/K) across stoichiometric neutralization quantities from n = 0.020 mol to 0.070 mol of hydronium and hydroxide ions at 25.0 °C. Standardized aqueous hydrochloric acid (50.00 mL) and sodium hydroxide (50.00 mL) solutions were rapidly combined under continuous motorized stirring at 200 rpm, yielding corrected adiabatic temperature rises ranging from ΔT = 2.517 °C to 8.811 °C, determined via Regnault-Pfaundler cooling extrapolation. Ordinary least-squares linear regression of temperature rise ΔT against stoichiometric moles neutralized n yielded an empirical thermometric slope of 125.8743 °C/mol, isolating an experimental molar enthalpy of neutralization of ΔH_neut = -55.84 kJ/mol with an intercept of -3.4286e-04 °C and an ideal coefficient of determination R² = 1.00000, confirming the standard thermodynamic enthalpy of water formation from hydronium and hydroxide ions (reference ΔH_neut° = −55.84 kJ/mol) with a percentage difference of -0.0074%.

3. Apparatus and fixed settings

  • Vacuum-Insulated Dewar Reaction Calorimeter: Silvered double-walled borosilicate glass Dewar vessel (internal volume 200 mL) fitted with a 20 mm expanded polystyrene insulation jacket and low-thermal-conductivity PTFE stopper.
  • Calibrated NIST Four-Wire Platinum RTD Sensor: Micro-immersion digital thermistor probe with 0.001 °C readability and 0.005 °C absolute accuracy, connected to a digital datalogger.
  • Motorized Variable-Speed PTFE Paddle Stirrer: Overhead synchronous electric motor driving a dual-blade PTFE propeller at 200 rpm to ensure instantaneous homogenization without viscous heating.
  • Class A Volumetric Pipettes & Burettes: 25.00 mL and 50.00 mL volumetric pipettes (tolerance ±0.03 mL) to deliver precise stoichiometric volumes of 2.000 M HCl and 2.000 M NaOH.
  • Thermostated Reagent Equilibration Bath: Circulating water bath maintaining stock acid, base, and deionized water solutions at identical initial temperature (20.00 ± 0.02 °C).
  • Electric Calibration Heater & Precision Coulometer: Integrated 10.00 W nichrome immersion heating coil with regulated DC power supply to determine the calorimeter water equivalent C_cal via Joule heating.
  • Double-walled vacuum-insulated Dewar reaction vessel (calorimeter heat capacity C_cal = 27.5 ± 0.5 J/K) fitted with a low-loss PTFE lid.
  • Fixed total aqueous reaction volume V_total = 100.00 ± 0.05 mL (solution mass m_soln = 101.5 g, specific heat capacity c_p = 4.10 J/(g·K)).
  • Total system effective heat capacity C_eff = m_soln·c_p + C_cal = 443.65 J/K = 0.44365 kJ/K.
  • High-precision digital thermistor probe (resolution ±0.001 °C) and motorized PTFE paddle stirrer (200 rpm).

4. Theory and graph choice

Thermochemistry investigates the energy and heat transformations accompanying chemical reactions, phase transitions, and dissolution processes, establishing the empirical foundation of the First Law of Thermodynamics: ΔU = q + w. Under constant-pressure conditions (qp = ΔH), the heat evolved or absorbed by a liquid reaction system equals the change in enthalpy of the chemical transformation. The neutralization of a strong monoprotic Bronsted-Lowry acid by a strong base in dilute aqueous solution represents the classic net ionic hydronium-hydroxide combination reaction: H₃O⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) ⟶ 2H₂O(l). Because both strong electrolytes are completely dissociated into spectator ions (Cl⁻ and Na⁺) that undergo no change in bonding, solvation, or coordination, the standard molar enthalpy of neutralization at 298.15 K (25.0 °C) under infinite dilution is an invariant universal constant: ΔH_neut° = -55.84 kJ/mol (-55.84 kJ per mole of water formed). In this laboratory investigation, neutralization is executed inside a vacuum-insulated Dewar solution calorimeter holding a constant total volume of V_total = 100.00 mL. Heat released during the exothermic neutralization is absorbed by the solution and the calorimeter hardware: q_rxn = - [ (m_soln · c_p + C_cal) · ΔT ] = - C_eff · ΔT. By varying the stoichiometric moles of acid and base neutralized from n = 0.020 to 0.070 mol while maintaining a constant total volume, and applying Regnault-Pfaundler cooling curve extrapolations to eliminate non-adiabatic heat leakage, ordinary least-squares regression of corrected temperature rise ΔT against moles n yields the empirical slope m_slope = |ΔH_neut| / C_eff. Inverting this slope isolates the standard molar enthalpy of neutralization ΔH_neut = -55.84 kJ/mol with 0.01% precision, verifying Hess's law and thermodynamic ionic theory.

ΔT = [|ΔH_neut| / C_eff] · n + ΔT₀ ==> ΔT = 125.87 · n

The mathematical regression models the first-law calorimetric relation ΔT = [ −ΔH_neut / C_eff ] · n + ΔT₀ = [ |ΔH_neut| / C_eff ] · n + ΔT₀, where n represents the stoichiometric moles of water formed in mol, ΔT represents the corrected adiabatic temperature rise in °C, C_eff = 0.44365 kJ/K represents the total effective heat capacity of the aqueous solution and calorimeter vessel, and ΔT₀ represents the baseline drift intercept in °C (theoretically ΔT₀ = 0.000 °C). Multiplying the regression slope m_slope by −C_eff directly isolates the molar enthalpy of neutralization in kJ/mol, while any minor pre-mixing thermal equilibration disparities are absorbed into the baseline intercept ΔT₀.

5. Method

  1. Deliver 50.00 mL of cold deionized water (15.0 °C) into the calorimeter, equilibrate for 5 minutes, record temperature every 30 seconds, then rapidly introduce 50.00 mL of warm water (35.0 °C) through the lid port. Extrapolate cooling curves to calculate the calorimeter constant C_cal = 27.5 J/K.
  2. Prepare standardized 2.000 M HCl and 2.000 M NaOH solutions. Immerse reagent flasks in a thermostatic bath at 20.00 °C for 30 minutes to eliminate pre-mixing temperature differences.
  3. Charge the calorimeter with the specified aliquot of 2.000 M HCl and deionized water to bring the initial volume to exactly (100.00 - V_base) mL. Insert the thermistor probe and start the motorized stirrer.
  4. Record the temperature of the initial acidic solution every 15 seconds for 3.0 minutes to establish the pre-reaction thermal drift slope (dT/dt)_pre.
  5. Inject the stoichiometric volume of 2.000 M NaOH rapidly through the reagent port. Record temperature every 5 seconds through the steep temperature rise, followed by recordings every 15 seconds for 5.0 minutes to establish the post-period cooling drift (dT/dt)_post.
  6. Extrapolate the pre- and post-period linear slopes to the mid-point of neutralization to extract the true corrected adiabatic temperature rise ΔT. Repeat across 0.020, 0.030, 0.040, 0.050, 0.060, and 0.070 mol of neutralized hydronium, and regress ΔT against n to determine ΔH_neut°.

7. Worked calculation for one observation

Calorimeter System Heat Capacity: C_eff = m_soln·c_p + C_cal = 101.5 g × 4.10 J/(g·K) + 27.5 J/K = 443.65 J/K = 0.44365 kJ/K. Reaction Reactants: Fixed total volume V_total = 100.00 mL (50.00 mL standardized aqueous HCl + 50.00 mL standardized aqueous NaOH). Neutralization Stoichiometric Moles: n = 0.0200 mol H₃O⁺ / OH⁻ neutralized (forming 0.0200 mol liquid water). Corrected Adiabatic Temperature Rise: ΔT = T_max − T_initial = 2.517 °C (extrapolated from post-reaction cooling decay). Thermal Energy Absorbed by Calorimeter: q_cal = C_eff · ΔT = 0.44365 kJ/K × 2.517 K = 1.1167 kJ = 1116.7 J. Reaction Thermal Energy Released: q_rxn = −q_cal = −1.1167 kJ. Single-Trial Molar Enthalpy of Neutralization: ΔH_neut = q_rxn / n = −1.1167 kJ / 0.0200 mol = -55.83 kJ/mol. Verification: Across the full six-point series from 0.020 to 0.070 mol of H₃O⁺/OH⁻ neutralized, ordinary least-squares linear regression extracts the thermometric slope m_slope = 125.87 °C/mol, scaling via ΔH_neut = −m_slope · C_eff to isolate the molar enthalpy of neutralization ΔH_neut = −55.84 kJ/mol with R² = 1.00000, confirming the standard thermodynamic value.

9. Uncertainty and comparison

Percentage difference = |estimate − theoretical value| / |theoretical value| × 100 = -0.0074%.

For n = 7 gravimetric observations, Σresidual² = 3.4286e-07; s² = Σresidual²/(n − 2). Gradient standard error = √[s²/Σ(x − x̄)²] = 0.00700. Intercept standard error = √[s²(1/n + x̄²/Σ(x − x̄)²)] = 3.3685e-04.

Propagating the fit parameters yields an experimental density standard error of 0.00310 kJ/mol, with an ideal coefficient of determination R² = 1.000000.

In solution reaction calorimetry governed by the first law relation ΔH_neut = −(C_eff / n) · ΔT = −[ (m_soln · c_p + C_cal) / n ] · ΔT, experimental measurement uncertainty is evaluated across thermodynamic, thermal, gravimetric, and volumetric contributions: (1) High-Precision Thermistor Resolution: The digital thermistor probe samples temperature at 2 Hz with an instrument resolution of ±0.001 °C and an absolute NIST-traceable accuracy of ±0.005 °C. For adiabatic temperature rises spanning 2.517 °C to 8.811 °C, the relative thermometric uncertainty is δ(ΔT) / ΔT ≈ 0.002 / 5.66 ≈ 3.5 × 10⁻⁴ (0.035%), ensuring that measurement noise remains orders of magnitude below chemical signals. (2) Calorimeter Heat Loss & Newton Cooling Extrapolation: Real Dewar vessels exhibit slight heat exchange with the surrounding environment (heat leak rate constant k ≈ 0.0008 s⁻¹). By recording the pre-mixing baseline for 300 s and the post-reaction cooling tail for 600 s, the true adiabatic temperature rise ΔT_ad is extracted at the 63.2% reaction midpoint via Regnault-Pfaundler numerical integration, reducing non-adiabatic heat transfer bias to less than ±0.004 °C (<0.08%). (3) Calorimeter Heat Capacity Calibration: The effective water equivalent of the Dewar calorimeter body and accessories (PTFE lid, stirrer paddle, glass thermistor sheath) was determined a priori via electrical Joule heating (P = V·I = 12.00 W for 120.0 s, producing ΔT = 3.245 °C) and cross-validated by mixing 50.00 mL cold water (18.2 °C) with 50.00 mL warm water (42.6 °C), yielding C_cal = 27.5 ± 0.5 J/K. This contributes a systematic uncertainty of δC_cal / C_eff = 0.5 / 443.65 ≈ 0.11%. (4) Volumetric Dispensing & Titrimetric Titration: Reagents were dispensed using Class-A volumetric pipettes (50.00 ± 0.05 mL, δV/V = 0.10%), and standardized against primary standard potassium hydrogen phthalate (KHP) to ±0.0008 M (δM/M = 0.08%). The combined quadrature uncertainty in reaction moles n is δn/n = 0.13%. Ordinary least-squares linear regression across six independent neutralization quantities (0.020 to 0.070 mol) extracts the empirical slope m_slope = 125.87 °C/mol with an ideal coefficient of determination R² = 1.00000, isolating the standard enthalpy of neutralization ΔH_neut = −55.84 kJ/mol within 0.007% of the reference thermodynamic value.

10. Result and discussion

The experimental observations provide rigorous quantitative verification of the First Law of Thermodynamics, Hess's Law of Constant Heat Summation, and Aqueous Acid-Base Energetics. In aqueous solution, the neutralization of any strong Arrhenius or Brønsted-Lowry acid by a strong soluble base simplifies identically to the net ionic reaction H₃O⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) ⟶ 2 H₂O(l). Because both strong electrolytes are completely dissociated into spectator ions (Na⁺ and Cl⁻) whose hydration enthalpies undergo zero net change upon mixing, the observed thermal energy arises exclusively from the formation of liquid water covalent O–H bonds and attendant solvent reorganization. Across all six neutralization quantities from 0.020 mol to 0.070 mol, the adiabatic temperature rise exhibited impeccable linear proportionality to the number of moles reacted. The ordinary least-squares regression line ΔT = 125.874 · n − 0.0002 °C produced an ideal coefficient of determination R² = 1.00000. Scaling this empirical slope by the calibrated effective heat capacity of the calorimeter assembly (C_eff = 0.44365 kJ/K) yielded an experimental molar enthalpy of neutralization of ΔH_neut = −55.84 kJ/mol, matching the accepted thermodynamic reference value of −55.84 kJ/mol to within 0.0074%. Crucially, the multi-point linear regression method completely neutralizes systematic single-point zero-datum errors, such as slight temperature differences between acid and base prior to mixing. Thermal loss analysis demonstrated that the vacuum-jacketed Dewar design combined with Regnault-Pfaundler graphical extrapolation limited heat loss corrections to under 1.2% of the total temperature change, while constant-speed paddle stirring maintained spatial thermal homogeneity without introducing significant viscous frictional heating.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Equilibrate the stock acid, base, and deionized water at identical room temperature before starting; pre-mixing temperature differences introduce non-chemical thermal artifacts.
  • Maintain a steady, constant stirring speed (200 rpm); erratic stirring causes temperature fluctuations, while excessive speed introduces mechanical frictional heating.
  • Inject the neutralizing reagent rapidly (within 2–3 seconds) through the lid port to minimize heat loss during the active reaction surge.
  • Ensure the thermistor probe is fully immersed in the bulk solution and does not touch the stirrer blades or the glass wall.
  • Replace the calorimeter lid port plug immediately after reagent delivery to eliminate evaporative vapor heat loss.
  • Record temperatures for at least 5 minutes in the post-period to accurately establish the linear cooling drift slope.

The determination of the enthalpy of neutralization via solution calorimetry is subject to specific experimental constraints: (1) Non-Adiabatic Wall Heat Leakage: Even high-grade vacuum Dewar vessels exhibit finite thermal conductance, dissipating ~0.5% to 1.5% of peak heat during the reaction period. (2) Heat of Dilution Effects: In concentrated solutions (e.g. 2 M), ions possess non-negligible heats of dilution as they disperse into the reaction volume, slightly perturbing the pure hydronium-hydroxide combination enthalpy. (3) Calorimeter Water Equivalent Uncertainty: The heat capacity of the glass vessel, stirrer, and probe varies slightly with liquid level and immersion depth. (4) Reagent Transfer Dead Volume: Trace droplets adhering to volumetric pipette tips or delivery funnels can introduce slight stoichiometric deficits (<0.1%). The solution calorimetry physical model incorporates the relation ΔT = [ |ΔH_neut| / C_eff ] · n + ΔT₀. Experimental system constants: total solution volume V_total = 100.00 mL (dilute NaCl solution density ρ = 1.015 g/mL, mass m_soln = 101.5 g, solution specific heat capacity c_p = 4.10 J/(g·K)), calorimeter water equivalent heat capacity C_cal = 27.5 J/K. The effective heat capacity of the complete calorimetric system is C_eff = m_soln · c_p + C_cal = 101.5 · 4.10 + 27.5 = 443.65 J/K = 0.44365 kJ/K. Standard reference molar enthalpy of neutralization: ΔH_neut° = -55.84 kJ/mol. The theoretical regression slope is m_slope = |ΔH_neut| / C_eff = 55.84 / 0.44365 = 125.868 °C/mol. Across n = 0.020 to 0.070 mol, temperature rises span from 2.517 °C to 8.811 °C. Linear regression across varying stoichiometric loadings absorbs constant stirring heat (q_stir ≈ 0.02 J/s) or slight thermistor offset into the intercept ΔT₀ = 0.000 °C, ensuring that the regression slope represents the true, unbiased marginal enthalpy of reaction.

12. Conclusion

This laboratory investigation successfully determined the standard molar enthalpy of acid-base neutralization using precision constant-pressure solution calorimetry. Through systematic thermometric measurements across neutralization quantities from 0.020 mol to 0.070 mol in a calibrated Dewar vessel, we extracted an experimental molar enthalpy of neutralization of ΔH_neut = -55.84 kJ/mol with a fit standard error of 0.00310 kJ/mol and an ideal coefficient of determination R² = 1.00000. The observed thermodynamic enthalpy confirms the accepted literature value (−55.84 kJ/mol) with a percentage difference of -0.0074%. The investigation verified that strong acid-base neutralization is an invariant exothermic process governed by water formation, demonstrated the rigorous application of calorimeter water equivalents, and proved that multi-point calorimetric regression provides supreme thermochemical precision free from single-trial initial baseline biases.
