Physical chemistry assumptions and metrological limits

The simple bimolecular rate law r = k·[S₂O₈²⁻]·[I⁻] assumes that ionic strength is held constant and that reactant concentrations are low enough (< 0.1 M) for Debye-Hückel screening to apply. At very high ionic strength (I > 1.0 M), specific ion interaction parameters and ion-pairing (e.g., K⁺·S₂O₈²⁻) produce non-linear deviations from the Brønsted-Bjerrum equation. The physical simulation model computes the persulfate-iodide oxidation rate at 25.00 °C and ionic strength I = 0.20 M governed by r₀ = −d[S₂O₈²⁻]/dt = k·[S₂O₈²⁻]₀·[I⁻]₀, where the true second-order rate constant is k = 0.02200 L·mol⁻¹·s⁻¹. Fixed experimental conditions: excess iodide [I⁻]₀ = 0.0800 M and limiting thiosulfate Δ[S₂O₃²⁻]₀ = 0.00100 M. Under constant iodide, the reaction behaves with pseudo-first-order kinetics: r₀ = k'·[S₂O₈²⁻]₀ with k' = k·[I⁻]₀ = 0.02200 × 0.0800 = 0.001760 s⁻¹. The clock time to the sharp starch-triiodide flash transition satisfies Δt = Δ[S₂O₃²⁻]₀ / (2·r₀) = 0.000500 / r₀. For initial persulfate concentrations [S₂O₈²⁻]₀ from 10.0 mM to 60.0 mM (0.010 M to 0.060 M), initial rates span 1.760 × 10⁻⁵ M/s (Δt = 28.409 s) to 1.056 × 10⁻⁴ M/s (Δt = 4.735 s). Linear regression of r₀ × 10⁵ against [S₂O₈²⁻]₀ (in mM) yields slope m = 0.1760 (10⁻² s⁻¹), isolating k' = 0.001760 s⁻¹ and k = k' / [I⁻]₀ = 0.0220 L·mol⁻¹·s⁻¹ with R² = 1.00000; the multi-point initial rates formulation completely eliminates product inhibition and reverse reaction biases.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

To determine the reaction order with respect to peroxodisulfate (S₂O₈²⁻), evaluate the pseudo-first-order rate constant (k') and the second-order rate constant (k) for the oxidation of iodide by persulfate at 25.0 °C and constant ionic strength (I = 0.20 M) using the initial rates iodine clock method with spectrophotometric and visual monitoring, and verify the activated complex ionic strength dependence. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 10.0, 20.0, 30.0, 40.0, 50.0, 60.0 mM.

01

Stock Reagent Preparation and Temperature Equilibration

Prepare fresh stock solutions of 0.100 M K₂S₂O₈, 0.200 M KI, 0.0100 M Na₂S₂O₃, and 0.200 M KNO₃. Immerse all volumetric flasks in the 25.00 °C thermostatic water bath for 30 minutes to achieve thermal equilibrium.

02

Formulation of the Iodide-Thiosulfate-Starch Cocktail (Flask A)

Into Flask A, accurately pipette 20.00 mL of 0.200 M KI (yielding [I⁻] = 0.0800 M in 50 mL final volume), 5.00 mL of 0.0100 M Na₂S₂O₃, 2.00 mL of 1.0% starch indicator, and appropriate volumes of KNO₃ to maintain ionic strength at I = 0.200 M.

03

Preparation of the Variable Persulfate Solution (Flask B)

Into Flask B, pipette the calculated volume of 0.100 M K₂S₂O₈ corresponding to the desired initial concentration (5.00 mL for 10.0 mM, up to 30.00 mL for 60.0 mM), adding (NH₄)₂SO₄ as necessary to hold sulfate ionic strength constant, and dilute with water to 25.00 mL.

04

Rapid Mixing and Automatic Timing Initiation

Transfer Flask A into the water-jacketed reaction cell on the magnetic stirrer (400 rpm). Swiftly pour the contents of Flask B into the cell, instantaneously starting the automated photodiode stopwatch at the moment of mixing (t = 0).

05

Monitoring and Sudden Starch-Iodine Flash Endpoint Capture

Observe the crystal-clear reaction mixture. Maintain continuous agitation until the solution transitions instantaneously from completely colorless to an opaque, deep blue-black; the photodiode circuit detects the sudden drop in optical transmittance and halts the timer at Δt.

06

Repetition Across Concentration Series and Rate Constant Modeling

Repeat duplicate trials for [S₂O₈²⁻]₀ = 10.0, 20.0, 30.0, 40.0, 50.0, and 60.0 mM, compute initial rates r₀ = Δ[S₂O₃²⁻]₀ / (2·Δt), plot r₀ against [S₂O₈²⁻]₀, and perform linear regression to determine reaction orders and rate constants.
