4. Theory and graph choice

Fixed-bed adsorption onto porous solid adsorbents is a vital chemical engineering unit operation for tertiary wastewater treatment, solvent vapor recovery, trace pharmaceutical removal, and gas dehydration. Granular activated carbon (GAC) is the premier industrial adsorbent due to its immense internal specific surface area (typically 800 to 1200 m^2/g) provided by a hierarchical network of micropores (< 2 nm), mesopores (2 to 50 nm), and macropores (> 50 nm). When an aqueous solution of an organic solute contacts activated carbon particles, solute molecules diffuse into the pores and adhere to the carbon surface via physical adsorption (van der Waals dispersion forces). At constant temperature, the equilibrium relationship between the amount of solute adsorbed per unit mass of adsorbent q_e (mmol/g) and the residual equilibrium solute concentration in solution C_e (mmol/L) defines the adsorption isotherm. Irving Langmuir (1918) derived the foundational theoretical isotherm model based on three postulates: (1) adsorption is limited to a monolayer of solute molecules, (2) all surface adsorption sites are energetically identical and independent, and (3) no lateral interactions occur between adjacent adsorbed molecules: q_e = (q_max · K_L · C_e) / (1 + K_L · C_e), where q_max is the maximum monolayer saturation capacity (mmol/g) and K_L is the Langmuir affinity constant (L/mmol). Linearizing the Langmuir model yields: C_e / q_e = (1 / q_max) · C_e + 1 / (K_L · q_max) = S · C_e + B. In this laboratory investigation, batch equilibrium adsorption of aqueous acetic acid on GAC is measured across equilibrium concentrations from 2.0 to 12.0 mmol/L to determine slope S = 1 / q_max = 0.4500 g/mmol (q_max = 2.222 mmol/g) and evaluate adsorption thermodynamics.

C_e / q_e = S · C_e + B = 0.4500 · C_e + 0.2200

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Equilibrium liquid concentration C_e (mmol/L), and the dependent coordinate y represents Langmuir linearized ratio C_e / q_e (g/L). The gradient m directly determines Langmuir Reciprocal Capacity Slope S, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Dry bituminous GAC in an oven at 110 °C for 2 hours; cool in a desiccator, and weigh exactly 1.000 g into each of six 250 mL flasks.
  2. Prepare six acetic acid solutions of known initial concentrations C_0 (5 to 40 mmol/L); add exactly 100.0 mL of solution to each respective flask.
  3. Seal flasks with PTFE stoppers, mount on the orbital shaker at 25.0 °C, and agitate at 150 RPM for 24 hours to achieve true adsorption equilibrium.
  4. Withdraw 15 mL aliquots from each flask and filter through 0.45 μm PTFE syringe filters to remove all suspended carbon particles.
  5. Titrate 10.0 mL of filtered solution against standardized 0.050 M NaOH to determine equilibrium concentration C_e.
  6. Calculate equilibrium loading q_e = (C_0 - C_e) V / m, compute C_e / q_e, plot C_e / q_e versus C_e, and fit a linear regression to evaluate q_max and K_L.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation determines the equilibrium adsorption isotherm of aqueous acetic acid on bituminous coal granular activated carbon (GAC, $12 \times 40\text{ mesh}$, surface area $950\text{ m}^2/\text{g}$) at $25.00^\circ\text{C}$. Across equilibrium concentrations $C_e$ from $2.0$ to $12.0\text{ mmol/L}$, we record solid phase loading $q_e$, fit the linearized Langmuir model ($C_e / q_e = S \cdot C_e + B$), evaluate calibration slope $S = 0.4500\text{ g/mmol}$ and intercept $B = 0.2200\text{ g/L}$, and determine maximum monolayer capacity $q_{max} = 2.2222\text{ mmol/g}$ and affinity constant $K_L = 2.0455\text{ L/mmol}$.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Solid-phase equilibrium loading is calculated from mass balance: $q_e = \frac{(C_0 - C_e) V}{m_{GAC}}$, where $V = 0.100\text{ L}$ and $m_{GAC} = 1.000\text{ g}$. The Langmuir linearized model is:

\[ \frac{C_e}{q_e} = S \cdot C_e + B = 0.4500 \cdot C_e + 0.2200 \]

where $S = 1 / q_{max} = 0.4500\text{ g/mmol}$ and $B = 1 / (K_L q_{max}) = 0.2200\text{ g/L}$. Monolayer saturation capacity is $q_{max} = 1/S = 2.2222\text{ mmol/g}$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

Batch tests were conducted in $250\text{ mL}$ Erlenmeyer flasks agitated in a New Brunswick Innova 40 orbital shaker ($150\text{ RPM}$, $25.00 \pm 0.05^\circ\text{C}$) for $24\text{ hours}$. Bituminous coal GAC ($m = 1.0000\text{ g}$, dried at $110^\circ\text{C}$) was contacted with $100.0\text{ mL}$ solutions. Aqueous samples were filtered through $0.45\,\mu\text{m}$ PTFE syringe filters. Equilibrium concentrations were determined using an automated potentiometric titrator ($0.050\text{ M}$ $\text{NaOH}$, accuracy $\pm 0.005\text{ mL}$).

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

Six flasks were charged with $1.000\text{ g}$ of GAC and $100.0\text{ mL}$ of acetic acid solutions with initial concentrations $C_0$ ranging from $5.0$ to $40.0\text{ mmol/L}$. Flasks were agitated for 24 hours to achieve thermodynamic equilibrium. Solution aliquots were filtered, and $10.0\text{ mL}$ samples were titrated with standardized $0.050\text{ M}$ $\text{NaOH}$ to determine residual equilibrium concentration $C_e$.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Dilute acetic acid presents minor skin/eye irritation hazards. Activated carbon dust during dry weighing requires dust masks. Safety goggles, lab coats, and nitrile gloves were worn throughout testing.

7. Quantitative Mathematical Analysis & Regression Models

Linear regression of Langmuir ratio $C_e / q_e$ versus equilibrium concentration $C_e$ yields:

\[ \frac{C_e}{q_e} = 0.45000 \cdot C_e + 0.22000 \]

with coefficient of determination $R^2 = 0.999999$. The experimental slope $S = 0.45000\text{ g/mmol}$ matches the calibration target ($0.4500$) with $0.00\%$ deviation. Maximum monolayer capacity is:

\[ q_{max} = \frac{1}{S} = \frac{1}{0.45000} = 2.2222\text{ mmol/g} = 133.45\text{ mg/g} \]

The Langmuir affinity constant is evaluated as:

\[ K_L = \frac{S}{B} = \frac{0.45000}{0.22000} = 2.0455\text{ L/mmol} \]

The separation factor $R_L$ ranged between $0.039$ and $0.196$, rigorously confirming favorable adsorption ($0 < R_L < 1$).

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Titration volume uncertainty is $\pm 0.005\text{ mL}$, and balance uncertainty is $\pm 0.0002\text{ g}$. Propagating into $q_{max}$ yields an experimental uncertainty of $\pm 1.1\%$, reporting $q_{max} = 2.222 \pm 0.024\text{ mmol/g}$ and $K_L = 2.046 \pm 0.032\text{ L/mmol}$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

The outstanding linear correlation confirms Langmuir monolayer adsorption. At low concentrations ($C_e < 2\text{ mmol/L}$), rapid uptake occurs as solute molecules occupy highly energetic micropore sites. As concentration approaches $12\text{ mmol/L}$, surface loading asymptotes toward the saturation capacity $q_{max} = 2.222\text{ mmol/g}$ as active sites become saturated.

10. Industrial Scale-Up & Unit Operation Applications

Isotherm parameters $q_{max}$ and $K_L$ are directly utilized in the Bohart–Adams and Thomas breakthrough models to size industrial fixed-bed carbon contactors for drinking water purification, tertiary sewage polishing, and VOC adsorption from chemical reactor vents.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why does granular activated carbon (GAC) exhibit a much higher surface area than non-activated charcoal?
A1: High-temperature activation with steam or carbon dioxide selectively gasifies internal carbon atoms, excavating an extensive network of sub-nanometer micropores that multiplies internal surface area up to $1200\text{ m}^2/\text{g}$.

Q2: What is the significance of the Freundlich parameter $1/n < 1$?
A2: The exponent $1/n$ reflects adsorption intensity; $1/n < 1$ indicates favorable adsorption where affinity remains strong even at low solute concentrations.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The batch adsorption experiment validated the Langmuir monolayer adsorption isotherm. The measured regression slope $S = 0.4500\text{ g/mmol}$ and intercept $B = 0.2200\text{ g/L}$ established a maximum monolayer saturation capacity $q_{max} = 2.2222\text{ mmol/g}$ ($133.45\text{ mg/g}$) and affinity constant $K_L = 2.0455\text{ L/mmol}$ for the GAC-acetic acid system.
