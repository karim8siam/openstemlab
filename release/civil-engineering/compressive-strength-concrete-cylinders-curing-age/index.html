FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the uniaxial compressive strength (f'c) of standard cast concrete cylinders across curing ages (3 to 28 days) according to ASTM C39 / BS EN 12390-3, and quantify the logarithmic hydration strength maturation rate. Standardized parameter settings: 3.0, 7.0, 14.0, 21.0, 28.0 days.

01

Specimen Retrieval & Surface Inspection

Remove cured concrete cylinders from the moist curing tank at designated maturities (3, 7, 14, 21, and 28 days); wipe excess surface moisture and verify identification markings.

02

Dimensional & Density Verification

Measure specimen diameter across two perpendicular diameters at mid-height with callipers to 0.1 mm; measure height and weigh to determine wet unit weight.

03

End Face Capping Verification

Inspect end bearing planes using a straightedge; apply high-strength sulfur capping compound if end planarity departs from 0.05 mm tolerance.

04

Specimen Centering in Compression Machine

Clean bearing surfaces of upper and lower platen; place cylinder centrally on the lower platen and align axis with spherical seated head center.

05

Axial Loading to Brittle Fracture

Apply continuous, shock-free compressive load at a controlled loading rate of 0.25 ± 0.05 MPa/s until ultimate load failure; record peak compressive force P_max.

06

Fracture Mode Classification

Record fracture pattern (Type 1 cone, Type 2 cone-and-split, Type 3 columnar shear) according to ASTM C39 visual standards.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

Uniaxial compressive strength (f'c) represents the primary mechanical design parameter in structural concrete engineering, governing structural capacity, elastic modulus, shear resistance, and durability classification. Under standard specifications (ASTM C39, BS EN 12390-3), standard cylindrical specimens (150 mm diameter by 300 mm height) are cast, moist-cured at 23.0 ± 2.0 °C and relative humidity ≥ 95%, and crushed in a stiff hydraulic compression testing frame at a prescribed axial loading rate of 0.25 ± 0.05 MPa/s until fracture. As anhydrous tricalcium silicate (C3S) and dicalcium silicate (C2S) react with water, they precipitate calcium silicate hydrate (C-S-H) gel, which progressively densifies the paste microstructure by filling capillary pore networks. Strength gain follows a characteristic logarithmic maturation curve where approximately 40% of 28-day strength is achieved at 3 days, 65% to 70% at 7 days, and 100% at the benchmark 28-day maturity. In this laboratory investigation, compressive crushing tests are executed on moist-cured cylinders across curing ages from 3 to 28 days to calibrate the strength maturation kinetics and verify structural compliance with Grade C25/30 specifications.
