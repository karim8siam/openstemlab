4. Theory and graph choice

Accurate measurement of open-channel water discharge is essential for fair water allocation in irrigation districts, monitoring canal conveyance efficiency, and preventing canal bank overtopping. The Parshall flume, developed by Ralph L. Parshall, is a specialized self-cleaning Venturi-type open channel constriction widely recognized as the international standard for irrigation flow measurement. It features a converging contracting entrance section with a level floor, a contracted throat section with a downward-sloping floor, and a diverging expanding exit section with an upward-sloping floor. The drop in throat elevation induces critical depth and supercritical flow, establishing a unique relationship between the upstream water depth H_a (measured in a stilling well at two-thirds the length of the converging section) and discharge Q. In free-flow conditions, the rating equation follows a power law Q = C·H_a^n (or ln Q = n·ln H_a + ln C). When downstream tailwater rises such that the submergence ratio S = H_b / H_a exceeds the critical limit (0.60 for small flumes, 0.70 for medium flumes), flow transitions to submerged flow requiring a submergence correction. In this experiment, a calibrated flume is tested across multiple flow rates to extract n and C.

ln(Q) = n · ln(H_a) + ln(C) = 1.5500 · ln(H_a) + 0.3800

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents ln(Upstream head H_a in m), and the dependent axis y represents ln(Discharge Q in m³/s). The gradient m directly determines Parshall Flume Exponent n, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Level the Parshall flume longitudinally and laterally in the channel. Set the stilling well point gauges to exactly zero against the flat crest of the flume converging floor.
  2. Lower the downstream tailwater gate completely to ensure free-fall discharge, ensuring the submergence ratio H_b / H_a remains well below 0.60.
  3. Regulate pump throttle to deliver initial low discharge producing an upstream head H_a = 0.05 m (ln H_a = -2.9957), allowing water levels in the stilling wells to stabilize.
  4. Record the upstream head H_a from the stilling well and the reference discharge Q from the magnetic flowmeter simultaneously over 3 minutes.
  5. Increment pump discharge in five uniform stages to achieve upstream heads of 0.08, 0.12, 0.18, 0.27, and 0.40 m, recording H_a and Q for each flow rate.
  6. Transform H_a and Q into natural logarithms, plot ln Q versus ln H_a, and perform linear regression to determine the exponent n and coefficient C.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Verify that the flume crest floor is perfectly level across both lateral and longitudinal directions using a precision spirit level.
  • Ensure stilling well piezometer tubes are free of trapped air bubbles and sediment silt.
  • Maintain approach flow conditions smooth and symmetrical without surface waves or cross-currents.
  • Check that the flume operates under free-flow conditions (H_b / H_a < 0.60) during calibration runs.

Flumes must be fabricated strictly to standardized ASTM/USBR dimensions; geometric scaling errors invalidate published discharge coefficients. Evaluated using a standard 6-inch (15.2 cm) throat width Parshall flume in free-flow regime across upstream heads from 0.05 m to 0.40 m.
