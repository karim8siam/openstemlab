1. Title and aim

Hohmann Coplanar Orbital Transfer and Delta-V Velocity Budgets. Calculate impulse velocity increments Delta V_1 and Delta V_2 required to transfer between circular orbits and evaluate delta-v budgets across orbit radius ratios.

4. Theory and graph choice

In astrodynamics and space mission design, transferring a spacecraft between two coplanar circular orbits with minimum propellant consumption is achieved using the classical Hohmann transfer ellipse, proposed by Walter Hohmann in 1925. The transfer orbit is an elliptical trajectory tangent to both the initial orbit of radius r_1 and the final target orbit of radius r_2. The semi-major axis of the transfer ellipse is a_trans = (r_1 + r_2) / 2. The transfer is executed using two instantaneous tangential thrust burns (delta-v impulses). The first burn Delta V_1 at periapsis accelerates the spacecraft from circular orbit velocity v_1 = sqrt(mu / r_1) onto the transfer ellipse: Delta V_1 = v_1 * [sqrt(2 * r_2 / (r_1 + r_2)) - 1]. After coasting through a true anomaly of 180 degrees (one-half of an orbit, taking transfer time t_trans = pi * sqrt(a_trans^3 / mu)), the spacecraft reaches apoapsis at radius r_2. The second burn Delta V_2 circularizes the orbit at final velocity v_2 = sqrt(mu / r_2): Delta V_2 = v_2 * [1 - sqrt(2 * r_1 / (r_1 + r_2))]. Total mission delta-v is Delta V_tot = Delta V_1 + Delta V_2. The propellant mass required scales exponentially with total delta-v according to Konstantin Tsiolkovsky's Rocket Equation: Delta m_fuel = m_0 * (1 - exp(-Delta V_tot / (I_sp * g_0))). In this laboratory experiment, an orbital maneuver simulator computes first-burn velocity increments Delta V_1 across transfer radius parameters from 0.08 to 0.38, linearizing the scaling against baseline LEO circular velocity v_1 = 7.780 km/s.

\Delta V_1 = v_1 \left[\sqrt{\frac{2 r_2}{r_1 + r_2}} - 1\right] \implies \Delta V_1 = m \cdot x + b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Radius Ratio Parameter sqrt(2*r_2 / (r_1+r_2)) - 1, and the dependent coordinate y represents First Burn Velocity Increment Delta V_1 (km/s). The slope m directly extracts Baseline LEO Velocity v_1, while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Set initial circular Low Earth Orbit at altitude h_1 = 300 km (r_1 = 6678.14 km), confirming circular speed v_1 = 7.780 km/s.
  2. Define target circular orbit radii r_2 corresponding to parameter x = sqrt(2*r_2/(r_1+r_2)) - 1 = 0.08, 0.14, 0.20, 0.26, 0.32, and 0.38.
  3. Apply impulsive tangential burn Delta V_1 at periapsis to inject spacecraft into the Hohmann transfer ellipse.
  4. Propagate the spacecraft along the elliptical arc over transfer time t_trans = pi * sqrt(a_trans^3 / mu) until apoapsis arrival.
  5. Apply circularizing impulse Delta V_2 at apoapsis to match target circular speed v_2, recording total delta-v budget.
  6. Plot Delta V_1 versus the radius parameter x, perform linear regression to determine slope v_1 = 7.780 km/s, and verify Tsiolkovsky propellant mass.

9. Uncertainty and comparison

Percentage difference = |estimate − theoretical value| / |theoretical value| × 100 = 0.0000%.

For n = 6 observations, Σresidual² = 5.423e-31; s² = Σresidual²/(n − 2). Gradient standard error = √[s²/Σ(x − x̄)²] = 1.467e-15. Intercept standard error = √[s²(1/n + x̄²/Σ(x − x̄)²)] = 3.694e-16.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure the first burn is executed precisely tangential to the velocity vector; radial velocity components waste propellant delta-v.
  • Verify that finite-burn duration losses (gravity losses) are accounted for if using low-thrust electric or long chemical burns.
  • Confirm that the target body (e.g. Moon or planet) phase angle is correctly aligned at departure (Hohmann launch window).
  • Calculate combined plane-change delta-v at the nodal crossing to avoid expensive separate inclination burns.

Hohmann transfer assumes impulsive (infinitesimally short) velocity changes; continuous low-thrust ion engines require spiral trajectories. Linear regression of the first-burn velocity increment Delta V_1 against the radius parameter yields a baseline circular velocity v_1 = 7.7800 km/s with R^2 > 0.999999.
