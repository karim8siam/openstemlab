Civil engineering mechanics & structural foundations of Hydraulic Jump Energy.

A hydraulic jump is a rapid, highly turbulent open-channel flow phenomenon occurring when supercritical flow (Froude number Fr > 1) abruptly transitions to subcritical flow (Fr < 1). In hydraulic infrastructure—including spillway stilling basins, sluice gate chutes, and canal drop structures—hydraulic jumps are intentionally generated as primary energy dissipators to convert catastrophic kinetic energy into internal thermal turbulence, preventing severe downstream bed scour and riverbank erosion. Applying the one-dimensional momentum conservation equation across the control volume while neglecting boundary friction yields the classical Bélanger equation (1828) relating initial supercritical depth y_1 to subcritical sequent (conjugate) depth y_2: y_2 / y_1 = 0.5 · [√(1 + 8·Fr_1²) - 1], where upstream Froude number Fr_1 = v_1 / √(g·y_1). Specific energy drops dramatically across the jump: head loss ΔE = E_1 - E_2 = (y_2 - y_1)³ / (4·y_1·y_2), representing macro-turbulent roller energy dissipation. Depending on initial Froude number Fr_1, jumps are categorized as undular (1.0 < Fr_1 < 1.7), weak (1.7 < Fr_1 < 2.5), oscillating (2.5 < Fr_1 < 4.5), steady (4.5 < Fr_1 < 9.0), or strong/choppy (Fr_1 > 9.0). In this laboratory flume experiment, supercritical flow is discharged beneath an adjustable sluice gate, and a downstream tailwater weir forces hydraulic jump stabilization to verify conjugate depths and energy dissipation efficiency.

Governing Momentum Principle in Rapid Open Channel Transitions

In a horizontal rectangular flume of width $B$, neglecting boundary shear stress over the short jump length $L_j \approx 6 y_2$, hydrostatic pressure forces and momentum flux balance:

\[ P_1 - P_2 = M_2 - M_1 \] \[ \frac{1}{2} \rho g B y_1^2 - \frac{1}{2} \rho g B y_2^2 = \rho Q (v_2 - v_1) \]

Substituting continuity $Q = B y_1 v_1 = B y_2 v_2$ and dividing by $\frac{1}{2} \rho g B$:

\[ y_1^2 - y_2^2 = \frac{2 q^2}{g} \left( \frac{1}{y_2} - \frac{1}{y_1} \right) = \frac{2 q^2 (y_1 - y_2)}{g y_1 y_2} \]

where $q = Q/B$ is discharge per unit width. Factoring $(y_1 - y_2)(y_1 + y_2)$ and dividing by $(y_1 - y_2)$ (since $y_1 \neq y_2$):

\[ y_1 + y_2 = \frac{2 q^2}{g y_1 y_2} = \frac{2 v_1^2 y_1}{g y_2} \]

Multiplying through by $y_2/y_1^2$ and defining upstream Froude number $Fr_1^2 = v_1^2 / (g y_1)$:

\[ \left( \frac{y_2}{y_1} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{y_2}{y_1} \right) - 2 Fr_1^2 = 0 \]

Solving the quadratic equation for positive depth ratio yields the historic Bélanger equation:

\[ \frac{y_2}{y_1} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \sqrt{1 + 8 Fr_1^2} - 1 \right) \]

Energy Loss and Dissipation Efficiency

Specific energy at sections 1 and 2 is $E_1 = y_1 + \frac{v_1^2}{2g}$ and $E_2 = y_2 + \frac{v_2^2}{2g}$. The head loss across the jump is:

\[ \Delta E = E_1 - E_2 = \frac{(y_2 - y_1)^3}{4 y_1 y_2} \]

The relative energy dissipation efficiency is $\eta = \Delta E / E_1$.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

Why is the momentum equation used instead of the energy equation to derive the Bélanger relation?

Across a hydraulic jump, intense turbulent mixing, surface air entrainment, and macroscopic eddies dissipate a large unknown amount of internal energy as heat, making the energy equation indeterminate; however, external friction on the short flume boundary is negligible, allowing conservation of momentum to solve conjugate depths exactly.

What is the physical meaning of the Froude number in open-channel hydraulics?

The Froude number Fr = v / √(gy) represents the ratio of inertial forces to gravitational forces, and equals the ratio of flow velocity v to the celerity c of elementary gravity surface waves.

Why cannot an open-channel transition from subcritical to supercritical occur abruptly as a 'negative' hydraulic jump?

A negative jump would require specific energy to increase spontaneously without external energy input, violating the Second Law of Thermodynamics (ΔE would be negative).

What happens to the location of the hydraulic jump if tailwater depth y_t is lower than theoretical sequent depth y_2?

The jump is swept downstream out of the stilling basin (repelled jump), causing high-velocity supercritical flow to attack the unprotected river bed.

How do chute blocks, baffle piers, and end sills improve stilling basin performance?

They exert large drag forces on the incoming jet, stabilizing jump position, shortening required basin length by up to 50%, and accelerating turbulent dissipation.

What is the primary operational hazard of an oscillating hydraulic jump (2.5 < Fr_1 < 4.5)?

Oscillating jumps generate severe surface wave surges that travel far downstream, overtopping canal banks and eroding riprap linings.

Experimental precautions and laboratory safety

  • Position the upstream point gauge outside the curved drawdown surface immediately prior to the turbulent jump toe.
  • Wait for the downstream surface roller to fully settle before measuring sequent depth y_2 to prevent turbulent air froth bias.
  • Adjust tailwater weir gradually to prevent surging jump displacement that overtops flume glass sidewalls.
  • Ensure flume glass walls are clean to clearly observe the boundary separation line of the bottom turbulent roller.

Theoretical assumptions & boundary condition limits

  • The civil engineering physical models apply within the specified calibrated operational window (1.5 to 4.5 ratio).
  • Assumes homogeneous, isotropic continuum material behavior without unmodeled micro-fracture anomalies.
  • Continuous sensor readings reflect ordinary least-squares regression across successive observations.
  • Extreme inelastic yielding or cyclic degradation requires non-linear constitutive continuum damage mechanics.
