Agricultural assumptions and boundary condition limits

Rigid-wall cells do not permit confining radial stress, making them susceptible to edge effects in swelling clay soils. Evaluated using undisturbed sandy loam soil core (diameter 7.5 cm, length 10.0 cm, dry bulk density 1.45 g/cm³) using de-aired water at 20°C.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the saturated hydraulic conductivity (K_sat) of agricultural soil cores using a constant-head permeameter and verify Darcy's linear filtration law. Standardized parameter settings: 0.5, 0.9, 1.3, 1.7, 2.1, 2.5 m/m.

01

Soil Core Preparation & Saturation

Trim the ends of the undisturbed soil core flush with the sample cylinder. Place the core in a vacuum saturator and slowly saturate from the bottom upward with de-aired water over 24 hours to eliminate entrapped air.

02

Permeameter Cell Assembly

Assemble the saturated core into the permeameter cell between fine mesh screens and saturated porous stones, tightening tie rods to seal against side-wall bypass leakage.

03

Constant-Head Gradient Setup

Connect the constant-head reservoir to establish an initial hydraulic head loss ΔH = 5.0 cm across the 10.0 cm core (gradient i = 0.50 m/m).

04

Steady Effluent Collection

Allow water to flow until effluent discharge reaches steady state, then collect water in a graduated cylinder over exactly 5.0 minutes, logging collected volume Q.

05

Gradient Stepping

Increment hydraulic head gradient in four stages up to i = 2.5 m/m (ΔH = 9.0, 13.0, 17.0, 21.0, 25.0 cm), collecting timed effluent for each gradient.

06

Darcy Velocity & K_sat Calculation

Calculate Darcy velocity v = Q / (A·Δt) (cm/min), plot v against hydraulic gradient i, and perform linear regression through the origin to determine K_sat.
