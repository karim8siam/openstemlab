Physical chemistry assumptions and metrological limits

The determination of surface tension via drop-weight stalagmometry is subject to specific experimental constraints: (1) Capillary Edge Boundary Pinning: Wetting fluids must pin cleanly to the sharp external circumference of the ground-glass tip; if the glass is contaminated, the liquid film can creep up the outer cylindrical wall, increasing the effective radius r. (2) Dynamic Surface Age and Adsorption Kinetics: For pure water, surface tension equilibrates in milliseconds; however, for surfactant solutions, slow diffusion to the dilating interface produces dynamic surface tension effects requiring long bubble-pressure or drop-shape analysis. (3) Satellite Droplet Recoil: During Rayleigh-Plateau neck rupture, microscopic satellite droplets (<1% of mass) can recoil or mist into ambient air. (4) Evaporative Vapor Deficit: Over a 60-drop run lasting ~10 minutes, minute evaporation through the needle-valve vent must be controlled with vapor-saturated pre-chambers. The drop-weight stalagmometry physical model embodies the relation m_total = m_drop · N + m₀, where m_drop = (2π · r · γ · φ) / g. Calibrated apparatus constants: stalagmometer external tip radius r = 0.003500 m (3.500 mm), pure water surface tension γ = 0.07280 N/m (72.80 mN/m) at 20.0 °C, water density ρ = 998.2 kg/m³, standard acceleration of gravity g = 9.80665 m/s². The single-drop volume is V = 0.10058 cm³ = 1.0058 × 10⁻⁷ m³, giving the dimensionless shape parameter r / V^{1/3} = 0.00350 / (1.0058 × 10⁻⁷)^{1/3} = 0.7526. The corresponding Harkins-Jordan correction factor is exactly φ = 0.6150. Under these parameters, the theoretical single-drop mass is m_drop = (2π · 0.00350 · 0.07280 · 0.6150) / 9.80665 = 0.00010040 kg = 0.10040 g/drop. Multi-point linear regression across N = 10 to 60 drops yields an empirical slope of m_slope = 0.10040 g/drop with R² = 1.00000. Any constant weighing bottle tare offset or initial wetting residue is absorbed into m₀ = 0.0000 g, guaranteeing that the slope serves as an unbiased estimator of single-drop mass.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the absolute liquid surface tension of ultra-pure water at 20.0 °C via precision drop-weight stalagmometry, applying Tate's law and Harkins-Jordan hydrodynamic detachment corrections across systematic drop count series, and extracting surface tension using ordinary least-squares regression. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60 drops.

01

Stalagmometer Cleaning and Tip Degreasing

Clean the stalagmometer with warm chromic-sulfuric acid cleaning mixture, rinse thoroughly with 500 mL of boiling deionized water, wash with absolute ethanol, and dry under filtered nitrogen to ensure the flat tip is perfectly hydrophilic and free of organic surfactant films.

02

Thermostatic Temperature Equilibration

Mount the stalagmometer inside the circulating glass water jacket set to 20.0 °C. Charge the upper reservoir with ultra-pure deionized water and allow 15 minutes for thermal equilibrium.

03

Droplet Detachment Rate Calibration

Adjust the micro-needle valve at the top of the stalagmometer until pendent droplets form smoothly and detach at a rate of exactly one drop every 8 to 10 seconds, ensuring quasi-static detachment without inertial jetting.

04

Tared Vessel Placement and Drop Collection

Tare a clean, dry, stoppered weighing bottle on the analytical balance to ±0.0001 g. Position the bottle directly beneath the stalagmometer tip inside the draft-shielded enclosure.

05

Multi-Drop Gravimetric Run

Uncap the weighing bottle, count exactly 10 drops as they detach from the tip into the bottle, immediately replace the stopper to prevent evaporation, and record the mass on the analytical balance.

06

Systematic Drop Count Series and Regression

Repeat the procedure for 20, 30, 40, 50, and 60 drops. Regress total collected mass against drop count to obtain the single drop mass m_drop, compute the Harkins-Jordan factor φ(r/V^{1/3}), and calculate absolute surface tension γ.
