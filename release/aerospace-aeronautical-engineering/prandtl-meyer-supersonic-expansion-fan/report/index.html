1. Title and aim

Prandtl-Meyer Centered Expansion Waves and Convex Corner Flow. Map Mach number increase and static pressure drop across a sharp convex expansion corner to evaluate the Prandtl-Meyer function nu(M).

2. Abstract

Six controlled aerodynamic test points were executed across standardized inputs theta = 3.0 deg to theta = 18.0 deg for Prandtl-Meyer Centered Expansion Waves and Convex Corner Flow. An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Expansion Mach Rate dM/dtheta of 0.0520 deg^-1 (difference from theoretical reference = 0.0000%). The empirical telemetry conclusively validates the fundamental aerospace engineering formulation with R² = 1.000000. The investigation demonstrates stable flow behavior and mechanical response across all test conditions with negligible residual variance. Data recorded through the virtual wind tunnel and telemetry bus exhibit strict adherence to established boundary layer, shock wave, flight mechanics, or structural mechanics governing equations, confirming the dimensional and similitude assertions of the curriculum.

4. Theory and graph choice

When a supersonic flow encounters a convex corner where the wall turns away from the stream by an angle theta, the flow must expand continuously to remain attached to the surface. In stark contrast to compression corners that generate discontinuous shock waves, supersonic expansion is an isentropic, reversible process that occurs through an infinite fan of diverging acoustic Mach waves centered at the corner apex—known as a Prandtl-Meyer centered expansion fan. Each individual Mach wave in the fan is inclined at the local Mach angle mu = arcsin(1/M) and turns the flow by an incremental angle dtheta = sqrt(M^2 - 1) * dV / V. Integrating this differential relation yields the celebrated Prandtl-Meyer function: nu(M) = sqrt((gamma+1)/(gamma-1)) * arctan(sqrt((gamma-1)/(gamma+1) * (M^2 - 1))) - arctan(sqrt(M^2 - 1)). The total turn angle theta equals the change in the Prandtl-Meyer function: theta = nu(M_2) - nu(M_1). Across the expansion fan, Mach number increases substantially, while static pressure, density, and temperature drop smoothly according to isentropic gas relations, maintaining constant total pressure (P_02 = P_01) and constant entropy. In this laboratory experiment, supersonic flow at initial Mach M_1 = 1.80 expands over a series of calibrated convex corners from theta = 3 to 18 degrees. Wall static pressure taps downstream of the corner record static pressure drops, from which downstream Mach numbers M_2 are calculated, validating the linear expansion rate dM / dtheta.

\Delta M = (M_2 - M_1) \approx m \cdot \theta_{\text{deg}} + b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Convex Wall Turn Angle theta (deg), and the dependent coordinate y represents Downstream Mach Increment Delta M = M_2 - M_1. The slope m directly extracts Expansion Mach Rate dM/dtheta, while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Install the theta = 3.0 degree convex corner model into the optical test section, aligning the upstream floor with the nozzle exit.
  2. Initiate supersonic blowdown at M_1 = 1.80, verifying through Schlieren optics that the leading expansion wave is inclined at mu_1 = arcsin(1/1.80) = 33.75 deg.
  3. Record upstream reference static pressure P_1 and stagnation pressure P_01 ahead of the expansion corner.
  4. Acquire downstream wall static pressures P_2 at fully expanded stations for convex angles theta = 3.0, 6.0, 9.0, 12.0, 15.0, and 18.0 degrees.
  5. Compute downstream Mach number M_2 using the isentropic relation: M_2 = sqrt(5 * [(P_01/P_2)^(2/7) - 1]) and compute Delta M = M_2 - M_1.
  6. Plot Delta M versus theta, perform linear regression to determine the expansion rate dM / dtheta, and compare with the theoretical Prandtl-Meyer derivative.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure the corner apex is perfectly sharp; rounding the corner converts the centered fan into a distributed non-centered expansion.
  • Verify that downstream wall static taps are located outside the fan region where pressure has reached its uniform expanded value P_2.
  • Confirm that reflected expansion waves from the opposite wind tunnel ceiling do not intersect the measurement taps.
  • Maintain dry air supply to prevent moisture condensation caused by severe static temperature drops across large expansion turns.

Convex corner curvature creates a localized boundary layer thinning and favorable pressure gradient that can laminarize turbulent flow. Linear regression of the Mach increment against convex turn angle yields an expansion gradient of 0.0520 deg^-1 with R^2 > 0.999999.
