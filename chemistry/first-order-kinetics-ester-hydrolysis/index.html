Pseudo-First-Order Chemical Kinetics: Acid-Catalyzed Hydrolysis of Methyl Acetate

To determine the pseudo-first-order rate constant (k') and reaction half-life (t₁/₂) for the acid-catalyzed hydrolysis of methyl acetate in 1.000 M hydrochloric acid at 25.00 ± 0.02 °C, verify first-order integrated rate kinetics via ice-water quenched titrimetry against standardized 0.200 M sodium hydroxide, and compare conventional infinity metrology with the Guggenheim infinity-free formulation. Physical chemistry assumptions and metrological limits

The pseudo-first-order approximation requires that water remain in massive stoichiometric excess and that the hydronium ion activity remain constant. At ester concentrations exceeding 1.0 M, the depletion of water and the formation of significant concentrations of weak acetic acid introduce minor deviations. Furthermore, heating the reaction mixture to 60 °C to determine V_∞ can induce minor volatile ester evaporation or secondary esterification equilibrium shifts if the digestion vessel is not hermetically sealed. Titrations must be executed rapidly (< 90 seconds) post-quench to avoid post-sampling hydrolysis warming errors. The physical simulation engine numerically integrates the multi-component kinetic and chemical equilibrium differential equations governing the acid-catalyzed hydrolysis of methyl acetate at 25.00 ± 0.02 °C in 1.000 M HCl. Baseline formulation: 5.00 mL of chilled methyl acetate (density d = 0.932 g/mL, molar mass M = 74.08 g/mol) is rapidly introduced into 100.0 mL of 1.000 M HCl, establishing an initial ester concentration [ester]₀ = (5.00 × 0.932 / 74.08) / 0.100 = 0.6291 M. Quenched aliquots of 5.00 mL are transferred into 25 mL of crushed ice-water (< 2 °C) and titrated with standardized 0.2000 M NaOH using phenolphthalein indicator. The stoichiometric titration endpoints evaluate to: V₀ = (0.00500 L × 1.000 M) / 0.200 M = 12.50 mL (neutralization of 0.00250 mol HCl catalyst); maximum acetic acid generated at 100% conversion = 0.00500 L × 0.6291 M = 0.003145 mol, consuming an additional 15.73 mL of titrant, yielding the infinity titer V_∞ = 12.50 + 15.73 = 28.23 mL. The true pseudo-first-order rate constant is k' = 0.006850 min⁻¹ (4.110 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹), corresponding to a reaction half-life t₁/₂ = ln(2)/k' = 101.19 minutes. For scheduled sampling intervals across 15.0, 30.0, 45.0, 60.0, 90.0, and 120.0 minutes, the simulated burette readings evaluate to 14.04, 15.42, 16.67, 17.80, 19.74, and 21.32 mL, generating logarithmic values y = ln[(V_∞ − V₀)/(V_∞ − V_t)] of 0.1030, 0.2053, 0.3080, 0.4109, 0.6167, and 0.8226. Linear regression of y against elapsed time t yields slope m = k' = 0.006855 min⁻¹ and intercept b = −0.00023 with an ideal correlation coefficient R² = 0.999999, isolating the rate constant within 0.08% of the accepted benchmark.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

To determine the pseudo-first-order rate constant (k') and reaction half-life (t₁/₂) for the acid-catalyzed hydrolysis of methyl acetate in 1.000 M hydrochloric acid at 25.00 ± 0.02 °C, verify first-order integrated rate kinetics via ice-water quenched titrimetry against standardized 0.200 M sodium hydroxide, and compare conventional infinity metrology with the Guggenheim infinity-free formulation. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 15.0, 30.0, 45.0, 60.0, 90.0, 120.0 min.

01

Reagent Preparation and Titrant Standardization

Prepare and standardize reagents: standard 0.200 M sodium hydroxide (standardized against potassium hydrogen phthalate), 1.000 M hydrochloric acid, and pure analytical-grade methyl acetate.

02

Thermal Conditioning of Reaction Mixture

Thermostat 100.0 mL of 1.000 M HCl solution in a ground-glass reaction flask inside the 25.00 ± 0.02 °C circulating water bath for 20 minutes.

03

Reaction Initiation and Time Zero (t = 0)

Rapidly pipet 5.00 mL of chilled methyl acetate into the acid solution, simultaneously starting the digital stopwatch timer (t = 0), and swirl vigorously to achieve complete homogeneity.

04

Zero-Time Blank Aliquot Titration (V₀)

Immediately withdraw a 5.00 mL aliquot at t ≈ 0, discharge into 25 mL of ice-cold deionized water to arrest hydrolysis, add 2 drops of phenolphthalein, and titrate to faint pink with 0.200 M NaOH to record V₀ (12.50 mL).

05

Periodic Aliquot Sampling and Quenched Titration

At scheduled time intervals (15.0, 30.0, 45.0, 60.0, 90.0, and 120.0 minutes), withdraw 5.00 mL aliquots, quench in ice water, and titrate rapidly with 0.200 M NaOH to record V_t.

06

Infinity Titer Evaluation (V_∞)

To obtain the infinity titer V_∞, transfer 20 mL of reaction mixture to a sealed flask, heat in a 60 °C water bath for 30 minutes to achieve quantitative hydrolysis, cool to 25.0 °C, titrate 5.00 mL, and record V_∞ (28.23 mL).
