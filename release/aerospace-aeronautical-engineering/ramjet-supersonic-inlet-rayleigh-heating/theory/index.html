Aerodynamic and physical foundations of Ramjet Aerodynamics & Heating.

The ramjet represents the purest and most mechanically elegant air-breathing propulsion system, eliminating all rotating turbomachinery (compressors and turbines) in favor of aerodynamic 'ram' compression. At high supersonic flight speeds (Mach 2.5 to 5.0), the massive kinetic energy of oncoming airflow is compressed through a series of external and internal oblique shock waves in a supersonic inlet diffuser, raising air pressure and temperature by an order of magnitude. The compressed air enters a combustion chamber at subsonic speed, where hydrocarbon or hydrogen fuel is injected and burned continuously. In 1D compressible gasdynamics, constant-area heat addition to a flowing gas is governed by Rayleigh line flow. The conservation equations dictate that adding heat (increasing stagnation temperature T_0) to a subsonic flow accelerates the fluid toward sonic speed (dM > 0), causing static pressure to drop according to P_2 / P_1 approx 1 - m * (Delta T_0 / T_01). If too much heat is added, the flow chokes at the combustor exit (M_2 = 1)—a condition known as thermal choking, which forces the inlet shock system to unstart and expel a violent bow shock out the front. Viva voce questions & analytical theory

Why can a ramjet not produce static thrust at zero airspeed (Mach 0)?

A ramjet has no rotating compressor blades to compress incoming air. It relies entirely on the 'ram' pressure of forward motion to compress air through shock waves in its inlet. At zero forward speed, dynamic pressure is zero, no compression occurs, and burning fuel in an open tube simply blows out both ends without generating thrust.

What is the Rayleigh line in 1D compressible gas dynamics?

The Rayleigh line describes the locus of thermodynamic states of a compressible gas flowing through a constant-area frictionless duct with heat addition or heat rejection. For subsonic flow, adding heat increases Mach number, velocity, and entropy, while decreasing static pressure and stagnation total pressure.

What is thermal choking in a ramjet combustor?

Because heat addition accelerates a subsonic stream toward sonic speed, there is a maximum amount of heat Q_max that can be added before the exit Mach reaches exactly M_2 = 1.0. If more heat is added beyond this limit, the flow cannot pass the required mass flow rate, forcing a shock wave to propagate upstream and unstarting the supersonic inlet.

What is an inlet unstart on a supersonic ramjet?

Inlet unstart occurs when excessive combustor back-pressure or high angle of attack forces the internal supersonic shock train out of the inlet throat. The shock system pops violently outside the cowl as a detached bow shock, causing an instantaneous collapse of engine thrust, massive spillage drag, and severe vehicle yawing moments.

What is the operating Mach regime of a ramjet and why does it transition to a scramjet at Mach 5+?

Ramjets operate efficiently between Mach 2.5 and 5.0. Above Mach 5, decelerating air from hypersonic speeds to subsonic speeds inside the combustor generates such extreme stagnation temperatures that fuel molecules dissociate endothermically rather than releasing heat, and shock losses destroy efficiency. Scramjets (Supersonic Combustion Ramjets) maintain supersonic flow through the combustor to avoid these losses.

How does a multi-shock supersonic inlet achieve higher total pressure recovery than a normal shock inlet?

At Mach 3.0, a single normal shock produces a devastating total pressure recovery of only 32% (P_02 / P_01 = 0.32). By utilizing a series of conical or wedge-shaped oblique shocks, each shock provides a gentle compression with minimal entropy generation, boosting total pressure recovery to over 75%.

Experimental precautions and wind tunnel safety

  • Monitor combustor exit Mach number to ensure heat addition does not trigger thermal choking (M_2 < 0.90).
  • Maintain water cooling on combustor duct walls to prevent thermal structural burn-through during sustained burning.
  • Verify fast-acting nitrogen purge system is armed to extinguish combustion within 100 milliseconds upon inlet unstart.
  • Wear thermal and acoustic personal protective equipment during high-pressure burner firing.

Theoretical assumptions & flow limits

  • 1D Rayleigh flow assumes frictionless, constant-area duct flow; real combustors experience wall friction and minor divergence.
  • High-temperature combustion causes chemical dissociation (CO2 and H2O splitting), reducing effective heat release.
  • Combustion flame-holder drag introduces a total pressure loss not captured in pure Rayleigh heat addition.
  • Connected-pipe testing does not simulate external cowl wave drag or spillage drag.
