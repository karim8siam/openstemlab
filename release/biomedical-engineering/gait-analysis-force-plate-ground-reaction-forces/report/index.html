1. Title and aim

Instrumented Gait Analysis Force Plate Ground Reaction Force & Velocity Scaling. Measure multi-axis ground reaction forces (GRF) using an instrumented piezoelectric force plate and evaluate the linear velocity scaling of the peak vertical force F_v/BW.

An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Vertical GRF Velocity Scaling Factor k_v of 0.2800 BW/(m/s) (difference from theoretical reference = 0.0000%).   • Multi-Component Piezoelectric Force Platform: Measures triaxial forces (Fx, Fy, Fz) and moments (Mx, My, Mz) with 0.1 N resolution and 1000 Hz bandwidth.

4. Theory and graph choice

Instrumented human gait analysis provides objective, quantitative biomechanical data essential for diagnosing cerebral palsy crouch gait, evaluating total knee arthroplasty functional recovery, optimizing prosthetic foot design, and assessing athletic performance. When a human walks across a floor, the foot exerts forces against the ground, and by Newton's third law, the ground exerts an equal and opposite Ground Reaction Force (GRF) vector back upon the body. In a gait laboratory, flush-mounted multi-axis force plates equipped with triaxial piezoelectric quartz or strain gauge load cells measure three orthogonal force components: vertical (F_z), anteroposterior braking/propulsion (F_y), and mediolateral (F_x), as well as center of pressure (COP) coordinates. During the stance phase of walking, the vertical GRF displays a characteristic bimodal 'M-shape' profile: an initial peak corresponding to heel strike and weight acceptance, a mid-stance trough during contralateral swing phase, and a second peak during terminal stance push-off. As walking velocity v_w increases, greater downward momentum and leg acceleration elevate the peak vertical ground reaction force. When normalized to subject body weight (BW = m·g), empirical biomechanical data demonstrate that peak vertical GRF F_v/BW scales linearly with walking velocity: F_v/BW = k_v · v_w + F_v0, where k_v is the velocity sensitivity scaling factor (BW/(m/s)). In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented walkway tracks force plate telemetry across controlled walking speeds to quantify vertical force scaling.

F_v/BW = k_v · v_w + F_v0 = 0.2800 · v_w + 0.8200

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Forward walking velocity v_w (m/s), and the dependent coordinate y represents Normalized peak vertical GRF F_v/BW (BW). The gradient m directly determines Vertical GRF Velocity Scaling Factor k_v, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Measure subject body mass on calibrated digital scale, compute static body weight BW = m·g, and zero the force plate charge amplifier.
  2. Position infrared timing gates 3.0 meters apart centered around the flush-mounted force platform and calibrate electronic timer.
  3. Allow subject several practice walking passes to establish natural stride length and ensure clean, un-targeted foot strikes onto the platform.
  4. Guide subject to walk at a standardized slow velocity (v_w = 0.80 m/s), verified by timing gate readout, and record 1000 Hz triaxial GRF telemetry.
  5. Repeat clean foot strike trials across walking speeds of 1.00, 1.20, 1.40, 1.60, and 1.80 m/s, capturing three valid trials per speed step.
  6. Extract peak weight-acceptance vertical force F_v, normalize to body weight (F_v/BW), plot versus velocity v_w, and calculate scaling factor k_v.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure force platform is mounted flush and mechanically isolated from the surrounding walkway floor to avoid foot impact vibrations.
  • Discard any trial where the foot partially misses the plate or where visible step-targeting occurs.
  • Filter raw 1000 Hz force data with a low-pass zero-lag 4th-order Butterworth filter (cutoff 20 Hz) to eliminate high-frequency plate resonance.
  • Verify timing gate alignment and ensure subjects do not accelerate or decelerate within the 3-meter capture zone.

The linear velocity model applies across normal walking speeds (0.8 to 1.8 m/s); running involves a discrete gait transition with different mechanics.
