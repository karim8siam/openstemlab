1. Title and aim

Soil Moisture Retention Characteristic (pF Curves) & Pressure Plate Extraction. Determine the soil water retention curve (pF curve) of an agricultural loam using a porous ceramic pressure plate extractor across varying matric suctions.

3. Apparatus and fixed settings

  • 15-Bar High-Pressure Ceramic Plate Extractor: Applies regulated pneumatic pressure from 10 to 1500 kPa across soil retaining rings.
  • Saturated High-Flow Ceramic Porous Plate: Allows liquid water passage while preventing air breakthrough up to its rated bubble pressure.
  • Precision Pressure Regulators & Manometers: Maintains constant chamber air pressure with 0.5 kPa accuracy.
  • Undisturbed Soil Retaining Core Rings: Confines soil cores (5.0 cm diameter, 1.0 cm height) on the ceramic plate surface.
  • Forced-Draft Drying Oven (105°C): Dries soil samples to constant weight after pressure equilibration.
  • High-Precision Analytical Balance (0.001 g): Measures soil core wet mass at each equilibrium suction stage.
  • Soil texture: Silt loam (porosity 0.48)
  • Ceramic plate: 15-bar rated
  • Test mode: Primary desorption

4. Theory and graph choice

The soil water retention characteristic curve (also termed the soil water characteristic curve, or pF curve) describes the thermodynamic relationship between matric suction (energy potential with which water is held by soil capillary and adsorptive forces) and volumetric water content θ. It is the defining constitutive property used to evaluate Field Capacity (FC, typically -33 kPa or pF 2.54), Permanent Wilting Point (PWP, -1500 kPa or pF 4.18), and Available Water Capacity (AWC = FC - PWP) for irrigation water management. Saturated porous ceramic plates inside sealed pressure chambers allow positive pneumatic air pressure to be applied to soil cores resting on water-saturated ceramic discs. The applied air pressure forces water out through microscopic ceramic pores into an atmospheric drain until matric potential inside the soil exactly balances the applied pneumatic chamber pressure. In the intermediate desaturation zone between air-entry suction and permanent wilting, the retention curve follows a linear semi-logarithmic relationship θ = -λ_ret·ln(ψ) + θ_0 in accordance with the Brooks-Corey model.

θ = -λ_ret · ln(ψ) + θ_0 = -0.0650 · ln(ψ) + 0.4800

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents ln(Matric suction ψ in kPa), and the dependent axis y represents Volumetric water content θ (m³/m³). The gradient m directly determines Retention Curve Slope λ_ret, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Submerge the porous ceramic plate in de-aired water for 24 hours to ensure 100% saturation of all ceramic microscopic pores, verifying zero air entry.
  2. Place soil sample rings onto the plate, add water to the plate surface, and allow soil cores to saturate by capillarity from the bottom for 18 hours.
  3. Transfer the loaded plate into the pressure vessel, connect the effluent drain tube, seal the chamber lid, and apply initial pneumatic pressure P = 2.7 kPa (ln ψ = 1.0).
  4. Maintain pressure until outflow from the drain tube ceases completely (typically 24 to 48 hours), confirming thermodynamic equilibrium between soil water and air pressure.
  5. Quickly depressurize the chamber, extract replicate soil rings, record wet mass, and oven-dry at 105°C for 24 hours to calculate volumetric water content θ.
  6. Repeat testing across suctions corresponding to ln ψ = 1.6, 2.2, 2.8, 3.4, and 4.0 (up to 54.6 kPa in the ceramic extractor), plotting θ versus ln ψ to evaluate λ_ret.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure ceramic plates are fully saturated before placing soil cores; dry spots prevent capillary drainage.
  • Verify that the outflow drain tube remains submerged in a water vial to prevent air back-diffusion into the drain line.
  • Depressurize and clamp drain tubes immediately when stopping the test to prevent back-absorption of water into the soil cores.
  • Maintain constant laboratory temperature to avoid thermal expansion and evaporation errors during multi-day equilibration.

Pressure plate extraction requires several days to reach true hydraulic equilibrium at high suctions. Evaluated using silt loam soil cores (dry bulk density 1.35 g/cm³, porosity 0.48 m³/m³) placed in a 15-bar pressure plate extractor.
