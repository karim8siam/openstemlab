Aerodynamic and physical foundations of Boundary Layer Profile.

The boundary layer concept introduced by Ludwig Prandtl in 1904 resolves the classical d'Alembert paradox by recognizing that fluid viscosity dominates within a remarkably thin shear layer adjacent to solid boundaries, while the outer flow behaves essentially invisibly. Over a smooth flat plate aligned parallel to a uniform stream of velocity U_inf, viscous shear slows the oncoming fluid from U_inf down to zero at the wall satisfying the no-slip condition. According to Blasius' exact self-similar solution for steady, laminar, incompressible flow, the boundary layer grows parabolically with distance from the leading edge: delta = 5.0 * sqrt(nu * x / U_inf). To characterize the aerodynamic blockage and momentum loss caused by this retarded layer, integral thicknesses are defined: displacement thickness delta* = int_0^inf (1 - u/U_inf) dy represents the outward deflection of external streamlines, while momentum thickness theta = int_0^inf (u/U_inf) * (1 - u/U_inf) dy measures the momentum deficit converted directly into skin friction drag. In this experiment, a flattened boundary layer micro-Pitot probe is stepped perpendicularly from the plate surface with micrometer precision. Plotting local velocity u versus height y in the near-wall linear sublayer reveals the velocity gradient du/dy, from which local wall shear stress tau_w = mu * (du/dy) and skin friction coefficient C_f are calculated.

u(y) \approx \left(\frac{du}{dy}\right)_w y \implies u = m \cdot y + b

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What is the physical definition of boundary layer displacement thickness delta*?

Displacement thickness delta* is the distance by which external inviscid streamlines are displaced away from the solid surface due to the reduction in mass flow velocity within the boundary layer: delta* = int_0^inf (1 - u/U_inf) dy.

How does momentum thickness theta relate to total skin friction drag on a flat plate?

According to the von Karman momentum integral equation for zero pressure gradient, the local skin friction coefficient equals twice the chordwise rate of growth of momentum thickness: C_f = 2 * d(theta)/dx. Total plate friction drag equals rho * U_inf^2 * theta_trailing_edge.

What is the shape factor H of a boundary layer and why is it important?

The shape factor H = delta* / theta is the ratio of displacement thickness to momentum thickness. For a laminar Blasius boundary layer, H = 2.59. For a turbulent boundary layer, H drops to between 1.3 and 1.4. An increasing H signals impending boundary layer separation.

Why does a flattened Pitot probe introduce a probe displacement error near the wall?

In a steep shear gradient, the total pressure registered by the probe does not correspond to its geometric center but to an effective aerodynamic center shifted toward the higher-velocity side away from the wall, requiring Young-Maas displacement corrections.

How does the local boundary layer thickness delta scale with distance x from the leading edge in laminar flow?

In laminar flow, boundary layer thickness grows parabolically: delta proportional to sqrt(x / U_inf), meaning the growth rate d(delta)/dx decreases downstream as viscous diffusion spreads into the fluid.

What Reynolds number typically marks natural laminar-to-turbulent transition on a flat plate?

On a smooth flat plate with low free-stream turbulence, transition typically begins around critical Reynolds number Re_x,crit approx 5 x 10^5, completing transition to fully turbulent flow by Re_x approx 10^6.

Experimental precautions and wind tunnel safety

  • Use electrical touch-down sensing to calibrate the true y = 0 wall position to prevent probe damage and datum offset errors.
  • Apply Young and Maas probe displacement corrections for the artificial velocity shift caused by probe blockage near the solid surface.
  • Verify that the flat plate trailing edge flap is trimmed to eliminate any longitudinal pressure gradient (maintain dP/dx = 0).
  • Keep the polished flat plate surface pristine and dust-free to avoid premature boundary layer tripping to turbulence.

Theoretical assumptions & flow limits

  • Laminar Blasius theory assumes infinite aspect ratio and negligible sidewall boundary layer cross-flow.
  • Finite probe tip height (0.25 mm) prevents velocity measurements at y < 0.15 mm without physical interference.
  • Viscous shear corrections must be applied when local probe Reynolds number Re_probe falls below 30.
  • Assumes steady flow without unsteadiness or acoustic resonance from wind tunnel fan blades.
