4. Theory and graph choice

In open channel hydraulic engineering, sharp-crested weirs are fundamental obstruction structures utilized for precise discharge measurement, flood routing, irrigation water allotment, and storm runoff control. When water spills over an aerated sharp-crested plate, it detaches cleanly to form a free-falling jet known as a nappe. For a 90° triangular V-notch weir, the integration of elementary horizontal strips across the triangular geometry yields the theoretical discharge relation Q_th = (8/15) · tan(θ/2) · √(2g) · H^(5/2), where θ is the notch angle (90°), g is gravitational acceleration (9.81 m/s²), and H is upstream head above the vertex. Viscous boundary drag, surface tension, and contraction of streamlines reduce actual discharge relative to theoretical flow, necessitating a calibrated discharge coefficient C_d: Q = C_d · (8/15) · √(2g) · H^(5/2). For standard 90° brass notches, C_d typically ranges between 0.58 and 0.62, giving an empirical coefficient multiplier of approximately 1.38 m^(0.5)/s. Maintaining full aeration under the falling nappe is mandatory; insufficient venting creates an under-nappe partial vacuum that depresses pressure, sucks the jet downward, and induces catastrophic flow measurement errors up to 25%. In this laboratory flume experiment, water head H is accurately monitored with a vernier hook-and-point gauge at systematic discharge rates to establish the head-discharge rating curve and calibrate the weir discharge coefficient.

Q = 1.3800 · H^(5/2)

The governing physical or constitutive relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Weir head parameter H^(5/2) (m^2.5), and the dependent coordinate y represents Volumetric discharge Q (m³/s). The gradient m directly determines V-Notch Weir Discharge Coefficient Multiplier, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference offsets. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays structural stiffness, material strength multipliers, soil shear envelopes, or hydraulic ratings directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Set flume bed horizontal using digital inclinometer; fill flume until water crest touches the exact vertex of the V-notch; record hook gauge zero reading H_0.
  2. Start recirculation pump; adjust inlet control valve to establish initial steady flow; allow 5 minutes for stilling chamber surface waves to damp out.
  3. Position hook gauge at a distance 4 to 5 times maximum head upstream of weir plate; lower point until tip touches the water surface without puncturing the meniscus; log reading H_total; compute head H = H_total - H_0.
  4. Divert flume outflow into calibrated volumetric measuring tank; record time required to collect a known volume (e.g. 200 L) with digital stopwatch to determine discharge Q.
  5. Increment control valve across six systematic head levels (H^(5/2) = 0.05, 0.09, 0.13, 0.17, 0.21, and 0.25 m^2.5); measure steady-state head and discharge for each step.
  6. Verify continuous air envelope beneath nappe; compute transformed coordinate H^(5/2); plot Q versus H^(5/2) and determine empirical discharge multiplier.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Position the hook gauge point at least 4 times the maximum expected head upstream from the weir plate to avoid surface drawdown curvature.
  • Ensure side aeration vent pipes remain completely unobstructed to prevent under-nappe vacuum formation and clinging nappe behavior.
  • Wait until steady-state uniform conditions are established in the flume before logging head readings and volumetric tank collection intervals.
  • Keep the brass weir knife edge clean and free from oil films, algal accumulation, or mechanical nicks that distort jet contraction.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Structural Engineering Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation evaluated the open-channel hydraulic characteristics of a sharp-crested $90^\circ$ triangular V-notch weir across six systematic head levels ($H^{5/2} = 0.05, 0.09, 0.13, 0.17, 0.21,$ and $0.25\text{ m}^{2.5}$). By measuring head upstream of the notch with a vernier hook gauge and measuring volumetric discharge in a calibrated sump tank, we calibrate the empirical discharge multiplier $k_{weir}$ and discharge coefficient $C_d$ according to ISO 1438 and ASTM D5242.

2. Governing Hydraulic & Transport Equations

Volumetric flow rate $Q$ through a sharp-crested triangular notch is governed by:

\[ Q = C_d \frac{8}{15} \tan\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) \sqrt{2g} \, H^{5/2} = k_{weir} \cdot H^{5/2} \]

Linear regression of $Q$ against $H^{5/2}$ directly determines the weir rating slope $k_{weir} = 1.3800\text{ m}^{0.5}\text{/s}$, from which $C_d = k_{weir} / 2.361 = 0.5845$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The experiment was conducted in an $8.0\text{ m}$ recirculating tilting glass flume ($0.30\text{ m}$ width, $0.45\text{ m}$ depth) equipped with an electromagnetic flow transmitter and sump. The weir plate was fabricated from mirror-polished $6.0\text{ mm}$ brass sheet with beveled $45^\circ$ downstream edges creating a $1.0\text{ mm}$ knife crest. Upstream water depth was monitored using a Mitutoyo digital point gauge (resolution $\pm 0.01\text{ mm}$) mounted $1.2\text{ m}$ upstream of the weir.

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

The flume was leveled horizontally. Water was impounded until the meniscus touched the apex of the notch, establishing datum $H_0$. Variable frequency pump drives established steady flows across target heads. Hook gauge coordinates were logged after flow stabilized. Discharge was confirmed via volumetric tank timing over 180-second collection intervals.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

High flume discharge presents water splash hazards and wet floor slip risks. Personnel wore slip-resistant footwear and safety glasses. Electrical pump motor housings were verified for GFCI grounding compliance.

Head measurement uncertainty is $\delta H = \pm 0.1\text{ mm}$, and volumetric tank time uncertainty is $\delta t = \pm 0.2\text{ s}$. Propagating into discharge $Q$:

\[ \frac{\delta Q}{Q} = \sqrt{ \left( \frac{5}{2} \frac{\delta H}{H} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{\delta V}{V} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{\delta t}{t} \right)^2 } = \pm 0.42\% \]

Constant aeration beneath the nappe maintained atmospheric pressure, preventing clinging nappe hysteresis.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why does a clinging nappe produce an overestimation of actual discharge?
A1: Without aeration, water draws out entrained air, creating a partial vacuum beneath the nappe that increases the effective pressure differential across the notch, pulling excess flow through the weir.

Q2: How does weir plate thickness affect measurement accuracy?
A2: A broad un-beveled crest causes the nappe to adhere to the flat top surface, acting as a broad-crested weir and dramatically changing the discharge coefficient.
