FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Evaluate the settling velocity and compactibility of biological activated sludge, measure 30-minute settled sludge volume (SSV30) in a 1-L cylinder, and determine the Sludge Volume Index (SVI) according to Standard Methods 2710 D. Standardized parameter settings: 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5, 4.0 g/L.

01

Mixed Liquor Sampling & Aeration Maintenance

Collect fresh mixed liquor from the aeration basin discharge; aerate gently during transport to prevent anoxic denitrification and rising sludge.

02

Biomass Concentration (MLSS) Determination

Filter a 50 mL aliquot through pre-weighed glass-fiber filter; dry at 105 °C to determine dry biomass concentration MLSS (g/L).

03

Target MLSS Batch Preparation

Prepare six 1000 mL settled cylinder samples across target MLSS concentrations (1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5, and 4.0 g/L) by dilution with clarifier supernatant.

04

Cylinder Filling & Quiescent Settling Initiation

Invert cylinder three times to homogenize flocs; place on level bench; start digital timer immediately; observe zone settling velocity.

05

Interface Tracking & 30-Minute Reading

Record the descending sludge-liquid interface elevation at 5-minute intervals; record final 30-minute settled volume SSV30 (mL/L).

06

SVI Calculation & Floc Microscopic Inspection

Compute SVI = SSV30 / MLSS; prepare wet mount slide under phase-contrast microscope to examine floc architecture and filamentous organism abundance.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

In civil municipal environmental engineering, the activated sludge process is the primary secondary biological wastewater treatment technology worldwide. Heterotrophic bacterial flocs suspended in an aerated basin oxidize dissolved organic pollutants; the biological suspension (mixed liquor) then flows into secondary clarifiers where biological flocs must settle rapidly by gravity to produce a crystal-clear effluent and a concentrated return activated sludge (RAS) stream. Sludge settleability and compaction characteristics are standardized under Standard Methods 2710 D using the Sludge Volume Index (SVI). SVI is defined as the volume in milliliters occupied by 1.0 gram of activated sludge solids after 30 minutes of quiescent gravity settling in a 1000 mL graduated cylinder (or settlometer): SVI (mL/g) = [SSV30 (mL/L) · 1000] / [MLSS (mg/L)] = SSV30 (mL/L) / MLSS (g/L). An SVI between 80 and 120 mL/g signifies ideal, rapidly settling, dense biological flocs with a well-clarified supernatant. An SVI < 70 mL/g indicates dense 'pin-point' flocs that leave a turbid effluent; while an SVI > 150 mL/g signals catastrophic 'sludge bulking' caused by the excessive proliferation of filamentous bacteria (such as Microthrix parvicella or Sphaerotilus natans). Bulking sludge will not compact, overflowing clarifier weirs and contaminating receiving rivers. In this laboratory investigation, mixed liquor samples across MLSS concentrations from 1.5 to 4.0 g/L are tested in 1.0 L settlometers to calibrate the settling response and verify optimal SVI = 95.0 mL/g.
