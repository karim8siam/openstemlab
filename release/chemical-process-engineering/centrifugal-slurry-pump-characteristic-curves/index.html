FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine head-capacity (H–Q), brake horsepower, and hydraulic efficiency curves of an industrial centrifugal slurry pump, verify pump affinity laws, and evaluate shutoff head H_0 and flow resistance coefficient. Standardized parameter settings: 5.0, 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 25.0, 30.0 m^3/h.

01

Pump Priming & Suction Verification

Fill the suction line and pump volute completely with water to eliminate all air; verify suction pressure gauge indicates positive flooded head.

02

Shutoff Head Datum Establishment

Close the discharge throttling valve fully (Q = 0 m^3/h), energize the motor at 2900 RPM, and immediately record shutoff head H_0 = 35.0 m before cracking open the valve to avoid overheating.

03

Initial Flow Rate Throttling

Open the discharge valve until flow reaches Q = 5.0 m^3/h; allow flow and motor power to stabilize for 60 seconds.

04

Pressure, Power & Flow Logging

Record suction pressure P_s, discharge pressure P_d, volumetric flow rate Q, and electrical shaft power P_sh.

05

Flow Increment Progression

Increment discharge rate through 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 25.0, and 30.0 m^3/h, recording complete hydraulic and electrical parameters at each operational point.

06

Characteristic Curve Regression

Calculate total dynamic head H, plot head drop (H_0 - H) versus Q^2, and fit a linear regression through the origin to determine curvature coefficient S.

THEORY AND UNIT OPERATIONS CONTEXT

Process engineering background & physical principles.

Centrifugal pumps are the most critical prime movers in the chemical process industries, responsible for circulating raw materials, transferring toxic solvents, pumping viscous slurries, and providing cooling utility flow. Fluid entering the eye of a rotating impeller is accelerated radially outward by centrifugal force; this high kinetic energy is subsequently decelerated in the volute casing or diffuser, converting dynamic velocity head into static pressure head. The relationship between total dynamic head H and volumetric discharge Q defines the fundamental pump characteristic curve: H(Q) = H_0 - S · Q^2, where H_0 is the shutoff head at zero flow and S represents internal hydraulic recirculation and friction resistance. As discharge flow increases, mechanical shaft power P_sh rises while hydraulic efficiency η = (ρ g Q H) / P_sh traverses a characteristic optimum known as the Best Efficiency Point (BEP). Furthermore, when impeller speed N or diameter D varies, pump performance scales according to the fundamental Affinity Laws. In this laboratory experiment, an instrumented centrifugal pump running at constant speed (2900 RPM) is throttled across discharge rates from 5 to 30 m^3/h to map H–Q performance, quantify internal curvature coefficient S, and establish shutoff head H_0.
