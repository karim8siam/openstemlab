FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Survey a closed polygon traverse using total station and prism targets, calculate angular closure, linear misclosure error, relative precision ratio (1/P), and apply Bowditch's rule to adjust latitudes and departures. Standardized parameter settings: 200.0, 400.0, 600.0, 800.0, 1000.0, 1200.0 m.

01

Station Reconnaissance & Monument Flagging

Select intervisible traverse stations forming a closed geometric polygon; verify clear line of sight; drive steel monument pins into ground.

02

Total Station Setup & Initial Azimuth Referencing

Set up total station over Station 1; center optical plummet over monument mark; level dual-axis compensator; sight reference benchmark to establish initial azimuth Az_12.

03

Interior Angle Measurement (Face I & Face II)

Sight backsight station; zero horizontal circle; sight foresight station; record interior angle in Face Left; plunge telescope and repeat in Face Right to eliminate transit collimation errors.

04

Traverse Leg Distance Measurement

Measure slope distance, zenith angle, and horizontal distance L_i to foresight prism; repeat distance measurement in reverse from foresight station to verify reciprocity.

05

Traverse Loop Completion

Advance instrument through all stations sequentially; close the loop back onto Station 1; compute angular misclosure and distribute evenly across stations.

06

Latitude, Departure & Bowditch Adjustment Computation

Compute raw latitudes ΔN = L·cos(Az) and departures ΔE = L·sin(Az); sum closures e_N and e_E; calculate linear misclosure e_L = √(e_N² + e_E²); apply Bowditch corrections to compute adjusted coordinates.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

In civil geomatics engineering, boundary land surveying, highway corridor alignment, and major construction site control networks, a closed polygon traverse establishes primary horizontal control coordinates (Northing, Easting) across a project area. A series of connected survey stations (A, B, C, D, ...) are occupied sequentially with a total station, measuring interior horizontal angles θ_i and horizontal leg distances L_i. In a mathematically perfect closed loop of n stations, the sum of interior angles must equal (n - 2) · 180°, and returning to the initial station must close geometrically back to its origin: the algebraic sum of latitudes (North-South components, ΔN = L · cos(Az)) must equal zero, and departures (East-West components, ΔE = L · sin(Az)) must equal zero. However, unavoidable random instrument measurement errors in angle pointing and distance measurement create a linear misclosure vector: e_L = √(e_N² + e_E²), where e_N = ΣΔN and e_E = ΣΔE. Survey accuracy is quantified by the Relative Precision Ratio: 1 / P = e_L / L_perimeter (typically specified as 1:5,000 for standard engineering surveys, and 1:20,000 for high-precision geodetic control). Formulated by American navigator and mathematician Nathaniel Bowditch in 1808, the Bowditch (Compass) Rule assumes that angle and distance measurements possess equal relative precision, distributing misclosure proportionally to each line length: Correction_Lat = -e_N · (L_i / L_perimeter), and Correction_Dep = -e_E · (L_i / L_perimeter). In this laboratory geomatics investigation, closed traverse loops across perimeters from 200.0 to 1200.0 m are surveyed to calibrate the linear misclosure gradient slope = 0.00035 m/m (precision ratio = 1:2,857).
