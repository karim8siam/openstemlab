Agricultural and physical foundations of Spray Nozzle Patternation.

Agricultural chemical application via tractor-mounted boom sprayers is critical for crop protection, weed management, and foliar fertilization. Hydraulic flat-fan spray nozzles convert pressurized liquid into a sheet of liquid that atomizes into millions of airborne droplets through aerodynamic surface wave instability. The flow rate through an orifice nozzle adheres strictly to the Torricelli orifice discharge law, scaling linearly with the square root of pressure: Q = C_n·√P. Simultaneously, pressure governs atomization quality: increasing operating pressure reduces the Volume Median Diameter (VMD or D_v0.5) of the droplets, shifting the spectrum toward fine mists (< 150 μm) that are highly susceptible to airborne spray drift. Conversely, insufficient pressure produces coarse droplets and narrow spray fan angles that cause streaking and non-uniform chemical deposition. Evaluating the transversal distribution pattern on a grooved patternator table allows calculation of Christiansen's Uniformity Coefficient (CU) and determination of the optimum boom height and nozzle overlap.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

State the Torricelli orifice flow law governing agricultural hydraulic spray nozzles.

Flow rate through a hydraulic orifice is Q = C_d·A·√(2·ΔP / ρ), where C_d is the discharge coefficient, A is orifice area, ΔP is pressure drop, and ρ is liquid density. Consolidating constants yields Q = C_n·√P.

What is Volume Median Diameter (VMD or D_v0.5)?

VMD is the droplet diameter such that 50% of the total sprayed liquid volume is contained in droplets larger than this diameter, and 50% is contained in droplets smaller, serving as the benchmark metric for spray coarseness.

Explain the trade-off between biological efficacy and airborne spray drift.

Smaller droplets (< 150 μm) provide superior leaf surface coverage and chemical uptake but drift off-target under wind currents, causing environmental contamination. Larger droplets (> 300 μm) resist drift but can bounce off leaves or provide inadequate pest coverage.

What is the standard nozzle spacing and overlap for a 110° flat-fan boom?

Standard nozzle spacing along the boom is 50 cm. At 50 cm boom height, 110° nozzles produce overlapping spray fans such that each point on the crop canopy receives 100% overlap (double coverage) from adjacent nozzles, ensuring uniform deposition.

How do air-induction (venturi) spray nozzles reduce drift?

Air-induction nozzles use a venturi orifice to aspirate air into the fluid stream, producing larger, air-filled liquid droplets that resist wind drift and shatter upon leaf impact to maintain surface spreading.

Why are nozzle spray tips rotated 5° to 10° relative to the boom line?

Rotating the tips slightly prevents the outer edges of adjacent overlapping spray fans from colliding in mid-air, which would cause droplet coalescence and heavy liquid streaks along the overlap zone.

Experimental precautions and agricultural field safety

  • Operate spray test equipment exclusively with clean water; never use hazardous crop protection chemicals in laboratory teaching patternators.
  • Inspect nozzle orifice under an optical microscope for erosion, scratches, or burrs prior to testing.
  • Ensure pressure transducer is mounted directly at the nozzle check-valve body to avoid boom friction pressure drops.
  • Wear protective safety goggles and operate exhaust ventilation during high-pressure mist trials.

Theoretical assumptions & biological limits

  • Water calibration curves differ slightly from commercial pesticide tank mixes containing surfactants, adjuvants, or crop oils that alter surface tension and viscosity.
  • Orifice wear increases C_n over time; nozzles discharging > 10% above nominal rating must be replaced.
  • Patternator table channels evaluate static 1D transverse profile without simulating tractor forward travel dynamics.
  • Drop size laser measurements can be distorted by multiple scattering in ultra-dense spray plumes.
