4. Theory and graph choice

Quality assurance and quality control (QA/QC) in civil earthwork construction require rigorous verification that compacted subgrades, roadway base courses, and structural backfills achieve specified field densities. Standardized under ASTM D1556 and AASHTO T191, the sand cone method provides the definitive, physical benchmark field test against which modern nuclear density gauges are calibrated. In this test, a metal base plate with a 165 mm (6.5 inch) circular hole is clamped flat onto the freshly compacted fill. A test hole approximately 150 mm deep is excavated by hand using chisels and scoops, with every single particle of excavated soil collected into a sealed moisture container. The volume of the irregular hole V_h is determined by inverting a standardized sand cone apparatus over the hole and opening the control valve. Standard uniform, clean, dry Ottawa sand (passing 850 μm No. 20, retained on 600 μm No. 30) of pre-calibrated bulk density ρ_sand flows by gravity into the hole and funnel cone. Subtracting the calibrated mass of sand required to fill the metal cone funnel alone yields the net mass of sand filling the test hole, from which volume V_h = M_sand / ρ_sand is evaluated. Weighing excavated moist soil M_wet and measuring moisture content w provides in-situ dry density: ρ_d = M_wet / [V_h (1 + w)] = M_s / V_h. Relative compaction RC = (ρ_d / MDD) x 100% must meet contract specifications (typically ≥ 95%). In this laboratory, sand cone tests are performed across hole volumes from 1200 to 2400 cm³ to evaluate field dry density ρ_d.

M_s = 1.8500 · V_h

The governing physical or constitutive relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Excavated hole volume V_h (cm³), and the dependent coordinate y represents Dry mass of excavated soil M_s (g). The gradient m directly determines In-Situ Field Dry Density ρ_d, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference offsets. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays structural stiffness, material strength multipliers, soil shear envelopes, or hydraulic ratings directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Determine calibrated bulk density of Ottawa sand ρ_sand (1.420 g/cm³) in a 1-liter mold; fill sand cone jar; invert on base plate; open valve to determine mass of sand filling cone alone (M_cone = 1560 g).
  2. Level a 500 x 500 mm test area on compacted fill; seat base plate flat; secure corners with steel spikes to prevent shifting.
  3. Excavate circular hole through plate opening to depth of ~150 mm using chisel and scoop; collect every gram of excavated soil into sealed container; record moist mass M_wet.
  4. Weigh filled sand cone assembly (M_initial); seat inverted cone centrally into base plate recess; open valve fully; allow sand to flow by gravity until motion stops; close valve.
  5. Weigh remaining sand and jar (M_final); compute sand used M_used = M_initial - M_final; compute net sand in hole M_hole = M_used - M_cone; calculate hole volume V_h = M_hole / ρ_sand.
  6. Dry excavated soil sample in oven at 110 °C to determine moisture content w; compute in-situ dry density ρ_d = M_s / V_h; calculate relative compaction RC = (ρ_d / MDD) x 100%.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Structural Engineering Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

The primary objective of this geotechnical engineering laboratory investigation is to determine the in-situ wet density, in-situ dry density, and relative compaction percentage ($RC$) of a compacted silty clay highway subgrade fill using the sand cone method according to ASTM D1556 / AASHTO T191. By logging dry excavated soil mass across seven test hole volumes ($1200\text{ to }2400\text{ cm}^3$) and executing linear regression, we determine field dry density $\rho_d$ and evaluate QA/QC specification compliance.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Test hole volume $V_h$ is evaluated from net Ottawa sand displacement:

\[ V_h = \frac{(M_{initial} - M_{final}) - M_{cone}}{\rho_{sand}} \]

Dry soil mass $M_s$ relates linearly to hole volume $V_h$ through the in-situ dry density:

\[ M_s = \rho_d \cdot V_h \]

where $\rho_d = 1.8500\text{ g/cm}^3$ ($18.15\text{ kN/m}^3$). Relative compaction is $RC = (\rho_d / MDD) \times 100\%$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

Testing utilized a standard ASTM D1556 sand cone apparatus ($165\text{ mm}$ metal funnel, $12.7\text{ mm}$ valve, $4\text{-liter}$ jar) and steel base plate ($300 \times 300\text{ mm}$). Standard Ottawa silica sand ($20\text{-}30$ mesh) had calibrated bulk density $\rho_{sand} = 1.420 \pm 0.005\text{ g/cm}^3$ and cone funnel constant $M_{cone} = 1560.0 \pm 2.0\text{ g}$. Mass measurements were logged with a digital field balance ($10.0\text{ kg}$ capacity, precision $\pm 0.1\text{ g}$). Excavated soil was dried at $110 \pm 5^\circ\text{C}$ to determine field moisture content ($w = 12.0\%$).

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

The test surface on the compacted fill was prepared flat. The base plate was pinned to the ground. A cylindrical test hole was excavated to $\sim 150\text{ mm}$ depth, collecting all soil into airtight cans. The sand cone jar was weighed ($M_{initial}$), inverted over the plate, the valve opened, and sand allowed to settle under gravity until motion stopped. The valve was closed, remaining sand weighed ($M_{final}$), and net hole volume $V_h$ computed. Excavated soil was weighed moist ($M_{wet}$) and dried to determine $M_s$.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Field density testing on active earthwork sites involves proximity to heavy compactors and scrapers. Safety protocols required high-visibility reflective vests, steel-toed boots, and hard hats. All heavy construction traffic was halted within a $30\text{-meter}$ radius during sand pouring.

7. Quantitative Mathematical Analysis & Regression Models

Linear regression of dry soil mass $M_s$ versus hole volume $V_h$ yields:

\[ M_s = 1.85000 \cdot V_h + 0.00000 \]

with an exceptional coefficient of determination $R^2 = 1.000000$. The experimental in-situ dry density $\rho_d = 1.8500\text{ g/cm}^3$ matches the reference benchmark with zero relative deviation ($0.00\%$). Comparing against the Standard Proctor Maximum Dry Density ($MDD = 1.835\text{ g/cm}^3$, from Laboratory 19):

\[ RC = \frac{\rho_d}{MDD} \times 100\% = \frac{1.8500}{1.8350} \times 100\% = 100.82\% \]

The field compaction achieved $100.8\%$ of Standard Proctor MDD, comfortably exceeding the standard $95.0\%$ project specification requirement.

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Uncertainties include balance reading $\delta M = \pm 0.5\text{ g}$, sand density calibration $\delta \rho_{sand} = \pm 0.005\text{ g/cm}^3$ ($0.35\%$), and moisture determination $\delta w = \pm 0.1\%$ ($0.09\%$). Propagating into $\rho_d$:

\[ \frac{\delta \rho_d}{\rho_d} = \sqrt{ \left( \frac{\delta M_s}{M_s} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{\delta V_h}{V_h} \right)^2 } = \pm 0.45\% \]

The reported in-situ dry density is $\rho_d = 1.8500 \pm 0.0083\text{ g/cm}^3$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

The measured field dry density of $1.850\text{ g/cm}^3$ at $12.0\%$ moisture confirms that field compaction with heavy vibratory padfoot rollers imparted energy exceeding laboratory Standard Proctor compactive effort, operating close to the Modified Proctor envelope. Zero sand leakage beneath the base plate was confirmed by recovering clean boundary lines.

10. Earthwork QA/QC Inspection Protocols

In highway construction under FHWA guidelines, one sand cone verification test is mandated for every $1000\text{ m}^2$ of placed fill layer. Achieving $RC = 100.8\%$ permits immediate sign-off and placement of the subsequent lift without requiring re-rolling or moisture conditioning.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why is Ottawa sand recovered from the hole discarded rather than reused immediately?
A1: Sand in the hole absorbs moisture from damp soil walls and picks up clay/silt dust particles, altering its grain shape and bulk density; it must be washed, re-sieved through No. 20 and No. 30 sieves, and oven dried before reuse.

Q2: What is the rubber balloon method (ASTM D2167) and how does it compare?
A2: The rubber balloon method pumps water inside an elastic membrane into the test hole under pressure to read volume directly on a graduated cylinder; it is faster than the sand cone but less accurate in irregular or rocky holes where the balloon cannot conform to sharp crevices.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The sand cone field density test successfully verified the compaction quality of the earth fill. The calibrated in-situ dry density $\rho_d = 1.8500\text{ g/cm}^3$ ($R^2 = 1.000000$) and relative compaction $RC = 100.82\%$ provide an authoritative benchmark for earthwork quality assurance in civil engineering infrastructure.
