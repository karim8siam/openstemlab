4. Theory and graph choice

Ventricular fibrillation (VF) is a lethal cardiac arrhythmia characterized by disorganized, chaotic electrical depolarization of ventricular myocytes, causing loss of coordinated mechanical pumping, immediate cessation of cardiac output, and sudden cardiac death within minutes. External defibrillation is the definitive clinical treatment, delivering a controlled electrical shock across the thoracic chest to simultaneously depolarize a critical mass (> 75–90%) of myocardium, extinguish re-entrant wavelets, and allow the intrinsic sinoatrial node to re-establish normal sinus rhythm. In classical Lown and Edmark RLC defibrillators, a high-voltage capacitor C (typically 32 to 50 μF) charged up to several thousand volts (1500 to 5000 V, delivering 200 to 360 Joules) discharges through an internal series inductor L (~50 to 100 mH) and the patient's transthoracic impedance R_th. The governing differential equation of the series RLC circuit is L · (d²i/dt²) + R_th · (di/dt) + i/C = 0. In human clinical resuscitation, transthoracic impedance R_th varies widely (from 25 to 150 Ω) depending on body mass, chest hair, electrode size, coupling gel, and electrode paddle contact pressure. Because higher patient impedance dampens the discharge waveform, the peak delivered electrical current I_peak scales inversely with patient impedance: I_peak = -k_r · R_th + I_0, where k_r is the impedance sensitivity constant (A/Ω). In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented high-voltage defibrillator test analyzer evaluates discharge currents across standardized non-inductive load resistors from 30 to 105 Ω.

I_peak = -k_r · R_th + I_0 = -0.3500 · R_th + 68.0000

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Transthoracic impedance R_th (Ω), and the dependent coordinate y represents Peak discharge current I_peak (A). The gradient m directly determines Current-Impedance Sensitivity Slope k_r, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Connect defibrillator paddle cables to high-voltage analyzer load bank; verify equipment grounding and post high-voltage safety warning signage.
  2. Power on defibrillator and select standard adult energy level: 200 Joules delivered into 50 Ω.
  3. Configure analyzer precision non-inductive load resistance to R_th = 30.0 Ω and arm the measurement digitizer.
  4. Charge the internal capacitor bank to full 200 J potential; clear the testing perimeter and depress the shock discharge button.
  5. Capture the damped sinusoidal current waveform on the analyzer oscilloscope and record peak current I_peak (A) and total delivered energy (J).
  6. Increment load resistance through 45.0, 60.0, 75.0, 90.0, and 105.0 Ω, executing standardized 200 J discharges and recording peak current at each step.
  7. Plot peak discharge current I_peak versus transthoracic impedance R_th and calculate sensitivity slope k_r and zero-impedance current I_0 via linear regression.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • High-voltage electrical hazard: Defibrillator discharges deliver kilovolt pulses; never touch test leads or analyzer contacts during firing.
  • Ensure analyzer load resistors are non-inductive and rated for high peak pulse energy (minimum 500 J surge capacity).
  • Allow 30 seconds between successive high-energy discharges to prevent thermal buildup in the internal defibrillator components and load bank.
  • Verify high-voltage probe ground lead is securely bonded to analyzer ground to prevent hazardous floating chassis potentials.

The linear current-impedance model applies across normal clinical human adult impedances (30 to 105 Ω); extreme impedances exhibit non-linear RLC damping transitions.
