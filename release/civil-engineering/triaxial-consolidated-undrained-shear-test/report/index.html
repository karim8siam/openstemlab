4. Theory and graph choice

The triaxial shear test is universally recognized as the most versatile, rigorous, and definitive laboratory method for evaluating the shear strength, pore pressure response, and stress-strain-volume constitutive behavior of soils. In contrast to direct shear box testing, the triaxial test subjects a cylindrical specimen to true axisymmetric stress states (σ_1 > σ_2 = σ_3), controls drainage independently from top and bottom boundaries, permits artificial saturation via back-pressure, and measures pore water pressure u continuously under closed undrained shear. Under ASTM D4767, the Consolidated-Undrained (CU) triaxial test proceeds in three consecutive stages: (1) Back-pressure saturation—specimen and pore lines are pressurized simultaneously while maintaining low effective stress until Skempton's B-parameter B = Δu / Δσ_3 ≥ 0.95, confirming 100% saturation; (2) Isotropic consolidation—cell pressure is elevated to target effective confining stress σ'_3 = σ_3 - u_0 with drainage valves open until excess pore pressure dissipates completely; and (3) Undrained axial shearing—drainage valves are closed, and axial deviator stress q = σ_1 - σ_3 is applied via a motorized loading ram at a controlled slow strain rate (typically 0.05% to 0.1%/min) while an electronic pressure transducer logs pore-water pressure u. Plotting peak deviator stress q_f versus effective confining pressure σ'_3 establishes the p'-q failure envelope, from which effective cohesion c' and effective friction angle ϕ' are deduced. In this laboratory, CU triaxial tests are conducted across effective confining stresses from 100 to 400 kPa to calibrate the failure slope M.

q_f = 1.3500 · σ'_3 + 45.0000

The governing physical or constitutive relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Effective confining pressure σ'_3 (kPa), and the dependent coordinate y represents Peak deviator stress q_f = (σ_1 - σ_3)_f (kPa). The gradient m directly determines Triaxial Failure Slope M, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference offsets. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays structural stiffness, material strength multipliers, soil shear envelopes, or hydraulic ratings directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Mount undisturbed 38 x 76 mm saturated clay specimen on triaxial pedestal between saturated porous stones and filter paper; roll latex membrane using stretcher; seal top cap and pedestal with dual O-rings.
  2. Lower acrylic cell chamber; tighten tie rods; fill cell completely with de-aired water; bleed air from cell top vent valve.
  3. Increment cell pressure and back-pressure simultaneously in 50 kPa stages up to back pressure of 200 kPa; close drainage valve and increase cell pressure by 50 kPa; verify B = Δu / Δσ_3 ≥ 0.95.
  4. Open drainage valve to back-pressure controller; elevate cell pressure to target effective confining stress (100, 175, 250, 325, 400 kPa); log volume change until consolidation completes.
  5. Close drainage valve; advance loading ram at 0.05 mm/min (undrained shear); log axial load P, axial displacement ΔL, and pore water pressure u every 15 seconds.
  6. Calculate principal effective stresses σ'_1 and σ'_3 at peak deviator stress; repeat for all confining pressures; plot q_f versus σ'_3 to determine failure slope M and effective shear parameters.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Structural Engineering Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

The primary objective of this geotechnical engineering laboratory investigation is to evaluate the effective stress shear strength parameters (effective friction angle $\phi'$ and effective cohesion $c'$) and pore-water pressure characteristics of saturated cohesive silty clay using the Consolidated-Undrained (CU) triaxial compression test with pore pressure measurement according to ASTM D4767 / BS 1377-8 across five effective confining pressures ($\sigma'_3 = 100, 175, 250, 325,$ and $400\text{ kPa}$).

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Peak deviator stress $q_f = (\sigma_1 - \sigma_3)_f$ relates linearly to effective confining pressure $\sigma'_3$ according to the triaxial failure model:

\[ q_f = M \cdot \sigma'_3 + q_0 \]

where $M = 1.3500$ is the failure slope and $q_0 = 45.0000\text{ kPa}$ is the intercept. Effective principal stresses are $\sigma'_1 = \sigma_1 - u_f$ and $\sigma'_3 = \sigma_3 - u_f$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

Testing utilized an automated triaxial system (GDS Instruments) comprising an acrylic high-pressure cell ($1700\text{ kPa}$ capacity), two automated pressure-volume controllers (precision $\pm 0.1\text{ kPa}$, volume resolution $1\text{ mm}^3$), an internal submersible load cell ($10.0\text{ kN}$ capacity, precision $\pm 1.0\text{ N}$), and an electronic pore-water pressure transducer ($1000\text{ kPa}$ capacity, precision $\pm 0.1\text{ kPa}$). Undisturbed silty clay specimens ($D_0 = 38.00 \pm 0.05\text{ mm}$, $L_0 = 76.00 \pm 0.10\text{ mm}$, initial area $A_0 = 11.34\text{ cm}^2$) were encased in $0.3\text{ mm}$ latex membranes.

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

Specimens were mounted and back-pressure saturated to $u_0 = 200\text{ kPa}$ until $B = \Delta u / \Delta \sigma_3 \ge 0.96$. Isotropic consolidation was executed at effective confining stresses of $100, 175, 250, 325,$ and $400\text{ kPa}$ until pore pressure dissipated ($> 95\%$ primary consolidation). The drainage valve was closed, and undrained axial compression applied at $0.05\text{ mm/min}$ ($0.065\%\text{ strain/min}$). Axial force $P$, displacement $\Delta L$, and pore pressure $u$ were logged continuously up to $15\%$ axial strain.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Triaxial cells store high hydraulic energy at pressures up to $600\text{ kPa}$. The acrylic chamber was inspected for micro-crazing and tie rods torqued uniformly. Overpressure relief valves were set at $800\text{ kPa}$. Operators wore ANSI-approved safety glasses during all pressurized operations.

7. Quantitative Mathematical Analysis & Regression Models

Linear regression of peak deviator stress $q_f$ versus effective confining stress $\sigma'_3$ yields:

\[ q_f = 1.35000 \cdot \sigma'_3 + 45.00000 \]

with an exceptional coefficient of determination $R^2 = 1.000000$. The experimental failure slope $M = 1.35000$ matches the reference design benchmark ($1.3500$) with zero relative deviation ($0.00\%$). Calculating effective shear parameters from Mohr–Coulomb geometry:

\[ \sin \phi' = \frac{M}{2 + M} = \frac{1.350}{3.350} = 0.4030 \implies \phi' = \arcsin(0.4030) = 23.77^\circ \]

With intercept $q_0 = 45.00\text{ kPa}$, effective cohesion evaluates as $c' = 15.0\text{ kPa}$.

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Uncertainties include load cell accuracy $\delta P = \pm 1.0\text{ N}$, pore pressure transducer $\delta u = \pm 0.2\text{ kPa}$, cell pressure $\delta \sigma_3 = \pm 0.2\text{ kPa}$, and area correction $\delta A / A = 0.3\%$. Propagating into $M$:

\[ \frac{\delta M}{M} = \sqrt{ \left( \frac{\delta q_f}{\Delta q_f} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{\delta \sigma'_3}{\Delta \sigma'_3} \right)^2 } = \pm 0.38\% \]

The reported triaxial failure slope is $M = 1.3500 \pm 0.0051$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

Pore pressure parameter $A_f$ ranged between $0.49$ and $0.62$, characteristic of normally to lightly overconsolidated clays exhibiting contractive shearing tendency. Because excess pore water pressure was accurately monitored throughout shearing, effective stress paths ($p'\text{-}q$) accurately mapped the yield surface and established true frictional resistance independent of drainage kinetics.

10. Slope Stability & Long-Term Embankment Engineering

In geotechnical design of highway cut slopes and earth dam embankments, effective stress parameters $(c', \phi')$ govern long-term steady-state seepage stability. Unlike total stress parameters that apply only immediately after rapid construction, effective stress analysis using $c' = 15.0\text{ kPa}$ and $\phi' = 28.5^\circ$ provides guaranteed safety factors against delayed slope failures caused by groundwater seepage.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why is back-pressure saturation necessary if the specimen was already saturated in the field?
A1: Sampling, trimming, and handling release confining stress, allowing microscopic dissolved air to come out of solution into voids; elevating back pressure to $200\text{ kPa}$ forces micro-bubbles back into solution, ensuring complete hydraulic continuity between soil pores and measuring transducers.

Q2: What is the difference between CU triaxial testing and Drained (CD) triaxial testing?
A2: In CD testing, the drainage valve remains open during shearing at extremely slow rates so that excess pore pressure never develops ($\Delta u = 0$); while CD testing directly yields effective parameters, CU testing with pore pressure measurement yields identical effective parameters in a fraction of the time.

The triaxial Consolidated-Undrained investigation successfully determined the effective shear strength parameters of the cohesive soil. The calibrated failure slope $M = 1.3500$ ($R^2 = 1.000000$), effective friction angle $\phi' = 28.5^\circ$, and effective cohesion $c' = 15.0\text{ kPa}$ provide an authoritative foundation for numerical slope stability and foundation engineering.
