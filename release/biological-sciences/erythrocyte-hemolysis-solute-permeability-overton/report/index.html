1. Title and Objective

Cell Membrane Permeability & Overton's Lipophilicity Correlation. Evaluate cell membrane passive non-electrolyte permeability by measuring red blood cell hemolysis times across alcohols with varying olive oil/water partition coefficients. This formal laboratory report documents experimental data acquisition, biological telemetry calibration, linear regression analysis, biochemical parameter estimation, and physical model verification in accordance with standard university biological sciences laboratory protocols.

2. Theoretical Principles

Biological Sciences investigates cellular mechanics, metabolic flux, and ecological interactions through objective quantitative measurement under controlled laboratory conditions. In Cell Biology & Biomembrane Transport, biological function is governed by physicochemical laws: enzyme-substrate affinity, membrane transport diffusion, nucleotide polymerization, and cellular respiration. By systematically perturbing the independent parameter—Solute Olive Oil/Water Partition Coefficient (K_ow)—this investigation measures corresponding variations in Reciprocal Hemolysis Transit Rate (1/t).

Under steady-state laboratory conditions, the biophysical response follows the calibrated regression function: Rate = 1.4500 · K_ow. The primary analytical objective is the empirical determination of Overton Lipid Membrane Permeability Slope, with an accepted biological reference benchmark of 1.45 s^-1 per AU.

9. Sources of Experimental Error and Mitigation

  • Temperature Fluctuations: Thermal variations alter enzyme reaction rates and gas solubility. Mitigation: Thermostatically regulated water baths (±0.1 °C).
  • Pipetting and Meniscus Errors: Inaccurate reagent volumes alter stoichiometry. Mitigation: Use of calibrated positive-displacement micro-pipettes.
  • Photometric Optical Scattering: Scratches or fingerprints on cuvettes distort absorbance. Mitigation: Quartz cuvettes inspected and wiped with optical lens paper.
  • Biological Reagent Degradation: Proteolytic enzyme digestion or nucleic acid nicking over time. Mitigation: Keeping biological reagents on ice prior to assay.
