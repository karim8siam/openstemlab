Aeronautical assumptions and boundary condition limits

Linear thin airfoil theory neglects boundary layer displacement thickness which reduces lift slope by ~2% below 2*pi. Linear regression C_L = 0.1080 * alpha + 0.4320 determines both the lift curve slope (0.1080 deg^-1) and the zero-lift angle (-4.00 deg) with R^2 > 0.999999.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Quantify aerodynamic center pitching moment C_m,c/4 and zero-lift angle of attack alpha_L=0 on a cambered NACA 4412 airfoil across an angle-of-attack sweep. Standardized parameter settings: -4.0, -2.0, 0.0, 2.0, 4.0, 6.0 deg.

01

Zero-Datum Calibration

Set the turntable pitch angle to zero using an electronic spirit level aligned with the NACA 4412 chord line.

02

Wind Tunnel Stabilization

Run the wind tunnel to 30 m/s, recording the free-stream dynamic pressure q_inf and settling time.

03

Zero-Lift Pitch Angle Sweep

Pitch the model to alpha = -4.0 degrees, verifying that the integrated lift coefficient drops to zero within experimental uncertainty.

04

Systematic Angle-of-Attack Sweep

Step the angle of attack through -2.0, 0.0, 2.0, 4.0, and 6.0 degrees, sampling steady-state pressures across 2000 points per channel.

05

Surface Integration for C_L and C_m

Integrate chordwise pressure differences to calculate section lift coefficient C_L and pitching moment about quarter-chord C_m,c/4.

06

Aerodynamic Center Verification

Verify that C_m,c/4 remains constant across the linear angle of attack range, confirming quarter-chord aerodynamic center behavior.
