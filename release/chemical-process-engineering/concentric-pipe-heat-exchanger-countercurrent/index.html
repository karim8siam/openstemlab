FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Evaluate the thermal duty q and overall heat transfer coefficient U of a concentric double-pipe heat exchanger under co-current and counter-current flow configurations as a function of cooling water flow rate. Standardized parameter settings: 3.0, 6.0, 9.0, 12.0, 15.0, 18.0 L/min.

01

Flow Configuration & Manifold Alignment

Configure the valve manifold for counter-current flow; start the hot water pump at 10.0 L/min (60.0 °C) and cold water at 9.0 L/min (15.0 °C) for 10 minutes to reach steady state.

02

Hot Water Baseline Verification

Verify inner-tube hot water flow is constant at 10.0 L/min and inlet temperature stable at 60.0 ± 0.1 °C.

03

Initial Cooling Flow Plateau

Adjust annulus cooling water flow rate to V_c = 3.0 L/min; allow thermal equilibrium and record all four terminal temperatures.

04

Thermal Duty Calculation & Heat Balance

Calculate heat gained by cold water q_c and heat lost by hot water q_h, ensuring closure within 3%.

05

Flow Rate Progression Protocol

Step cooling water flow through 6.0, 9.0, 12.0, 15.0, and 18.0 L/min, logging terminal temperatures and calculating LMTD at each step.

06

Thermal Duty Linear Regression

Plot counter-current thermal duty q versus cooling flow rate V_c, fitting a linear regression through the origin to determine sensitivity S.

THEORY AND UNIT OPERATIONS CONTEXT

Process engineering background & physical principles.

The concentric double-pipe heat exchanger represents the simplest, most fundamental tubular heat transfer device, consisting of one pipe positioned coaxially inside a larger pipe. Fluid 1 flows through the inner tube, while Fluid 2 flows through the annular space between the two pipes. Because of its straightforward geometry, it provides an ideal platform for directly comparing co-current (parallel) and counter-current flow arrangements. In co-current flow, both hot and cold fluids enter at the same end and travel in the same direction; the maximum attainable exit temperature of the cold stream is thermodynamically limited by the hot fluid exit temperature. In counter-current flow, the fluids enter at opposite ends and flow in reverse directions, maintaining a favorable, nearly uniform temperature difference throughout the exchanger length, enabling the cold outlet temperature to surpass the hot outlet temperature. Thermal duty q transferred between streams is governed by the enthalpy balance: q = m_dot_c · c_p,c · (T_c,out - T_c,in) = U · A · ΔT_lm. In this laboratory experiment, a 2.0-meter double-pipe heat exchanger is operated with hot water in the inner tube and cooling water in the outer annulus across flow rates from 3.0 to 18.0 L/min to quantify the linear thermal duty sensitivity S and compare co-current vs counter-current heat recovery.

q = S · V_c = 0.4200 · V_c
