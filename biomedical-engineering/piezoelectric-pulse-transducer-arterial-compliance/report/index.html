1. Title and aim

Piezoelectric Sensor Charge Output & Arterial Pulse Sensitivity. Determine the dynamic piezoelectric voltage generation and charge sensitivity of a PVDF film transducer under simulated peripheral arterial pressure pulses.

2. Abstract

Six controlled biomedical engineering observations were evaluated across standardized settings Pulse ΔP = 2.0 kPa to Pulse ΔP = 12.0 kPa for Piezoelectric Sensor Charge Output & Arterial Pulse Sensitivity. An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Pressure Voltage Sensitivity k_p of 14.2000 mV/kPa (difference from theoretical reference = 0.0000%).   • Hydraulic Radial Artery Pulsation Phantom: Provides compliant silicone artery embedded in tissue-mimicking phantom driven by pulsatile pump.

  • Pulsation frequency: 1.2 Hz (72 BPM) radial pulse simulation
  • Amplifier: Low-noise charge amplifier (τ = 2.5 s)

4. Theory and graph choice

Cardiovascular diagnosis and continuous non-invasive vital sign monitoring rely on detecting peripheral arterial pulse waveforms at superficial arterial sites (such as the radial, carotid, and superficial temporal arteries). Piezoelectric polymer films, particularly polarized polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF), are widely utilized in biomedical pulsation pickups, acoustic phonocardiography, and sleep apnea respiratory effort belts due to their exceptional dynamic responsiveness, wide mechanical bandwidth, high piezoelectric voltage coefficient (g_33), and acoustic impedance matching biological soft tissues. Unlike piezoresistive sensors that measure static pressure, piezoelectric transducers generate surface electrical charge Q solely in response to dynamic, time-varying mechanical stress: Q = d_33 · F = d_33 · A · ΔP, where d_33 is the longitudinal piezoelectric charge coefficient and A is the sensor active area. When coupled to a high-input-impedance charge amplifier, the generated peak open-circuit voltage V_p is directly proportional to applied pulse pressure amplitude ΔP: V_p = k_p · ΔP. In this laboratory investigation, a calibrated PVDF arterial pulse transducer mounted on a phantom radial artery hydraulic pulsation bench is evaluated across clinical pulse pressure increments to determine sensitivity and verify dynamic transduction linearity.

V_p = k_p · ΔP = 14.2000 · ΔP

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Pressure pulse amplitude ΔP (kPa), and the dependent coordinate y represents Peak sensor voltage V_p (mV). The gradient m directly determines Pressure Voltage Sensitivity k_p, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Prime the silicone radial artery phantom with degassed physiological saline and calibrate the inline reference pressure transducer to read true systolic and diastolic pressures.
  2. Position the PVDF pulse sensor over the superficial arterial phantom site with an elastic strap establishing a baseline contact pre-load of 2.0 kPa.
  3. Set the pulsatile actuator to resting heart rate cadence (72 BPM, 1.2 Hz) and establish an initial pulse pressure amplitude ΔP of 2.0 kPa (15 mmHg).
  4. Capture 10 consecutive pulse cycles on the digital oscilloscope and record the ensemble-averaged peak-to-peak output voltage V_p in millivolts.
  5. Increment dynamic pulse pressure amplitude through 4.0, 6.0, 8.0, 10.0, and 12.0 kPa, logging peak piezoelectric voltage at each stage.
  6. Plot peak voltage V_p against pulse pressure ΔP, performing linear regression through the origin to determine pressure voltage sensitivity k_p.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Maintain uniform hold-down strap tension throughout testing to avoid pre-load coupling artifacts.
  • Ensure coaxial cable between sensor and charge amplifier is immobilized to eliminate triboelectric cable noise.
  • Verify absence of air bubbles inside the arterial fluid column that could damp pulse pressure gradients.
  • Allow the charge amplifier output to re-zero after adjusting pulse pressure increments.

The linear sensitivity model applies across the physiologic pulse range (2.0 to 12.0 kPa).
