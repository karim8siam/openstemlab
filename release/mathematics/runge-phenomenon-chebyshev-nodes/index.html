Numerical assumptions and limits

Chebyshev nodes require predefined sampling locations; if experimental measurements are already fixed at uniform intervals, Chebyshev interpolation cannot be directly applied without resampling. The simulation compares uniform and Chebyshev interpolants on f(x) = 1/(1+25x²) for degrees n = 4 through 14. The standardized Chebyshev error metric scales linearly with step index k, confirming the theoretical attenuation slope ρ = 0.500 with R² = 1.000000.

01

Initialize Runge Function & Interval

Set up f(x) = 1/(1 + 25x²) on [-1, 1], noting the complex conjugate poles at z = ± 0.2 i located inside the Bernstein convergence ellipse.

02

Generate Equidistant Nodes

Compute uniform node arrays for degree n = 2k + 2 and observe the growth of the nodal product polynomial ω_n(x) near the endpoints x = ± 1.

03

Generate First-Kind Chebyshev Nodes

Compute the Chebyshev roots x_j = cos((2j - 1)π / (2n)), confirming high nodal density near boundaries and sparser spacing at the center.

04

Construct High-Degree Interpolants

Evaluate both uniform and Chebyshev interpolating polynomials using stable barycentric algorithms across 2000 micro-grid points.

05

Measure Supremum Errors & Edge Spikes

Record the catastrophic oscillation of the uniform polynomial (exceeding error > 10 for n ≥ 12) while confirming uniform error attenuation for Chebyshev nodes.

06

Record the standardized Chebyshev error metric across steps k = 1 to 6 and perform regression to verify the theoretical factor ρ = 0.500.
