FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the indentation resistance, macro-hardness, and empirical ultimate tensile strength of structural steel and structural aluminum alloys using Brinell and Rockwell hardness methods according to ASTM E10 / ASTM E18. Standardized parameter settings: 100.0, 200.0, 300.0, 400.0, 500.0 kgf.

01

Specimen Surface Grinding & Planar Preparation

Grind specimen surface flat and parallel to the bottom face using 240 and 400 grit silicon carbide abrasive paper; ensure testing face is free of scale, oil, or decarb.

02

Machine Calibration Verification

Verify tester calibration by performing three indentations on standard NIST-certified reference test blocks; ensure indicated hardness matches block within ± 1%.

03

Specimen Placement on Anvil

Place specimen firmly on the flat testing anvil; rotate elevating screw to bring specimen into contact with indenter.

04

Major Load Application & Standard Dwell

Engage automatic loading cycle; apply specified major load smoothly without impact shock; maintain dwell time for exactly 10 to 15 seconds.

05

Load Removal & Indentation Inspection

Release major load; lower anvil; examine indentation under optical microscope; measure impression diameter d along two perpendicular axes to 0.01 mm.

06

Hardness Calculation & Tensile Estimation

Calculate mean diameter d; compute Brinell Hardness Number (HBW); apply empirical conversion factor to estimate ultimate tensile strength.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

Hardness is a fundamental mechanical surface property defining a material's localized resistance to permanent plastic indentation, scratching, and abrasive wear. In civil engineering quality assurance, hardness testing provides a rapid, non-destructive, and economical method to evaluate alloy heat treatment condition, verify weld heat-affected zone (HAZ) softening, and estimate ultimate tensile strength without machining complex tensile dogbone specimens. Under ASTM E10, the Brinell hardness test presses a hardened tungsten carbide ball indenter (10 mm diameter) into the flat ground surface of the specimen under a heavy static major load (typically 3000 kgf for structural steels, 500 kgf for aluminum alloys) for a standardized dwell time of 10 to 15 seconds. The diameter of the recovered spherical indentation impression d is measured in orthogonal directions using a calibrated optical microscope, and the Brinell Hardness Number (HBW) is calculated as applied force divided by the curved surface area of the spherical cap. For structural carbon steels, a robust empirical rule-of-thumb links Brinell hardness directly to ultimate tensile strength: f_u ≈ 3.45 · HBW in MPa. In this laboratory, indentation tests are performed across loading parameters from 100 to 500 kgf to calibrate hardness loading response.
