Agricultural assumptions and boundary condition limits

Rubber rolls soften as operational temperature rises (> 60°C), slightly shifting the effective friction coefficient and hulling efficiency during prolonged continuous runs. FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Evaluate the dehusking efficiency, brown rice breakage percentage, and head rice recovery yield in an agricultural rubber-roll paddy husker as a function of roller speed differential. Standardized parameter settings: 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5, 4.0 m/s.

01

Roller Clearance & Durometer Inspection

Inspect rubber roller surfaces for even wear and hardness (Shore A 85 to 90 durometer). Set the roll clearance to exactly 0.8 mm using a thickness feeler gauge.

02

Initial Speed Differential Setup

Set slow roller peripheral velocity to v_slow = 8.0 m/s and fast roller to v_fast = 9.5 m/s, establishing an initial speed differential Δv = 1.5 m/s.

03

Standardized Batch Feeding

Feed exactly 1000.0 g of clean, graded paddy rice through the vibrating hopper over 60 seconds into the husking nip.

04

Aspiration & Fraction Sorting

Collect the discharge from the aspirator column, separating the three fractions: separated husks, dehulled brown rice (m_brown), and unhusked paddy (m_unhusked).

05

Hulling Efficiency & Breakage Calculation

Calculate dehusking hulling efficiency η_h = [m_brown / (m_brown + m_unhusked)] × 100%, and screen brown rice through the slotted sieve to determine broken rice percentage.

06

Speed Differential Stepping

Increment fast roller speed across five additional stages to achieve Δv = 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5, and 4.0 m/s, plotting η_h versus Δv to extract sensitivity b_h.
