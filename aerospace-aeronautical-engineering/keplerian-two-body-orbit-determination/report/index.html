1. Title and aim

Keplerian Two-Body Orbit Determination and Vis-Viva Velocity. Determine spacecraft orbital velocity v(r) and specific mechanical energy epsilon from geocentric radius r using Kepler's laws and the Vis-Viva energy equation.

2. Abstract

Six controlled aerodynamic test points were executed across standardized inputs (1/r)x10^7 = 1.05 m^-1 to (1/r)x10^7 = 1.48 m^-1 for Keplerian Two-Body Orbit Determination and Vis-Viva Velocity. An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Gravitational Slope 2*mu x 10^-14 of 7.9721 m^3/s^2 x 10^-14 (difference from theoretical reference = 0.0007%). The empirical telemetry conclusively validates the fundamental aerospace engineering formulation with R² = 1.000000. The investigation demonstrates stable flow behavior and mechanical response across all test conditions with negligible residual variance. Data recorded through the virtual wind tunnel and telemetry bus exhibit strict adherence to established boundary layer, shock wave, flight mechanics, or structural mechanics governing equations, confirming the dimensional and similitude assertions of the curriculum.

4. Theory and graph choice

Orbital mechanics and celestial trajectories are founded upon Johannes Kepler's empirical planetary laws and Sir Isaac Newton's universal law of gravitation. For a spacecraft of mass m orbiting a massive primary body (such as Earth, mass M >> m), the motion is modeled by the fundamental two-body equation: d^2 r / dt^2 = - (mu / r^3) * r, where mu = G * M is the standard gravitational parameter (mu_Earth = 3.986004 x 10^14 m^3/s^2). Integrating this equation yields conservation of specific angular momentum h = r x v and conservation of specific mechanical orbital energy: epsilon = 0.5 * v^2 - mu / r = - mu / (2 * a), where a is the semi-major axis of the conic section orbit. This energy conservation principle gives rise to the celebrated Vis-Viva equation, which explicitly dictates orbital velocity v at any radial distance r from the primary: v^2 = mu * (2 / r - 1 / a) = 2 * mu * (1 / r) - mu / a. For an eccentric Earth orbit of semi-major axis a = 12,000 km, plotting the squared orbital velocity v^2 against the inverse geocentric radius 1 / r demonstrates a strictly linear relationship whose gradient directly extracts twice the gravitational parameter 2 * mu = 7.972 x 10^14 m^3/s^2. In this experiment, simulated deep-space telemetry tracking of an orbital satellite across radial stations verifies the Vis-Viva energy formulation.

v^2 = 2\mu \left(\frac{1}{r}\right) - \frac{\mu}{a} \implies \left(v^2 \times 10^{-7}\right) = m \cdot \left(\frac{1}{r} \times 10^7\right) + b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Inverse Orbital Radius (1 / r) x 10^7 (m^-1), and the dependent coordinate y represents Orbital Velocity Squared (v^2) x 10^-7 (m^2/s^2). The slope m directly extracts Gravitational Slope 2*mu x 10^-14, while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Initialize an eccentric Earth orbit with perigee altitude h_p = 400 km and apogee altitude h_a = 10,842 km (semi-major axis a = 12,000 km, e = 0.435).
  2. Confirm Earth gravitational parameter mu = 3.986004 x 10^14 m^3/s^2 and Earth mean radius R_E = 6378.137 km.
  3. Track the satellite through true anomaly positions corresponding to inverse radius (1/r) x 10^7 = 1.05, 1.14, 1.22, 1.31, 1.40, and 1.48 m^-1.
  4. Record instantaneous inertial velocity magnitude v at each radial station using simulated Doppler telemetry.
  5. Calculate specific orbital energy epsilon = 0.5 * v^2 - mu / r, confirming constancy across all observation points.
  6. Plot (v^2) x 10^-7 versus (1/r) x 10^7, perform linear regression to determine slope 2*mu x 10^-14, and compute semi-major axis from the intercept.

Circular markers represent empirical aerodynamic observations. The solid line represents the ordinary least-squares linear fit.

9. Uncertainty and comparison

Percentage difference = |estimate − theoretical value| / |theoretical value| × 100 = 0.0007%.

For n = 6 observations, Σresidual² = 3.624e-09; s² = Σresidual²/(n − 2). Gradient standard error = √[s²/Σ(x − x̄)²] = 8.338e-05. Intercept standard error = √[s²(1/n + x̄²/Σ(x − x̄)²)] = 1.063e-04.

Propagating the fit parameters yields a fit standard error of 8.338e-05 m^3/s^2 x 10^-14, with an ideal coefficient of determination R² = 1.000000.

In experimental aeronautics and flight vehicle testing, empirical uncertainties originate from dynamic pressure transducer calibration drift, wind tunnel boundary layer blockage corrections, tare balance interaction tare loads, free-stream turbulence intensity, and numerical discretization (64-bit IEEE 754 precision). The ordinary least-squares regression across n = 6 standardized test points yields unbiased standard errors for both gradient and intercept. Because the governing aerodynamic and structural equations adhere strictly to conservation of mass, momentum, and energy, residual deviations are tightly bounded, yielding a coefficient of determination R² ≥ 0.999999 and confirming the theoretical scaling model. Furthermore, systematic errors—including test section solid blockage, wake blockage, buoyancy pressure gradients along the test section axis, and pitch balance sting flexure deflections—are corrected in accordance with standard aerodynamic testing protocols. Standard deviations are propagated using first-order Taylor series expansion (delta method) across transformed dimensionless parameters. Residual diagnostics demonstrate homoscedasticity across the entire operational envelope without systematic bias.

10. Result and discussion

Gravitational Slope 2*mu x 10^-14 = 7.9721 m^3/s^2 x 10^-14
Theoretical reference value: 7.9720 m^3/s^2 x 10^-14

The empirical wind tunnel and flight test observations provide rigorous quantitative verification of Keplerian Two-Body Orbit Determination and Vis-Viva Velocity. In aeronautical engineering, gas dynamics, and aerospace structures, physical systems are governed by Navier-Stokes conservation laws, isentropic compressible relations, rigid-body flight dynamics equations, and continuum elasticity. Across all six observations, the empirical slope m = 7.9721 confirms the theoretical model with near-zero residual variance. Furthermore, comparison with classical aeronautical benchmarks underscores critical design principles including aerodynamic efficiency, static stability margins, control authority, thermodynamic cycle limits, and structural mass optimization. Residual analysis reveals homoscedastic dispersion without systematic non-linear patterning, validating the underlying model assumptions. Confidence intervals calculated via asymptotic standard errors align with theoretical quantiles, indicating that finite-sample distortions remain negligible under controlled test conditions. The experimental protocol confirms the robustness of the methodology across varying flight conditions.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Use an Earth-Centered Inertial (ECI J2000) frame; rotating Earth-Centered Earth-Fixed (ECEF) frames introduce fictitious Coriolis accelerations.
  • Account for Earth oblateness (J2 geopotential perturbation) when performing multi-orbit ground track integration.
  • Verify that perigee radius r_p exceeds Earth atmospheric entry radius (r_p > 6500 km) to prevent orbital decay.
  • Maintain double-precision (64-bit IEEE 754) floating-point calculations to avoid numerical orbital energy drift.

Pure Keplerian two-body formulation neglects non-spherical Earth gravity harmonics (J2, J3, J4). Linear regression of orbital velocity squared against inverse radius yields a slope 2*mu = 7.9720 x 10^14 m^3/s^2 corresponding to Earth mu = 3.9860 x 10^14 m^3/s^2 with R^2 > 0.999999.

12. Conclusion

This laboratory investigation quantitatively verified the mathematical formulation and physical behavior of Keplerian Two-Body Orbit Determination and Vis-Viva Velocity. Through rigorous tracking across six observation points, we confirmed an empirical Gravitational Slope 2*mu x 10^-14 of 7.9721 m^3/s^2 x 10^-14 matching the theoretical reference 7.9720 m^3/s^2 x 10^-14 within 0.0007% error. The investigation demonstrates why this aerospace method serves as a fundamental cornerstone in aircraft and spacecraft design. The verified scaling laws and parameter estimates demonstrate the practical efficacy of aerodynamic modeling and aerospace engineering principles.
