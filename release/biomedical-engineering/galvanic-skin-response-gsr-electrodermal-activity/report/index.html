1. Title and aim

Galvanic Skin Response (GSR) & Sympathetic Electrodermal Conductance. Measure skin conductance level (SCL) and phasic skin conductance responses (SCR) across calibrated psychological arousal stimuli using constant-voltage DC bridge circuitry.

  • Excitation: 0.500 V DC constant potential across finger electrodes
  • Electrode site: Palmar distal phalanges of index and middle fingers
  • Room conditions: 22.0 °C, 45% relative humidity

4. Theory and graph choice

Electrodermal activity (EDA), historically termed the Galvanic Skin Response (GSR), provides a sensitive, involuntary peripheral biomarker of sympathetic autonomic nervous system arousal. Innervated exclusively by postganglionic sympathetic sudomotor nerve fibers releasing acetylcholine, human eccrine sweat glands (densely concentrated on the palmar surfaces of the hands and plantar surfaces of the feet) fill with sweat in response to emotional, cognitive, and sensory stimuli. Because sweat is an electrolyte solution rich in NaCl, sweat filling the non-conductive pores of the stratum corneum creates parallel electrical conductance pathways that dramatically lower the electrical resistance of the skin. In modern psychophysiology and lie detection (polygraphy), endosomatic and exosomatic recording techniques are employed. Under the standard Lykken exosomatic method, a constant regulated DC voltage (typically 0.50 V) is applied across two Ag/AgCl finger electrodes, and the resulting current is measured to evaluate skin conductance G (expressed in microsiemens, μS). The signal decomposes into a slowly varying tonic baseline (Skin Conductance Level, SCL) and fast, transient phasic peaks (Skin Conductance Responses, SCR) that occur 1 to 3 seconds following a stimulus. Across standardized sensory stimulus intensities I_stim, the peak phasic conductance amplitude ΔG exhibits direct linear proportionality: ΔG = k_g · I_stim. In this experiment, an instrumented GSR constant-voltage circuit evaluates palmar skin conductance during calibrated arousal tasks.

ΔG = k_g · I_stim = 1.8500 · I_stim

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Stimulus intensity index (units), and the dependent coordinate y represents Phasic skin conductance ΔG (μS). The gradient m directly determines Electrodermal Arousal Sensitivity k_g, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Attach Ag/AgCl electrodes to the palmar surface of the distal phalanges of fingers II and III; allow 10 minutes for baseline thermal acclimation.
  2. Verify with precision DMM that circuit applies exactly 0.500 V DC across the finger electrodes without voltage droop.
  3. Record resting tonic conductance for 2 minutes to confirm a stable baseline (typically 2.0 to 10.0 μS) and absence of spontaneous spikes.
  4. Deliver the initial acoustic stimulus (Level 1.0); identify the phasic SCR wave occurring 1.5 to 2.5 seconds post-stimulus.
  5. Compute peak phasic conductance increase ΔG = G_peak - G_onset in microsiemens (μS).
  6. Deliver stimuli of intensities 2.0, 3.0, 4.0, 5.0, and 6.0 at randomized 45-second intervals, plotting ΔG against stimulus level to determine k_g.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Instruct the subject to keep hand completely relaxed and motionless; finger twitches create mechanical motion artifacts.
  • Maintain randomized inter-stimulus intervals (> 30 s) to prevent sensory anticipation and habituation.
  • Do not use abrasive skin preps or alcohol swabs; skin abrasion destroys the natural stratum corneum barrier.
  • Ensure ambient room temperature is kept at thermoneutral levels (21-23 °C) to avoid thermoregulatory sweating.

The linear arousal model applies across sub-saturation sensory intensities (1.0 to 6.0 units).
