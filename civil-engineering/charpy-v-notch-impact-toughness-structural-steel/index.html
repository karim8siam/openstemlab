FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the impact absorbed energy (K_V) and ductile-to-brittle transition temperature (DBTT) of structural bridge steel under high-strain-rate impact loading according to ASTM E23 / BS EN ISO 148-1. Standardized parameter settings: -30.0, -10.0, 10.0, 30.0, 50.0 °C.

01

Specimen Dimensional & Notch Verification

Inspect 10 x 10 x 55 mm steel specimens on the optical projector; verify notch depth (2.00 mm), notch radius (0.25 mm), and 45° angle.

02

Thermal Conditioning at Target Temperatures

Immerse specimens in the liquid bath at target temperatures (-30, -10, +10, +30, and +50 °C); condition for at least 10 minutes to achieve uniform core temperature.

03

Pendulum Initial Elevation & Zero Check

Latch pendulum hammer at standard initial angle (300 J potential energy); release with empty anvils to record zero-friction windage loss calibration.

04

Rapid Specimen Transfer & Centering

Using chilled tongs, remove specimen from bath and place on testing anvils centered with notch facing away from striker tup within strictly 5.0 seconds.

05

Pendulum Release & Impact Fracture

Immediately trip the pneumatic pendulum release latch; allow hammer to sever the specimen and swing through; engage automatic mechanical brake.

06

Energy Logging & Fracture Face Analysis

Read absorbed energy K_V in Joules from digital encoder; measure lateral expansion at notch root with micrometer gauge; determine percentage shear area.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

Catastrophic brittle fractures of steel structures—such as the infamous WWII Liberty ship fractures and civil bridge collapses in sub-zero winter temperatures—revealed that static tensile yield strength is insufficient to guarantee structural safety under dynamic shock loading. Body-centered cubic (BCC) ferritic structural steels undergo a profound transformation from ductile, high-energy plastic tearing at warm temperatures to brittle, low-energy transgranular cleavage fracture at freezing temperatures. Standardized under ASTM E23 and ISO 148-1, the Charpy V-notch (CVN) impact test utilizes a standardized square specimen (10 x 10 x 55 mm) with a precision 45° V-notch machined to a depth of 2 mm and root radius of 0.25 mm. The specimen is supported as a simple beam and struck on the face opposite the notch by a calibrated heavy swinging pendulum with striking energy of 300 ± 10 J at impact velocity ~5.2 m/s. The energy absorbed in fracturing the specimen (K_V) is evaluated directly from the difference between pendulum initial release height and post-fracture follow-through height. In this laboratory, impact toughness is tested across temperatures from -30 °C to +50 °C to evaluate impact sensitivity and ensure compliance with AASHTO bridge fracture-critical specifications (minimum 27 J at -20 °C).
