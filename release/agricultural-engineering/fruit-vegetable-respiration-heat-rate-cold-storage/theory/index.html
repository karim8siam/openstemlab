Agricultural and physical foundations of Produce Respiration Kinetics.

Fresh agricultural horticultural produce (fruits, vegetables, and ornamentals) remains metabolically active living tissue following post-harvest harvest. Cellular respiration consumes stored carbohydrates (primarily glucose) and oxygen while generating carbon dioxide, water vapor, and vital metabolic heat: C6H12O6 + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + 2870 kJ/mol. In cold storage design and refrigeration load calculations, respiration heat represents a dynamic, internal thermal load that must be continuously extracted by the evaporator coils to prevent temperature rise, quality degradation, and senescent breakdown. In accordance with Arrhenius and van 't Hoff biological temperature kinetics, respiration rate accelerates markedly with increasing pulp temperature, approximately doubling or tripling for each 10 °C temperature increase (Q10 coefficient). Within the commercial refrigerated storage window of 2 °C to 22 °C, the carbon dioxide evolution rate R_CO2 displays a predictable linear sensitivity k_r to storage temperature. In this laboratory experiment, a batch of fresh Gala apples is sealed inside a temperature-controlled hermetic respiration chamber equipped with non-dispersive infrared (NDIR) CO2 gas analyzers and oxygen sensors to measure respiration kinetics and determine refrigeration heat loads.

R_CO2 = k_r · T + R_0 = 1.4500 · T + 3.2000

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

How is respiration heat related quantitatively to carbon dioxide evolution?

Based on complete glucose oxidation, production of 1 mg of CO2 releases approximately 10.7 Joules of metabolic heat. Therefore, multiplying R_CO2 (mg/(kg·h)) by 10.7 / 3600 converts respiration directly into specific thermal power in W/kg.

What is the respiratory quotient (RQ) and what does its value indicate?

The respiratory quotient is the molar ratio of CO2 produced to O2 consumed (RQ = mol CO2 / mol O2). An RQ of 1.0 indicates pure carbohydrate oxidation; RQ < 1.0 indicates lipid/protein oxidation, while RQ > 1.3 signifies onset of anaerobic fermentation.

What is the temperature coefficient Q10 in post-harvest biology?

Q10 is the factor by which respiration rate increases when temperature is raised by 10 °C: Q10 = (R2 / R1)^(10 / (T2 - T1)). For most agricultural crops, Q10 ranges between 2.0 and 3.0 in the 0 °C to 20 °C range.

How does controlled atmosphere (CA) storage retard produce respiration?

CA storage lowers oxygen concentration (typically to 1% to 3%) and elevates CO2 (to 1% to 5%), slowing down respiratory enzyme kinetics, suppressing ethylene biosynthesis, and extending post-harvest storage life.

What is the climacteric rise in fruit ripening?

Climacteric fruits (e.g., apples, bananas, tomatoes) exhibit a dramatic surge in respiration rate and ethylene production at the onset of ripening, whereas non-climacteric fruits (e.g., citrus, grapes) show a steady, gradual decline.

Why must CO2 concentrations not exceed critical thresholds during respiration tests?

Excessive CO2 buildup (above 1% to 2%) can induce physiological injury, alter natural enzyme activity, and trigger anaerobic fermentation, distorting true aerobic respiration measurements.

Experimental precautions and agricultural field safety

  • Ensure produce pulp core temperature reaches true thermal equilibrium before initiating gas collection.
  • Verify airtight seal integrity with pressure decay test prior to each gas accumulation run.
  • Limit measurement duration so that O2 depletion remains under 1.5% and CO2 buildup does not exceed 1.0%.
  • Calibrate the NDIR CO2 analyzer with certified zero and span gases before testing.

Theoretical assumptions & biological limits

  • The linear kinetic model applies within the commercial refrigerated storage range of 2 °C to 22 °C.
  • Assumes pre-climacteric or steady ripening state without sudden ethylene-triggered respiratory spikes.
  • Neglects potential latent heat of transpiration occurring simultaneously inside the chamber.
  • Specific to Gala apple cultivars; other commodities exhibit distinct basal respiration magnitudes.
