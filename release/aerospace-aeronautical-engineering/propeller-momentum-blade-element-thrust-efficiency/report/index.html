1. Title and aim

Aircraft Propeller Momentum and Blade Element Aerodynamic Performance. Measure propeller thrust coefficient C_T, power coefficient C_P, and propulsive efficiency eta_p across advance ratios J = V / (n D) to evaluate blade element theory.

4. Theory and graph choice

The aircraft propeller converts shaft torque delivered by an engine into usable forward propulsive thrust through aerodynamic lift generated by its rotating twisted airfoil blades. Propeller aerodynamics is modeled by combining Rankine-Froude axial momentum theory (which treats the propeller as an idealized semi-permeable actuator disk accelerating an incoming streamtube) with Stefan Drzewiecki's Blade Element Theory (BET), which analyzes each radial blade section as an autonomous 2D airfoil operating at local relative velocity and effective angle of attack. Propeller operating states are governed by the non-dimensional advance ratio J = V_inf / (n * D), where V_inf is flight airspeed, n is rotational frequency (rev/s), and D is propeller tip diameter. The non-dimensional thrust coefficient is defined as C_T = T / (rho * n^2 * D^4), and power coefficient is C_P = P_shaft / (rho * n^3 * D^5). Net propulsive efficiency is eta_p = (T * V_inf) / P_shaft = (C_T / C_P) * J. In the linear operating regime, thrust coefficient decays linearly with advance ratio as effective blade angle of attack decreases: C_T = C_T0 - m * J. In this experiment, a motorized propeller test stand in a wind tunnel records thrust T and torque Q across advance ratios from J = 0.30 to 0.80, verifying the linear thrust gradient dC_T / dJ.

(C_{T0} - C_T) = m \cdot J + b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Advance Ratio J = V_inf / (n*D), and the dependent coordinate y represents Thrust Coefficient Deficit C_T0 - C_T. The slope m directly extracts Propeller Thrust Slope dC_T/dJ, while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Set wind tunnel speed to zero, spin up propeller to static RPM, and verify static thrust coefficient C_T0 = 0.1250.
  2. Set motor speed to constant n = 50.0 rev/s (3000 RPM) using the closed-loop optical tachometer.
  3. Step wind tunnel velocity through V_inf = 4.5, 6.0, 7.5, 9.0, 10.5, and 12.0 m/s (corresponding to J = 0.30 to 0.80).
  4. Sample 3000 points of axial thrust T and torque Q at each advance ratio plateau.
  5. Calculate non-dimensional thrust coefficient C_T and power coefficient C_P, computing propulsive efficiency eta_p.
  6. Plot thrust deficit (C_T0 - C_T) versus J, perform linear regression to determine slope dC_T / dJ, and find zero-thrust advance ratio J_0.

9. Uncertainty and comparison

Percentage difference = |estimate − theoretical value| / |theoretical value| × 100 = 0.0000%.

For n = 6 observations, Σresidual² = 2.407e-34; s² = Σresidual²/(n − 2). Gradient standard error = √[s²/Σ(x − x̄)²] = 1.854e-17. Intercept standard error = √[s²(1/n + x̄²/Σ(x − x̄)²)] = 1.068e-17.

Propagating the fit parameters yields a fit standard error of 1.854e-17 dimensionless, with an ideal coefficient of determination R² = 1.000000.

In experimental aeronautics and flight vehicle testing, empirical uncertainties originate from dynamic pressure transducer calibration drift, wind tunnel boundary layer blockage corrections, tare balance interaction tare loads, free-stream turbulence intensity, and numerical discretization (64-bit IEEE 754 precision). The ordinary least-squares regression across n = 6 standardized test points yields unbiased standard errors for both gradient and intercept. Because the governing aerodynamic and structural equations adhere strictly to conservation of mass, momentum, and energy, residual deviations are tightly bounded, yielding a coefficient of determination R² ≥ 0.999999 and confirming the theoretical scaling model. Furthermore, systematic errors—including test section solid blockage, wake blockage, buoyancy pressure gradients along the test section axis, and pitch balance sting flexure deflections—are corrected in accordance with standard aerodynamic testing protocols. 11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure safety blast shield is securely locked around the wind tunnel propeller disk during operation.
  • Account for wind tunnel solid blockage and slipstream constriction using standard Glauert propeller corrections.
  • Verify dynamic balance of propeller blades before high-RPM testing to prevent destructive motor bearing vibration.
  • Do not exceed maximum allowable motor winding temperature during continuous high-thrust test points.

Blade Element Momentum Theory (BEMT) assumes steady axisymmetric inflow without blade tip vortex compressibility. Linear regression of the thrust coefficient deficit against advance ratio yields a slope dC_T/dJ = 0.1350 with R^2 > 0.999999.
