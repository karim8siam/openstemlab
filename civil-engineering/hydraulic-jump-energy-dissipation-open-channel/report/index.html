4. Theory and graph choice

A hydraulic jump is a rapid, highly turbulent open-channel flow phenomenon occurring when supercritical flow (Froude number Fr > 1) abruptly transitions to subcritical flow (Fr < 1). In hydraulic infrastructure—including spillway stilling basins, sluice gate chutes, and canal drop structures—hydraulic jumps are intentionally generated as primary energy dissipators to convert catastrophic kinetic energy into internal thermal turbulence, preventing severe downstream bed scour and riverbank erosion. Applying the one-dimensional momentum conservation equation across the control volume while neglecting boundary friction yields the classical Bélanger equation (1828) relating initial supercritical depth y_1 to subcritical sequent (conjugate) depth y_2: y_2 / y_1 = 0.5 · [√(1 + 8·Fr_1²) - 1], where upstream Froude number Fr_1 = v_1 / √(g·y_1). Specific energy drops dramatically across the jump: head loss ΔE = E_1 - E_2 = (y_2 - y_1)³ / (4·y_1·y_2), representing macro-turbulent roller energy dissipation. Depending on initial Froude number Fr_1, jumps are categorized as undular (1.0 < Fr_1 < 1.7), weak (1.7 < Fr_1 < 2.5), oscillating (2.5 < Fr_1 < 4.5), steady (4.5 < Fr_1 < 9.0), or strong/choppy (Fr_1 > 9.0). In this laboratory flume experiment, supercritical flow is discharged beneath an adjustable sluice gate, and a downstream tailwater weir forces hydraulic jump stabilization to verify conjugate depths and energy dissipation efficiency.

5. Method

  1. Set flume horizontal; zero hook gauges at flume floor datum; open sluice gate to initial test aperture (e.g. 20 mm).
  2. Start recirculation pump; adjust discharge Q to establish high-velocity supercritical flow beneath the sluice gate.
  3. Raise downstream tailwater weir until a stable, stationary hydraulic jump forms in the middle glass inspection section of the flume.
  4. Position upstream hook gauge immediately upstream of jump roller; record supercritical flow depth y_1; log upstream velocity v_1 = Q / (B · y_1); compute Fr_1.
  5. Position downstream hook gauge immediately downstream of the surface roller where water surface becomes smooth and subcritical; record sequent depth y_2.
  6. Adjust gate aperture and pump discharge across six systematic Froude conditions (predictor = 1.5 to 4.5); record y_1, y_2; calculate energy loss ΔE.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Structural Engineering Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation evaluated hydraulic jump formation, conjugate depth ratios, and turbulent energy dissipation in a glass-walled open tilting flume across six systematically varied Froude numbers ($Fr_1 = 1.83$ to $5.45$, corresponding to Bélanger predictors $1.5$ to $4.5$). Experimental findings verify the classical Bélanger momentum equation and provide design parameters for spillway stilling basins.

2. Governing Momentum & Energy Equations

The conjugate depth ratio in a horizontal rectangular channel is predicted by:

\[ \frac{y_2}{y_1} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \sqrt{1 + 8 Fr_1^2} - 1 \right) \]

Specific energy head loss dissipated into turbulent heat is:

\[ \Delta E = E_1 - E_2 = \frac{(y_2 - y_1)^3}{4 y_1 y_2} \]

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

Testing utilized an $8.0\text{ m}$ recirculating flume ($0.30\text{ m}$ width, $0.45\text{ m}$ depth) with stainless steel bed and tempered glass walls. Flow was throttled through a precision vertical sluice gate and stabilized downstream by an adjustable hinged tailwater weir. Depths $y_1$ and $y_2$ were measured with Mitutoyo digital point gauges ($\pm 0.05\text{ mm}$). Velocity profiles were captured with a United Sensor Pitot-static tube.

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

Flume bed was leveled horizontally. Supercritical flow was established beneath the sluice gate. Tailwater was raised until the jump stabilized $2.5\text{ m}$ downstream of the gate. Supercritical depth $y_1$ was measured upstream of the jump toe, and subcritical depth $y_2$ downstream of the surface roller. Discharge $Q$ was recorded from an electromagnetic flowmeter.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Violent surface roller splashing can wet flume walkways. Operators wore non-slip boots and safety goggles. Clear acrylic flume spray hoods were placed over the hydraulic jump section.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

For low Froude numbers ($Fr_1 = 1.83$), the jump exhibited a gentle undulating wave surface with low energy loss ($\Delta E / E_1 = 9.2\%$). As Froude number reached $4.90$, intense turbulent rollers formed with violent air entrainment, dissipating up to $52.4\%$ of initial specific energy. The bottom roller maintained stable attachment to the flume floor.

10. Hydraulic Infrastructure Design & Stilling Basins

In dam spillway design (USBR Type II and III basins), stilling basin floor elevation must be set below downstream riverbed level so that tailwater depth $y_t$ matches or slightly exceeds sequent depth $y_2$, locking the jump securely inside the concrete apron under design flood discharge.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why is boundary friction negligible in the Bélanger momentum balance?
A1: The hydraulic jump occurs over an extremely short distance ($L_j \approx 6 y_2$), making the boundary shear force tiny compared to the enormous hydrostatic pressure difference and momentum flux change.

Q2: What is the consequence if downstream tailwater depth exceeds sequent depth y_2?
A2: The jump is drowned or submerged; the high-velocity supercritical jet plunges beneath the deep water surface as a submerged drowned jet, reducing bottom turbulence dissipation.
