4. Theory and graph choice

The electrocardiogram (ECG) represents the spatial summation of electrical action potentials propagating through myocardial conduction pathways and ventricular muscle fibers during each cardiac cycle. Willem Einthoven in 1901 formalized the mathematical foundation of electrocardiology by conceptualizing the human torso as an equilateral triangle with the heart at the electrical center. By convention, bipolar limb leads measure potential differences between electrodes placed on the limbs: Lead I measures right arm to left arm (V_LA - V_RA), Lead II measures right arm to left leg (V_LL - V_RA), and Lead III measures left arm to left leg (V_LL - V_LA). Kirchoff's voltage law dictates Einthoven's fundamental triangle law: Lead II = Lead I + Lead III. During ventricular depolarization (QRS complex), the instantaneous spatial dipole vector can be projected onto the hexaxial frontal reference system to calculate the mean electrical cardiac axis (normally between -30° and +90°). Deviations from this normal axis provide crucial clinical diagnostic markers for left ventricular hypertrophy (left axis deviation), right ventricular strain, pulmonary embolism, and bundle branch conduction blocks. In this laboratory experiment, a multi-lead medical electrocardiograph is coupled to an anatomical thoracic cardiac vector phantom to record standard frontal limb leads, evaluate lead proportionality, and calculate the anatomical cardiac electrical axis.

R_II = k_axis · R_I + R_0 = 1.2500 · R_I + 0.1500

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Lead I QRS amplitude R_I (mV), and the dependent coordinate y represents Lead II QRS amplitude R_II (mV). The gradient m directly determines Cardiac Frontal Vector Ratio k_axis, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Affix Ag/AgCl electrodes to standard phantom limb terminals: RA (Right Arm), LA (Left Arm), LL (Left Leg), and RL (Right Leg ground reference).
  2. Set the internal dipole generator angle to standard anatomical normal orientation of +51.3° in the frontal plane.
  3. Record baseline resting traces; verify that instantaneous voltages satisfy Einthoven's law: V_II(t) = V_I(t) + V_III(t) at all points along the QRS.
  4. Set cardiac synthesizer dipole strength to produce a Lead I QRS peak amplitude R_I of 0.20 mV; log stabilized Lead II amplitude R_II in millivolts.
  5. Increment dipole strength so that Lead I reads 0.40, 0.60, 0.80, 1.00, and 1.20 mV, recording corresponding Lead II amplitudes.
  6. Plot R_II versus R_I to determine vector ratio k_axis, calculating frontal plane axis angle θ = arctan[(2·R_II - R_I) / (√3 · R_I)].
