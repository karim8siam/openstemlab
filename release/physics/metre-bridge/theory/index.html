Metre bridge theory

Understand metre bridge: the equations, assumptions, supporting concepts and measurement method.

WorkbenchDetailed theoryMethodMy resultsExample report
THE WORKING RELATIONSHIP
R/S = l/(100 − l); (100 − l)/l = mS + b; R = 1/m

Keep the physical quantities, graph variables and units consistent.

01Bridge geometry and symbols

Place the unknown R in the left gap and known S in the right gap. The wire runs from A at 0 cm to B at 100 cm. A galvanometer joins the gap junction to a movable jockey J on the wire. Define l as AJ, measured from the unknown-resistor end. A formula remembered without this orientation can invert the resistance ratio.

Left wire length = l; right wire length = L − l; L = 100.0 cm
From which end is l measured in this lesson?

From the 0 cm end adjacent to the unknown resistance R.

02Why a null gives a ratio

At balance no current flows through the galvanometer, so its two terminals are at equal potential. The gap resistors form one potential divider and the two wire segments form another. Equating the fractional voltage drops gives R/S equal to the ratio of the two wire resistances. This equality uses the null condition, not the assumption that all currents in the circuit are equal.

R/S = r_AJ/r_JB at balance
Does zero galvanometer current mean every part of the bridge carries zero current?

No. Current still flows through the two main branches; only the detector branch is at null.

03Uniform wire and length ratio

For a wire of uniform resistivity and cross-sectional area, each segment resistance is proportional to length. The common factor cancels in the ratio, giving R/S = l/(L − l). Both lengths must use the same unit. If S = R, the model balance lies at the midpoint. Wire nonuniformity and resistance at the ends disturb this ideal relation.

R = S l/(100.0 − l), with l in cm
What balance length is expected when R = S?

50.0 cm for a 100.0 cm ideal bridge.

04From balance readings to a straight line

Rearrange the bridge equation as (100.0 − l)/l = S/R. Plot known resistance S in Ω horizontally and the dimensionless ratio y = (100.0 − l)/l vertically. The slope has unit Ω⁻¹ and equals 1/R; R = 1/m. A free intercept helps reveal systematic departures but cannot correct every end effect.

y = mS + b; m = 1/R; b_ideal = 0
What is the vertical-axis unit?

It is dimensionless because it is a ratio of two lengths in the same unit.

05Why central balances are preferred

For a fixed absolute length uncertainty, the relative resistance error from l is approximately L u(l)/[l(L − l)]. This is smallest near the midpoint and grows towards either end. Choose S similar to R in a physical experiment so balances usually lie near the centre; an exploratory range can demonstrate why end positions are less suitable. The available 1 Ω setting gives a balance near 83.3 cm and should not be treated as a preferred physical setting.

u(R)/R ≈ L u(l)/[l(L − l)] for the length contribution
Does a balance very close to an end improve precision?

No. The ratio becomes highly sensitive to a small length error.

06Reversal, contact effects and uncertainty

In physical work exchange R and S, measure the new balance from the same geometric zero, and apply the corresponding reversed formula R = S(L − l_reversed)/l_reversed. Compare both determinations; interchange can diagnose and reduce some end effects, without removing arbitrary wire nonuniformity. Include uncertainty of S, null detection and l, and inspect drift. Ordinary least squares here treats S as exact and does not weight the transformed length uncertainties.

u(R)/R ≈ sqrt[(u(S)/S)² + (L u(l)/(l(L − l)))²]
After interchanging the gap resistors, can the original formula be used unchanged?

No. The orientation has changed, so the resistance ratio must be written for the new arrangement.

Connect the equations to a measurement.

The model places unknown R = 5 Ω on the left and known S on the right. It computes l = 100R/(R + S), rounded to 0.1 cm. The fitted graph uses x = S and y = (100 − l)/l, so resistance is 1/gradient. Range endpoints are available for sensitivity exploration; real measurements should favor central balances.
