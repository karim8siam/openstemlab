FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the California Bearing Ratio (CBR) of compacted subgrade soil under soaked and unsoaked conditions, evaluate penetration resistance force (P), and determine pavement design thickness according to ASTM D1883 / AASHTO T193. Standardized parameter settings: 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5, 4.0, 4.5, 5.0 mm.

01

Soil Moisture Dosing & Compaction

Mix 6.0 kg of base course soil at Optimum Moisture Content (OMC); compact into 152.4 mm CBR mold in five layers with 56 blows per layer of 4.54 kg rammer; trim surface flush.

02

Surcharge Placement & 96-Hour Soaking

Place perforated swell plate and 4.54 kg surcharge weights on soil; mount swell dial gauge; submerge mold in water bath for 96 hours (4 days); record daily swell percentage.

03

Post-Soak Draining & Surface Drying

Remove mold from bath; allow water to drain freely through baseplate for 15 minutes; remove surcharge weights; wipe excess surface water.

04

CBR Machine Mounting & Seating

Place mold on machine platen; reposition 4.54 kg surcharge weights; seat penetration piston centrally with a small seating load of 45 N; zero load and displacement channels.

05

Constant-Rate Piston Penetration

Engage machine at constant rate of 1.27 mm/min (0.05 in/min); log penetration force P at depths of 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5, 4.0, 4.5, and 5.0 mm.

06

CBR Calculation & Pavement Sizing

Plot force versus penetration depth; apply initial concave-upward zero correction if necessary; evaluate CBR at 2.5 mm and 5.0 mm; determine governing design CBR%.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

The California Bearing Ratio (CBR) is the universal empirical design parameter used worldwide in AASHTO, FAA, and Asphalt Institute methodologies for determining the structural thickness of flexible asphalt pavements and airport runways. Conceived by the California Division of Highways in the late 1920s, the CBR test (ASTM D1883) evaluates the mechanical shearing resistance of compacted subgrade, subbase, and base course soils relative to a standard crushed limestone base material. Soil prepared at Optimum Moisture Content (OMC) is compacted into a heavy cylindrical steel mold (152.4 mm diameter, 177.8 mm height) in five layers using a 4.54 kg rammer. To simulate the worst-case moisture scenario during seasonal spring thaws and monsoon floods, specimens are subjected to a 96-hour (4-day) soaking period in a water bath under surcharge weights simulating pavement overburden (minimum 4.54 kg), while swell dial gauges monitor vertical volumetric swelling. The soaked mold is then mounted in a motorized compression frame, and a hardened steel piston (49.63 mm diameter, cross-sectional area 1935 mm² / 3.0 in²) penetrates the soil at a standardized constant rate of 1.27 mm/min (0.05 in/min). Penetration force P is recorded at standard depths, notably 2.54 mm (0.1 in) and 5.08 mm (0.2 in). The CBR percentage is calculated by dividing measured piston resistance by the standard penetration resistance of high-grade crushed California stone (13.34 kN at 2.54 mm, and 20.00 kN at 5.08 mm): CBR% = (P / P_std) x 100%. In this laboratory, penetration force is logged across penetration depths from 1.5 to 5.0 mm to calibrate the penetration stiffness slope and extract the design CBR.
