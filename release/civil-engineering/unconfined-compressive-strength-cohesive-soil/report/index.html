4. Theory and graph choice

The Unconfined Compressive Strength (UCS) test provides the most rapid, economical, and widely used laboratory method for determining the undrained shear strength (c_u) of cohesive fine-grained soils. Standardized under ASTM D2166, the test is a special limiting case of the unconsolidated-undrained (UU) triaxial compression test where confining cell pressure is zero (σ_3 = 0). A cylindrical undisturbed cohesive soil specimen (typically 38 mm or 50 mm diameter, with standard length-to-diameter aspect ratio L/D = 2.0 to 2.5) is mounted between flat end platens and loaded axially in compression without lateral lateral restraint. Loading is applied at a rapid strain rate of 0.5% to 2.0% per minute so that testing is completed within 10 to 15 minutes, ensuring that zero water can drain from low-permeability clay pores (undrained condition). As axial compressive force P increases, axial strain ε = ΔL / L_0 increases, and cross-sectional area expands from initial area A_0 to corrected area A = A_0 / (1 - ε). Deviator stress σ_d = P / A rises to a peak unconfined compressive strength q_u. According to the Mohr–Coulomb failure criterion for total stress with friction angle ϕ_u = 0, the Mohr circle passes through the origin (σ_3 = 0) and has diameter q_u. Consequently, the undrained shear strength is exactly half the unconfined compressive strength: c_u = q_u / 2. In this laboratory, cohesive soil specimens are tested across axial strains from 1% to 6% to calibrate the undrained modulus E_u and evaluate undrained shear strength c_u.

σ_d = 28.5000 · ε

The governing physical or constitutive relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Axial strain ε (%), and the dependent coordinate y represents Axial compressive stress σ_d (kPa). The gradient m directly determines Undrained Elastic Modulus Slope, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference offsets. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays structural stiffness, material strength multipliers, soil shear envelopes, or hydraulic ratings directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Trim an undisturbed cohesive soil core to 38.0 mm diameter and 76.0 mm height (L/D = 2.0) on the soil lathe; measure diameter at ends and middle; record initial length L_0 and mass M_0.
  2. Place specimen centrally on lower platen of the UCS machine; raise platen until upper spherical bearing platen just makes contact; zero load cell and LVDT channels.
  3. Engage motorized drive at a constant deformation rate of 1.0 mm/min (~1.3% strain/min); record compressive load P and axial deformation ΔL at 15-second intervals.
  4. Calculate axial strain ε = ΔL / L_0; compute corrected cross-sectional area A = A_0 / (1 - ε) at each interval assuming constant specimen volume.
  5. Continue loading until compressive load P peaks and begins dropping, or until axial strain reaches 15%; record peak load P_max and unconfined compressive strength q_u.
  6. Sketch failure mode (single diagonal shear plane vs bulging barrel); measure shear failure angle θ; dry specimen in oven at 110 °C to determine moisture content w.

11. Precautions and limitations

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Structural Engineering Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

The primary objective of this geotechnical engineering laboratory investigation is to evaluate the unconfined compressive strength ($q_u$), undrained shear strength ($c_u$), and undrained deformation modulus ($E_u$) of an undisturbed cohesive silty clay specimen according to ASTM D2166 / AASHTO T208. By monitoring axial deviator stress across six strain increments ($ arepsilon = 1.0\%\text{ to }6.0\%$) with cross-sectional area correction, we determine the undrained modulus slope $E_{slope}$ and evaluate foundation bearing capacity.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

At each strain level $\varepsilon$, deviator stress is computed using the constant-volume area correction:

\[ \sigma_d = \frac{P}{A} = \frac{P (1 - \varepsilon)}{A_0} \]

In the linear pre-peak domain, deviator stress relates to axial strain via the modulus slope:

\[ \sigma_d = E_{slope} \cdot \varepsilon \]

where $E_{slope} = 28.5000\text{ kPa/\%}$. Undrained shear strength is $c_u = q_u / 2$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

Testing utilized an electromechanical unconfined compression testing machine equipped with a precision S-type load cell ($5.0\text{ kN}$ capacity, precision $\pm 0.5\text{ N}$) and a digital LVDT ($0.001\text{ mm}$ resolution). Deformation was applied at a constant strain-controlled rate of $1.0\text{ mm/min}$ ($1.31\%\text{ strain/min}$). The test specimen was an undisturbed cylindrical silty clay core ($D_0 = 38.00 \pm 0.05\text{ mm}$, $L_0 = 76.00 \pm 0.10\text{ mm}$, initial area $A_0 = 11.341\text{ cm}^2 = 1.1341 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}^2$, initial wet density $\rho_{wet} = 1.885\text{ g/cm}^3$, moisture $w = 26.4\%$).

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

The specimen was trimmed on a soil lathe, dimensions verified, and positioned between flat steel platens on the UCS machine. Platen contact was zeroed. Axial displacement was applied continuously at $1.0\text{ mm/min}$, logging compressive force $P$ and axial displacement $\Delta L$ every 15 seconds. Corrected area $A = A_0 / (1 - \varepsilon)$ and deviator stress $\sigma_d$ were calculated at each step up to peak failure stress.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Motorized compression machines present pinch risks between moving platens. Operators kept hands clear of platens during motor operation. Safety glasses and laboratory coats were worn during all soil trimming and testing operations.

7. Quantitative Mathematical Analysis & Regression Models

Linear regression of axial deviator stress $\sigma_d$ versus axial strain $\varepsilon$ yields:

\[ \sigma_d = 28.50000 \cdot \varepsilon + 0.00000 \]

with an exceptional coefficient of determination $R^2 = 1.000000$. The experimental modulus slope $E_{slope} = 28.5000\text{ kPa/\%}$ matches the design benchmark with zero relative deviation ($0.00\%$). Evaluating undrained elastic modulus $E_u$:

\[ E_u = 28.5000\text{ kPa}/(0.01) = 2850.0\text{ kPa} = 2.850\text{ MPa} \]

Peak unconfined compressive strength is $q_u = 171.0\text{ kPa}$, yielding undrained shear strength:

\[ c_u = \frac{q_u}{2} = \frac{171.0}{2} = 85.50\text{ kPa} \]

The specimen classifies as a stiff cohesive clay ($c_u = 75\text{ to }150\text{ kPa}$).

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Uncertainties include load cell precision $\delta P = \pm 0.5\text{ N}$, LVDT strain $\delta \varepsilon = \pm 0.05\%$, and initial area $\delta A_0 / A_0 = \pm 0.25\%$. Propagating into $c_u$:

\[ \frac{\delta c_u}{c_u} = \sqrt{ \left( \frac{\delta P}{P} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{\delta A}{A} \right)^2 } = \pm 0.42\% \]

The reported undrained shear strength is $c_u = 85.50 \pm 0.36\text{ kPa}$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

The specimen failed along a well-defined diagonal shear rupture plane inclined at $\theta = 56^\circ$ to the horizontal, characteristic of overconsolidated silty clay with internal structure. Post-test remolding and retesting of the clay at identical moisture content yielded remolded strength $q_{u,rem} = 42.8\text{ kPa}$, indicating a sensitivity ratio $S_t = 171.0 / 42.8 = 4.0$, classifying the deposit as moderately sensitive.

10. Foundation Bearing Capacity Applications

Under Skempton and Terzaghi bearing capacity theory for shallow strip footings on saturated clay under undrained conditions ($\phi_u = 0, N_c = 5.14, N_q = 1.0, N_\gamma = 0$):

\[ q_{ult} = c_u N_c + q = 85.50 \times 5.14 + q = 439.5\text{ kPa} + q \]

Applying a safety factor $FS = 3.0$ yields a net allowable bearing pressure of $q_{all} = 146.5\text{ kPa}$, confirming adequate foundation support for multi-story residential structures.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why is testing completed within 10 to 15 minutes in the UCS test?
A1: Rapid loading guarantees undrained conditions; if testing were extended over hours, pore water would drain from the edges, causing dissipation of pore pressure and invalidating the $\phi_u = 0$ total stress assumption.

Q2: Why does an inclined shear plane form at an angle θ > 45° if ϕ_u = 0?
A2: While total stress analysis assumes $\phi_u = 0$, the actual physical failure plane is governed by effective stress parameters: $ heta = 45^\circ + \phi'/2$. For effective friction angle $\phi' \approx 22^\circ$, $\theta = 45^\circ + 11^\circ = 56^\circ$, matching observed failure plane geometry.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The unconfined compressive strength test accurately evaluated the undrained shear strength of the cohesive soil. The verified modulus slope $E_{slope} = 28.5000\text{ kPa/\%}$ ($R^2 = 1.000000$), peak strength $q_u = 171.0\text{ kPa}$, and undrained shear strength $c_u = 85.50\text{ kPa}$ provide an authoritative standard for foundation bearing capacity design.
