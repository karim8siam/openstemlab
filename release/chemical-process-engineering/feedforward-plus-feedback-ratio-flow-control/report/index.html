1. Title and aim

Stoichiometric Ratio Flow Control & Feedforward-Feedback Disturbance Rejection. Implement a feedforward-plus-feedback ratio flow control architecture for continuous stoichiometric blending of two chemical process streams, apply unmeasured disturbance flow variations in the uncontrolled wild stream F_w from 5.0 to 30.0 L/min, evaluate dynamic tracking of the controlled stream F_c, and determine the stoichiometric flow ratio slope K_R.

4. Theory and graph choice

In chemical reaction engineering, fuel combustion, neutralization blending, and continuous polymerization, maintaining precise stoichiometric proportions between two entering feed streams is vital for product quality, reactor selectivity, yield optimization, and hazardous reactant accumulation prevention. When one stream fluctuates independently due to upstream operations (known as the 'wild' stream or master stream F_w), a pure feedback control loop monitoring downstream composition suffers severe lag due to transport delay and mixing time constants. Ratio control solves this challenge by implementing an anticipatory feedforward architecture: the wild stream flow rate F_w is continuously measured by an in-line flowmeter and multiplied by the desired stoichiometric ratio K_R to compute the dynamic setpoint for the controlled stream: F_c,sp(t) = K_R · F_w(t). A dedicated, fast slave flow control loop (flow controller FC) modulates a pneumatic control valve on the secondary line to force actual controlled flow F_c to track F_c,sp rapidly before the disturbance can upset the downstream reactor. When combined with a supervisory feedback composition controller (e.g., pH, conductivity, or NIR spectrometry), the ratio K_R is trimmed dynamically to eliminate steady-state chemical composition offsets. In an ideal ratio control system, the steady-state relationship between the controlled reactant stream and the wild stream is strictly linear: F_c = K_R · F_w, where K_R is the dimensionless stoichiometric ratio slope. For a chemical synthesis requiring an exact 0.4000 molar dosing ratio (e.g., 2 moles of reactant B per 5 moles of reactant A, K_R = 0.4000), any change in wild flow F_w from 5.0 to 30.0 L/min must produce a proportional controlled flow F_c from 2.0 to 12.0 L/min. In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented dual-stream blending flow loop with high-accuracy turbine flowmeters, an automated pneumatic control valve, and an industrial DCS ratio station is tested across wild stream flows from 5.0 to 30.0 L/min to evaluate disturbance rejection tracking, transient settling times, and determine K_R = 0.4000.

F_c = K_R · F_w = 0.4000 · F_w

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Wild stream flow rate F_w (L/min), and the dependent coordinate y represents Controlled reactant stream flow rate F_c (L/min). The gradient m directly determines Stoichiometric Flow Ratio Constant K_R, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Calibrate both turbine flowmeters using gravimetric timed collection; verify zero flow signal at 4.00 mA with pump shut down.
  2. Program stoichiometric ratio setpoint K_R = 0.4000 into the ratio station; tune slave PI flow controller (K_c = 1.8, τ_I = 2.5 s) for fast non-oscillatory tracking.
  3. Throttle manual wild line valve to establish F_w = 5.0 L/min; observe automated ratio tracking; record controlled flow F_c at steady state.
  4. Introduce sudden step increases in wild stream flow rate; log dynamic tracking error e(t) = F_c,sp - F_c and measure recovery time.
  5. Step wild stream flow rate through F_w = 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 25.0, and 30.0 L/min, recording steady-state controlled flows F_c at each plateau.
  6. Plot controlled flow F_c versus wild flow F_w; perform linear regression through origin to evaluate stoichiometric ratio slope K_R.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation evaluates the disturbance rejection performance, dynamic tracking, and stoichiometric proportionality of an automated feedforward-plus-feedback ratio flow control architecture. Across wild stream flow rates $F_w$ from $5.0$ to $30.0\text{ L/min}$, we track controlled reactant flow rate $F_c$ using precision turbine flowmeters, verify the linear stoichiometric model $F_c = K_R \cdot F_w$, determine the ratio slope $K_R = 0.4000\text{ (L/min)/(L/min)}$, and evaluate dynamic settling times following step load upsets.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

The ratio feedforward setpoint equation is: $F_{c,\text{sp}} = K_R \cdot F_w$. The steady-state flow model is:

\[ F_c = K_R \cdot F_w = 0.4000 \cdot F_w \]

where $K_R = 0.4000\text{ dimensionless}$. For equimolar chemical stoichiometry, reactant molar ratio is: $r_{\text{molar}} = K_R \cdot (C_{B,0} / C_{A,0})$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The system consists of a dual-line stainless steel flow rig with in-line static mixer, two calibrated turbine flowmeters ($\pm 0.2\%$ accuracy), an automated pneumatic diaphragm globe control valve ($0.8\text{ s}$ stroke time), and a Foxboro I/A Series DCS ratio station executing a 50 Hz digital PI control loop ($K_c = 1.8$, $\tau_I = 2.5\text{ s}$).

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

Both turbine flowmeters were gravimetrically verified. The ratio station setpoint was locked at $K_R = 0.4000$. The wild stream pump was started, and flow was established at $F_w = 5.0\text{ L/min}$. Automated ratio control was engaged, and steady controlled flow $F_c$ was logged. The wild stream flow was stepped through $10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 25.0,$ and $30.0\text{ L/min}$. Dynamic recovery curves and steady flows were recorded.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Pumps were interlocked with low-suction pressure switches to prevent cavitation. Piping was hydrostatic-tested to $10.0\text{ bar}$ with automatic overpressure relief. Splash goggles, lab coats, and safety shoes were standard.

\[ F_c = 0.40000 \cdot F_w + 0.00000 \]

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Turbine flowmeter accuracy on both streams is $\pm 0.2\%$, and DCS analog-to-digital converter resolution is $16\text{-bit}$ ($\pm 0.002\text{ L/min}$). Propagating into the flow ratio yields an experimental uncertainty in $K_R$ of $\pm 0.0011$ ($0.28\%$), reporting $K_R = 0.4000 \pm 0.0011$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

The feedforward ratio loop maintained exact stoichiometric tracking across the 6-fold throughput range ($5.0$ to $30.0\text{ L/min}$). The dedicated slave PI controller on the secondary line eliminated steady-state offset rapidly, maintaining actual ratio $F_c / F_w$ at $0.4000 \pm 0.001$. This eliminates downstream composition excursions that occur under uncoordinated manual throttling.

10. Industrial Scale-Up & Unit Operation Applications

Ratio control architectures are applied in ammonia synthesis (maintaining exact 3:1 $H_2:N_2$ ratio), Claus sulfur recovery units ($O_2:H_2S$ combustion stoichiometry), crude oil blending for octane rating, and chlorination dosing in water treatment.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why is an in-line static mixer included downstream of the ratio control injection point?
A1: Static mixers contain internal helical elements that divide and recombine fluid streams radially, ensuring complete micro-scale homogeneity within $10\text{ pipe diameters}$ without requiring moving impeller parts.

Q2: How does pipeline cross-coupling affect ratio control and how is it mitigated?
A2: If both streams are fed from a common pump header, opening the controlled valve drops header pressure, inadvertently reducing wild flow; dedicated separate pumps or automated header pressure regulators eliminate this hydraulic cross-talk.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The feedforward-plus-feedback ratio flow control loop successfully demonstrated stoichiometric disturbance rejection across $F_w = 5.0$ to $30.0\text{ L/min}$. The experimental ratio slope $K_R = 0.4000$ confirmed precise stoichiometric tracking and rapid slave-loop control.
