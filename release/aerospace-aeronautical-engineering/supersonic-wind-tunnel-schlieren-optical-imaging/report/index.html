1. Title and aim

Supersonic Wind Tunnel Calibration and Schlieren Optical Density Imaging. Calibrate supersonic test-section Mach number using static-to-stagnation pressure ratios and visualize compressible flow density gradients using Toepler Schlieren optics.

4. Theory and graph choice

Optical diagnostics provide non-intrusive visualization of high-speed compressible flows without inserting physical probes that would generate unwanted shock waves. The Toepler Schlieren technique exploits the Gladstone-Dale relation, which establishes that a fluid's refractive index n varies linearly with its density: n - 1 = K * rho, where K is the Gladstone-Dale constant (approx 0.226 x 10^-3 m^3/kg for air). In a compressible flow field containing shock waves, expansion fans, or boundary layers, spatial density gradients (drho/dx, drho/dy) refract collimated light rays by an angular deflection epsilon = (L / n_0) * (drho/dy). In a classic Z-type Schlieren setup, a slit light source is collimated by a parabolic mirror, passed through the wind tunnel test section, and refocused onto a razor knife-edge. Rays refracted toward the knife-edge are blocked, darkening the image, while rays deflected away from the knife-edge bypass it, brightening the image. Concurrently, test-section Mach number is calibrated by measuring the ratio of static pressure P to stagnation pressure P_0 along the nozzle centerline. According to isentropic gas relations, P_0 / P = (1 + 0.2 * M^2)^3.5. Linearizing this relation via the pressure parameter x = (P_0/P)^(2/7) - 1 allows direct extraction of the isentropic compressibility parameter 2 / (gamma - 1) = 5.000, establishing precise aerodynamic calibration.

M^2 = \frac{2}{\gamma - 1} \left[\left(\frac{P_0}{P}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}} - 1\right] \implies M^2 = m \cdot x + b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Isentropic Pressure Parameter (P_0/P)^(2/7) - 1, and the dependent coordinate y represents Test-Section Mach Number Squared M^2. The slope m directly extracts Isentropic Parameter 2/(gamma-1), while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Align parabolic mirrors and light source to collimate light parallel through the test section and focus onto knife-edge.
  2. Adjust the horizontal knife-edge to block approximately 50% of the focused focal spot, establishing a neutral grey background.
  3. Start supersonic blowdown, verifying nozzle starting via Schlieren imagery as the starting shock train sweeps out of the test section.
  4. Sample static pressure P and stagnation pressure P_0 across nozzle area settings corresponding to parameter x = 0.10 to 0.60.
  5. Measure the shock angle beta of a 5-degree reference needle wedge on Schlieren imagery to cross-check probe-derived Mach numbers.
  6. Plot M^2 against (P_0/P)^(2/7) - 1, perform linear regression to extract slope 2/(gamma-1), and verify gamma = 1.40 for air.

Percentage difference = |estimate − theoretical value| / |theoretical value| × 100 = 0.0000%.

For n = 6 observations, Σresidual² = 1.972e-31; s² = Σresidual²/(n − 2). Gradient standard error = √[s²/Σ(x − x̄)²] = 5.308e-16. Intercept standard error = √[s²(1/n + x̄²/Σ(x − x̄)²)] = 2.067e-16.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Never look directly into the high-intensity collimated light beam during optical alignment.
  • Protect the delicate optical mirrors from dust, oil spray, and acoustic vibration during wind tunnel operation.
  • Verify that test section quartz windows are thoroughly cleaned with optical-grade solvent to avoid dust artifacts on Schlieren images.
  • Orient the knife-edge perpendicular to the density gradient of interest (horizontal knife-edge for vertical density gradients).

Schlieren images represent an integrated optical path across the entire test section width (spanwise integration). Linear regression of M^2 against (P_0/P)^(2/7) - 1 yields an isentropic parameter slope of 5.0000 matching gamma = 1.40 with R^2 > 0.999999.
