Physical chemistry assumptions and metrological limits

Theoretical Freundlich parameters: capacity parameter K_F = 2.500 mmol/g · (L/mol)^(1/n), heterogeneity exponent 1/n = 0.4500 (n = 2.222). Across the six equilibrium concentration coordinates C_eq = [0.030, 0.060, 0.120, 0.220, 0.350, 0.550] M, the corresponding specific adsorption capacities are q = x/m = [0.516, 0.705, 0.963, 1.265, 1.559, 1.910] mmol/g. Logarithmic coordinates are x = log10(C_eq) = [−1.5229, −1.2218, −0.9208, −0.6576, −0.4559, −0.2596] and y = log10(x/m) = [−0.2874, −0.1519, −0.0164, 0.1020, 0.1928, 0.2811]. Ordinary least-squares regression yields an empirical slope 1/n = 0.45002 and Y-intercept log10(K_F) = 0.39795 (K_F = 2.500 mmol/g) with Pearson correlation coefficient R² = 0.999999990. The empirical exponent 1/n = 0.450 exhibits near-perfect concordance within 0.005% of the 0.450 literature benchmark, confirming favorable physical adsorption on a heterogeneous microporous carbon matrix.

Fluted quantitative filter paper cones and glass funnels pre-rinsed with deionized water to isolate clear aqueous filtrates from the activated charcoal slurry without acid retention.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the adsorption isotherm of aqueous acetic acid on activated charcoal at 25.0 °C and evaluate the Freundlich adsorption parameters (exponent 1/n and capacity constant K_F) and Langmuir parameters. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 0.03, 0.06, 0.12, 0.22, 0.35, 0.55 M.

01

Massing of Activated Charcoal and Initial Solution Preparation

Accurately weigh six 2.000 ± 0.001 g samples of dried activated charcoal on an analytical balance and transfer into six clean, dry 250 mL glass-stoppered Erlenmeyer flasks labelled 1 through 6. Prepare 100.0 mL portions of standardized acetic acid solutions with initial concentrations C0 = 0.050, 0.100, 0.200, 0.350, 0.500, and 0.750 M in Class-A volumetric flasks.

02

Addition of Acid Solutions and Thermostatic Shaker Equilibration

Add exactly 100.0 mL of each acetic acid solution to the corresponding charcoal flask. Firmly insert ground-glass stoppers, secure flasks in the clamps of the thermostatted water bath shaker at 25.0 ± 0.1 °C, and agitate at 150 rpm for 90 minutes to ensure heterogeneous solid-liquid adsorption equilibrium is completely established.

03

Filtration and Rejection of Initial Column Wash Filtrate

Filter each equilibrated suspension through dry Whatman No. 42 quantitative filter paper into clean, dry Erlenmeyer flasks. Discard the first 15 mL of filtrate from each flask to eliminate experimental errors caused by minor preferential adsorption of acetic acid or water by the cellulose filter paper matrix.

04

Quantitative Titrimetric Analysis of Residual Acetic Acid

Pipette duplicate aliquots of the clear equilibrium filtrate (20.00 mL for flasks 1–3; 10.00 mL for flasks 4–6) into clean 125 mL titration flasks. Add 3 drops of 0.1% phenolphthalein indicator and titrate with standardized 0.1000 M NaOH until the persistent faint pink endpoint (stable for > 30 seconds). Record burette volumes to ±0.02 mL.

05

Calculation of Equilibrium Concentration and Specific Adsorption

Determine the equilibrium concentration C_eq = (V_NaOH · M_NaOH) / V_aliquot in mol/L. Compute the total millimoles of acetic acid adsorbed: x = (C0 − C_eq) · V_total (where V_total = 100.0 mL = 0.1000 L). Calculate the specific adsorption capacity q = x / m (in mmol of acetic acid adsorbed per gram of charcoal).

06

Linear Regression and Freundlich Isotherm Model Evaluation

Evaluate the logarithmic coordinates X = log10(C_eq) and Y = log10(x/m) for all six flasks. Execute ordinary least-squares linear regression to determine the slope (Freundlich heterogeneity exponent 1/n) and Y-intercept (log10 K_F). Evaluate the correlation coefficient R² and compare with the linearized Langmuir model (C_eq/q vs C_eq) to confirm the heterogeneous nature of the charcoal surface.
