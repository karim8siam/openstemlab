1. Title and aim

Electromagnetic Blood Flowmeter Probe Calibration & Faraday Induction. Determine the induced transverse bio-potential across cannulated physiological saline flow under an applied transverse magnetic field and verify Faraday's law of induction.

4. Theory and graph choice

In cardiovascular surgery, extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO), and organ perfusion hemodynamics, real-time continuous measurement of volumetric blood flow rate is essential for evaluating cardiac output and graft patency. Invented by Alexander Kolin in 1936, the electromagnetic blood flowmeter operates on the fundamental physical principle of Faraday's law of induction: when an electrically conductive fluid (such as blood containing dissociated Na⁺, K⁺, and Cl⁻ electrolytes) moves with mean velocity v through a transverse magnetic field of flux density B, moving ions experience Lorentz forces (F = q · (v × B)) that deflect positive and negative ions toward opposite vessel walls. This creates a transverse electric potential V_i perpendicular to both the flow velocity vector and magnetic field vector: V_i = B · d · v, where d is the inner lumen vessel diameter. Substituting volumetric flow rate Q = v · (π·d² / 4) yields a strictly linear relationship: V_i = [4·B / (π·d)] · Q = k_q · Q. To eliminate electrochemical DC half-cell offset drift, modern medical flowmeter probes utilize low-frequency square-wave or sinusoidal AC electromagnet excitation (typically 200 Hz to 400 Hz). In this experiment, a calibrated cannulated electromagnetic flow probe is tested with physiological saline across controlled volumetric flow rates to evaluate linear induction sensitivity k_q.

V_i = k_q · Q = 0.3600 · Q

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Volumetric flow rate Q (mL/min), and the dependent coordinate y represents Induced potential V_i (μV). The gradient m directly determines Flowmeter Induction Constant k_q, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Fill the flow loop with 0.9% physiological saline at 37.0 °C, purge all air bubbles from the probe lumen, stop pump, and adjust zero offset to 0.00 μV.
  2. Energize probe electromagnet with 250 Hz square-wave excitation; verify magnetic flux density B = 0.050 T at center bore.
  3. Set perfusion pump to deliver steady 50 mL/min volumetric flow rate, verified via timed gravimetric collection on electronic balance.
  4. Sample the synchronously gated microvolt potential V_i across platinum pickup electrodes over a 30-second integration window.
  5. Increment pump delivery to 100, 150, 200, 250, and 300 mL/min, recording stabilized induced voltage V_i at each flow step.
  6. Plot induced voltage V_i against volumetric flow rate Q, computing the induction constant k_q via linear regression through the origin.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Purge all microscopic air bubbles from the sensor lumen; air is an electrical insulator that open-circuits the electrode path.
  • Ensure grounding of the fluid reservoir to avoid stray electrostatic charge buildup in plastic tubing.
  • Verify that magnetic excitation frequency is locked to synchronous demodulator sampling gates.
  • Maintain constant fluid temperature (37.0 °C) to stabilize electrolyte conductivity.

The linear induction model applies across non-turbulent and turbulent regimes up to 300 mL/min for the 6 mm probe. Evaluated using a 6.0 mm internal diameter cannulated flow probe with a 250 Hz square-wave AC magnetic excitation field (B = 0.050 Tesla). Perfusion fluid is physiological saline (0.9% NaCl, electrical conductivity 1.6 S/m) pumped from 50 to 300 mL/min.
