Aeronautical assumptions and boundary condition limits

Bredt-Batho theory assumes constant shear flow along each segment without localized rivet hole stress concentrations. Linear regression of the rate of twist against applied torque yields a compliance slope of 1.1765 mrad/(kN-m^2) corresponding to GJ = 850.0 kN-m^2 with R^2 > 0.999999.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Measure shear flow q_1, q_2 in a 2-cell thin-walled aircraft wing box using Bredt-Batho theory and determine torsional stiffness constant J. Standardized parameter settings: 0.6, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0 kN-m.

01

Wing Box Cantilever Root Clamping

Rigidly clamp the root bulkhead into the heavy reaction frame, ensuring zero rotational compliance at z = 0.

02

Torque Disk Calibration

Mount the tip torque loading disk, aligning hydraulic actuators to generate pure couple torque without net shear.

03

Initial Torque Loading

Apply initial torque T = 0.6 kN-m, recording skin shear strains and spanwise rotation angles.

04

Systematic Torque Stepping

Step the torque through T = 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, and 3.0 kN-m, allowing 30 seconds at each plateau for strain stabilization.

05

Twist Rate and Shear Flow Extraction

Compute rate of twist dtheta/dz = (theta_tip - theta_mid) / Delta z and extract shear flows q_1 and q_2 from skin rosette strains.

06

Torsional Compliance Linear Regression

Plot (dtheta/dz) x 10^3 versus T, perform linear regression to determine slope 10^3 / (GJ), and compute experimental torsional constant J.
