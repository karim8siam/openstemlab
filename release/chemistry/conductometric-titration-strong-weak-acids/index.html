Physical chemistry assumptions and metrological limits

['Conductometric titration accuracy degrades at high ionic strengths (> 0.1 M) due to interionic Debye-Huckel-Onsager electrophoretic and relaxation effects, which cause slight non-linearity in the conductance branches.', 'The presence of high background concentrations of non-reactive inert salts (e.g., 1 M NaNO₃ or K₂SO₄) elevates total solution conductance to high baseline levels, reducing relative signal-to-noise sensitivity for the titration inflection points.', 'Near the equivalence point of very weak acids (pK_a > 7) or very dilute solutions (< 10⁻³ M), hydrolysis of the conjugate base produces rounded branch vertices rather than sharp linear intersections, requiring extrapolation from points well outside the equivalence zone.'] The conductometric titration of an acid mixture is governed by electrolytic conduction theory, Kohlrausch's law of independent ionic migration, and chemical equilibrium thermodynamics. As standardized sodium hydroxide is progressively added, the titration proceeds through three distinct chemical regimes: (1) neutralization of hydrochloric acid, where highly mobile hydronium ions (λ° = 349.8 S·cm²·mol⁻¹) are consumed and replaced by sodium ions (λ° = 50.11 S·cm²·mol⁻¹), generating a steep linear conductance drop (slope ≈ -0.355 mS·mL⁻¹); (2) neutralization of acetic acid, where unionized molecular CH₃COOH is converted into sodium acetate, steadily increasing conductance (slope ≈ +0.091 mS·mL⁻¹); and (3) addition of excess sodium hydroxide, where unreacted hydroxide ions (λ° = 198.6 S·cm²·mol⁻¹) cause a steep conductance rise (slope ≈ +0.249 mS·mL⁻¹). Applying the volume dilution correction factor G_corr = G_raw × (V_0 + V) / V_0 yields strictly linear segments whose intersections precisely define the equivalence volumes V_eq,1 and V_eq,2, enabling accurate determination of individual acid concentrations with relative uncertainties below 0.3%.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the individual concentrations of hydrochloric acid (strong acid) and acetic acid (weak acid) in an unknown binary mixture by conductometric titration with standardized sodium hydroxide. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 1.0, 2.5, 4.0, 5.5, 7.0, 8.5 mL.

01

Conductivity Cell Calibration & Constant Verification

Rinse the dip-type platinized platinum cell thoroughly with triple-distilled deionized water and blot gently with lint-free optical paper. Immerse the cell into certified 0.0100 M KCl calibration standard equilibrated at 25.00 °C. Measure electrolytic conductivity κ_std = 1.4087 mS/cm and verify that the measured conductance confirms cell constant K_cell = 1.000 ± 0.005 cm⁻¹.

02

Titration Vessel Assembly & Analyte Loading

Pipette exactly 25.00 mL of the unknown binary acid mixture (containing hydrochloric acid and acetic acid) into the double-walled thermostatic cell. Add 75.00 mL of freshly boiled, cooled deionized water (initial volume V_0 = 100.0 mL) to ensure the platinum electrode plates are fully submerged by at least 15 mm.

03

Thermostatic Equilibration & Baseline Conductance Acquisition

Turn on circulating water bath at 25.00 ± 0.05 °C and engage the magnetic stirrer at 350 RPM. Allow the reaction mixture to achieve steady thermal equilibrium over 10 minutes. Record the initial unneutralized solution conductance G_0 on the digital meter.

04

Strong Acid Neutralization Titration (Branch 1)

Add standardized 0.1000 M NaOH titrant from the micro-burette in increments of 1.00 mL to 1.50 mL. After each addition, stir for 15 seconds to achieve chemical uniformity, pause stirring for 5 seconds to ensure quiescent liquid between electrode plates, and log the stable conductance reading G in mS.

05

Weak Acid Neutralization & Excess Base Titration (Branches 2 & 3)

Continue progressive titrant dispensing through the conductance minimum at V ≈ 10.0 mL. Observe the moderate upward conductance trajectory during acetic acid neutralization up to the second equivalence point at V ≈ 25.0 mL. Extend additions past the second break point up to 35.0 mL to define the steep excess hydroxide branch.

06

Dilution Correction & Multi-Branch Equivalence Point Extraction

Calculate corrected conductance G_corr = G × (V_0 + V) / V_0 for every titration point. Perform ordinary least-squares linear regressions on Branch 1 (strong acid) and Branch 2 (weak acid). Solve for the first equivalence volume V_eq,1 from the intersection, and determine the concentration of hydrochloric acid c_HCl = (V_eq,1 × c_NaOH) / V_aliquot.
