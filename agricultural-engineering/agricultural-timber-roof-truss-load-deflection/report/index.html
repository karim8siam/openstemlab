1. Title and aim

Agricultural Timber Roof Truss Load-Deflection Mechanics. Investigate vertical ridge and mid-span flexural deflections of a full-scale timber Fink roof truss under symmetrical distributed vertical roof loading.

3. Apparatus and fixed settings

  • Heavy Structural Reaction Frame: Provides rigid steel reaction columns and cross-beams capable of withstanding 50 kN structural reactions.
  • Agricultural Timber Fink Truss: Full-scale 9.0-meter span test truss (4:12 pitch, Southern Pine chords, toothed metal plate joints).
  • Hydraulic Cylinder Whiffletree Loading Linkage: Applies symmetrical vertical point loads to purlin node joints simulating uniform roof snow loading.
  • Calibrated Precision Load Cell: Measures total vertical hydraulic force applied to the truss system with 0.05 kN resolution.
  • Linear Variable Differential Transformers (LVDTs): Monitors vertical deflections at ridge apex, lower chord mid-span, and quarter points to 0.01 mm accuracy.
  • Lateral Bracing Restraint System: Prevents out-of-plane lateral-torsional buckling of the top compression chord during gravity loading.
  • Truss type: 9.0 m span Fink 'W' (4:12 pitch)
  • Lumber grade: No. 2 Southern Pine (MOE = 9.6 GPa)
  • Connectors: 20-gauge galvanized steel tooth plates

4. Theory and graph choice

Agricultural post-frame buildings, grain flat storage sheds, and livestock shelters require economical, wide-span roof structural framing capable of resisting heavy design dead, snow, and wind loads. Light-frame timber roof trusses (commonly configured as Fink 'W', Howe, or Pratt planar frameworks) assembled with punched metal-plate tooth connectors provide high strength-to-weight ratios and structural stiffness. Structural safety and serviceability design (under ASABE EP486 and National Design Specification for Wood Construction) require that elastic vertical deflections under service snow and gravity loads remain well below serviceability thresholds (typically span/240 to span/360) to prevent roof ponding, ceiling finish cracking, and purlin displacement. In linear structural mechanics, overall elastic deflection δ at the apex ridge joint is directly proportional to applied total gravity load W through the structural flexural compliance coefficient C_f = δ / W (the inverse of truss stiffness). Compliance integrates axial elasticity of chords and webs, joint rotational semi-rigidity, and metal tooth connector tooth slip. In this experiment, a full-scale 9.0-meter span agricultural timber Fink truss mounted in a heavy reaction loading frame is subjected to hydraulic cylinder gravity loads to quantify load-deflection linearity and serviceability deflection limits.

δ = C_f · W = 1.8500 · W

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents Total applied load W (kN), and the dependent axis y represents Ridge deflection δ (mm). The gradient m directly determines Truss Flexural Compliance C_f, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Position the test truss onto pin and roller support fixtures, verifying span alignment and securing low-friction lateral guide rollers.
  2. Mount digital LVDT sensors beneath the apex ridge joint, mid-span bottom chord, and node joints, zeroing all telemetry channels.
  3. Apply a seating preload of 1.0 kN to engage whiffletree linkages and bedding of metal plate teeth, then release back to zero.
  4. Apply vertical gravity loads in 2.0 kN increments from 2.0 kN up to 12.0 kN using the hydraulic servo-valve controller.
  5. Hold load constant for 3 minutes at each increment to record stabilized vertical deflections and verify absence of joint slippage.
  6. Plot apex ridge deflection δ against total applied load W, computing the structural flexural compliance C_f via linear regression.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Install rigid lateral guide braces to prevent out-of-plane truss buckling while ensuring zero vertical friction.
  • Verify that load application pads are aligned exactly over panel nodes to avoid inducing parasitic local bending moments.
  • Ensure hydraulic loading rate does not exceed 1.0 kN/min to prevent dynamic inertia shock.
  • Verify solid seating of end reaction bearings to prevent rigid-body settlement errors in deflection readings.

The linear compliance model applies strictly within the elastic working range (W ≤ 12.0 kN) prior to wood yielding. Evaluated on a 9.0-meter span, 4:12 pitch timber Fink roof truss fabricated with No. 2 Southern Pine lumber and 20-gauge punched metal connector plates. Symmetrical vertical loads are applied through multi-point hydraulic whiffletree loading linkages.
