Aerodynamic and physical foundations of Wind Tunnel Calibration.

In experimental aerodynamics, wind tunnel calibration forms the indispensable prerequisite for all quantitative aerodynamic investigations. A subsonic wind tunnel accelerates air through a high-contraction nozzle into an optical test section where flow uniformity and turbulence intensity must be verified. According to Bernoulli's principle for steady incompressible flow, the total stagnation pressure P_0 measured at the nose of a Pitot-static probe equals the sum of static pressure P_inf and dynamic pressure q = 0.5 * rho * V_inf^2. By traversing a calibrated Pitot-static tube across the test section and recording the differential pressure Delta P = P_0 - P_inf with an inclined multi-tube manometer or digital differential pressure transducer, the true dynamic pressure is acquired. Plotting dynamic pressure against the square of the free-stream velocity V_inf^2 yields a strictly linear relationship whose gradient directly reveals the local ambient air density rho. Correcting for ambient atmospheric pressure and temperature via the ideal gas equation of state validates test section flow quality, wall boundary layer displacement, and fan motor RPM frequency calibration before mounting aerodynamic lifting models.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

How does a Pitot-static tube distinguish between total pressure and static pressure?

The forward-facing central orifice brings the oncoming streamline to rest isentropically, registering stagnation total pressure P_0. The circumferential side taps are parallel to streamlines, measuring undisturbed static pressure P_inf. The difference P_0 - P_inf equals dynamic pressure q.

Why is the relationship between dynamic pressure and velocity squared strictly linear in subsonic flow?

Under steady, inviscid, incompressible flow assumptions, Euler's momentum equation integrates directly along a streamline to Bernoulli's equation: P_0 = P + 0.5 * rho * V^2. Dynamic pressure is explicitly defined as 0.5 * rho * V^2, which scales linearly with V^2.

What is the purpose of the wind tunnel contraction cone?

The contraction cone accelerates airflow from the settling chamber into the test section while dramatically suppressing spatial velocity non-uniformities and reducing longitudinal turbulence intensity by the contraction ratio squared.

How does ambient temperature influence the slope of the calibration curve?

By the ideal gas law rho = P / (R * T), higher ambient temperatures decrease air density, decreasing the slope m = 0.5 * rho of the dynamic pressure versus velocity squared line.

What threshold Mach number marks the transition where compressibility corrections become mandatory?

At Mach numbers M > 0.3 (approximately 100 m/s in air), density variations exceed 5%, requiring the compressible Rayleigh or isentropic supersonic Pitot formula.

How is flow quality in a wind tunnel test section quantified?

Flow quality is quantified through dynamic pressure spatial uniformity across the test core, pitch and yaw flow angularity (typically < 0.2 deg), and turbulence intensity Tu = u'_rms / U_inf (typically < 0.1%).

Experimental precautions and wind tunnel safety

  • Ensure the Pitot-static probe is aligned within +/- 1 degree of the flow direction to avoid yaw-induced static tap pressure errors.
  • Allow at least 45 seconds of settling time after each fan speed adjustment for acoustic reverberations and vortex shedding to decay.
  • Verify that all flexible vinyl tubing connections to the differential manometer are free of pinch kinks or moisture condensation.
  • Keep the wind tunnel intake settling screens and honeycomb flow straighteners clean and free of airborne particulate accumulation.

Theoretical assumptions & flow limits

  • Incompressible Bernoulli formulation assumes Mach number remains below 0.3; at 60 m/s (M = 0.176), compressibility effects contribute < 0.8% error.
  • Solid wall boundary layer displacement thickness slightly constricts effective core flow area as airspeed increases.
  • Viscous effects on Pitot tube nose are neglected at high Reynolds numbers (Re_d > 1000).
  • Assumes isothermal air expansion through the nozzle contraction without significant frictional heating.
