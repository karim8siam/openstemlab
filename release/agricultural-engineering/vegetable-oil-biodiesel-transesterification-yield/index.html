Agricultural assumptions and boundary condition limits

The linear yield relation applies in the sub-saturation molar ratio range (3:1 to 8:1); beyond 9:1, excess alcohol hinders glycerol separation. Evaluated on jatropha seed oil (free fatty acids < 1.0%) using 1.0% wt/wt KOH catalyst at constant reaction temperature of 60.0 °C and 60 minutes agitation time. FAME conversion yield is measured after gravitational glycerol settling and water washing.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Measure the ester conversion yield and kinematic viscosity reduction of non-edible agricultural seed oil during alkaline transesterification across varying methanol-to-oil molar ratios. Standardized parameter settings: 3.0, 4.0, 5.0, 6.0, 7.0, 8.0 mol/mol.

01

Feedstock Dehydration and Catalyst Preparation

Heat 500 g of jatropha oil to 105 °C to drive off moisture; dissolve 5.0 g of anhydrous KOH into designated methanol volumes.

02

Molar Ratio Batch Charging

Prepare six distinct batches corresponding to methanol-to-oil molar ratios of 3:1, 4:1, 5:1, 6:1, 7:1, and 8:1.

03

Controlled Transesterification Reaction

Charge the pre-heated oil (60 °C) with potassium methoxide solution, seal the reflux condenser, and agitate at 600 RPM for 60 minutes.

04

Phase Separation and Glycerol Draining

Transfer the reaction mixture to a separatory funnel and allow gravity separation for 120 minutes; drain the dense lower glycerol layer.

05

Water Washing and Fuel Drying

Wash the upper ester phase with warm acidified distilled water (50 °C) to remove residual catalyst and soap, followed by drying at 110 °C.

06

Yield Calculation and Regression Analysis

Weigh purified FAME to calculate conversion yield Y = (m_FAME / m_oil) × 100% and plot Y against molar ratio R_m to determine b_m.
