FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Measure frictional head losses (h_f) in smooth and rough pipes over a wide range of flow velocities, compute the Darcy-Weisbach friction factor f, and validate the Moody diagram and Colebrook-White equation. Standardized parameter settings: 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0 m/s.

01

Piezometer Purging & Air Bleeding

Start recirculation pump at low flow; open bleed valves on differential pressure lines to purge all trapped air bubbles from manometer tubes.

02

Zero Differential Head Verification

Close test pipe flow control valve; verify that differential manometer columns read identical elevations (zero differential head).

03

Initial Velocity Flow Setting

Open flow valve to set initial velocity v = 0.5 m/s based on pipe cross-sectional area A = πD²/4; allow flow to stabilize for 2 minutes.

04

Frictional Head Loss Acquisition

Record differential manometer deflection Δh (head loss h_f in meters of water); record water temperature and volumetric flow rate Q.

05

Systematic Velocity Escalation

Increase flow velocity across six systematic intervals (0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, and 3.0 m/s); log differential pressure for each trial.

06

Friction Factor & Moody Diagram Comparison

Plot h_f versus v²; determine linear slope k = fL/(2gD); calculate Darcy friction factor f; compute Re = vD/ν and compare against Moody chart.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

In civil municipal water distribution networks, cross-country transmission mains, wastewater force mains, and industrial cooling loops, pressurized fluid flow experiences continuous energy dissipation due to viscous boundary layer shear and turbulent momentum exchange. Formulated by Henry Darcy and Julius Weisbach in the mid-19th century, the Darcy-Weisbach equation provides the fundamental theoretical basis for pipe friction head loss: h_f = f · (L / D) · (v² / 2g), where h_f is head loss (m of fluid), f is the dimensionless Darcy friction factor, L is pipe length (m), D is internal pipe diameter (m), v is mean flow velocity (m/s), and g is gravitational acceleration (9.81 m/s²). Unlike empirical formulas such as Hazen-Williams, the Darcy-Weisbach equation is dimensionally rigorous and universally applicable to any Newtonian fluid. In laminar flow (Reynolds number Re = ρvD/μ < 2000), friction factor depends strictly on viscous dissipation: f = 64 / Re (Hagen-Poiseuille law). In turbulent flow (Re > 4000), f depends on both Reynolds number and relative wall roughness (ε/D), as characterized by the Colebrook-White implicit equation and Moody diagram. In this laboratory pipe apparatus, differential pressure piezometers record frictional head loss h_f across a 5.0 m test length of smooth copper tubing (D = 24.0 mm) across velocities from 0.5 to 3.0 m/s to calibrate the quadratic resistance coefficient fL/(2gD) = 0.4200 s²/m.
