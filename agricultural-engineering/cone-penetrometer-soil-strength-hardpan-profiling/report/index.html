1. Title and aim

Cone Penetrometer Soil Strength & Hardpan Identification. Measure vertical cone penetration resistance force across soil depth to identify tillage pans, compaction layers, and calculate the ASABE Cone Index (CI).

3. Apparatus and fixed settings

  • Motorized Hydraulic Penetrometer Rig: Drives the penetrometer shaft vertically into soil at standard ASABE velocity of 30 mm/s (±5 mm/s).
  • ASABE Standard 30° Stainless Steel Cone: Provides standardized soil cutting geometry with base diameter 20.27 mm (base area 323 mm²).
  • Tension-Compression S-Beam Load Cell: Measures vertical penetration resistance force with 1.0 N resolution up to 2000 N capacity.

  • Soil classification: Silt loam (A-horizon)

  • Cone size: ASABE Large (323 mm² base area, 30° apex)

4. Theory and graph choice

In terramechanics and agricultural soil management, soil mechanical strength dictates root penetration resistance, crop seedling emergence, field trafficability, and the draft energy required for tillage implements. Heavy agricultural machinery traffic (tractors, grain carts, combine harvesters) induces subsoil compaction, creating dense 'plow pans' or 'traffic hardpans' that restrict root elongation and impede water infiltration. Under ASABE Standard S313 and EP542, the standard instrument for quantifying in-situ soil compaction is the soil cone penetrometer, which consists of a 30° circular stainless steel cone driven vertically into the soil at a uniform penetration rate of 30 mm/s (1.8 m/min). The penetrometer measures the axial insertion force F_p, which is normalized by the base area of the cone (typically 323 mm² for a large cone or 130 mm² for a small cone) to yield the ASABE Cone Index (CI = F_p / A_c, in kPa or MPa). In homogeneous topsoil profiles, insertion force increases linearly with depth with a characteristic penetration resistance gradient k_p governed by soil texture, bulk density, and soil matric water potential. In this experiment, an automated motor-driven hydraulic penetrometer rig equipped with a precision load cell and linear displacement transducer profiles an agricultural silt loam field to map penetration resistance and pinpoint subsurface compaction horizons.

F_p = k_p · z = 18.5000 · z

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents Insertion depth z (cm), and the dependent axis y represents Penetration force F_p (N). The gradient m directly determines Penetration Resistance Gradient k_p, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Position the mobile penetrometer frame over the surveyed test grid and anchor hydraulic outriggers to eliminate frame deflection.
  2. Inspect the 30° cone for surface wear, align the shaft vertically with a spirit level, and zero the load cell in free air above the soil.
  3. Set the electric-hydraulic servo actuator to maintain a constant vertical advance velocity of 30 mm/s.
  4. Initiate automated insertion down to 30 cm depth while logging instantaneous force F_p and depth z continuously.
  5. Extract representative penetration force readings at standard 5 cm depth increments (5.0, 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 25.0, 30.0 cm).
  6. Calculate Cone Index CI = F_p / A_c and plot F_p against depth z, performing linear regression to evaluate the penetration gradient k_p.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Maintain strictly constant penetration velocity (30 mm/s) to prevent strain-rate measurement artifacts.
  • Verify vertical shaft alignment to prevent bending moments that cause parasitic friction in the guide bushings.
  • Inspect the cone tip periodically; replace any cone showing visible rounding, nicking, or dimension loss.
  • Collect concurrent soil moisture readings within 10 cm of the insertion hole to contextualize resistance data.
