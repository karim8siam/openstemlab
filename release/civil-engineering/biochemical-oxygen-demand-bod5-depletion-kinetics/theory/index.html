Civil engineering mechanics & structural foundations of BOD5 Kinetics.

In civil environmental sanitary engineering, municipal sewage treatment design, and receiving water pollution impact assessments, Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD) is the primary bio-chemical parameter quantifying biodegradable organic carbon pollutant loading. Aerobic heterotrophic microorganisms consume dissolved molecular oxygen (DO) to oxidize organic matter into carbon dioxide, water, and new microbial biomass: Organics + O₂ + Nutrients → CO₂ + H₂O + NH₃ + New Cells. Standardized under Standard Methods 5210 B, the classic BOD5 test incubates wastewater samples in airtight 300 mL glass BOD bottles in total darkness at 20.0 ± 1.0 °C for exactly 5 days. Dissolved oxygen is measured initially (DO_0) and after 5 days (DO_5) using the iodometric Winkler method or luminescent dissolved oxygen (LDO) probes. BOD5 is computed as: BOD5 = (DO_0 - DO_5) / P, where P is the decimal volumetric dilution fraction (V_sample / 300 mL). Deoxygenation kinetics follow a first-order rate law: L_t = L_0 · (1 - e^(-k·t)), where L_0 is ultimate carbonaceous oxygen demand, k is the base-e deoxygenation rate constant (typically 0.15 to 0.30 day⁻¹ for municipal sewage at 20 °C), and t is incubation time in days. In this laboratory investigation, domestic wastewater aliquots across dilution metrics from 1.0 to 6.0 mL per 300 mL are analyzed to determine the 5-day oxygen consumption rate, evaluate the deoxygenation rate constant, and calculate raw municipal wastewater BOD5.

BOD = 32.5000 · P

First-Order Deoxygenation Rate Kinetics

Biochemical oxidation of organic matter follows first-order reaction kinetics with respect to remaining unoxidized organic concentration $L_t$. The rate of deoxygenation is:

\[ \frac{dL_t}{dt} = -k L_t \]

Integrating with initial condition $L_t = L_0$ at $t = 0$ (where $L_0$ is ultimate carbonaceous BOD):

\[ L_t = L_0 e^{-k t} \]

The oxygen consumed (BOD exerted) up to time $t$ is $y(t) = L_0 - L_t$:

\[ y(t) = L_0 \left( 1 - e^{-k t} \right) \]

For the standard 5-day incubation period ($t = 5\text{ days}$):

\[ \text{BOD}_5 = L_0 \left( 1 - e^{-5k} \right) \]

For typical domestic sewage with $k = 0.23\text{ day}^{-1}$ at $20^\circ\text{C}$, the 5-day BOD represents approximately $1 - e^{-5(0.23)} = 68.3\%$ of ultimate carbonaceous oxygen demand.

Temperature Correction for Reaction Rate Constant

The deoxygenation rate constant $k$ varies with ambient temperature according to the van 't Hoff-Arrhenius relationship:

\[ k_T = k_{20} \theta^{T - 20} \]

where empirical thermal coefficient $\theta = 1.135$ for $4^\circ\text{C} < T < 20^\circ\text{C}$ and $\theta = 1.056$ for $20^\circ\text{C} < T < 30^\circ\text{C}$.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

Why is the standard BOD incubation carried out at exactly 20 °C in total darkness for 5 days?

20 °C represents average temperate river water temperature; darkness is mandatory to prevent photosynthetic algae from producing oxygen and masking true microbial respiration; 5 days was historically chosen in the UK because river water reaches the ocean from headwaters within 5 days.

What is the difference between carbonaceous BOD (CBOD) and nitrogenous BOD (NBOD)?

Carbonaceous BOD represents oxidation of organic carbon to CO2 by heterotrophic bacteria (active from day 0); nitrogenous BOD represents oxidation of ammonia to nitrite and nitrate by autotrophic bacteria (Nitrosomonas, Nitrobacter), which typically begins after 5 to 8 days unless chemically inhibited.

Why is a nitrification inhibitor (TCMP) added to wastewater samples in the BOD test?

TCMP suppresses nitrifying bacteria, ensuring that the test measures strictly carbonaceous oxygen demand (CBOD5), avoiding false high readings from ammonia oxidation.

What are the standard EPA validity criteria for a 5-day BOD test dilution?

For a dilution to be mathematically valid, oxygen depletion (DO_0 - DO_5) must be at least 2.0 mg/L, and final residual dissolved oxygen DO_5 must be at least 1.0 mg/L.

How does chemical oxygen demand (COD) relate theoretically to biochemical oxygen demand (BOD)?

COD uses boiling potassium dichromate in acid to chemically oxidize virtually all organic matter (both biodegradable and recalcitrant); therefore, COD is always greater than or equal to ultimate BOD (typical domestic sewage ratio: COD/BOD5 ≈ 1.8 to 2.2).

How does the Streeter-Phelps oxygen sag equation utilize BOD kinetics?

The Streeter-Phelps model balances wastewater deoxygenation rate (kd · L) against atmospheric surface reaeration rate (ka · D) along a river, predicting the critical dissolved oxygen deficit point downstream of a wastewater discharge.

Experimental precautions and laboratory safety

  • Siphon dilution water down the inside neck of BOD bottles without splashing to avoid introducing micro-air bubbles.
  • Maintain water seals in the flared rims of BOD bottles throughout the 5-day incubation period to prevent air leakage.
  • Ensure sample bottles contain no toxic heavy metals or chlorine residuals that would kill the microbial seed population.
  • Verify that initial dissolved oxygen does not exceed 9.2 mg/L at 20 °C (supersaturation causes oxygen degassing and invalid tests).

Theoretical assumptions & boundary condition limits

  • The civil engineering physical models apply within the specified calibrated operational window (1.0 to 6.0 mL/300mL).
  • Assumes homogeneous, isotropic continuum material behavior without unmodeled micro-fracture anomalies.
  • Continuous sensor readings reflect ordinary least-squares regression across successive observations.
  • Extreme inelastic yielding or cyclic degradation requires non-linear constitutive continuum damage mechanics.
