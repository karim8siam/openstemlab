Biomedical and physiological foundations of Cartilage Aggregate Modulus.

Articular cartilage is a specialized avascular, aneural connective tissue that lines the articulating surfaces of diarthrodial joints (e.g., knee and hip), providing near-frictionless gliding and load transmission across millions of gait cycles. Mechanically, cartilage is modeled using the biphasic theory formulated by Van C. Mow, which treats the tissue as a mixture of an incompressible porous-permeable solid matrix (composed of a type-II collagen fibrillar network entrapping negatively charged aggregating proteoglycans/aggrecan) and an interstitial fluid phase (water with mobile physiological electrolytes, comprising 65–80% of wet weight). Under confined compression within an impermeable chamber loaded via a rigid porous filter platen, fluid exudes axially until interstitial fluid pressure dissipates completely. At creep equilibrium (as t → ∞), fluid flow ceases, the solid matrix alone supports the total applied compressive stress σ_c, and the tissue strain ε_c reaches a steady plateau governed by the aggregate modulus: σ_c = H_A · ε_c. The aggregate modulus H_A represents the intrinsic stiffness of the cartilage solid matrix in uniaxial confined compression, combining Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio: H_A = E·(1 - ν)/[(1 + ν)·(1 - 2ν)]. In this experiment, cylindrical osteochondral cartilage plugs are tested in a precision confined compression chamber to evaluate equilibrium stress-strain behavior and quantify H_A.

σ_c = H_A · ε_c = 0.6500 · ε_c

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What is the physical interpretation of the aggregate modulus H_A?

The aggregate modulus H_A is the apparent equilibrium compressive stiffness of the porous-permeable solid matrix under zero lateral strain confinement; it quantifies the combined resistance of the collagen network and fixed proteoglycan charge density to compression after fluid flow ceases.

How does interstitial fluid pressurization protect articular cartilage during joint loading?

During rapid physiological loading (e.g., heel strike during running), interstitial water cannot escape immediately due to low matrix permeability; the pressurization of the fluid phase carries over 90% of the joint contact stress, shielding the solid collagen-proteoglycan matrix from destructive shear stresses.

Why does cartilage require 30 to 60 minutes to reach creep equilibrium in confined compression?

Cartilage has extremely low hydraulic permeability (k ≈ 10⁻¹⁵ m⁴/(N·s)). The high frictional drag between flowing interstitial water and the microscopic pore spaces of the dense solid matrix severely slows fluid exudation, creating prolonged viscoelastic creep.

What causes Donnan osmotic swelling pressure in articular cartilage?

Sulfated glycosaminoglycan (GAG) chains on aggrecan molecules possess dense negative carboxyl and sulfate charges (fixed charge density, FCD). To maintain electroneutrality, mobile sodium ions are drawn into the tissue, creating an osmotic pressure that swells and pretensions the collagen network.

How does osteoarthritis alter cartilage mechanical properties?

In early osteoarthritis, superficial collagen fibrils disrupt and proteoglycan content is lost, drastically reducing the aggregate modulus H_A and increasing hydraulic permeability k, impairing fluid pressurization and causing rapid wear.

Why is confined compression used instead of unconfined compression to measure H_A?

Confined compression enforces one-dimensional axial displacement with zero lateral strain (ε_x = ε_y = 0), matching the mathematical boundary conditions of Mow's 1D biphasic equations and allowing direct decoupling of solid matrix aggregate modulus from fluid permeability.

Experimental precautions and patient safety

  • Ensure perfect cylindrical fit of the specimen in the confining well to eliminate lateral fluid leakage around the sample perimeter.
  • Maintain constant 0.15 M NaCl ionic strength; hypoosmotic solutions cause proteoglycan swelling that alters baseline thickness.
  • Wait until creep rate satisfies the rigorous equilibrium criterion (dε/dt < 10⁻⁶ s⁻¹) before recording equilibrium stress.
  • Keep testing temperature stable at 22 °C to avoid thermal expansion drift of the displacement transducer frame.

Theoretical assumptions & biological limits

  • The linear aggregate modulus model applies to infinitesimal to moderate strains (ε_c ≤ 0.30); higher compressions exhibit strain-dependent stiffening.
  • Assumes homogeneous solid matrix properties through depth, whereas cartilage exhibits depth-dependent zoning (superficial, middle, deep).
  • Applies to equilibrium conditions after fluid flow ceases; dynamic viscoelastic response requires full biphasic poroelastic modeling.
  • Testing is performed in simple saline without synovial fluid lubricin or hyaluronic acid boundary lubricants.
