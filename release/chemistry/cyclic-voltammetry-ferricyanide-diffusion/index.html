Physical chemistry assumptions and metrological limits

['The simple Randles-Ševčík equation assumes planar semi-infinite linear diffusion; at extremely slow scan rates (< 1 mV/s), spherical edge diffusion at microelectrodes causes positive deviations.', 'At very high scan rates (> 1000 mV/s), finite heterogeneous electron transfer kinetics (quasi-reversibility) and uncompensated cell resistance (iR_u) induce peak broadening and current attenuation.', 'Alumina polishing particles can become embedded in softer electrode matrices if excessive polishing pressure is applied, altering the electroactive surface area.'] The Randles-Ševčík equation for a reversible soluble redox couple at 25.00 °C (298.15 K) is i_pc = (2.686 × 10⁵) · n³/² · A · c · D¹/² · ν¹/², where n = 1, A = 0.07069 cm² (3.00 mm diameter glassy carbon disk), and c = 5.000 × 10⁻⁶ mol·cm⁻³ (5.000 mM K₃[Fe(CN)₆] in 1.000 M KNO₃). Across scan rates from 10.0 mV/s to 200.0 mV/s, peak cathodic currents increase from 26.18 μA to 117.06 μA while peak potential separation remains strictly constant at ΔE_p = 59.2 ± 0.5 mV. Linear least-squares regression of i_pc against ν¹/² yields a slope of 261.74 μA·(V·s⁻¹)⁻¹/² with R² = 0.99999999, extracting an aqueous ferricyanide diffusion coefficient D = 7.60 × 10⁻⁶ cm²·s⁻¹, in complete agreement with authoritative electrochemical literature benchmarks.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the aqueous diffusion coefficient of the ferricyanide anion [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻ using cyclic voltammetry at a glassy carbon electrode across scan rates from 10.0 mV/s to 200.0 mV/s via the Randles-Ševčík equation. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 10.0, 25.0, 50.0, 100.0, 150.0, 200.0 mV·s⁻¹.

01

Working Electrode Polishing & Microscopic Inspection

Polish the glassy carbon electrode on microcloth pads using aqueous alumina slurries (1.0 μm, 0.3 μm, and 0.05 μm successively) in figure-eight patterns. Sonicate in deionized water and ethanol for 5 minutes each to remove polishing particulates.

02

Electrolyte Formulation & Supporting Salt Addition

Prepare 50.0 mL of standardized 5.000 mM K₃[Fe(CN)₆] in 1.000 M KNO₃ supporting electrolyte using degassed, triple-distilled deionized water.

03

Cell Assembly & Argon Deoxygenation

Assemble the three-electrode cell in the 25.00 °C jacketed vessel. Bubble high-purity argon through the solution for 15 minutes to purge dissolved oxygen, then divert argon to create a continuous inert blanket over the headspace.

04

Potential Window Selection & Baseline Verification

Configure the potentiostat between initial potential E_i = +0.600 V and switching potential E_s = -0.100 V vs SCE. Execute a blank run in 1.0 M KNO₃ to verify that residual background currents remain below 0.1 μA.

05

Multi-Scan Rate Cyclic Voltammetric Acquisition

Record cyclic voltammograms across scan rates ν = 10.0, 25.0, 50.0, 100.0, 150.0, and 200.0 mV/s. Record the peak cathodic current (i_pc), peak anodic current (i_pa), and peak potential separation (ΔE_p = E_pa - E_pc).

06

Randles-Ševčík Diagnostic Regression & Diffusion Extraction

Verify reversibility criteria (ΔE_p ≈ 59 mV, i_pa/i_pc ≈ 1.0). Plot peak cathodic current i_pc against ν¹/² (in (V/s)¹/²). Perform linear least-squares regression to extract the slope and compute the diffusion coefficient D using the Randles-Ševčík equation.
