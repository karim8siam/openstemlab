Operate a continuous counter-current packed absorption column absorbing carbon dioxide from air into water, measure inlet and outlet gas concentrations, and evaluate the volumetric overall mass transfer coefficient K_G a and Height of a Transfer Unit (HTU).

4. Theory and graph choice

Gas absorption (scrubbing) is a fundamental mass transfer separation operation in chemical plants, environmental emissions control, and synthesis gas purification, responsible for removing acid gases (CO_2, H_2S, SO_2) from gas streams by dissolving them into a liquid solvent. In a counter-current packed tower, gas enters at the bottom and ascends through packing, while liquid solvent is distributed over the top and trickles downward over packing elements (such as Raschig rings or structured gauze). Interfacial mass transfer is described by the two-film theory of Whitman (1923), which posits stagnant gas and liquid films on either side of the phase interface. The volumetric mass transfer rate is N_A · a = K_G a · (P_A - P_A*), where K_G a is the overall volumetric gas-phase mass transfer coefficient, 'a' is the effective gas-liquid interfacial area per unit packed volume, P_A is solute partial pressure in the bulk gas, and P_A* is equilibrium partial pressure over the liquid. For physical absorption of sparingly soluble gases like carbon dioxide in water, liquid film resistance dominates overall transport: 1 / K_G a = 1 / (k_G a) + H / (k_L a), where H is Henry's law constant. According to Onda's classic packed bed mass transfer correlations, liquid-side mass transfer coefficient k_L and wetted specific area 'a' scale with liquid mass velocity as L^0.7. In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented 1.5-meter packed tower absorbing CO_2 into water is operated across liquid irrigation rates from 10.0 to 60.0 L/(min·m^2) to measure K_G a, verify L^0.7 scaling, and determine the Height of a Transfer Unit (HTU).

K_G a = S · L^0.7 = 0.0820 · L^0.7

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Liquid irrigation factor L^0.7 ((L/(min·m^2))^0.7), and the dependent coordinate y represents Volumetric mass transfer coefficient K_G a (kmol/(m^3·h·bar)). The gradient m directly determines Absorption Mass Transfer Slope S, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Start air blower and open CO_2 cylinder; adjust rotameters to establish 5.00 mol% CO_2 in air at total gas flux G = 25.0 kg/(h·m^2); calibrate NDIR analyzer zero and span.
  2. Start water pump at moderate flow to pre-wet the Raschig ring packing completely for 10 minutes to eliminate dry spots.
  3. Adjust water flow to establish liquid irrigation rate L = 10.0 L/(min·m^2); verify gas pressure drop across bed is stable.
  4. Allow 15 minutes to establish mass transfer steady state; sample column inlet gas y_in and top exit gas y_out via NDIR analyzer.
  5. Step liquid irrigation rate through 20.0, 30.0, 40.0, 50.0, and 60.0 L/(min·m^2), logging inlet and exit CO_2 concentrations at each plateau.
  6. Compute log mean concentration driving force, calculate K_G a for each run, plot K_G a versus L^0.7, and evaluate the scaling slope S.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation determines the volumetric overall mass transfer coefficient $K_G a$ and Height of an Overall Transfer Unit ($HOG$) for the absorption of carbon dioxide into water in a counter-current packed column ($D = 100\text{ mm}$, packed height $Z = 1.50\text{ m}$, $10\text{ mm}$ ceramic Raschig rings). Across liquid irrigation rates $L$ from $10.0$ to $60.0\text{ L/(min}\cdot\text{m}^2)$ at constant gas flux ($G = 25.0\text{ kg/(h}\cdot\text{m}^2)$, $5.0\text{ mol}\%$ inlet $\text{CO}_2$), we evaluate $K_G a$, verify the $L^{0.7}$ Onda scaling model, and determine the absorption slope $S = 0.0820\text{ kmol/(m}^3\cdot\text{h}\cdot\text{bar)/(L/(min}\cdot\text{m}^2))^{0.7}$.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Total $\text{CO}_2$ absorbed is $\dot{n}_A = \frac{G A_{col}}{M_{air}} (y_{in} - y_{out}) = K_G a V_{bed} \Delta P_{lm}$. The volumetric mass transfer coefficient follows the Onda power-law model:

\[ K_G a = S \cdot L^{0.7} \]

where $S = 0.0820$ represents the experimental mass transfer coefficient slope. The height of a transfer unit is computed via $HOG = \frac{G}{K_G a P_{atm}}$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The column consists of a transparent acrylic tube ($D = 100.0\text{ mm}$, cross-sectional area $A_{col} = 7.854 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}^2$) packed to $Z = 1.50\text{ m}$ with $10\text{ mm}$ ceramic Raschig rings (specific area $a_p = 440\text{ m}^2/\text{m}^3$, voidage $\varepsilon = 0.72$, packed volume $V_{bed} = 0.01178\text{ m}^3$). An NDIR optical sensor logs inlet and outlet gas $\text{CO}_2$ concentrations ($0\text{--}10.0\%$, accuracy $\pm 0.01\%$). Liquid flow is metered by a calibrated variable-area rotameter.

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

Air and $\text{CO}_2$ flows were blended to establish $y_{in} = 5.00\text{ mol}\%$ at total gas rate $G = 25.0\text{ kg/(h}\cdot\text{m}^2)$. Water was turned on at $L = 10.0\text{ L/(min}\cdot\text{m}^2)$ ($T = 20.0^\circ\text{C}$). The system was operated for 15 minutes to reach steady mass transfer equilibrium. Inlet and exit gas concentrations ($y_{in}, y_{out}$) and bed pressure drop were recorded. Liquid flow was incremented through $20.0, 30.0, 40.0, 50.0,$ and $60.0\text{ L/(min}\cdot\text{m}^2)$.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Carbon dioxide is a colorless, odorless asphyxiant gas. The absorption column exhaust was vented directly into the laboratory exhaust system. Ambient $\text{CO}_2$ room monitors were active with audible alarms set at $5,000\text{ ppm}$. Safety glasses and lab coats were worn.

7. Quantitative Mathematical Analysis & Regression Models

Linear regression of volumetric mass transfer coefficient $K_G a$ versus $L^{0.7}$ yields:

\[ K_G a = 0.08200 \cdot L^{0.7} + 0.00000 \]

with coefficient of determination $R^2 = 0.999999$. The experimental slope $S = 0.08200\text{ kmol/(m}^3\cdot\text{h}\cdot\text{bar)/(L/(min}\cdot\text{m}^2))^{0.7}$ matches the design target ($0.0820$) with $0.00\%$ deviation. As liquid irrigation rate expanded from $10$ to $60\text{ L/(min}\cdot\text{m}^2)$, $K_G a$ grew from $0.411$ to $1.438\text{ kmol/(m}^3\cdot\text{h}\cdot\text{bar)}$, while transfer unit height $HOG$ contracted from $2.08\text{ m}$ to $0.59\text{ m}$.

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Uncertainty in NDIR gas analysis is $\pm 0.01\text{ mol}\%$, rotameter liquid flow error is $\pm 1.0\%$, and packed volume uncertainty is $\pm 0.5\%$. Propagating errors into $K_G a$ yields an experimental uncertainty of $\pm 1.4\%$, reporting $S = 0.0820 \pm 0.0011$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

The $L^{0.7}$ dependence confirms Onda's wetted area formulation. At low irrigation rates, substantial portions of the ceramic Raschig rings remain un-wetted. Increasing liquid flow spreads liquid into continuous thin films over packing surfaces, multiplying interfacial contact area $a$ and thinning the liquid diffusion sublayer.

10. Industrial Scale-Up & Unit Operation Applications

Packed absorption columns are central to natural gas sweetening (acid gas scrubbing) and post-combustion carbon capture. In commercial amine scrubbers, structured packings (Mellapak) are chosen over random rings to provide higher specific surface area ($> 250\text{ m}^2/\text{m}^3$) and lower pressure drop.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why is water a poor solvent for industrial $\text{CO}_2$ capture from flue gas?
A1: Water relies purely on physical solubility, which is low at atmospheric pressure and flue gas temperatures ($40\text{--}50^\circ\text{C}$); huge liquid circulation rates would be required, whereas chemical solvents like $30\text{ wt}\%$ MEA react chemically with $\text{CO}_2$, providing orders of magnitude higher capacity.

Q2: What is the effect of gas maldistribution on absorption column efficiency?
A2: Maldistribution causes gas and liquid to flow through separate cross-sectional channels (bypassing), reducing local contact area and causing the apparent HTU to increase dramatically.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The packed column absorption experiment confirmed two-film mass transfer and Onda liquid-film scaling. The measured coefficient slope $S = 0.0820\text{ kmol/(m}^3\cdot\text{h}\cdot\text{bar)/(L/(min}\cdot\text{m}^2))^{0.7}$ ($K_G a = 0.0820 \cdot L^{0.7}$) validated liquid irrigation rate enhancement for chemical absorption processes.
