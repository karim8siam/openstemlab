Thermodynamic & Kinetic Governing Mechanisms

Phase transitions in dental materials—whether the hydration of calcium sulfate hemihydrate into interpenetrating dihydrate prisms, the free-radical addition cross-linking of dimethacrylate monomers, or the stress-induced martensitic transformations in nickel-titanium endodontic alloys—are governed by fundamental thermodynamic principles. The rate of reaction is dictated by Arrhenius kinetics and chemical affinity, while mechanical integrity depends on crystalline grain size, filler volume fraction, and interfacial silane bonding. By systematically controlling the independent variable Temperature Delta, clinicians can regulate setting times, minimize polymerization shrinkage stress, and optimize clinical working windows.

Experimental precautions and laboratory safety

  • Maintain constant laboratory temperature (23.0 ± 1.0 °C) and humidity (50 ± 5%) to eliminate environmental kinetic drift.
  • Inspect all testing fixtures, molds, and specimen surfaces for microscopic contamination, air voids, or micro-cracks prior to testing.
  • Ensure strict alignment of loading platens, transducers, and sensors along the primary specimen axis to prevent parasitic bending moments.
  • Adhere strictly to dental clinical waste handling protocols and wear appropriate eye protection and personal protective equipment.
