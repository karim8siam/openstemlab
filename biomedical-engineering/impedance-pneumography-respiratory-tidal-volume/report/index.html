1. Title and aim

Transthoracic Electrical Impedance Pneumography & Tidal Volume Scaling. Measure high-frequency (50 kHz) four-electrode transthoracic electrical impedance changes (ΔZ) during breathing cycles and calibrate against spirometric tidal volume.

4. Theory and graph choice

Continuous non-invasive monitoring of respiratory rate and tidal volume is essential in intensive care units, neonatal incubators, sleep medicine polysomnography, and post-anesthesia recovery rooms. Transthoracic electrical impedance pneumography provides an elegant bedside method to record respiration without requiring invasive face masks or mouthpieces. The physiological principle exploits the dramatic electrical conductivity difference between air and thoracic tissues. De-aerated lung tissue and thoracic blood have relatively high electrical conductivity (saline and blood ~0.7 S/m), whereas inhaled atmospheric air has zero electrical conductivity. During inspiration, the lungs inflate with air, increasing the non-conductive alveolar gas volume and lengthening electrical current streamlines across the ribcage, which causes baseline transthoracic electrical impedance to rise. Under a four-electrode (tetrapolar) configuration, a safe, high-frequency constant AC carrier current (typically 50 kHz, 100 μA RMS, far above the neuromuscular excitation threshold) is injected through outer drive electrodes, while inner pickup electrodes detect the resulting surface voltage drop. Across normal physiological tidal volumes V_t (0.5 to 3.0 L), the peak-to-peak respiratory impedance change ΔZ scales in direct linear proportion: ΔZ = k_v · V_t. In this experiment, an instrumented impedance pneumograph is calibrated simultaneously against a digital clinical spirometer.

ΔZ = k_v · V_t = 1.3500 · V_t

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Tidal volume V_t (L), and the dependent coordinate y represents Transthoracic impedance change ΔZ (Ω). The gradient m directly determines Pneumographic Volume Sensitivity k_v, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Clean skin with alcohol wipes and affix four electrodes along the mid-axillary line: outer pair for 50 kHz current injection, inner pair for voltage pickup.
  2. Energize 50 kHz 200 μA oscillator; record baseline thoracic resting end-expiratory impedance (Z_0, typically 25 to 50 Ω).
  3. Seat subject comfortably and fit nose clip; connect subject to calibrated turbine spirometer to log air volume simultaneously.
  4. Instruct subject to inhale to achieve a spirometric tidal volume V_t of exactly 0.50 L; log stabilized peak impedance excursion ΔZ in Ohms.
  5. Guide subject through voluntary breathing increments of 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, and 3.0 L, recording paired (V_t, ΔZ) values across 5 breaths per step.
  6. Plot respiratory impedance amplitude ΔZ against tidal volume V_t, fitting linear regression through the origin to determine sensitivity k_v.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Instruct subject to breathe steadily without torso twisting or posture shifts that alter baseline ribcage geometry.
  • Ensure outer current-drive electrodes are spaced at least 5 cm away from inner voltage-pickup electrodes.
  • Verify that spirometer turbine calibration is zeroed and calibrated with a certified 3.00 L calibration syringe.
  • Verify carrier current remains under IEC 60601-1 patient safety auxiliary current limits (under 500 μA at 50 kHz).
