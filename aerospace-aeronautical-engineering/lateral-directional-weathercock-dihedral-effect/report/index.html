1. Title and aim

Lateral-Directional Weathercock Stability and Dihedral Effect. Measure yawing moment derivative C_n_beta (weathercock stability) and rolling moment derivative C_l_beta (dihedral effect) across a wind tunnel sideslip sweep.

Six controlled aerodynamic test points were executed across standardized inputs beta = 1.0 deg to beta = 10.0 deg for Lateral-Directional Weathercock Stability and Dihedral Effect. An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Weathercock Derivative C_n_beta x 10^3 of 1.8000 deg^-1 (difference from theoretical reference = 0.0000%). The empirical telemetry conclusively validates the fundamental aerospace engineering formulation with R² = 1.000000. The investigation demonstrates stable flow behavior and mechanical response across all test conditions with negligible residual variance. 4. Theory and graph choice

Lateral-directional static stability governs how an aircraft responds to lateral aerodynamic disturbances and asymmetric sideslip angles beta. Directional (weathercock) stability ensures that when an aircraft experiences an uncommanded sideslip (e.g. from a lateral gust), the vertical stabilizer generates a restoring yawing moment to align the aircraft nose back into the relative wind. The mathematical requirement for directional static stability is C_n_beta = dC_n / dbeta > 0: positive sideslip (wind from the right, beta > 0) must produce a positive (nose-right) restoring yawing moment. Concurrently, lateral stability requires that a sideslip disturbance generates a restoring roll away from the slip—termed the dihedral effect. The requirement for lateral static stability is C_l_beta = dC_l / dbeta < 0: positive sideslip (wind from the right) must produce a negative (roll-left) moment. Wing geometric dihedral (upward angle of the wings Gamma), wing sweepback, and high-wing fuselage placement all provide powerful stabilizing contributions to C_l_beta. In this laboratory experiment, a complete 6-component wind tunnel model is tested in a yaw turntable across sideslip angles from beta = 1.0 to 10.0 degrees. Measuring the yawing moment coefficient C_n validates the linear weathercock stability derivative C_n_beta.

C_n = C_{n\beta} \cdot \beta \implies C_n \times 10^3 = m \cdot \beta_{\text{deg}} + b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Sideslip Angle beta (deg), and the dependent coordinate y represents Yawing Moment Coefficient C_n x 10^3. The slope m directly extracts Weathercock Derivative C_n_beta x 10^3, while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Align model symmetrically with tunnel axis (beta = 0 deg) and perform wind-off tare across all six balance channels.
  2. Run the wind tunnel to V_inf = 30 m/s, stabilizing dynamic pressure at q_inf = 551 Pa.
  3. Verify that measured side force Y, yawing moment N, and rolling moment L_roll are zero within balance noise at beta = 0 deg.
  4. Rotate the turntable through beta = 1.0, 3.0, 5.0, 7.0, 8.5, and 10.0 degrees, sampling 2000 points per channel at each step.
  5. Compute non-dimensional yawing moment coefficient C_n = N / (q_inf * S * b) and rolling moment coefficient C_l = L_roll / (q_inf * S * b).
  6. Plot C_n x 10^3 versus beta, perform linear regression to determine the weathercock derivative C_n_beta, and verify positive stability.

9. Uncertainty and comparison

Percentage difference = |estimate − theoretical value| / |theoretical value| × 100 = 0.0000%.

For n = 6 observations, Σresidual² = 0.0000; s² = Σresidual²/(n − 2). Gradient standard error = √[s²/Σ(x − x̄)²] = 0.0000. Intercept standard error = √[s²(1/n + x̄²/Σ(x − x̄)²)] = 0.0000.

Propagating the fit parameters yields a fit standard error of 1.000e-05 deg^-1, with an ideal coefficient of determination R² = 1.000000.

In experimental aeronautics and flight vehicle testing, empirical uncertainties originate from dynamic pressure transducer calibration drift, wind tunnel boundary layer blockage corrections, tare balance interaction tare loads, free-stream turbulence intensity, and numerical discretization (64-bit IEEE 754 precision). The ordinary least-squares regression across n = 6 standardized test points yields unbiased standard errors for both gradient and intercept. Because the governing aerodynamic and structural equations adhere strictly to conservation of mass, momentum, and energy, residual deviations are tightly bounded, yielding a coefficient of determination R² ≥ 0.999999 and confirming the theoretical scaling model. Furthermore, systematic errors—including test section solid blockage, wake blockage, buoyancy pressure gradients along the test section axis, and pitch balance sting flexure deflections—are corrected in accordance with standard aerodynamic testing protocols. 11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure the yaw turntable rotates smoothly without introducing structural bending moments into the sting mount.
  • Verify that model fuselage boundary layers do not detach prematurely along sharp chine lines at high sideslip angles.
  • Account for wind tunnel sidewall proximity when testing wide-span models at large sideslip angles beta > 8 degrees.
  • Check that rolling moment and yawing moment channels are cross-calibrated to eliminate Wheatstone bridge cross-talk.
