4. Theory and graph choice

In industrial bioprocess engineering, aerobic fermentation (e.g., antibiotic synthesis, monoclonal antibody production, yeast cultivation, and wastewater bio-oxidation) requires continuous delivery of dissolved oxygen to metabolizing microbial cells. Because oxygen is sparingly soluble in aqueous nutrient broths (saturation concentration C* is only ~8.0 mg/L at 25 °C), the rate of biological oxygen consumption by dense cultures can deplete dissolved oxygen in seconds unless oxygen is continuously transferred across the gas-liquid interface. The volumetric rate of oxygen transfer (OTR) is governed by two-film mass transfer theory: OTR = k_L a · (C* - C_L), where k_L is the liquid-side film mass transfer coefficient, 'a' is the specific gas-liquid interfacial area per unit liquid volume, C* is saturation dissolved oxygen concentration, and C_L is bulk dissolved oxygen concentration. The lumped parameter k_L a (units h^-1) represents the aeration capacity of the bioreactor. In the dynamic gassing-out method, liquid is first deoxygenated by sparging nitrogen gas until C_L = 0; nitrogen is then shut off, air sparging is initiated, and the transient reoxygenation curve C_L(t) is recorded using a fast-response polarographic dissolved oxygen (DO) probe: dC_L / dt = k_L a · (C* - C_L) -> ln[(C* - C_L(t)) / C*] = -k_L a · t. According to van't Riet's classical correlations for aerated stirred tanks, volumetric mass transfer coefficient k_L a scales with mechanical agitation speed as N^1.2 and superficial gas velocity as v_s^0.5: k_L a ∝ (P_g / V)^0.4 · v_s^0.5 ∝ N^1.2. In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented 3.0-liter glass stirred-tank bioreactor is operated across impeller speeds from 100 to 600 RPM at constant air sparging rate to measure reoxygenation curves, verify N^1.2 scaling, and determine the aeration scaling slope S = 0.0240 h^-1/RPM^1.2.

k_L a = S · N^1.2 = 0.0240 · N^1.2

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Agitation speed factor N^1.2 (RPM^1.2), and the dependent coordinate y represents Volumetric oxygen mass transfer coefficient k_L a (h^-1). The gradient m directly determines Bioreactor Aeration Scaling Slope S, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Calibrate the DO probe at 0% saturation using zero-oxygen sodium sulfite solution (0.1 M Na2SO3 with CoCl2 catalyst) and 100% saturation in air-saturated water at 25.0 °C.
  2. Fill reactor with 2.0 L of deionized water; set water jacket to 25.0 °C and turn on Rushton impeller at target test speed.
  3. Sparge nitrogen gas at 2.0 L/min through the ring sparger until dissolved oxygen C_L drops below 1.0% saturation.
  4. Instantly switch gas supply from nitrogen to compressed air at 2.0 L/min (1.0 vvm, t = 0); log transient DO percentage C_L(t) at 5 Hz.
  5. Plot ln[(C* - C_L) / C*] versus time t over the 20% to 80% DO window to extract volumetric mass transfer coefficient k_L a.
  6. Repeat dynamic reoxygenation procedure at impeller speeds N = 100, 200, 300, 400, 500, and 600 RPM; plot k_L a versus N^1.2 to determine slope S.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation determines the volumetric oxygen mass transfer coefficient $k_L a$ in a $3.0\text{ L}$ stirred-tank bioreactor ($V_L = 2.0\text{ L}$, Rushton turbine $D_i = 45\text{ mm}$) using the dynamic gassing-out method with a fast polarographic dissolved oxygen probe. Across impeller agitation speeds $N$ from $100$ to $600\text{ RPM}$ at constant air sparging rate ($2.0\text{ L/min}$, $1.0\text{ vvm}$) and $25.00^\circ\text{C}$, we record reoxygenation curves, verify the van't Riet scaling model $k_L a = S \cdot N^{1.2}$, and evaluate the aeration scaling slope $S = 0.0240\text{ h}^{-1}\text{/RPM}^{1.2}$.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Dynamic reoxygenation obeys $\ln\left( \frac{C^* - C_L(t)}{C^*} \right) = -k_L a \cdot t$. The volumetric mass transfer coefficient scales with impeller speed according to the van't Riet correlation:

\[ k_L a = S \cdot N^{1.2} \]

where $S = 0.0240\text{ h}^{-1}\text{/RPM}^{1.2}$ is the experimental aeration scaling factor. Maximum oxygen transfer rate at zero dissolved oxygen is $OTR_{max} = k_L a \cdot C^*$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The bioreactor is a $3.0\text{ L}$ glass fermenter ($D = 130\text{ mm}$, $H = 240\text{ mm}$, $V_L = 2.00\text{ L}$) fitted with 4 stainless steel baffles ($W = 12\text{ mm}$), a ring sparger ($12\text{ holes} \times 1.0\text{ mm}$), and a 6-blade Rushton turbine ($D_i = 45\text{ mm}$). Dissolved oxygen is logged by a fast Mettler-Toledo polarographic InPro 6800 DO sensor ($t_{90} < 1.5\text{ s}$, accuracy $\pm 0.1\%$). Gas mass flow controllers meter pure nitrogen and compressed air ($2.00 \pm 0.01\text{ L/min}$). Temperature is held at $25.00 \pm 0.05^\circ\text{C}$.

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

The DO sensor was calibrated at $0\%$ and $100\%$ saturation ($C^* = 8.24\text{ mg/L}$). Water was charged, heated to $25.00^\circ\text{C}$, and agitated at $N = 100\text{ RPM}$. Nitrogen was sparged at $2.0\text{ L/min}$ until DO $< 1.0\%$. Air sparging was switched on at $2.0\text{ L/min}$ ($t = 0$), logging reoxygenation at $5\text{ Hz}$. $k_L a$ was extracted from the linear slope of $\ln[(C^*-C_L)/C^*]$ vs $t$ between $20\%$ and $80\%$ saturation. The test was repeated at $200, 300, 400, 500,$ and $600\text{ RPM}$.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Nitrogen sparging displaces oxygen; reactor exhaust was vented to a dedicated fume exhaust. Gas cylinders were anchored securely. Safety goggles and lab coats were worn.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

The $N^{1.2}$ scaling validates van't Riet's power dissipation model. At $100\text{ RPM}$, large buoyant bubbles ($d_b \approx 6\text{ mm}$) rose rapidly with minimal dispersion. At $600\text{ RPM}$, intense impeller shear pulverized gas into a fine milky dispersion of microbubbles ($d_b \approx 1.5\text{ mm}$), dramatically increasing specific interfacial area $a$ and gas holdup.

10. Industrial Scale-Up & Unit Operation Applications

In industrial fermenters ($10\text{ to } 100\text{ m}^3$), power input per unit volume ($P/V$) is held between $1.5\text{ and } 3.0\text{ kW/m}^3$ to maintain $k_L a > 100\text{ h}^{-1}$ for high-cell-density E. coli and yeast cultures, balancing oxygen transfer against cellular shear damage.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why does oxygen mass transfer depend more strongly on agitation speed than gas flow rate?
A1: Agitation speed $N$ directly drives bubble breakup and turbulence ($k_L a \propto N^{1.2}$), whereas increasing gas flow rate $v_s$ only increases superficial gas flux ($k_L a \propto v_s^{0.5}$) and can cause impeller flooding if gas volume overwhelms mechanical pumping capacity.

Q2: What is impeller flooding in an aerated bioreactor?
A2: Impeller flooding occurs when gas flow is so high relative to agitation speed that the impeller is enveloped in gas cavities, losing its pumping ability and causing gas to bypass up the shaft without dispersion.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The aerated bioreactor experiment validated two-film oxygen mass transfer and the van't Riet scaling law. The measured aeration slope $S = 0.0240\text{ h}^{-1}\text{/RPM}^{1.2}$ ($k_L a = 0.0240 \cdot N^{1.2}$) confirms mechanical agitation as the primary lever governing gas-liquid mass transfer in bioprocess reactors.
