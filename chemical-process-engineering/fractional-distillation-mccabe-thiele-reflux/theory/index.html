Chemical engineering transport & equilibrium foundations of Fractional Distillation Column.

Fractional distillation is the most widely deployed thermal separation process in the chemical, petrochemical, and pharmaceutical industries, responsible for purifying petroleum fractions, petrochemical intermediates, and alcoholic spirits. In a staged tray column, liquid flows downward across horizontal perforated trays while vapor bubbles upward through perforations or bubble caps, facilitating intimate vapor-liquid mass transfer. On each tray, the more volatile component (ethanol) preferentially vaporizes into the ascending vapor stream, while the less volatile component (water) condenses into the descending liquid stream. Warren L. McCabe and Ernest W. Thiele (1925) formulated the seminal graphical method for determining the theoretical number of equilibrium stages required for a specified separation. Crucially, column performance is governed by the external reflux ratio R = L / D (liquid returned to the column divided by overhead distillate product withdrawn). Increasing the reflux ratio steepens the rectifying operating line (slope = R / (R + 1)), expanding the driving force between the operating line and the vapor-liquid equilibrium (VLE) curve. Consequently, distillate overhead purity x_D increases systematically toward the ethanol-water azeotrope (89.4 mol% ethanol at 1 atm), causing the enrichment factor 1 / (1 - x_D) to scale linearly with reflux ratio R. In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented 10-sieve-tray glass distillation column is operated across reflux ratios from 1.0 to 6.0 to evaluate distillate enrichment slope S and calculate overall Murphree tray efficiency.

1/(1 - x_D) = S · R + c_0 = 2.8500 · R + 1.2500

1. Principles of Staged Vapor-Liquid Equilibrium Separations

Distillation exploits differences in relative volatility $\alpha_{AB} = (y_A / x_A) / (y_B / x_B)$ to separate liquid mixtures through repeated vaporization and condensation. In a continuous fractionating column, descending liquid reflux scrubs heavier components from ascending vapor, while ascending vapor strips volatile components from descending liquid.

2. The McCabe–Thiele Graphical Method

Under the assumption of Constant Molar Overflow (CMO), the molar liquid flow rate $L$ and vapor flow rate $V$ remain constant within the rectifying section. An envelope mass balance around the top of the column yields the rectifying operating line:

\[ y_{n+1} = \frac{L}{V} x_n + \frac{D}{V} x_D = \frac{R}{R + 1} x_n + \frac{1}{R + 1} x_D \]

where $R = L / D$ is the external reflux ratio. As $R$ increases, the slope $R / (R + 1)$ approaches unity, shifting the operating line closer to the $45^\circ$ diagonal line ($y = x$) and widening the vertical driving force $(y^* - y)$ between the operating line and the vapor-liquid equilibrium (VLE) curve.

3. Distillate Enrichment & Reflux Ratio Modeling

As reflux ratio $R$ increases, distillate overhead purity $x_D$ approaches the thermodynamic azeotropic limit ($x_{az} \approx 0.894$ for ethanol-water at $1\text{ atm}$). The remaining impurity $(1 - x_D)$ decreases inversely with reflux ratio. Defining the enrichment factor $Y = 1 / (1 - x_D)$:

\[ Y = \frac{1}{1 - x_D} = S \cdot R + c_0 \]

where $S$ is the reflux separation enrichment slope and $c_0$ is the minimum enrichment intercept. Plotting $1 / (1 - x_D)$ versus $R$ yields a straight line whose slope $S$ quantifies column rectifying performance.

4. Overall Murphree Tray Efficiency

The overall column efficiency $E_o$ compares the number of theoretical equilibrium stages $N_{theor}$ stepped off on the McCabe–Thiele diagram to the actual physical number of trays $N_{act}$ ($10\text{ trays}$):

\[ E_o = \frac{N_{theor}}{N_{act}} \]

For industrial sieve trays in ethanol-water service, overall tray efficiency typically ranges between $65\%$ and $80\%$.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What is the physical meaning of minimum reflux ratio R_min and total reflux R = ∞?

At minimum reflux ratio R_min, an infinite number of trays is required to achieve the desired separation because the operating line touches the equilibrium curve (pinch point); at total reflux (R = ∞, D = 0), the minimum number of theoretical stages (Fenske limit) is required.

How is the Murphree vapor tray efficiency E_MV defined and calculated?

Murphree vapor efficiency is E_MV = (y_n - y_n+1) / (y*_n - y_n+1), comparing the actual vapor enrichment across tray n to the ideal enrichment that would occur if exiting vapor were in thermodynamic equilibrium with exiting liquid.

What is the ethanol–water azeotrope and why can it not be separated by conventional distillation?

At 89.4 mol% (95.6 wt%) ethanol at 1 atm (boiling point 78.15 °C), the liquid and vapor equilibrium compositions become identical (x = y); relative volatility α = 1.0, rendering further separation by standard distillation thermodynamically impossible.

How does feed thermal condition (the q-value) affect distillation column operation?

The feed quality q represents the fraction of liquid in the feed; q > 1 is subcooled liquid, q = 1 is saturated liquid, 0 < q < 1 is two-phase, q = 0 is saturated vapor, and q < 0 is superheated vapor, determining the slope of the feed line (-q / (1 - q)).

What are weeping and entrainment (flooding) in sieve tray distillation columns?

Weeping occurs at low vapor velocity when liquid drains prematurely through tray perforations instead of flowing down downcomers; flooding occurs at excessive vapor velocity when liquid droplets are carried upward into the tray above, destroying separation.

Why is the McCabe–Thiele method based on the assumption of Constant Molar Overflow (CMO)?

CMO assumes molar heats of vaporization of both components are equal and sensible heat/heat of mixing effects are negligible, meaning one mole of vapor condensing evaporates exactly one mole of liquid, keeping liquid and vapor molar flow rates constant in each section.

Experimental precautions and process safety

  • Ethanol vapors are flammable; ensure all joints are gas-tight and conduct testing inside a certified fume hood.
  • Verify cooling water is circulating through the overhead condenser before turning on the reboiler heating mantle.
  • Allow at least 20 minutes of steady operation after altering reflux ratio to establish true compositional equilibrium.
  • Cool drawn samples to exactly 20.0 °C before injecting into the digital density meter.

Theoretical assumptions & boundary condition limits

  • The transport and thermodynamic models apply within the specified calibrated operating window (1.0 to 6.0 ratio).
  • Assumes ideal continuum flow and homogeneous phase mixing without unmodeled parasitic wall losses.
  • Continuous sensor readings reflect ordinary least-squares regression across successive observations.
  • High-temperature or extreme concentration excursions require non-ideal equation-of-state corrections.
