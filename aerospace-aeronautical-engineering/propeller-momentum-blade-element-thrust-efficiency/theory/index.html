Aerodynamic and physical foundations of Propeller Performance & Thrust.

The aircraft propeller converts shaft torque delivered by an engine into usable forward propulsive thrust through aerodynamic lift generated by its rotating twisted airfoil blades. Propeller aerodynamics is modeled by combining Rankine-Froude axial momentum theory (which treats the propeller as an idealized semi-permeable actuator disk accelerating an incoming streamtube) with Stefan Drzewiecki's Blade Element Theory (BET), which analyzes each radial blade section as an autonomous 2D airfoil operating at local relative velocity and effective angle of attack. Propeller operating states are governed by the non-dimensional advance ratio J = V_inf / (n * D), where V_inf is flight airspeed, n is rotational frequency (rev/s), and D is propeller tip diameter. The non-dimensional thrust coefficient is defined as C_T = T / (rho * n^2 * D^4), and power coefficient is C_P = P_shaft / (rho * n^3 * D^5). Net propulsive efficiency is eta_p = (T * V_inf) / P_shaft = (C_T / C_P) * J. In the linear operating regime, thrust coefficient decays linearly with advance ratio as effective blade angle of attack decreases: C_T = C_T0 - m * J. In this experiment, a motorized propeller test stand in a wind tunnel records thrust T and torque Q across advance ratios from J = 0.30 to 0.80, verifying the linear thrust gradient dC_T / dJ.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What is the advance ratio J of a propeller and why is it the fundamental scaling parameter?

The advance ratio J = V_inf / (n * D) is the non-dimensional forward distance the propeller advances during one complete revolution, divided by diameter. It dictates the helical angle of the relative airflow seen by every blade element, directly determining the effective angle of attack of the blade sections.

Why does propeller thrust coefficient C_T decrease as flight airspeed increases at constant RPM?

As flight speed V_inf increases, the forward velocity component flattens the relative airflow vector hitting the rotating blade elements. This reduces the effective angle of attack (alpha_eff = theta_blade - phi_inflow), causing blade lift and total forward thrust to decrease linearly until reaching zero at J_0.

How does Blade Element Theory (BET) model a twisted propeller blade?

BET slices the propeller blade into multiple narrow radial strips of chord c(r) and pitch angle theta(r). Each strip is treated as an autonomous 2D airfoil experiencing a local relative velocity vector W = sqrt(V_inf^2 + (2*pi*n*r)^2). Local lift and drag are integrated radially from root to tip to compute total thrust and torque.

Why are propeller blades built with geometric twist (washout) from root to tip?

Rotational speed increases linearly with radius (u_rot = 2*pi*n*r), so blade elements near the tip encounter a much steeper relative airflow angle than elements near the hub. Geometric blade twist gradually reduces the blade angle theta(r) from hub to tip, ensuring that all radial sections operate at their optimum lift-to-drag angle of attack.

What is the difference between a fixed-pitch propeller and a constant-speed propeller?

A fixed-pitch propeller has rigid blades optimized for only one flight condition (climb or cruise). A constant-speed propeller uses a hydraulic or electric governor to automatically vary the blade pitch angle in flight, maintaining optimal engine RPM and peak propulsive efficiency (eta_p > 85%) across all flight airspeeds.

What is propeller windmilling and why does it generate dangerous drag after engine failure?

If an engine fails in flight and the propeller is not feathered, the oncoming airflow drives the propeller like a windmill. Windmilling blades operate at negative angles of attack, absorbing aerodynamic kinetic energy and generating massive parasitic drag. Feathering turns the blades edge-on to the flow (theta approx 90 deg) to eliminate windmilling drag.

Experimental precautions and wind tunnel safety

  • Ensure safety blast shield is securely locked around the wind tunnel propeller disk during operation.
  • Account for wind tunnel solid blockage and slipstream constriction using standard Glauert propeller corrections.
  • Verify dynamic balance of propeller blades before high-RPM testing to prevent destructive motor bearing vibration.
  • Do not exceed maximum allowable motor winding temperature during continuous high-thrust test points.

Theoretical assumptions & flow limits

  • Blade Element Momentum Theory (BEMT) assumes steady axisymmetric inflow without blade tip vortex compressibility.
  • At high rotational speeds (tip Mach M_tip > 0.75), blade tip compressibility reduces efficiency and increases noise.
  • Assumes isolated propeller nacelle without wing or fuselage aerodynamic blockage interference.
  • Motor electrical resistance heating introduces minor torque sensor thermal zero-drift requiring compensation.
