1. Title and aim

4. Theory and graph choice

In cardiovascular hemodynamics, Otto Frank's classic 1899 Windkessel model represents the arterial system as an elastic reservoir (analogous to the antique German fire-engine air chamber, 'Windkessel') that cushions pulsatile stroke volume from the left ventricle into continuous peripheral capillary perfusion. In the two-element Windkessel circuit, the systemic vasculature is modeled as a parallel electrical analog: total arterial compliance C_a (capacitance, storing elastic strain energy during systolic distension) discharging through total peripheral resistance R_p (resistor, representing arteriolar resistance) to central venous pressure. During diastole, the aortic valve is closed, ventricular ejection is zero, and the elastic recoil of the stretched aorta and large conduit arteries drives blood into the periphery. The governing differential equation during diastole is C_a · (dP/dt) + P/R_p = 0. Solving this gives an exponential pressure decay: P(t) = P_es · exp[-t / (R_p · C_a)], where P_es is end-systolic incisura pressure at t = 0 (aortic dicrotic notch) and τ = R_p · C_a is the arterial time constant (typically 800 ms in healthy young adults). Taking the natural logarithm linearizes the decay: ln(P/P_es) = -k_w · t, where k_w = 1 / (R_p · C_a) is the diastolic runoff rate constant (ms⁻¹). In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented cardiovascular pulse simulator reproduces systemic aortic pressure waves to evaluate diastolic pressure runoff and determine compliance C_a.

ln(P/P_es) = -k_w · t = -0.00125 · t

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Diastolic decay time t (ms), and the dependent coordinate y represents Natural log pressure ratio ln(P/P_es). The gradient m directly determines Windkessel Diastolic Decay Rate k_w, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Prime flow loop with 40% glycerol-water blood analog (viscosity 3.8 cP at 22 °C), purge air, and calibrate Millar pressure transducer against mercury manometer.
  2. Set pneumatic driver to 72 BPM (stroke volume 70 mL, systolic duration 300 ms, diastolic duration 533 ms); verify stable 120/80 mmHg aortic pressure wave.
  3. Locate the dicrotic notch (closure of aortic valve) marking end-systole (t = 0, P_es = 100.0 mmHg) to define the onset of pure diastolic runoff.
  4. Sample decaying arterial pressure P(t) at standardized post-incisura intervals: t = 100, 200, 300, 400, 500, and 600 ms across 10 cardiac cycles.
  5. Compute normalized pressure ratio P(t)/P_es and take natural logarithm y = ln(P/P_es) at each time step.
  6. Plot ln(P/P_es) versus decay time t, determine slope -k_w via linear regression, and calculate total arterial compliance C_a = 1 / (R_p · k_w).

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure zero air bubbles in the aortic silicone chamber; stray air acts as an uncalibrated pneumatic spring that falsely inflates compliance.
  • Identify the dicrotic notch accurately; including late-systolic ejection data distorts the pure exponential decay regression.
  • Verify that Millar solid-state pressure catheter is zeroed at mid-aortic height to avoid hydrostatic pressure offsets.
  • Maintain constant stroke volume and heart rate throughout the logging sequence.

The two-element Windkessel model neglects wave reflection phenomena that create localized systolic pressure amplification in peripheral arteries.
