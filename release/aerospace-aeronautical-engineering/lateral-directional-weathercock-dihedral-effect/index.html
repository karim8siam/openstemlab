Aeronautical assumptions and boundary condition limits

Linear derivative C_n_beta is valid for attached flow on the vertical fin (beta < 12 deg); high sideslip causes vertical fin stall. Linear regression of yawing moment coefficient against sideslip angle yields a weathercock derivative C_n_beta x 10^3 = 1.8000 deg^-1 with R^2 > 0.999999.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Measure yawing moment derivative C_n_beta (weathercock stability) and rolling moment derivative C_l_beta (dihedral effect) across a wind tunnel sideslip sweep. Standardized parameter settings: 1.0, 3.0, 5.0, 7.0, 8.5, 10.0 deg.

01

Balance Taring in Zero Sideslip

Align model symmetrically with tunnel axis (beta = 0 deg) and perform wind-off tare across all six balance channels.

02

Wind Tunnel Speed Stabilization

Run the wind tunnel to V_inf = 30 m/s, stabilizing dynamic pressure at q_inf = 551 Pa.

03

Zero-Sideslip Symmetry Check

Verify that measured side force Y, yawing moment N, and rolling moment L_roll are zero within balance noise at beta = 0 deg.

04

Systematic Sideslip Angle Sweep

Rotate the turntable through beta = 1.0, 3.0, 5.0, 7.0, 8.5, and 10.0 degrees, sampling 2000 points per channel at each step.

05

Coefficient Normalization

Compute non-dimensional yawing moment coefficient C_n = N / (q_inf * S * b) and rolling moment coefficient C_l = L_roll / (q_inf * S * b).

06

Weathercock Derivative Linear Regression

Plot C_n x 10^3 versus beta, perform linear regression to determine the weathercock derivative C_n_beta, and verify positive stability.
