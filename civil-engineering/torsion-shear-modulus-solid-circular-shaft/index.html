FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the torsional shear modulus (G), elastic shear stress distribution (τ), and angle of twist per unit length (θ/L) of a solid circular structural steel shaft according to ASTM E143 / BS 3518. Standardized parameter settings: 20.0, 40.0, 60.0, 80.0, 100.0, 120.0, 140.0 N·m.

01

Specimen Characterization

Measure diameter d of the solid circular shaft at four points using micrometer to 0.005 mm; calculate polar moment of inertia J = π d^4 / 32.

02

Mounting in Torsion Machine Grips

Insert shaft ends into machine drive head and torque reaction head collets; clamp firmly ensuring zero axial thrust or bending restraint.

03

Troptometer Setup & Zeroing

Clamp optical rotary angle encoder onto the shaft spanning gauge length L = 500.0 mm; tare torque and angle channels in data acquisition software.

04

Incremental Torsional Loading

Apply twisting torque T incrementally in steps of 20.0 N·m through 20.0, 40.0, 60.0, 80.0, 100.0, 120.0, and 140.0 N·m; record angle of twist θ at each plateau.

05

Unloading Hysteresis Verification

Decrement torque back to zero in steps; verify that angle of twist returns to zero (< 0.02°) to confirm purely elastic deformation.

06

Shear Modulus & Stress Calculation

Convert angle θ to radians; plot θ versus T; evaluate torsional compliance slope to calculate shear modulus G and surface shear stress τ_max.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

Torsion represents a primary state of shear deformation occurring in structural drive shafts, crane hoist mechanisms, curved bridge box girders, and building spandrel beams supporting eccentrically loaded floor slabs. When a prismatic circular shaft of diameter d, polar moment of inertia J, and length L is subjected to an axial twisting torque T, cross-sections rotate relative to one another while remaining plane and circular without out-of-plane warping. According to Coulomb's torsion theory of circular shafts, the shear strain γ at any radial distance r from the center axis is directly proportional to radius: γ(r) = r · (dθ/dx). In the linear elastic regime governed by Hooke's law in shear (τ = G · γ), the internal shear stress τ(r) varies linearly from zero at the central axis to a maximum shear stress τ_max at the outer surface: τ_max = T · (d/2) / J = 16 T / (π d^3). Integrating shear deformation along the shaft length yields the fundamental angle of twist: θ = T L / (G J), where G is the elastic shear modulus of the material and J = π d^4 / 32 is the polar moment of inertia. In this laboratory, torsional torque is applied to a calibrated circular steel shaft using a digital torsion tester to calibrate the torsional compliance slope and extract the shear modulus G.
