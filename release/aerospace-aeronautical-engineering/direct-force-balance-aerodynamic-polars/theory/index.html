Aerodynamic and physical foundations of Aerodynamic Drag Polar.

The aerodynamic drag polar represents the fundamental performance envelope connecting the lift coefficient C_L and drag coefficient C_D of a flight vehicle. According to classical aerodynamic theory, total drag in subsonic flow comprises parasite drag C_D0 (composed of skin friction drag and form pressure drag) and lift-induced drag C_Di resulting from the downwash created by trailing wingtip vortices. The drag polar is conventionally formulated as C_D = C_D0 + k * C_L^2, where the induced drag factor k equals 1 / (pi * e * AR), with e being the Oswald span efficiency factor and AR the wing aspect ratio. In this laboratory experiment, a finite rectangular wing model is mounted to a multi-component strain-gauge balance in the wind tunnel test section. The balance measures lift, drag, and pitching moment across a sweep of angles of attack from pre-stall through moderate stall. By plotting total drag coefficient C_D against the square of the lift coefficient C_L^2 in the linear attached-flow regime, students extract the minimum parasite drag C_D0 as the vertical intercept and determine the induced drag factor k as the slope. This polar curve allows direct graphical determination of the maximum lift-to-drag ratio (L/D)_max, tangent speed for minimum drag, and the aerodynamic efficiency boundaries vital for aircraft range and endurance optimization.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What are the two primary components of total subsonic aerodynamic drag?

Total drag comprises parasite drag C_D0 (skin friction drag from viscous shear stresses and form drag from boundary layer pressure separation) and induced drag C_Di (drag caused by downwash and wingtip vortex generation).

Why does induced drag scale with the square of the lift coefficient (C_L^2)?

Induced drag is the rearward component of the tilted lift vector: D_i = L * sin(alpha_i) approx L * (w / V_inf). Since downwash w is directly proportional to circulation and lift, and D_i is proportional to L * w, induced drag scales quadratically with lift: C_Di = k * C_L^2.

How is the maximum lift-to-drag ratio (L/D)_max calculated from the drag polar parameters?

Differentiating (L/D) = C_L / (C_D0 + k * C_L^2) with respect to C_L yields the optimum condition where parasite drag equals induced drag: C_Di = C_D0. Thus C_L,opt = sqrt(C_D0 / k), and (L/D)_max = 1 / (2 * sqrt(k * C_D0)).

What is the physical meaning of the Oswald span efficiency factor e?

The Oswald factor e accounts for non-elliptical lift distributions, planform taper imperfections, and fuselage interference. For an ideal elliptical wing, e = 1.0; for real aircraft configurations, e typically ranges between 0.75 and 0.88.

What is the difference between body axes and wind axes in force balance measurements?

Body axes are fixed to the model chord and span, measuring normal force N (perpendicular to chord) and axial force A (parallel to chord). Wind axes are aligned with oncoming airflow, measuring Lift L (perpendicular to V_inf) and Drag D (parallel to V_inf).

How do wind tunnel wall interference corrections affect the measured induced drag factor k?

Solid wind tunnel walls constrain the trailing vortex downwash, artificially reducing induced drag compared to free air. Standard Glauert wall corrections add an induced drag increment Delta C_Di = delta * (S/C_test) * C_L^2 to restore free-air equivalence.

Experimental precautions and wind tunnel safety

  • Perform weight tare calibrations with the tunnel off at the exact angles of attack tested to eliminate model gravitational bias.
  • Account for balance strut aerodynamic interference drag by performing a separate strut-only wind-on tare run.
  • Ensure the wing model mounting clamp is rigidly locked to avoid structural aeroelastic deflection under aerodynamic load.
  • Verify that Reynolds number remains constant throughout the run to prevent laminar-turbulent transition shifts in C_D0.

Theoretical assumptions & flow limits

  • Parabolic polar relationship C_D = C_D0 + k * C_L^2 is valid strictly for attached flow prior to the stall boundary.
  • Strut tare corrections may introduce small residual wake interference near the wing-strut junction.
  • Finite test section walls induce upwash that requires standard Glauert lift and induced drag corrections.
  • Assumes constant Oswald efficiency factor e across the linear lift regime; minor variations occur near stall.
