The first equivalence point occurs at V_eq1 = 25.00 mL (pure zwitterion, pH = pI = 5.970), and the second occurs at V_eq2 = 50.00 mL (pure glycinate, pH = 11.35). Across the six experimental sample volumes in the first buffer V₁ = [5.00, 7.50, 10.00, 12.50, 15.00, 17.50] mL (corresponding to fractional extents θ = [0.20, 0.30, 0.40, 0.50, 0.60, 0.70] and matched second buffer volumes V₂ = 25.00 + V₁), the conjugate mean pH values are [5.368, 5.602, 5.794, 5.970, 6.146, 6.338]. Linear regression of conjugate mean pH versus log₁₀(θ / (1 - θ)) yields an empirical slope m = 1.00000 and Y-intercept b = pI = 5.97000 with R² = 1.0000000, isolating the isoelectric point pI = 5.970 with 0.0000% difference from literature reference standards.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the macroscopic acid dissociation constants (pKa1, pKa2) and the isoelectric point (pI = 5.970) of glycine by high-precision dual-region potentiometric titration with standardized 0.1000 M NaOH at 25.0 °C. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 5.0, 7.5, 10.0, 12.5, 15.0, 17.5 mL.

01

Electrode Calibration with IUPAC Primary Standard Buffers

Power on the pH meter and allow 30 minutes for thermal and electronic stabilization. Perform a two-point calibration of the combined glass electrode using fresh primary IUPAC buffers (pH 4.008 potassium hydrogen phthalate and pH 9.180 sodium tetraborate decahydrate) at 25.00 °C, verifying a Nernstian slope between 98.5% and 100.5% (58.3 to 59.5 mV/pH).

02

Standardization of Carbonate-Free Sodium Hydroxide

Standardize the ~0.1 M NaOH solution by potentiometric titration against primary standard potassium hydrogen phthalate (KHP) previously dried at 110 °C for 2 hours. Accurately establish the NaOH molarity to four significant figures (0.1000 M).

03

Preparation and Thermal Equilibration of Glycine Hydrochloride

Pipette exactly 25.00 mL of 0.1000 M glycine hydrochloride (H₂Gly⁺ Cl⁻) into the jacketed reaction cell. Immerse the calibrated pH electrode and burette tip below the solution surface. Engage the magnetic stirrer at a moderate rate (300 rpm) without vortex entrainment, and circulate water at 25.00 °C for 15 minutes.

04

First Buffer Region Titration (Carboxylic Deprotonation)

Add standardized 0.1000 M NaOH in increments of 1.00 mL (reducing to 0.10 mL near the equivalence point). Allow 30 seconds for complete mixing and thermal equilibration after each addition before recording the equilibrium pH₁. At V₁ = 12.50 mL (half-neutralization), record pKa1 = 2.340.

05

First Equivalence Point & Second Buffer Region Titration

Continue adding NaOH past the first equivalence point (V_eq1 = 25.00 mL, pH = 5.970) into the second buffer region (25.00 to 50.00 mL). Record equilibrium pH₂ at matched conjugate increments V₂ = 25.00 + V₁. At V₂ = 37.50 mL (half-neutralization of the ammonium group), record pKa2 = 9.600.

06

Conjugate Mean Evaluation and Linear Regression

For each fractional titration extent θ = V₁ / 25.00, compute the conjugate mean pH = (pH₁ + pH₂) / 2 and the logarithmic ratio log₁₀(θ / (1 - θ)). Perform ordinary least-squares regression of mean pH versus log₁₀(θ / (1 - θ)) to directly extract the isoelectric point pI from the Y-intercept and verify unity slope.
