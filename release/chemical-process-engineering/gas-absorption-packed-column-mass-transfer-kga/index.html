FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Operate a continuous counter-current packed absorption column absorbing carbon dioxide from air into water, measure inlet and outlet gas concentrations, and evaluate the volumetric overall mass transfer coefficient K_G a and Height of a Transfer Unit (HTU). Standardized parameter settings: 10.0, 20.0, 30.0, 40.0, 50.0, 60.0 L/(min·m^2).

01

Gas Blending & Baseline Calibration

Start air blower and open CO_2 cylinder; adjust rotameters to establish 5.00 mol% CO_2 in air at total gas flux G = 25.0 kg/(h·m^2); calibrate NDIR analyzer zero and span.

02

Liquid Distributor Priming

Start water pump at moderate flow to pre-wet the Raschig ring packing completely for 10 minutes to eliminate dry spots.

03

Initial Irrigation Rate Setting

Adjust water flow to establish liquid irrigation rate L = 10.0 L/(min·m^2); verify gas pressure drop across bed is stable.

04

Steady-State Concentration Logging

Allow 15 minutes to establish mass transfer steady state; sample column inlet gas y_in and top exit gas y_out via NDIR analyzer.

05

Liquid Rate Progression Protocol

Step liquid irrigation rate through 20.0, 30.0, 40.0, 50.0, and 60.0 L/(min·m^2), logging inlet and exit CO_2 concentrations at each plateau.

06

Mass Transfer & HTU Regression Analysis

Compute log mean concentration driving force, calculate K_G a for each run, plot K_G a versus L^0.7, and evaluate the scaling slope S.

THEORY AND UNIT OPERATIONS CONTEXT

Process engineering background & physical principles.

Gas absorption (scrubbing) is a fundamental mass transfer separation operation in chemical plants, environmental emissions control, and synthesis gas purification, responsible for removing acid gases (CO_2, H_2S, SO_2) from gas streams by dissolving them into a liquid solvent. In a counter-current packed tower, gas enters at the bottom and ascends through packing, while liquid solvent is distributed over the top and trickles downward over packing elements (such as Raschig rings or structured gauze). Interfacial mass transfer is described by the two-film theory of Whitman (1923), which posits stagnant gas and liquid films on either side of the phase interface. The volumetric mass transfer rate is N_A · a = K_G a · (P_A - P_A*), where K_G a is the overall volumetric gas-phase mass transfer coefficient, 'a' is the effective gas-liquid interfacial area per unit packed volume, P_A is solute partial pressure in the bulk gas, and P_A* is equilibrium partial pressure over the liquid. For physical absorption of sparingly soluble gases like carbon dioxide in water, liquid film resistance dominates overall transport: 1 / K_G a = 1 / (k_G a) + H / (k_L a), where H is Henry's law constant. According to Onda's classic packed bed mass transfer correlations, liquid-side mass transfer coefficient k_L and wetted specific area 'a' scale with liquid mass velocity as L^0.7. In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented 1.5-meter packed tower absorbing CO_2 into water is operated across liquid irrigation rates from 10.0 to 60.0 L/(min·m^2) to measure K_G a, verify L^0.7 scaling, and determine the Height of a Transfer Unit (HTU).
