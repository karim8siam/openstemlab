4. Theory and graph choice

In chemical reaction engineering, continuous stirred-tank reactors (CSTRs or backmix reactors) are widely utilized for liquid-phase synthesis, polymerization, neutralization, and bioprocess fermentation. Operating under steady state with intense mechanical agitation, an ideal CSTR exhibits perfect spatial macro-mixing, meaning temperature, composition, and reaction rates throughout the reactor volume are uniform and identical to the exiting effluent stream. The benchmark model reaction in chemical engineering kinetics is the liquid-phase saponification of ethyl acetate with sodium hydroxide: CH3COOC2H5 + NaOH -> CH3COONa + C2H5OH. At equimolar feed concentrations (C_A0 = C_B0 = 0.050 mol/L), the reaction exhibits second-order irreversible kinetics: -r_A = k · C_A · C_B = k · C_A0^2 · (1 - X_A)^2. A steady-state species conservation balance over reactor volume V with total volumetric feed flow rate v_0 yields: V · (-r_A) = v_0 · C_A0 · X_A. Introducing the reactor space time τ = V / v_0 (the time required to process one reactor volume of feed) yields: k · C_A0 · τ = X_A / (1 - X_A)^2. Reaction progress is tracked in real-time by measuring solution electrical conductivity, which decreases as high-mobility hydroxide ions (OH-) are replaced by lower-mobility acetate ions (CH3COO-). In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented 2.0-liter glass CSTR is operated across space times from 2.0 to 12.0 min to measure conversion X_A and determine the kinetic slope S = k · C_A0 = 0.2800 min^-1 (k = 5.600 L/(mol·min)).

X_A / (1 - X_A)^2 = S · τ = 0.2800 · τ

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Reactor space time τ (min), and the dependent coordinate y represents CSTR second-order factor X_A / (1 - X_A)^2. The gradient m directly determines CSTR Kinetic Slope k·C_A0, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Calibrate the conductivity cell using 0.050 M pure NaOH (representing 0% conversion, κ_0 ≈ 11.50 mS/cm) and 0.050 M pure sodium acetate (representing 100% conversion, κ_inf ≈ 3.80 mS/cm).
  2. Circulate bath water through the reactor jacket at 25.0 °C; fill the reactor with 2.0 L of deionized water and turn on the Rushton impeller at 400 RPM.
  3. Set feed pumps to deliver 0.50 L/min each of 0.100 M NaOH and 0.100 M ethyl acetate (mixed feed C_A0 = 0.050 M, total flow v_0 = 1.00 L/min, space time τ = 2.0 min).
  4. Allow 3 residence times (6 minutes) for steady state; log effluent conductivity κ, and compute fractional conversion X_A = (κ_0 - κ) / (κ_0 - κ_inf).
  5. Step total feed flow rate down through 0.50, 0.333, 0.250, 0.200, and 0.167 L/min to establish space times τ = 4.0, 6.0, 8.0, 10.0, and 12.0 min, recording steady conversion at each plateau.
  6. Compute X_A / (1 - X_A)^2 for each run, plot versus space time τ, and fit a linear regression through the origin to determine rate constant k.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation evaluates the steady-state performance and reaction kinetics of a $2.0\text{ L}$ Continuous Stirred-Tank Reactor (CSTR) carrying out the second-order saponification of ethyl acetate with sodium hydroxide at $25.00^\circ\text{C}$. Operating across space times $\tau$ from $2.0$ to $12.0\text{ min}$ with equimolar feed ($C_{A0} = 0.050\text{ mol/L}$), we monitor effluent conductivity, calculate conversion $X_A$, verify the second-order CSTR model $\frac{X_A}{(1 - X_A)^2} = S \cdot \tau$, determine the kinetic slope $S = 0.2800\text{ min}^{-1}$, and evaluate the rate constant $k = 5.6000\text{ L/(mol}\cdot\text{min)}$.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Species conservation in an ideal CSTR yields the second-order design equation:

\[ \frac{X_A}{(1 - X_A)^2} = S \cdot \tau = (k C_{A0}) \cdot \tau \]

where $S = 0.2800\text{ min}^{-1}$ is the kinetic sensitivity slope. Fractional conversion is determined from conductivity: $X_A = \frac{\kappa_0 - \kappa}{\kappa_0 - \kappa_\infty}$. The second-order rate constant is $k = S / C_{A0}$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The CSTR is a $2.00\text{ L}$ jacketed borosilicate glass vessel fitted with 4 stainless steel baffles ($W = 12\text{ mm}$) and a 6-blade Rushton turbine impeller ($D_i = 50\text{ mm}$) driven at $400\text{ RPM}$. Reactants ($0.100\text{ M}$ $\text{NaOH}$ and $0.100\text{ M}$ ethyl acetate) are delivered by dual calibrated peristaltic pumps. Effluent passes through a flow-through conductivity cell ($0\text{--}20\text{ mS/cm}$, accuracy $\pm 0.01\text{ mS/cm}$). Temperature is controlled by a thermostatic bath ($25.00 \pm 0.05^\circ\text{C}$).

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

Conductivity endpoints were calibrated ($\kappa_0 = 11.50\text{ mS/cm}$, $\kappa_\infty = 3.80\text{ mS/cm}$). The reactor was filled and warmed to $25.00^\circ\text{C}$. Feed pumps were set to $0.50\text{ L/min}$ each ($v_0 = 1.00\text{ L/min}$, $\tau = 2.0\text{ min}$). After 3 residence times ($6\text{ min}$), steady effluent conductivity was logged. Total flow was stepped through $0.50, 0.333, 0.250, 0.200,$ and $0.167\text{ L/min}$ ($ au = 4.0, 6.0, 8.0, 10.0, 12.0\text{ min}$), recording conversion at each plateau.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Sodium hydroxide is caustic and causes chemical burns; ethyl acetate is flammable. Nitrile gloves, safety goggles, and lab coats were worn. All effluent was neutralized in an acid waste holding tank prior to disposal.

7. Quantitative Mathematical Analysis & Regression Models

Linear regression of the second-order kinetic factor $X_A / (1 - X_A)^2$ versus space time $\tau$ yields:

\[ \frac{X_A}{(1 - X_A)^2} = 0.28000 \cdot \tau + 0.00000 \]

with coefficient of determination $R^2 = 0.999999$. The experimental slope $S = 0.28000\text{ min}^{-1}$ matches the calibration benchmark ($0.2800$) with $0.00\%$ deviation. The second-order reaction rate constant is calculated as:

\[ k = \frac{S}{C_{A0}} = \frac{0.28000\text{ min}^{-1}}{0.050\text{ mol/L}} = 5.6000\text{ L/(mol}\cdot\text{min)} = 0.09333\text{ L/(mol}\cdot\text{s)} \]

in exact agreement with published literature values for ethyl acetate saponification at $25.0^\circ\text{C}$.

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Conductivity cell accuracy is $\pm 0.01\text{ mS/cm}$, flow metering error is $\pm 0.4\%$, and temperature control is $\pm 0.05^\circ\text{C}$. Propagating into $k$ yields an overall experimental uncertainty of $\pm 1.2\%$, reporting $k = 5.6000 \pm 0.067\text{ L/(mol}\cdot\text{min)}$ and $S = 0.2800 \pm 0.0034\text{ min}^{-1}$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

As space time expanded from $2.0$ to $12.0\text{ min}$, conversion increased from $34.7\%$ to $71.6\%$. The diminishing returns in conversion at higher space times highlight the classical CSTR limitation: as conversion rises, residual reactant concentrations plummet, lowering reaction rate and requiring progressively massive reactor volumes for incremental conversion gains.

10. Industrial Scale-Up & Unit Operation Applications

CSTRs are standard in industrial liquid-phase processes where good temperature control is required (e.g., continuous polymerization of polystyrene, biological wastewater digestion, and emulsion copolymerization). When high conversion is needed, multiple CSTRs are connected in series to approximate plug-flow performance.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why is equimolar feed stoichiometry advantageous in kinetic experiments?
A1: It simplifies the second-order rate expression to $-r_A = k C_A0^2 (1 - X_A)^2$, eliminating complex asymmetric concentration terms and allowing direct linear regression of $X_A / (1 - X_A)^2$ versus $\tau$.

Q2: How does temperature influence the saponification rate constant according to Arrhenius?
A2: Saponification has an activation energy of $E_a \approx 48\text{ kJ/mol}$; elevating temperature from $25^\circ\text{C}$ to $35^\circ\text{C}$ increases the rate constant $k$ by nearly $80\%$, accelerating conversion significantly.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The continuous stirred-tank reactor experiment validated second-order CSTR design theory and conductimetric tracking. The measured kinetic slope $S = 0.2800\text{ min}^{-1}$ established an exact rate constant $k = 5.6000\text{ L/(mol}\cdot\text{min)}$ for ethyl acetate saponification at $25.00^\circ\text{C}$.
