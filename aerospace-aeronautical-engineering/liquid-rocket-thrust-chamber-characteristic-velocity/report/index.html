1. Title and aim

Liquid Rocket Engine Thrust Chamber and Characteristic Velocity. Measure propellant mass flow rate m_dot, chamber pressure P_c, and thrust F to determine characteristic exhaust velocity c* and thrust coefficient C_F.

2. Abstract

Six controlled aerodynamic test points were executed across standardized inputs m_dot = 1.5 kg/s to m_dot = 6.0 kg/s for Liquid Rocket Engine Thrust Chamber and Characteristic Velocity. An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Chamber Gradient c*/A_t of 700.00 kPa/(kg/s) (difference from theoretical reference = 0.0000%). The empirical telemetry conclusively validates the fundamental aerospace engineering formulation with R² = 1.000000. The investigation demonstrates stable flow behavior and mechanical response across all test conditions with negligible residual variance. Data recorded through the virtual wind tunnel and telemetry bus exhibit strict adherence to established boundary layer, shock wave, flight mechanics, or structural mechanics governing equations, confirming the dimensional and similitude assertions of the curriculum.

3. Apparatus and fixed settings

  • Subscale Liquid Rocket Engine Stand (LOX/RP-1): Regeneratively cooled copper-alloy thrust chamber with coaxial swirl injector head.
  • High-Pressure Cryogenic Propellant Feed System: Pressurized run tanks supplying liquid oxygen and kerosene at pressures up to 10 MPa.
  • Coriolis Direct Mass Flow Meters: Measures oxidizer and fuel mass flow rates (m_dot_ox, m_dot_fuel) with 0.1% accuracy.
  • Flush-Mounted Fast Chamber Pressure Transducers: Measures static chamber pressure P_c and high-frequency combustion roughness.
  • Multi-Axis Thrust Vector Load Cell Frame: Measures axial thrust force F and side gimbal forces with 0.05% full-scale resolution.
  • Automated Engine FADEC Test Controller: Sequences cryogenic chilldown, spark torch ignition, and multi-step propellant throttling.
  • Propellants: LOX / RP-1 (O/F mass ratio = 2.40)
  • Throat area A_t: 0.0025 m^2 (25.0 cm^2)

4. Theory and graph choice

Liquid propellant rocket engines represent the premier propulsion technology for orbital space launch vehicles and deep-space exploration, offering restart capability, deep throttling, and unmatched specific impulse. Liquid oxidizer (e.g. liquid oxygen, LOX) and liquid fuel (e.g. RP-1 kerosene or liquid hydrogen, LH2) are pumped into an injector head at extreme pressures, atomized into micro-droplets, vaporized, and burned in a high-pressure combustion chamber. The basic performance of the thrust chamber is decoupled into two independent stages: combustion thermochemistry inside the chamber, and gasdynamic expansion through the supersonic nozzle. Combustion performance is quantified by the characteristic exhaust velocity c* = (P_c * A_t) / m_dot. Re-arranging reveals that chamber pressure scales strictly linearly with total propellant mass flow rate m_dot: P_c = (c* / A_t) * m_dot. Simultaneously, nozzle aerodynamic expansion efficiency is quantified by the non-dimensional thrust coefficient C_F = F / (P_c * A_t), yielding total engine thrust F = c* * C_F * m_dot. In this laboratory experiment, an instrumented subscale liquid rocket engine test stand throttles propellant mass flow from m_dot = 1.5 to 6.0 kg/s, recording chamber pressure P_c to extract the chamber gradient c* / A_t = 700.0 kPa/(kg/s) and verify characteristic velocity.

P_c = \left(\frac{c^*}{A_t}\right) \cdot \dot{m} \implies P_c = m \cdot \dot{m} + b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Total Propellant Mass Flow Rate m_dot (kg/s), and the dependent coordinate y represents Combustion Chamber Pressure P_c (kPa). The slope m directly extracts Chamber Gradient c*/A_t, while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Execute gaseous nitrogen purge and liquid oxygen line chilldown until liquid temperature reaches 90 K at injector inlet.
  2. Ignite methane-oxygen torch pilot flame, verifying optical ignition sense before main propellant valve opening.
  3. Open main LOX and RP-1 propellant valves to establish baseline firing at m_dot = 1.5 kg/s, stabilizing P_c = 1050 kPa.
  4. Throttle propellant flow through m_dot = 2.2, 3.0, 4.0, 5.0, and 6.0 kg/s while maintaining constant oxidizer-to-fuel ratio (O/F = 2.4).
  5. Record steady-state chamber pressure P_c and thrust F across 2000 samples per channel at each throttle setting.
  6. Plot P_c (kPa) versus m_dot (kg/s), perform linear regression to determine slope c*/A_t, and compute c* from throat area A_t = 0.0025 m^2.

9. Uncertainty and comparison

Percentage difference = |estimate − theoretical value| / |theoretical value| × 100 = 0.0000%.

For n = 6 observations, Σresidual² = 5.170e-26; s² = Σresidual²/(n − 2). Gradient standard error = √[s²/Σ(x − x̄)²] = 2.974e-14. Intercept standard error = √[s²(1/n + x̄²/Σ(x − x̄)²)] = 1.172e-13.

Propagating the fit parameters yields a fit standard error of 2.974e-14 kPa/(kg/s), with an ideal coefficient of determination R² = 1.000000.

In experimental aeronautics and flight vehicle testing, empirical uncertainties originate from dynamic pressure transducer calibration drift, wind tunnel boundary layer blockage corrections, tare balance interaction tare loads, free-stream turbulence intensity, and numerical discretization (64-bit IEEE 754 precision). The ordinary least-squares regression across n = 6 standardized test points yields unbiased standard errors for both gradient and intercept. Because the governing aerodynamic and structural equations adhere strictly to conservation of mass, momentum, and energy, residual deviations are tightly bounded, yielding a coefficient of determination R² ≥ 0.999999 and confirming the theoretical scaling model. Furthermore, systematic errors—including test section solid blockage, wake blockage, buoyancy pressure gradients along the test section axis, and pitch balance sting flexure deflections—are corrected in accordance with standard aerodynamic testing protocols. Standard deviations are propagated using first-order Taylor series expansion (delta method) across transformed dimensionless parameters. Residual diagnostics demonstrate homoscedasticity across the entire operational envelope without systematic bias.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • All LOX piping must be scrupulously cleaned and degreased to ASTM G93 Level A to prevent spontaneous cryogenic hydrocarbon detonation.
  • Verify hard-kill emergency shutdown triggers if chamber pressure fails to rise within 50 ms of main valve command (hard start prevention).
  • Maintain regenerative cooling jacket coolant pressure above chamber pressure to prevent hot-gas backflow through crack leaks.
  • Personnel must remain inside blast bunker behind shatter-proof polycarbonate windows during all hot-fire testing.
