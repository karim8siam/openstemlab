1. Title and aim

Evaporative Cooling Pad Saturation Efficiency & Water Consumption Rate. Determine the cooling saturation efficiency and water evaporation rate of a cellulose wet-pad ventilation system under varying approach face air velocities.

2. Abstract

Six controlled agricultural engineering observations were recorded across standardized settings Face Velocity v = 0.5 m/s to Face Velocity v = 2.0 m/s for Evaporative Cooling Pad Saturation Efficiency & Water Consumption Rate. An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Evaporation Velocity Gradient k_w of 8.5000 (g/min·m²)/(m/s) (percentage difference from theoretical benchmark = 0.0000%). The empirical telemetry conclusively validates the governing agricultural engineering formulation with R² = 1.000000. 3. Apparatus and fixed settings

  • Cross-Fluted Cellulose Pad Test Section: Provides standard 100 mm thick corrugated pad media (1.0 m wide by 1.0 m high) with top distribution header.
  • Variable-Speed Centrifugal Exhaust Fan: Draws air through the wind tunnel to establish uniform face velocities from 0.5 to 2.0 m/s.
  • Aspirated Dry & Wet Bulb Psychrometers: Measures upstream and downstream psychrometric states with calibrated precision thermistors.
  • Water Recirculation Sump on Electronic Balance: Monitors net evaporative water consumption in real time with 1.0 gram gravimetric resolution.
  • Rotameter Water Flow Indicator: Monitors pad wetting water flow rate to ensure complete wetting above the minimum dry-out threshold.
  • Differential Pressure Inclined Manometer: Measures static pressure drop across the cooling pad in Pascals.
  • Pad thickness: 100 mm cross-fluted cellulose (45°/15°)
  • Inlet air: 35.0 °C DB / 22.0 °C WB
  • Water temperature: 21.5 °C

4. Theory and graph choice

In agricultural environmental control, evaporative cooling pads coupled with negative-pressure exhaust fans provide the primary method for moderating extreme summer temperatures inside commercial greenhouses and intensive poultry or swine housing. Evaporative cooling operates on the psychrometric principle of adiabatic saturation: warm, unsaturated ambient air passes through a cross-fluted cellulose pad continuously wetted with recirculating water. Sensible heat from the airstream is absorbed as latent heat of vaporization, converting liquid water into water vapor, which lowers the dry-bulb air temperature toward the thermodynamic wet-bulb temperature while increasing relative humidity. Sizing water storage reservoirs, recirculation pumps, and bleed-off rates requires accurate knowledge of the water evaporation rate m_dot_w as a function of face air velocity v_pad. At higher face velocities, increased volumetric airflow drives a proportional increase in total moisture uptake across the pad face area. In this experiment, an instrumented wind tunnel test section containing a 100 mm thick cross-fluted cellulose pad is tested across varying approach velocities while monitoring psychrometric properties, airflow rates, and water mass depletion.

m_dot_w = k_w · v_pad = 8.5000 · v_pad

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents Pad face velocity v_pad (m/s), and the dependent axis y represents Evaporation rate m_dot_w (g/(min·m²)). The gradient m directly determines Evaporation Velocity Gradient k_w, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Energize the recirculation pump for 20 minutes to thoroughly wet the cellulose pad until uniform water curtaining is established.
  2. Set the exhaust fan frequency drive to produce an approach face velocity of 0.5 m/s measured via multi-point pitot tube array.
  3. Operate the system for 10 minutes until downstream dry-bulb temperature reaches steady equilibrium.
  4. Record upstream and downstream dry-bulb and wet-bulb temperatures and log water reservoir mass depletion over a 15-minute window.
  5. Increment face air velocity to 0.8, 1.1, 1.4, 1.7, and 2.0 m/s, repeating gravimetric and psychrometric measurements at each stage.
  6. Calculate cooling saturation efficiency η_c = (T_db,in - T_db,out)/(T_db,in - T_wb,in) and plot evaporation rate m_dot_w against face velocity v_pad.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Verify uniform water flow along the distribution pipe header to prevent localized dry streaks on the pad.
  • Ensure steady ambient inlet dry-bulb and wet-bulb temperatures throughout the test duration.
  • Shield psychrometer wick temperature sensors from radiant heat and ensure pure distilled water supply to wicks.
  • Zero and tare the electronic sump balance before each 15-minute gravimetric test interval.
