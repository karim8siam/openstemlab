Civil engineering mechanics & structural foundations of Coarse Aggregate Absorption.

Specific gravity and water absorption are fundamental physical properties of coarse aggregates directly governing concrete mix proportioning, yield calculations, and batch water corrections. Coarse aggregates possess permeable internal pores (voids accessible to water) and impermeable sealed pores. When aggregate is immersed in water for 24 ± 4 hours, capillary action saturates the accessible pore system. In the Saturated Surface-Dry (SSD) condition, all internal permeable pores are filled with water while the external particle surface is completely dry. If aggregate is batched into a mixer in a dry state, it absorbs water from the cement paste, drastically reducing fresh workability and effective water-cement ratio; conversely, wet aggregate with free surface moisture contributes excess water, lowering hardened compressive strength. Under ASTM C127, aggregate mass is weighed in three distinct moisture states: oven-dry mass A, saturated surface-dry mass B, and submerged buoyant mass C using a wire basket suspended in a water tank. In this laboratory experiment, coarse limestone aggregate is tested across sample sizes from 1000 to 3000 g to determine specific gravity metrics and quantify the water absorption coefficient.

1. Porosity Structure & Moisture States of Aggregate

Coarse aggregates are heterogeneous natural rock materials containing microscopic void structures. Aggregate volume is partitioned into three distinct components:

\[ V_{total} = V_s + V_p + V_i \]

where $V_s$ is solid impermeable mineral skeleton volume, $V_p$ is permeable capillary pore volume accessible to water, and $V_i$ is isolated sealed pore volume. Depending on ambient conditions, aggregate exists in four moisture states: (1) Oven-dry (zero moisture), (2) Air-dry (partially dry pores), (3) Saturated Surface-Dry (SSD, pores 100% full, surface dry), and (4) Wet (pores full plus free surface water film).

2. Archimedes' Buoyancy Principle & Volume Measurement

Direct volumetric measurement of irregular angular aggregate particles via calipers is physically impossible. ASTM C127 exploits Archimedes' hydrostatic buoyancy principle. When a saturated aggregate mass of total volume $V_{total}$ is submerged in water of density $\rho_w$, the buoyant force equals the weight of displaced water. The apparent submerged mass $C$ is:

\[ C = B - \rho_w V_{total} \implies V_{total} = \frac{B - C}{\rho_w} \]

where $B$ is the mass in air in the SSD condition. Similarly, the impermeable solid volume $V_{solid} = V_s + V_i$ is evaluated by subtracting submerged mass $C$ from oven-dry mass $A$:

\[ V_{solid} = \frac{A - C}{\rho_w} \]

3. Specific Gravity Metrics & Absorption Relation

Three distinct dimensionless specific gravities are defined relative to water at $23^\circ\text{C}$:

\[ \text{Bulk Specific Gravity (Dry)} = \frac{A}{B - C} \] \[ \text{Bulk Specific Gravity (SSD)} = \frac{B}{B - C} \] \[ \text{Apparent Specific Gravity} = \frac{A}{A - C} \]

Because $B - C > A - C$, Apparent Specific Gravity is always strictly greater than Bulk Specific Gravity. The water absorption capacity $W_{abs}$ (percentage of dry mass) is:

\[ W_{abs} = \frac{B - A}{A} \times 100\% \implies B = \left( 1 + \frac{W_{abs}}{100} \right) A = k_{abs} \cdot A \]

For high-quality crushed limestone with $1.85\%$ absorption capacity, the saturation coefficient is $k_{abs} = 1.0185$.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What is the physical distinction between Bulk Specific Gravity and Apparent Specific Gravity?

Bulk Specific Gravity includes both solid mineral volume and internal permeable pore volume in the denominator (B - C), whereas Apparent Specific Gravity excludes permeable pore volume, considering only impermeable solid mineral volume (A - C).

How is the Saturated Surface-Dry (SSD) condition defined and identified experimentally?

The SSD condition represents aggregate whose internal permeable pores are 100% saturated with water, but whose external surface has zero free liquid film; it is identified when surface moisture sheen just disappears upon rolling in absorbent cloth.

Why is the SSD condition chosen as the standard reference state for concrete mix design?

In the SSD state, aggregate neither absorbs mixing water from the cement paste nor contributes free surface water to the mix, ensuring that the design water-cement ratio remains unaltered.

What happens if concrete is batched using bone-dry aggregate without water absorption compensation?

The dry aggregate absorbs water from the fresh paste during mixing and transit, causing rapid slump loss, stiffening, poor pumpability, and honeycombed consolidation defects.

What typical range of water absorption is expected for normal-weight structural concrete aggregates?

Normal-weight granite, basalt, and dense limestone aggregates typically exhibit water absorption between 0.5% and 2.5%; values above 3% indicate highly porous or weathered rock.

Why is water temperature maintained at 23.0 ± 2.0 °C during buoyant submerged weighing?

Water density varies with temperature; calibrating buoyant mass at 23 °C ensures stable water density (0.9975 g/cm^3) for Archimedes' volume displacement calculations.

Experimental precautions and laboratory safety

  • Agitate the wire basket vigorously underwater to dislodge all trapped air bubbles before recording submerged mass C.
  • Avoid over-drying aggregates past the SSD state with hot air or sunlight, which drains moisture from internal pores.
  • Ensure the suspension wire connecting the basket to the balance under-hook does not touch the tank rim or water surface meniscus.
  • Perform SSD weighing rapidly to prevent evaporative moisture loss from saturated pores into ambient dry air.
