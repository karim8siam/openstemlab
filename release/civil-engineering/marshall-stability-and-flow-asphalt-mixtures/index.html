FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine Marshall stability (peak compressive resistance) and plastic flow value of cylindrical bituminous paving mixtures compacted at 60°C to design optimum binder content (OBC) according to ASTM D6927 / AASHTO T245. Standardized parameter settings: 3.5, 4.0, 4.5, 5.0, 5.5, 6.0 %.

01

Aggregate Batching & Thermal Conditioning

Batch coarse aggregate, sand, and mineral filler to target gradation (NMAS 12.5 mm); heat 1200 g aggregate batches in oven at 160 °C for 4 hours; heat PG 64-22 bitumen to 150 °C.

02

Mixing at Target Binder Contents

Add bitumen at target percentages (3.5, 4.0, 4.5, 5.0, 5.5, and 6.0% by total mix weight); mix thoroughly in mechanical mixer at 150 °C until all aggregates are uniformly coated.

03

Hammer Compaction in Cylindrical Mold

Transfer mixture into preheated 101.6 mm mold lined with silicone paper; spade 15 times with hot spatula around perimeter and 10 times in interior; compact with 75 blows per face.

04

Specimen Extrusion & Volumetric Measurement

Cool specimen to room temperature; extrude using hydraulic jack; measure height, dry mass in air, submerged mass in water, and saturated surface dry (SSD) mass to determine bulk specific gravity G_mb.

05

Water Bath Conditioning at 60 °C

Submerge compacted briquettes in thermostatic water bath at 60.0 ± 1.0 °C for exactly 35 minutes prior to testing.

06

Marshall Breaking Test & Parameter Recording

Transfer briquette into pre-warmed Marshall breaking head within 30 seconds; apply compressive displacement at 50.8 mm/min; record maximum load (Stability in kN) and deformation at failure (Flow in mm).

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

In civil transportation highway pavement engineering, Hot-Mix Asphalt (HMA) is the primary surfacing material for flexible pavements, carrying millions of equivalent single axle loads (ESALs) over design lifespans. Developed by Bruce Marshall in the 1940s and standardized under ASTM D6927 / AASHTO T245, the Marshall Mix Design method establishes the Optimum Binder Content (OBC) that balances structural stability, durability, fatigue resistance, and skid resistance. Asphalt concrete specimens (101.6 mm diameter, 63.5 mm height) are batched with mineral aggregate blends and varying asphalt binder contents (e.g. 3.5% to 6.0% by total mix mass), heated to 145–150 °C, and compacted in a preheated cylindrical steel mold using a 4.536 kg sliding drop hammer falling 457.2 mm (applying 50 or 75 blows per face for medium or heavy traffic). Compacted briquettes are conditioned in a thermostatic water bath at 60.0 ± 1.0 °C for 30 to 40 minutes (simulating extreme summer pavement surface temperatures) and sheared in a Marshall breaking head at a constant deformation rate of 50.8 mm/min (2.0 in/min). Two fundamental parameters are recorded: (1) Marshall Stability: the maximum compressive load (kN) sustained before plastic failure; and (2) Marshall Flow: the total plastic deformation (in units of 0.25 mm or mm) recorded at the instant peak load is reached. In this laboratory investigation, stability response across binder contents from 3.5% to 6.0% is evaluated to calibrate mix stiffness sensitivity slope = 2.8500 kN/% (yielding peak stability = 14.25 kN at 5.0% bitumen).
