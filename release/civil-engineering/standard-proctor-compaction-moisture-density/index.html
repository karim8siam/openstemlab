FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the relationship between moisture content and dry density of compacted soil, establish the maximum dry density (MDD), and identify the optimum moisture content (OMC) according to ASTM D698 / AASHTO T99. Standardized parameter settings: 8.0, 10.0, 12.0, 14.0, 16.0, 18.0, 20.0 %.

01

Soil Preparation & Initial Moisture Dosing

Air-dry 15 kg of subgrade soil passing the 4.75 mm (No. 4) sieve; split into 2.5 kg batches; mix initial batch with target moisture content (e.g. 8.0%); seal in plastic bag for 16 hours to equilibrate.

02

Mold Assembly & Tare Weighing

Clean and weigh empty Proctor mold with baseplate (without collar) to the nearest 1.0 g; record mass M_mold; attach collar securely.

03

Three-Layer Standard Compaction

Place soil into mold in three approximately equal layers; compact each layer with exactly 25 uniform blows of the 2.495 kg rammer dropping from 304.8 mm.

04

Collar Removal & Surface Screeding

Carefully remove collar; trim excess soil projecting above top of mold flush with straightedge using sawing motions; patch minor surface voids with fines.

05

Mold Weighing & Moist Density Calculation

Clean exterior of mold and baseplate; weigh mold with moist soil to nearest 1.0 g (M_total); calculate wet density ρ_wet = (M_total - M_mold) / 944 cm³.

06

Moisture Content Determination & Curve Plotting

Extrude soil core; extract moisture samples from top, middle, and bottom; dry in oven at 110 °C to determine w; calculate ρ_d = ρ_wet / (1 + w); repeat at 10%, 12%, 14%, 16%, 18%, and 20% to plot the compaction curve.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

In geotechnical earthworks—including highway embankments, earth dams, airport subgrades, and structural foundation pads—soil must be mechanically compacted to densify its particulate matrix, increase shear strength, reduce hydraulic conductivity (permeability), and minimize post-construction settlement. Developed by Ralph R. Proctor in 1933, the Standard Proctor Compaction test (ASTM D698) standardizes mechanical compaction effort at 592 kJ/m³ (12,375 ft-lbf/ft³). A soil sample prepared at a known moisture content is compacted in three equal layers into a cylindrical steel mold (101.6 mm diameter, 116.4 mm height, volume 944 cm³) using 25 blows per layer of a 2.495 kg (5.5 lbf) mechanical rammer dropping freely from a height of 304.8 mm (12 inches). Wet density ρ_wet is determined from the mold mass, and moisture content w is measured by oven drying to compute dry density: ρ_d = ρ_wet / (1 + w). Plotting dry density against moisture content yields a classic bell-shaped compaction curve peaking at Maximum Dry Density (MDD) and Optimum Moisture Content (OMC). At low moisture ('dry of optimum'), soil particles are rigid and resist rearrangement; added water forms lubricating double-layer films allowing particles to pack densely. Beyond OMC ('wet of optimum'), incompressible water fills the pore space, displacing soil solids and lowering dry density toward the Zero Air Voids (ZAV) theoretical saturation limit. In this laboratory, the compaction response is evaluated across moisture levels from 8% to 20% to calibrate density sensitivity.
