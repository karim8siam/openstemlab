Biomedical and physiological foundations of SPR Immunosensor Kinetics.

In modern pharmacology, biomarker discovery, and clinical immunology, Surface Plasmon Resonance (SPR) is the gold-standard label-free optical biosensing technology for measuring real-time macromolecular binding kinetics (antibody-antigen affinity, protein-protein interactions, and DNA hybridization). Under the Kretschmann prism-coupling optical configuration, p-polarized monochromatic laser light undergoes Total Internal Reflection (TIR) at the interface of a high-refractive-index glass prism coated with a 50 nm thin gold film. At a critical resonance angle θ_spr, photon energy couples into collective oscillations of free conduction-band electrons at the gold-dielectric interface, generating non-radiative surface plasmon polaritons that propagate along the metal surface. This resonance produces a sharp, localized intensity dip in the reflected light spectrum. The evanescent electromagnetic field decays exponentially into the sample solution (penetration depth ~200 nm), making the resonance angle exquisitely sensitive to minute refractive index changes in the immediate dielectric vicinity. When target antigen proteins bind specifically to immobilized antibodies on the sensor chip surface, the local mass density and refractive index increase, causing the SPR angle dip to shift by Δθ. In the sub-saturation Langmuir binding regime, angle shift Δθ is strictly linear with analyte concentration: Δθ = k_spr · C_ag. In this experiment, a functionalized anti-IgG gold sensor chip is tested across nano-molar antigen concentrations.

Δθ = k_spr · C_ag = 8.4000 · C_ag

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What physical phenomenon occurs at the resonance angle θ_spr in an SPR biosensor?

At the resonance angle, the momentum of incoming p-polarized photons matches the momentum of surface plasmons (collective electron oscillations) at the gold-dielectric boundary. Photons couple their energy into the plasmon wave, resulting in a dramatic loss of reflected light intensity (a sharp absorption dip).

Why does biomolecular binding shift the SPR angle to a higher value?

The evanescent wave penetrates approximately 200 nm into the liquid medium. When target biomolecules bind to capture antibodies on the gold surface, they displace water molecules with denser protein, raising the local refractive index. Higher refractive index increases plasmon momentum, shifting the resonance angle outward to a higher value.

What is a 'Resonance Unit' (RU) in commercial Biacore SPR instruments?

One Resonance Unit (RU) corresponds to an angle shift of 0.0001° (0.1 millidegree). In typical protein interactions, 1000 RU corresponds approximately to a surface protein mass density of 1.0 ng/mm².

What are the key advantages of SPR over traditional ELISA assays?

SPR is completely label-free (no fluorescent tags or secondary enzyme-linked antibodies needed), provides real-time kinetic binding data (association rate k_on and dissociation rate k_off), and can be performed automatically in minutes.

Why is an inline reference flowcell used in dual-channel SPR instruments?

Injecting sample solutions causes a bulk refractive index shift due to differences in buffer salt or DMSO content. Subtracting the non-specific reference flowcell response (Fc2 - Fc1) eliminates this bulk refractive shift, isolating true molecular binding.

What chemical reagent is commonly used to regenerate the antibody surface?

Low pH buffers (such as 10 mM Glycine-HCl, pH 2.0 to 2.5) disrupt electrostatic interactions between antibody and antigen, dissociating the bound target while leaving the covalently immobilized capture antibody intact and ready for the next test.

Experimental precautions and patient safety

  • Degas all running buffers thoroughly; micro-bubbles in the flowcell create massive optical scattering spikes.
  • Maintain strictly constant flow rate (30 μL/min) to prevent mass-transport-limited binding artifacts.
  • Control optical deck temperature within ±0.01 °C; refractive index is highly sensitive to temperature.
  • Ensure regeneration pulses are brief (30 s) to avoid irreversible antibody denaturation.

Theoretical assumptions & biological limits

  • The linear sensitivity model applies in the low-concentration sub-saturation regime (C_ag ≤ 30 nM).
  • Evanescent wave decays exponentially; molecules binding beyond 200 nm from the gold surface are invisible.
  • Small molecules (< 300 Daltons) produce very small mass changes, requiring high-sensitivity competitive assays.
  • Non-specific adsorption of sticky proteins from serum samples can cause false-positive refractive shifts.
