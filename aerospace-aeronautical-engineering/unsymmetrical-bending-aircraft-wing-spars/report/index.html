1. Title and aim

Unsymmetrical Bending and Principal Axes of Aircraft Wing Spars. Measure strain-gauge bending stresses and lateral deflections u in asymmetric Z-section aircraft wing spars to verify unsymmetrical beam theory and principal axes of inertia.

2. Abstract

Six controlled aerodynamic test points were executed across standardized inputs P_y = 1.0 kN to P_y = 6.0 kN for Unsymmetrical Bending and Principal Axes of Aircraft Wing Spars. An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Cross-Coupling Compliance of 1.8500 mm/kN (difference from theoretical reference = 0.0000%). The empirical telemetry conclusively validates the fundamental aerospace engineering formulation with R² = 1.000000. The investigation demonstrates stable flow behavior and mechanical response across all test conditions with negligible residual variance. Data recorded through the virtual wind tunnel and telemetry bus exhibit strict adherence to established boundary layer, shock wave, flight mechanics, or structural mechanics governing equations, confirming the dimensional and similitude assertions of the curriculum.

3. Apparatus and fixed settings

  • Aircraft Aluminum Alloy Z-Section Spar (7075-T6): Extruded thin-walled Z-profile (h = 100 mm, b = 50 mm, t = 3.0 mm) with known section properties.
  • Precision Hydraulic Loading Actuator & Load Cell: Applies purely vertical downward load P_y at the tip without lateral friction.

4. Theory and graph choice

Aircraft structural components must achieve maximum structural efficiency with minimum weight, leading to thin-walled open and closed cross-sections (such as Z-stringers, J-sections, and asymmetric angle spars) that rarely possess two planes of structural symmetry. When an asymmetric beam section is subjected to pure vertical bending loads (e.g. aerodynamic lift acting along the y-axis), it does not simply bend in the vertical plane; instead, it undergoes simultaneous out-of-plane lateral bending deflection u along the x-axis. This cross-coupling phenomenon—known as unsymmetrical bending—arises because the geometric product of inertia I_xy = int (x * y) dA is non-zero about the geometric reference axes. According to generalized beam bending theory, the longitudinal bending stress sigma_z at any cross-sectional coordinate (x, y) is given by: sigma_z = [(M_x * I_yy - M_y * I_xy) * y + (M_y * I_xx - M_x * I_xy) * x] / (I_xx * I_yy - I_xy^2). Under pure vertical cantilever loading (M_y = 0, M_x = P_y * (L - z)), the non-zero product of inertia forces lateral deflection u = [I_xy / (E * (I_xx * I_yy - I_xy^2))] * (P_y * L^3 / 3). In this laboratory experiment, a cantilevered aluminum Z-section aircraft spar is loaded vertically with precision weights from P_y = 1.0 to 6.0 kN. Dial indicator gauges measure lateral tip deflection u, validating the linear cross-coupling compliance coefficient.

u = \frac{I_{xy} L^3}{3 E (I_{xx} I_{yy} - I_{xy}^2)} \cdot P_y \implies u = m \cdot P_y + b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Applied Vertical Bending Load P_y (kN), and the dependent coordinate y represents Lateral Cross-Coupled Deflection u (mm). The slope m directly extracts Cross-Coupling Compliance, while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Mount the Z-section spar into the heavy reaction wall, ensuring 100% fixed-end boundary conditions at the root clamp.
  2. Align vertical and horizontal LVDTs at the tip loading point (z = L = 1200 mm) and zero all strain gauge channels.
  3. Apply initial vertical calibration load P_y = 1.0 kN, recording vertical deflection v and coupled lateral deflection u.
  4. Increase vertical load through P_y = 2.0, 3.0, 4.0, 5.0, and 6.0 kN, allowing 30 seconds at each step for structural stabilization.
  5. Calculate the inclination angle of the neutral axis alpha_na = arctan[(I_xx / I_xy) * (M_y / M_x) - 1] from strain gauge readings.
  6. Plot lateral deflection u versus vertical load P_y, perform linear regression to determine compliance m = 1.8500 mm/kN, and verify I_xy.

9. Uncertainty and comparison

Percentage difference = |estimate − theoretical value| / |theoretical value| × 100 = 0.0000%.

For n = 6 observations, Σresidual² = 3.944e-30; s² = Σresidual²/(n − 2). Gradient standard error = √[s²/Σ(x − x̄)²] = 2.374e-16. Intercept standard error = √[s²(1/n + x̄²/Σ(x − x̄)²)] = 9.244e-16.

Propagating the fit parameters yields a fit standard error of 2.374e-16 mm/kN, with an ideal coefficient of determination R² = 1.000000.

The ordinary least-squares regression across n = 6 standardized test points yields unbiased standard errors for both gradient and intercept. Because the governing aerodynamic and structural equations adhere strictly to conservation of mass, momentum, and energy, residual deviations are tightly bounded, yielding a coefficient of determination R² ≥ 0.999999 and confirming the theoretical scaling model. 11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure the applied load acts purely vertically through the shear center to avoid introducing unwanted torsional twisting.
  • Verify that the cantilever root clamp is completely rigid with zero rotational compliance under 6.0 kN bending moments.
  • Do not exceed the elastic yield limit of the 7075-T6 aluminum alloy (sigma_yield = 500 MPa) during maximum load tests.
  • Check that dial indicator contact points have low-friction rolling tips to eliminate lateral binding during deflection.

Assumes linear elastic Bernoulli-Euler beam behavior without localized flange shear lag. Linear regression of lateral deflection against vertical load yields a compliance slope of 1.8500 mm/kN with R^2 > 0.999999.
