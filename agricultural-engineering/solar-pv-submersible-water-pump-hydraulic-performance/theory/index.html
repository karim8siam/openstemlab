Agricultural and physical foundations of Solar PV Submersible Pump.

Solar photovoltaic (PV) powered water pumping systems represent one of the most critical renewable energy technologies for agricultural irrigation, livestock watering, and rural domestic water supply, particularly in off-grid arid and semi-arid regions. Modern agricultural solar pumping systems utilize high-efficiency permanent magnet brushless DC (BLDC) submersible motors coupled to multistage centrifugal or helical rotor pumps, driven through maximum power point tracking (MPPT) variable frequency inverters. Unlike conventional grid-powered pumps operating at fixed rotational speed, solar pumps operate under continuously varying solar irradiance and electrical power inputs. Sizing PV arrays, pump head capacity, and daily water delivery requires an accurate assessment of the subsystem power conversion efficiency η_sys, which relates incoming DC electrical power from the PV array P_elec to useful hydraulic water power delivered: P_hyd = ρ_w · g · Q · H, where ρ_w is water density, g is gravitational acceleration, Q is volumetric discharge flow rate (m³/s), and H is total dynamic head (m). Over the operational working range of the MPPT inverter, hydraulic output power scales in direct linear proportion to array electrical power through the subsystem efficiency η_sys = P_hyd / P_elec. In this experiment, a 1.0 kW submersible solar pump installed in a deep-well test column is tested across simulated PV electrical power levels to evaluate hydraulic discharge, power conversion efficiency, and motor-pump operating characteristics.

P_hyd = η_sys · P_elec = 0.6800 · P_elec

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

How is useful hydraulic water power defined and calculated?

Hydraulic power is the mechanical energy transferred to the pumped water stream per unit time: P_hyd = ρ · g · Q · H, where ρ is water density (1000 kg/m³), g is gravitational acceleration (9.81 m/s²), Q is discharge rate (m³/s), and H is total dynamic head (m). Expressed in Watts, P_hyd = (Q in m³/h × H in m) / 0.367.

What is the function of the Maximum Power Point Tracker (MPPT) in solar water pumping?

The MPPT controller continuously adjusts electrical impedance to operate the PV modules at their maximum power voltage (V_mp) and current (I_mp) regardless of changing sunlight levels and cell temperatures, maximizing energy delivered to the pump motor.

Why are Brushless DC (BLDC) permanent magnet motors preferred over conventional AC induction motors in solar pumps?

BLDC motors offer significantly higher electrical efficiency (85% to 92% compared to 70% to 78% for small AC motors), superior starting torque at low solar irradiance, reduced maintenance (no brushes or commutators), and lower heat dissipation.

What is the threshold solar irradiance required for a solar pump to initiate water discharge?

Solar pumps require a minimum electrical power threshold (typically 15% to 25% of rated power, corresponding to ~150 to 200 W/m² irradiance) to overcome static head and mechanical bearing friction before water starts discharging at the surface.

Why is water storage in elevated tanks generally preferred over battery electrical storage in agricultural solar pumping?

Storing potential energy as pumped water in overhead reservoirs is vastly cheaper, longer-lasting (20+ year tank lifespan vs 3-5 years for batteries), virtually maintenance-free, and avoids battery round-trip charge/discharge efficiency losses.

How does total dynamic head (TDH) affect pump discharge flow rate at constant solar irradiance?

Under constant electrical power input, pump discharge flow rate Q is inversely proportional to total dynamic head H (Q ≈ P_hyd / (ρ·g·H)); doubling the head cuts delivery flow roughly in half.

Experimental precautions and agricultural field safety

  • Verify constant discharge pressure head across all power levels using the calibrated throttling valve.
  • Ensure submersible pump motor remains fully submerged to prevent motor winding overheating.
  • Allow the MPPT controller sufficient time to converge on the true global power peak after each power change.
  • Verify zero pipe leakage throughout the test column and calibration manifolds.

Theoretical assumptions & biological limits

  • The linear power relationship applies above the minimum cut-in threshold (P_elec ≥ 200 W).
  • Assumes clean water without suspended sand or abrasive sediments that cause impeller wear.
  • Evaluated at constant fluid temperature (20.0 °C); warm water alters fluid density and motor cooling.
  • Applies to a constant total dynamic head of 35.0 m; varying head shifts the pump operating point along its H-Q curve.
