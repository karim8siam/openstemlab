Aerodynamic and physical foundations of Transonic Drag Divergence.

The transonic flight regime (approximately 0.75 < M_inf < 1.2) represents one of the most demanding challenges in aerospace engineering. As an aircraft accelerates beyond its critical Mach number M_crit, an embedded supersonic pocket forms over the upper surface of the wing. As the flow continues downstream into the recompression region, this supersonic pocket must terminate through a nearly normal shock wave standing directly on the wing surface. This shock wave introduces an entirely new physical drag component: transonic wave drag C_D,wave. Wave drag arises from the irreversible entropy generation across the shock wave and the severe shock-induced boundary layer separation occurring at its foot. As free-stream Mach number increases beyond the drag divergence Mach number M_dd (conventionally defined as the Mach number where dC_D / dM = 0.10), total drag climbs catastrophically according to a non-linear scaling law: Delta C_D,wave approx k_w * (M_inf - M_crit)^3. In this laboratory experiment, a supercritical airfoil model (such as the NASA SC(2)-0714) is tested in a slotted-wall transonic wind tunnel across Mach numbers from M_inf = 0.68 to 0.88. Measuring total drag on a high-precision force balance captures the sudden onset of drag divergence, extracting the cubic wave drag scaling constant k_w and verifying transonic area-rule and supercritical airfoil benefits.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What is the physical cause of transonic wave drag?

Transonic wave drag is caused by the formation of shock waves on the wing surface. When local flow becomes supersonic, it must terminate through a shock wave. The irreversible entropy generation across this shock wave dissipates mechanical energy, and the severe adverse pressure gradient thickens or separates the boundary layer, creating a massive pressure drag rise.

How is the drag divergence Mach number M_dd defined?

The drag divergence Mach number M_dd is defined by aerodynamic convention as the flight Mach number where the rate of drag increase with respect to Mach number reaches a specified threshold, typically dC_D / dM = 0.10 (or where C_D rises by 0.0020 above its subcritical baseline).

What are the design features of a supercritical airfoil?

A supercritical airfoil has a relatively flat upper surface (which weakens the suction peak and broadens the supersonic pocket, producing a weaker shock wave further aft), a large leading-edge radius, and pronounced aft camber (which restores lift lost from flattening the upper surface). These features delay M_dd by Mach 0.05 to 0.08.

What is the Whitcomb Area Rule and how does it reduce transonic wave drag?

Richard Whitcomb discovered that transonic wave drag depends primarily on the longitudinal distribution of the vehicle's total cross-sectional area. By contouring the fuselage (the 'wasp-waist' or coke-bottle fuselage) where the wings attach, the cross-sectional area distribution remains smooth, drastically reducing wave drag.

What happens to the standing shock wave on the wing as Mach number increases toward 1.0?

As free-stream Mach number increases, the supersonic zone expands and the shock wave strengthens and moves progressively aft toward the trailing edge. Near Mach 1.0, the shock reaches the trailing edge, forming a fish-tail shock pattern.

Why do slotted or perforated walls enable transonic wind tunnel testing?

In a solid-walled wind tunnel, shock waves reflect from the walls back onto the model, and the test section chokes near Mach 1. Slotted or perforated walls allow excess mass to vent into a surrounding plenum chamber, absorbing shock and expansion waves and eliminating wall reflections.

Experimental precautions and wind tunnel safety

  • Use slotted or perforated test section walls to prevent shock reflection back onto the model from the solid ceiling and floor.
  • Monitor wind tunnel model sting vibrations to prevent destructive transonic aileron/wing flutter.
  • Ensure moisture in the air supply is below 5 ppm to prevent condensation shock waves from contaminating aerodynamic drag data.
  • Verify that Reynolds number remains constant during Mach sweeps by adjusting wind tunnel total pressure P_0.

Theoretical assumptions & flow limits

  • Transonic wall interference corrections require complex slotted-wall porosity models.
  • Severe shock-induced boundary layer separation causes localized flow unsteadiness near the drag divergence boundary.
  • Assumes steady-state conditions; transonic buffet unsteadiness introduces minor RMS drag fluctuations.
  • Cubic scaling law is valid within the initial transonic drag rise region (M_crit < M < 1.05).
