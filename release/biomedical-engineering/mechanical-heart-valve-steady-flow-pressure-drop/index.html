Biomedical assumptions and boundary condition limits

Steady forward flow does not simulate transient valve opening/closing leaflet dynamics or dynamic regurgitation phases. Evaluated on a 25 mm bileaflet pyrolytic carbon mechanical aortic valve mounted in an acrylic aortic root chamber meeting ISO 5840 standards. Perfusion fluid is 36% glycerol blood analog (density ρ = 1055 kg/m³, viscosity 3.6 cP at 37 °C). Velocities v = 1.0, 1.2, 1.4, 1.6, 1.8, 2.0 m/s correspond to v² = 1.00, 1.44, 1.96, 2.56, 3.24, 4.00 m²/s². Quadratic Bernoulli slope k_v = 3.8500 mmHg/(m²/s²).

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Measure the transvalvular pressure gradient ΔP across a 25 mm bileaflet mechanical aortic heart valve under steady forward flow and evaluate the quadratic Bernoulli loss coefficient k_v. Standardized parameter settings: 1.0, 1.2, 1.4, 1.6, 1.8, 2.0 m/s.

01

Valve Mounting and Alignment Verification

Mount 25 mm mechanical valve securely in acrylic test chamber, ensuring leaflets open symmetrically without sewing ring impingement.

02

Perfusion Fluid Priming and Temperature Stabilization

Fill system with 36% glycerol-water blood analog, purge all trapped air from pressure lines, and heat reservoir to 37.0 °C.

03

Zero Differential Pressure Baseline

Open transducer bypass valve at zero flow to establish precise 0.00 mmHg baseline differential pressure.

04

Velocity Increment and Stabilization

Adjust centrifugal pump to establish transvalvular jet velocity v = 1.0 m/s (verified by flowmeter Q and effective orifice area) and record steady pressure drop ΔP.

05

Velocity Progression Sequence

Increment jet velocity through 1.2, 1.4, 1.6, 1.8, and 2.0 m/s, recording stabilized pressure gradient ΔP over 30 seconds at each step.

06

Bernoulli Quadratic Regression Analysis

Plot transvalvular pressure drop ΔP against v², compute the slope k_v via linear regression through the origin, and calculate effective orifice area EOA.
