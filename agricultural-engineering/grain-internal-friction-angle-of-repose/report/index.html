1. Title and aim

Static & Dynamic Angle of Repose and Grain-on-Surface Friction Coefficients. Measure the static and dynamic angle of repose of agricultural grains using a circular heaping platform and determine grain-on-metal sliding friction coefficients on a tilting plane.

4. Theory and graph choice

The angle of repose and surface friction coefficients of bulk granular agricultural products govern the structural design of storage silos, self-emptying hopper bottoms, grain chutes, conveyor spouts, and front-end loader buckets. The angle of repose θ is the maximum angle of slope at which loose, cohesionless bulk material will remain stationary without sliding down the conical pile face. It is directly related to the internal friction angle of the grain mass: tan θ = μ_internal. When grain is poured freely onto a circular horizontal platform with a retained boundary rim, the excess grain rolls over the edge, forming a stable natural cone of height H and base radius R such that H = R·tan θ. The dynamic (emptying) angle of repose is similarly evaluated by draining grain through an orifice at the bottom of a bin. Furthermore, the external friction coefficient μ_s = tan α (where α is the angle of inclination of a tilting steel plate at which grain begins to slide) defines the minimum valley angle required for self-cleaning gravity discharge hoppers (typically 40° to 50° for galvanized sheet steel). In this test, an adjustable circular platform and tilting friction plane evaluate repose geometry.

H = tan(θ) · R = 0.5774 · R

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents Platform base radius R (cm), and the dependent axis y represents Conical heap height H (cm). The gradient m directly determines Tangent of Angle of Repose tan(θ), while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Mount the 10.0 cm radius circular platform on the rigid test base, verifying horizontal level with a bubble spirit level. Center the discharge funnel 10 cm above the platform.
  2. Fill the funnel with clean wheat, release the bottom gate, and allow grain to pour continuously until excess grain rolls off all sides and a stable symmetrical cone is formed.
  3. Carefully lower the vernier height gauge point to touch the exact central apex of the conical grain pile, recording cone height H (cm).
  4. Repeat the conical formation on circular platforms of 15, 20, 25, 30, and 35 cm radius, measuring cone height H for each base radius.
  5. Place a bottomless wooden box filled with grain onto the horizontal galvanized steel plate, slowly elevating the plate with the lead screw until the box begins sliding, logging the friction angle α.
  6. Plot cone height H against radius R, perform linear regression through the origin, and determine the slope tan θ to calculate the angle of repose θ = arctan(slope).

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Pour grain smoothly without pausing; pulsating flow creates stair-stepped irregularities on the conical heap face.
  • Avoid vibrating the test bench; floor vibrations shake the heap, causing premature avalanching and falsely lowering the angle of repose.
  • Ensure the height gauge point touches the peak gently without plunging into the loose grain apex.
  • Clean the tilting friction plate with solvent before testing to remove oil films and dust.

Small laboratory platforms (R < 10 cm) exhibit edge effects where individual kernel size forms a significant fraction of radius. Evaluated using dry clean wheat grain on a circular retained platform (target angle of repose θ = 30.0°, tan 30° = 0.5774).
