Simulation assumptions and limits

The teaching model assumes an idealized planar simple pendulum with knife-edge suspension. In a physical laboratory, small corrections for the finite radius of the spherical bob, the non-zero mass of the suspension wire, ambient air buoyancy, and support stand compliance must be applied in ultra-high precision gravimetry. The coincidence method provides an extraordinary illustration of phase vernier metrology in experimental physics. By measuring the coincidence interval between the test pendulum and a standard master seconds clock, the period T is determined with a precision of one part in ten thousand, enabling an exceptionally accurate determination of the local acceleration due to gravity g.

Know your apparatus.

Precision Variable-Length Simple Pendulum

Comprising a dense turned brass spherical bob suspended by a fine unspun invar wire from a hardened agate knife-edge bearing, allowing strictly planar oscillation without torsional twisting or lateral wobble.

Standard Master Seconds Clock Pendulum

A temperature-compensated invar master pendulum maintaining a reference period of T₀ = 2.00000 s (frequency f₀ = 0.50000 Hz) with an illuminated vertical index mark.

Optical Slit Coincidence Viewing Telescope

Equipped with crosshairs and aligned with the common vertical equilibrium plane of both pendulums to observe the exact instant of transit coincidence without parallax error.

Precision Digital Lap-Timer / Chronometer

Resolving to 0.01 s to register the precise epochs of successive transit coincidences across multiple coincidence periods.

Precision Vertical Cathetometer / Steel Comparator Scale

Equipped with a vernier scale resolving to 0.05 mm to measure the true vertical distance from the knife-edge bearing axis to the center of mass of the spherical bob.

Rigid Anti-Vibration Wall-Bracket Stand

A massive cast-iron wall mount bolted to a load-bearing laboratory pillar to eliminate virtual lengthening caused by support stand compliance.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the local acceleration due to gravity g to high metrological precision by measuring the coincidence beat intervals between a variable-length simple pendulum and a standard seconds pendulum. Suggested independent settings: 70.0, 75.0, 80.0, 85.0, 90.0 cm.

1

Mounting and Crosshair Fiducial Alignment

Mount the test pendulum on the rigid wall-bracket stand and align the viewing telescope crosshair with the vertical equilibrium index mark shared by both the test pendulum and the master seconds pendulum.

Why this step matters

Precise optical alignment at the equilibrium line ensures that transit coincidences are observed at the exact moment of maximum bob velocity, minimizing trigger timing uncertainty.

2

Effective Length Cathetometer Measurement

Using the precision cathetometer, measure the total suspended length L from the knife-edge pivot line to the top of the spherical bob, add the bob radius r = d/2, and record the effective length L to the nearest 0.1 mm.

Why this step matters

The true pendulum length must extend from the rotational axis to the center of mass of the sphere; adding the radius r prevents systematic underestimation of L.

3

Synchronous Plane Swing Initiation

Set the master seconds pendulum into steady oscillation. Displace the test pendulum through a small angle (θ ≤ 2.5°) in the strictly planar direction of swing and release it smoothly from rest without lateral wobble.

Why this step matters

Restricting amplitude to ≤ 2.5° keeps circular arc period elongation below 0.012% and prevents coupling into lateral or torsional swinging modes.

4

Initial Coincidence Epoch Registration

Observe through the telescope as both pendulums oscillate. Note the initial transit coincidence where both wires simultaneously cross the crosshair traveling in the same direction (e.g. left to right), and start the chronometer.

Why this step matters

Triggering on simultaneous transits in the identical direction of swing defines phase difference zero (Δφ = 0 mod 2π), initiating the coincidence timing interval.

5

Consecutive Transit Counting and Interval Timing

Count the intervening swings and record the chronometer reading at the second, third, and fourth consecutive coincidences traveling in the same direction. Compute the mean coincidence interval t_c.

Why this step matters

Averaging across multiple coincidence epochs divides human stopwatch trigger latency by the large number of elapsed oscillation cycles, boosting precision by two orders of magnitude.

6

Period Calculation and Regression Verification

Calculate the high-precision period T = (T₀ · t_c) / (t_c + T₀). Repeat the procedure across five distinct pendulum lengths L from 70.0 cm to 90.0 cm, construct a plot of T² versus L, and determine the acceleration of gravity g from the regression slope.

Why this step matters

The linear regression of T² against L isolates g = 4π²/S, eliminating any constant systematic length offset and providing rigorous metrological validation.
