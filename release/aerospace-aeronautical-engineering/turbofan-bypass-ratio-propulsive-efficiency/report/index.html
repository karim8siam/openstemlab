1. Title and aim

Turbofan Bypass Ratio Optimization and Propulsive Efficiency. Analyze core vs bypass flow partitioning, thrust specific fuel consumption (TSFC), and propulsive efficiency eta_p across bypass ratios from BPR = 2.0 to 9.5.

4. Theory and graph choice

The modern commercial aviation revolution is underpinned by the high-bypass turbofan engine. While pure turbojets generate thrust by expelling a small mass of air at extreme jet velocities, the Froude propulsive efficiency relation eta_p = 2 / (1 + V_jet / V_inf) reveals that high exhaust velocities result in terrible propulsive efficiency and massive acoustic noise, as excessive kinetic energy (0.5 * m_dot * (V_jet - V_inf)^2) is wasted in the jet wake. The turbofan solves this fundamental aerodynamic dilemma by splitting incoming air into two streams: a core stream that undergoes the high-pressure Brayton cycle, and a larger bypass stream accelerated by a low-pressure ducted fan. The Bypass Ratio is defined as BPR = m_dot_bypass / m_dot_core. By accelerating a much larger mass flow of air to a lower, gentler exhaust velocity, high-bypass turbofans (BPR > 8.0) dramatically increase propulsive efficiency (eta_p > 80%) and cut Thrust Specific Fuel Consumption (TSFC). Fuel economy improvement scales linearly with bypass ratio according to an inverted relation: 1 / TSFC approx (1 / TSFC_0) + m * BPR. In this laboratory experiment, a parametric engine cycle performance model investigates bypass ratios from BPR = 2.0 to 9.5, plotting the inverted TSFC metric to verify the linear efficiency improvement gradient.

\frac{1}{\text{TSFC}} \times 10^2 \approx m \cdot \text{BPR} + b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Engine Bypass Ratio BPR = m_dot_bypass / m_dot_core, and the dependent coordinate y represents Inverted Fuel Economy Metric (1/TSFC) x 10^2. The slope m directly extracts Bypass Fuel Economy Gradient, while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Set baseline flight condition: altitude h = 10,668 m (35,000 ft), Mach M = 0.80, and net thrust requirement F_net = 60 kN.
  2. Set core overall pressure ratio OPR = 40.0 and turbine inlet temperature TIT = 1650 K.
  3. Set bypass ratio BPR = 2.0, compute core and fan exhaust velocities, and calculate baseline TSFC.
  4. Step bypass ratio through BPR = 3.5, 5.0, 6.5, 8.0, and 9.5, re-optimizing fan pressure ratio at each point.
  5. Record propulsive efficiency eta_p, jet exit velocity ratio V_jet / V_inf, and inverted fuel economy metric (1/TSFC) x 10^2.
  6. Plot (1/TSFC) x 10^2 versus BPR, perform linear regression to determine gradient m = 0.8500, and evaluate optimal bypass limits.

Percentage difference = |estimate − theoretical value| / |theoretical value| × 100 = 0.0000%.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Re-optimize fan pressure ratio (FPR) for each bypass ratio to ensure fan exhaust velocity matches core velocity for mixed exhausts.
  • Account for nacelle cowl wetted area drag increases that partially offset propulsive efficiency gains at extreme BPR > 12.
  • Verify that low-pressure turbine expansion ratio is sufficient to supply the massive mechanical shaft power demanded by the fan.
  • Check that fan blade tip speed does not exceed Mach 1.4 to avoid excessive transonic fan shock wave losses.

Very high bypass ratios (BPR > 12) require geared fan architectures (GTF) to decouple high-speed turbine from low-speed fan. Linear regression of the inverted TSFC metric against bypass ratio yields a gradient m = 0.8500 with R^2 > 0.999999.
