Ramjet Aerodynamics, Supersonic Inlet Recovery, and Rayleigh Line Heating. Evaluate supersonic inlet shock recovery eta_r, combustor Rayleigh flow heat addition, and static pressure drop across simulated flight Mach numbers from 2.0 to 4.5.

4. Theory and graph choice

The ramjet represents the purest and most mechanically elegant air-breathing propulsion system, eliminating all rotating turbomachinery (compressors and turbines) in favor of aerodynamic 'ram' compression. At high supersonic flight speeds (Mach 2.5 to 5.0), the massive kinetic energy of oncoming airflow is compressed through a series of external and internal oblique shock waves in a supersonic inlet diffuser, raising air pressure and temperature by an order of magnitude. The compressed air enters a combustion chamber at subsonic speed, where hydrocarbon or hydrogen fuel is injected and burned continuously. In 1D compressible gasdynamics, constant-area heat addition to a flowing gas is governed by Rayleigh line flow. The conservation equations dictate that adding heat (increasing stagnation temperature T_0) to a subsonic flow accelerates the fluid toward sonic speed (dM > 0), causing static pressure to drop according to P_2 / P_1 approx 1 - m * (Delta T_0 / T_01). If too much heat is added, the flow chokes at the combustor exit (M_2 = 1)—a condition known as thermal choking, which forces the inlet shock system to unstart and expel a violent bow shock out the front. In this laboratory experiment, an aerothermodynamic ramjet simulator evaluates combustor static pressure drop across heat addition parameters Delta T_0 / T_0 from 0.6 to 3.0, extracting the Rayleigh pressure loss coefficient.

\left(1 - \frac{P_2}{P_1}\right) = m \cdot \left(\frac{\Delta T_0}{T_{01}}\right) + b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Combustor Heat Addition Parameter Delta T_0 / T_01, and the dependent coordinate y represents Static Pressure Drop Fraction 1 - P_2/P_1. The slope m directly extracts Rayleigh Pressure Loss Coefficient, while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Start facility air heaters, establishing vitiated airflow at stagnation temperature T_01 = 600 K and Mach M_inlet = 3.0.
  2. Verify through Schlieren optics that the multi-shock system is swallowed and started, delivering subsonic flow (M_1 = 0.25) to the combustor.
  3. Record baseline static pressure P_1 at the combustor entrance under zero fuel flow conditions.
  4. Inject fuel to achieve heat addition parameters Delta T_0 / T_01 = 0.6, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, and 3.0.
  5. Sample downstream static pressure P_2 at each heat addition plateau, computing pressure drop fraction 1 - P_2/P_1.
  6. Plot (1 - P_2/P_1) versus Delta T_0 / T_01, perform linear regression to determine slope m, and evaluate proximity to thermal choking.

Percentage difference = |estimate − theoretical value| / |theoretical value| × 100 = 0.0000%.

For n = 6 observations, Σresidual² = 7.126e-33; s² = Σresidual²/(n − 2). Gradient standard error = √[s²/Σ(x − x̄)²] = 2.076e-17. Intercept standard error = √[s²(1/n + x̄²/Σ(x − x̄)²)] = 4.052e-17.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Monitor combustor exit Mach number to ensure heat addition does not trigger thermal choking (M_2 < 0.90).
  • Maintain water cooling on combustor duct walls to prevent thermal structural burn-through during sustained burning.
  • Verify fast-acting nitrogen purge system is armed to extinguish combustion within 100 milliseconds upon inlet unstart.
  • Wear thermal and acoustic personal protective equipment during high-pressure burner firing.

1D Rayleigh flow assumes frictionless, constant-area duct flow; real combustors experience wall friction and minor divergence. Linear regression of the static pressure drop fraction against the heat addition parameter yields a Rayleigh slope of 0.1800 with R^2 > 0.999999.
