FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the hydraulic conductivity (coefficient of permeability, k) of coarse-grained cohesionless granular soils under steady-state laminar seepage using the constant-head permeameter according to ASTM D2434 / AASHTO T215. Standardized parameter settings: 10.0, 20.0, 30.0, 40.0, 50.0, 60.0 cm.

01

Permeameter Cell Assembly & Sand Placement

Place lower porous stone into permeameter cell; pour oven-dried clean sand in uniform layers using a funnel; tamp gently to target density; seat upper porous stone; record specimen length L and area A.

02

De-Aired Water Saturation & Bubble Purging

Connect bottom inflow port to de-aired water supply; allow water to percolate upward slowly under low head to displace all entrapped pore air; purge manometer lines.

03

Constant-Head Reservoir Elevation

Adjust overhead water tank height to establish initial hydraulic head difference Δh = 10.0 cm between manometer ports; allow steady seepage to establish for 5 minutes.

04

Volumetric Flow Rate Measurement

Place dry graduated cylinder under the effluent outlet; start stopwatch simultaneously; collect outflow for 60 seconds; record collected volume V (cm³); calculate Q = V / t.

05

Head Differential Sweep

Increase reservoir elevation to establish head differentials of 20.0, 30.0, 40.0, 50.0, and 60.0 cm; record steady discharge flow rate Q at each plateau.

06

Temperature Correction & Permeability Calculation

Measure effluent water temperature T; plot Q versus Δh; evaluate slope to compute hydraulic conductivity k_T and standardize to 20 °C (k_20).

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

Hydraulic conductivity (permeability, k) is the primary geotechnical transport property governing groundwater seepage beneath earth dams, dewatering rates for deep foundation excavations, drainage through highway base courses, and consolidation settlement kinetics in saturated soil deposits. Formulated empirically by Henry Darcy in 1856 through public water fountain filtration experiments in Dijon, France, Darcy's law states that the apparent discharge velocity v of fluid through a porous medium is directly proportional to the hydraulic gradient i: v = k · i, where i = Δh / L (head loss per unit seepage length). The volumetric discharge rate Q through soil of cross-sectional area A is Q = k · i · A = k · (Δh / L) · A. Under ASTM D2434, the constant-head permeameter test is specifically designed for coarse-grained permeable soils (hydraulic conductivity k > 10^-4 cm/s, such as sands and gravels). A cylindrical soil specimen is held in a transparent permeameter cell connected to an adjustable constant-head reservoir with an overflow weir that maintains an invariant hydraulic head difference Δh. Manometer piezometer ports tapped along the specimen length monitor internal head loss across a known spacing L. In this laboratory, discharge flow rate Q is measured across head differentials from 10 to 60 cm to verify Darcy's linear seepage law and determine hydraulic conductivity k.
