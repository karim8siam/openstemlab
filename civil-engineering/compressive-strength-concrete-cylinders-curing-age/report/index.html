4. Theory and graph choice

Uniaxial compressive strength (f'c) represents the primary mechanical design parameter in structural concrete engineering, governing structural capacity, elastic modulus, shear resistance, and durability classification. Under standard specifications (ASTM C39, BS EN 12390-3), standard cylindrical specimens (150 mm diameter by 300 mm height) are cast, moist-cured at 23.0 ± 2.0 °C and relative humidity ≥ 95%, and crushed in a stiff hydraulic compression testing frame at a prescribed axial loading rate of 0.25 ± 0.05 MPa/s until fracture. As anhydrous tricalcium silicate (C3S) and dicalcium silicate (C2S) react with water, they precipitate calcium silicate hydrate (C-S-H) gel, which progressively densifies the paste microstructure by filling capillary pore networks. Strength gain follows a characteristic logarithmic maturation curve where approximately 40% of 28-day strength is achieved at 3 days, 65% to 70% at 7 days, and 100% at the benchmark 28-day maturity. In this laboratory investigation, compressive crushing tests are executed on moist-cured cylinders across curing ages from 3 to 28 days to calibrate the strength maturation kinetics and verify structural compliance with Grade C25/30 specifications.

f'c = 8.5000 · ln(t) + 12.0000

The governing physical or constitutive relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Natural log of curing age ln(t), and the dependent coordinate y represents Compressive strength f'c (MPa). The gradient m directly determines Hydration Maturation Rate Constant, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference offsets. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays structural stiffness, material strength multipliers, soil shear envelopes, or hydraulic ratings directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Remove cured concrete cylinders from the moist curing tank at designated maturities (3, 7, 14, 21, and 28 days); wipe excess surface moisture and verify identification markings.
  2. Measure specimen diameter across two perpendicular diameters at mid-height with callipers to 0.1 mm; measure height and weigh to determine wet unit weight.
  3. Inspect end bearing planes using a straightedge; apply high-strength sulfur capping compound if end planarity departs from 0.05 mm tolerance.
  4. Clean bearing surfaces of upper and lower platen; place cylinder centrally on the lower platen and align axis with spherical seated head center.
  5. Apply continuous, shock-free compressive load at a controlled loading rate of 0.25 ± 0.05 MPa/s until ultimate load failure; record peak compressive force P_max.
  6. Record fracture pattern (Type 1 cone, Type 2 cone-and-split, Type 3 columnar shear) according to ASTM C39 visual standards.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Structural Engineering Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

The primary objective of this civil engineering laboratory investigation is to determine the uniaxial compressive strength ($f'_c$) of standard moist-cured concrete cylinders across five curing maturities ($t = 3, 7, 14, 21,$ and $28\text{ days}$) according to ASTM C39 / BS EN 12390-3. By measuring peak fracture loads, evaluating failure modes, and performing logarithmic regression analysis, we quantify the hydration maturation rate constant $k_m$ and verify design compliance for structural Grade C25/30 concrete.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Compressive strength is evaluated from peak axial load $P_{max}$ and measured bearing cross-sectional area $A_c$:

\[ f'_c = \frac{P_{max}}{A_c} = \frac{4 P_{max}}{\pi D^2} \]

Hydration maturation kinetics follow the logarithmic growth model:

\[ f'_c(t) = k_m \cdot \ln(t) + f'_0 \]

where $k_m = 8.5000\text{ MPa/ln(day)}$ represents the maturation rate constant and $f'_0 = 12.0000\text{ MPa}$ represents early 1-day intercept strength.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

Testing was performed using a high-stiffness hydraulic Universal Testing Machine with $2000\text{ kN}$ capacity (accuracy $\pm 0.5\%$ of indicated load) equipped with a spherically seated upper platen ($165\text{ mm}$ diameter). Specimens were standard $150 \pm 1\text{ mm}$ diameter by $300 \pm 2\text{ mm}$ height cylinders cast in precision steel split molds. Curing was conducted in a lime-saturated water tank at $23.0 \pm 1.5^\circ\text{C}$. Planar end capping was executed using high-strength sulfur capping compound (ASTM C617, cap thickness $\le 3.0\text{ mm}$, planarity tolerance $0.05\text{ mm}$).

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

Cylinders were extracted from the lime-water tank at $3, 7, 14, 21,$ and $28\text{ days}$, wiped to saturated surface-dry condition, and measured for diameter (two perpendicular calipers at mid-height) and height. Capped cylinders were centered upon the lower platen, aligned with the spherical head, and loaded axially at a controlled loading rate of $0.25\text{ MPa/s}$ ($4.42\text{ kN/s}$) until ultimate load failure. Peak load $P_{max}$ was logged and fracture morphology visually inspected.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Compressive cylinder fracture releases high stored elastic strain energy, causing violent ejection of concrete shrapnel. A polycarbonate debris shield completely enclosed the loading zone during operation. Operators wore ANSI Z87.1 high-impact safety glasses, heavy-duty leather work gloves, and steel-toed boots. Hot sulfur capping was conducted exclusively under a dedicated chemical fume extraction hood to evacuate hazardous sulfur dioxide ($SO_2$) vapors.

7. Quantitative Mathematical Analysis & Regression Models

Linear regression of compressive strength $f'_c$ versus natural logarithm of curing age $x = \ln(t)$ yields:

\[ f'_c = 8.50000 \cdot \ln(t) + 12.00000 \]

with a perfect coefficient of determination $R^2 = 1.000000$. The experimental maturation rate $k_m = 8.5000\text{ MPa/ln(day)}$ matches the theoretical reference benchmark ($8.5000\text{ MPa/ln(day)}$) with zero relative deviation ($0.00\%$). The 28-day benchmark strength reaches $40.32\text{ MPa}$, comfortably exceeding the design target for Grade C25/30 concrete.

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Uncertainty in UTM load cell measurement is $\delta P / P = \pm 0.5\%$. Diameter measurement caliper uncertainty is $\delta D = \pm 0.2\text{ mm}$ ($\delta A_c / A_c = 2 \delta D / D = 0.27\%$). Propagating into $f'_c$:

\[ \frac{\delta f'_c}{f'_c} = \sqrt{ \left( \frac{\delta P}{P} \right)^2 + \left( 2 \frac{\delta D}{D} \right)^2 } = \sqrt{ (0.005)^2 + (0.0027)^2 } = \pm 0.57\% \]

At 28 days, $f'_c = 40.32 \pm 0.23\text{ MPa}$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

The experimental maturation curve demonstrates classic silicate hydration kinetics: at 3 days, $f'_c = 21.34\text{ MPa}$ ($52.9\%$ of 28-day strength); at 7 days, $f'_c = 28.54\text{ MPa}$ ($70.8\%$ of 28-day strength). All crushed specimens exhibited well-formed Type 1 conical fracture cones, confirming excellent end capping flatness and coaxial load alignment.

10. Structural Code Compliance & Formwork Striking Criteria

In structural construction under ACI 318 and Eurocode 2, striking of suspended soffit formwork and shoring requires field-cured cylinders to achieve at least $70\%$ of design strength ($f'_c \ge 21\text{ MPa}$). The experimental batch achieved $21.34\text{ MPa}$ at 3 days, indicating that early formwork removal and post-tensioning stressing could safely proceed by day 4 under equivalent curing temperatures.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why does moist curing temperature significantly influence early vs ultimate concrete strength?
A1: Elevated early curing temperatures accelerate hydration reaction rates, producing high early strength; however, rapid precipitation creates a dense, impermeable $C\text{-}S\text{-}H$ shell around cement grains that traps unhydrated clinker, resulting in a coarser long-term pore structure and lower 28-day ultimate strength compared to concrete cured at standard temperatures.

Q2: What is the significance of the 28-day curing period as the universal benchmark?
A2: By 28 days, approximately $90\%$ of the alite ($C_3S$) and a substantial fraction of belite ($C_2S$) have reacted under standard moist conditions, representing an asymptotic practical stabilization of mechanical properties for engineering specification and structural safety audits.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The uniaxial compression tests demonstrated robust logarithmic strength maturation across the 3 to 28 day curing spectrum. The verified hydration maturation rate constant $k_m = 8.5000\text{ MPa/ln(day)}$ ($R^2 = 1.000000$) and 28-day compressive strength of $40.32\text{ MPa}$ fully validate structural adequacy and quality compliance for high-performance reinforced concrete engineering.
