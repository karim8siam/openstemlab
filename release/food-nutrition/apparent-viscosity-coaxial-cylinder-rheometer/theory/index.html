Thermodynamic, Kinetic & Biochemical Mechanisms

Biochemical and biophysical transitions in foods—such as the thermal denaturation of globular proteins, the enzymatic hydrolysis of polysaccharides by amylases, the free-radical propagation of lipid hydroperoxides, and the phase separation of emulsion droplets—are governed by fundamental thermodynamic and kinetic laws. Reaction rates frequently follow Arrhenius temperature dependence and Michaelis-Menten substrate kinetics, while extraction yields depend on partition coefficients, mass transfer diffusion, and solvent polarity gradients. By systematically controlling the independent variable Shear Rate, food scientists can map kinetic pathways, evaluate processing efficiency, and establish critical shelf-life thresholds.

Experimental precautions and laboratory safety

  • Maintain constant analytical laboratory temperature (20.0 ± 1.0 °C) and protect light-sensitive reagents from ambient photo-oxidation.
  • Inspect all volumetric glassware, crucibles, and reaction vessels for chemical residues, chips, or moisture before commencing assays.
  • Use analytical reagent grade (AR) chemicals, fresh deionized water (18.2 MΩ·cm), and standardized titrants calibrated against primary standards.
  • Wear protective laboratory coats, chemical splash goggles, and nitrile gloves during acid digestions and solvent reflux procedures.

Theoretical assumptions & boundary condition limits
