4. Theory and graph choice

Physical and gravimetric properties of agricultural seeds—including bulk density (test weight), true particle density, porosity, thousand-grain weight (TGW), and sphericity—are primary engineering parameters in bulk storage silo sizing, aeration resistance modeling, commercial grain grading, and pneumatic handling. Bulk density ρ_b is the mass of grain divided by the total volume occupied including inter-granular air voids, measured in standard bushel testers or Winchester volume cylinders. True density ρ_t is the density of the individual grain kernel excluding interstitial pores, measured via gas pycnometry or liquid displacement using non-wetting fluids (toluene or kerosene). Inter-granular porosity ε = (1 - ρ_b / ρ_t) × 100% defines the void fraction available for airflow during aeration and drying. Sphericity Φ = (L·W·T)^(1/3) / L expresses the degree to which an ellipsoidal grain kernel resembles a true sphere. In this laboratory investigation, standardized volumetric measures quantify the linear relationship m_b = ρ_b·V_b across graduated volumes from 100 to 500 mL.

m_b = ρ_b · V_b = 0.7850 · V_b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents Bulk volume V_b (mL), and the dependent axis y represents Bulk grain mass m_b (g). The gradient m directly determines Bulk Density ρ_b, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Position the standard filling funnel 5.0 cm above the graduated cylinder, ensuring the discharge shutter is centered and level.
  2. Pour wheat into the funnel, open the shutter to allow free-fall filling into the 100 mL cylinder until overflowing, and strike off excess grain level with the rim using a wooden straightedge.
  3. Weigh the filled cylinder, subtract tare mass, and record bulk grain mass m_b (g) for V_b = 100.0 mL.
  4. Repeat standardized filling across cylinders of 180, 260, 340, 420, and 500 mL, recording mass m_b for each volume.
  5. Place 50 g of wheat into the helium pycnometer to measure true volume and calculate true density ρ_t, then compute porosity ε = (1 - ρ_b / ρ_t) × 100%.
  6. Plot bulk mass m_b versus volume V_b, perform linear regression through the origin, and determine the bulk density slope ρ_b.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Always use a standard funnel with uniform drop height; shaking or tapping the cylinder compacts grain and falsely elevates bulk density by up to 8%.
  • Use a single, smooth stroke of the straightedge held at a 45° angle to strike off grain flush with the container rim.
  • Ensure seed samples are clean and free of chaff, broken kernels, and weed seeds before density measurements.
  • Verify balance calibration using certified analytical check weights before each run.

Laboratory small-cylinder bulk densities are slightly lower than deep silo densities where thousands of tons of overburden compact the grain. Evaluated using clean seed wheat at 12.5% moisture (true kernel density ρ_t = 1.340 g/mL, porosity ε = 41.4%) across standardized graduated cylinders.
