FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the workability, rheological consistency, and slump loss of freshly mixed Portland cement concrete under variable water-cement ratios (w/c) using the standard Abrams slump cone according to ASTM C143 / BS EN 12350-2. Standardized parameter settings: 0.4, 0.45, 0.5, 0.55, 0.6, 0.65 ratio.

01

Apparatus Cleaning & Moisten Conditioning

Clean interior surface of the slump cone and base plate thoroughly; moisten with a damp sponge to remove surface dust and eliminate dry suction absorption.

02

Base Plate Positioning & Clamping

Place the cone firmly on the level base plate, standing on the foot rests to ensure a rigid seal and prevent lateral movement during rodding.

03

First Layer Filling & Rodding

Fill the cone to approximately one-third its volume (depth of ~70 mm); rod uniformly with 25 strokes distributed evenly over the cross section.

04

Second & Third Layer Compaction

Fill the second layer to two-thirds volume (~155 mm) and rod 25 times penetrating into bottom layer; fill third layer overflowing the top, rod 25 times penetrating second layer.

05

Strike-Off & Vertical Mold Extraction

Strike off excess concrete flush with the top rim using the tamping rod with a rolling screeding motion; clean spilled concrete from base plate; lift cone vertically in 5 ± 2 seconds without rotational jerking.

06

Slump Measurement & Inspection

Invert cone next to concrete mass; lay tamping rod across cone top and measure vertical distance to the displaced center of the slumped mass; record slump type (true, shear, collapse).

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

Workability is among the most vital rheological properties of freshly mixed concrete, defining the ease, homogeneity, and cohesiveness with which concrete can be mixed, transported, placed, compacted, and finished without destructive honeycombing or segregation. Developed by Duff Abrams in the early 20th century, the slump test employs a truncated conical mold (100 mm top diameter, 200 mm base diameter, and 300 mm height). When fresh concrete is filled in three equal layers, consolidated with 25 standard strokes of a 16 mm bullet-nosed tamping rod, and the mold is smoothly raised vertically in 5 ± 2 seconds, the unsupported concrete mass subsides under its own self-weight. The vertical drop between the original top surface and the displaced centroid defines the slump S. In structural civil engineering, slump classifications dictate placement applications: true slump (25 to 100 mm) for heavily reinforced foundations and columns, shear slump indicating plastic collapse or lack of cohesion, and collapse slump (>150 mm) observed in superplasticized self-consolidating mixes. In this laboratory investigation, fresh concrete batches with identical aggregate proportions are tested across water-cement ratios from 0.40 to 0.65 to establish the linear workability response and calibrate water-demand sensitivity.
