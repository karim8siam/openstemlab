1. Title and Objective

One-Compartment Intravenous Pharmacokinetics: Elimination Constant (ke) & Half-Life (t1/2). Model intravenous bolus drug disposition using monoexponential decay to derive elimination rate constant (ke), biological half-life (t1/2), and apparent volume of distribution (Vd). This formal laboratory report documents experimental data acquisition, physiological telemetry calibration, linear regression analysis, biomedical parameter estimation, and physical model verification in accordance with standard preclinical medical education protocols.

2. Theoretical Principles

Basic Medical Sciences investigates human organ systems and cellular mechanics through objective quantitative measurement under tightly regulated preclinical laboratory conditions. In Histopathology, Preclinical Pharmacology & Pharmacokinetics, physiological function is governed by physicochemical laws: membrane transport potentials, enzyme catalysis, hemodynamics, and gas exchange dynamics. By systematically perturbing the independent parameter—Post-Injection Elapsed Time (hr)—this investigation measures corresponding variations in Natural Log Plasma Drug Concentration ln(Cp).

Under steady-state laboratory conditions, the biophysical response follows the calibrated regression function: ln(Cp) = -0.2166 · t + 4.6052. The primary analytical objective is the empirical determination of First-Order Elimination Rate Constant (ke), with an accepted physiological reference benchmark of 0.2166 hr^-1.

9. Sources of Experimental Error and Mitigation

  • Thermal Fluctuations: Ambient temperature shifts alter enzyme kinetics and gas solubility. Mitigation: Thermostatically regulated water baths (37.0 ± 0.1 °C).
  • Pipetting and Dilution Errors: Volumetric meniscus reading errors. Mitigation: Use of calibrated positive-displacement micro-pipettes with verified tips.
  • Photometric & Optical Cuvette Scratches: Optical scattering increases baseline absorbance. Mitigation: Optical-grade quartz cuvettes inspected and cleaned with lens paper.
  • Biological Specimen Degradation: Proteolytic enzyme degradation or cell lysis over time. Mitigation: Keeping biological reagents on ice prior to assay and completing trials within 2 hours of preparation.
