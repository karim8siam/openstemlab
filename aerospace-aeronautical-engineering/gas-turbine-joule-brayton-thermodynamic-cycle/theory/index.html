Aerodynamic and physical foundations of Turbojet Brayton Cycle.

The modern aviation gas turbine operates on the continuous, open-circuit Joule-Brayton thermodynamic cycle, converting the chemical energy of aviation fuel into high-velocity kinetic energy or shaft power. Ambient air enters through an inlet diffuser (1-2), is compressed through an axial or centrifugal compressor (2-3), mixes with fuel and combusts at near-constant pressure in the combustion chamber (3-4), expands through a turbine that extracts shaft work to drive the compressor (4-5), and finally accelerates through an exhaust nozzle to produce thrust (5-8). In an ideal isentropic cycle, thermal efficiency is governed solely by the overall compressor pressure ratio: eta_th = 1 - pi_c^(-(gamma-1)/gamma). In real engines, aerodynamic losses in turbomachinery are characterized by isentropic component efficiencies (compressor efficiency eta_c approx 0.85 to 0.90, turbine efficiency eta_t approx 0.88 to 0.92). The real specific compressor work required scales directly with the compressor pressure parameter: w_c = c_p * T_01 * (pi_c^((gamma-1)/gamma) - 1) / eta_c. In this laboratory experiment, a gas turbine test bench instrumented with stagnation pressure probes and thermocouple rakes operates across pressure ratio settings from pi_c^0.286 - 1 = 0.45 to 1.40. Measuring specific compressor work confirms the thermodynamic energy balance.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What are the four fundamental thermodynamic processes in the ideal Brayton cycle?

1. Isentropic compression in the intake/compressor (1-2); 2. Isobaric heat addition in the combustor (2-3); 3. Isentropic expansion in the turbine and nozzle (3-4); and 4. Isobaric heat rejection to the ambient atmosphere (4-1).

How do turbomachinery component efficiencies (eta_c and eta_t) degrade the real Brayton cycle?

Viscous friction, shock losses, and boundary layer separation in compressor and turbine blades generate internal entropy. Real compression requires more work than isentropic compression (w_c = w_c,s / eta_c), and real turbine expansion produces less work (w_t = eta_t * w_t,s), dramatically reducing net work and thermal efficiency.

Why does increasing the overall pressure ratio (OPR) increase gas turbine thermal efficiency?

In the Brayton cycle, thermal efficiency is eta_th = 1 - (1 / pi_c)^((gamma-1)/gamma). Higher compressor pressure ratios compress air to higher temperatures before combustion, allowing heat addition to occur at higher average thermodynamic temperatures, which fundamentally improves Carnot conversion efficiency.

What limits the maximum allowable temperature at the turbine inlet (TIT)?

Turbine Inlet Temperature (TIT) is strictly limited by the high-temperature creep, thermal fatigue, and oxidation limits of first-stage turbine blade nickel-based superalloys. Modern engines use single-crystal superalloys with internal serpentine cooling channels and ceramic thermal barrier coatings (TBC) to tolerate gas temperatures above 1800 K.

What is the work-balance requirement between the compressor and turbine on a single-shaft turbojet?

In steady-state operation, the work output of the turbine must exactly equal the work absorbed by the compressor plus mechanical bearing friction: m_dot_air * w_c = (m_dot_air + m_dot_fuel) * w_t * eta_mech. The turbine extracts just enough energy to drive the compressor, leaving remaining enthalpy to produce high-velocity jet thrust.

What is the difference between thermal efficiency, propulsive efficiency, and overall efficiency?

Thermal efficiency eta_th is the ratio of net mechanical kinetic energy generated to chemical fuel energy burned. Propulsive efficiency eta_p is the ratio of useful aircraft thrust work (T * V) to the jet kinetic energy produced. Overall efficiency is the product: eta_overall = eta_th * eta_p.

Experimental precautions and wind tunnel safety

  • Monitor Turbine Inlet Temperature (TIT / T_04) continuously to ensure it never exceeds the turbine blade metallurgical limit (1050 K).
  • Verify emergency fuel shut-off solenoid operates within 50 milliseconds upon over-temperature or over-speed trigger.
  • Maintain sound-attenuated test cell ventilation and fire suppression systems during engine runs.
  • Allow the engine to idle for 3 minutes before shutdown to cool turbine bearings and prevent oil coking.

Theoretical assumptions & flow limits

  • Combustion pressure loss (Delta P_b / P_03 approx 3-5%) introduces slight non-ideal deviation from isobaric combustion.
  • Specific heat c_p increases at high combustion temperatures (c_p,gas approx 1.15 kJ/kg-K), requiring variable-gamma calculations aloft.
  • Turbine blade cooling bleed air leeches approximately 5-10% of compressor mass flow on full-scale engines.
  • Assumes steady-state thermal equilibrium at each throttle test point.
