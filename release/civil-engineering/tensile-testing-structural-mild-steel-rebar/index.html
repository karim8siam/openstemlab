FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the engineering stress-strain curve, Young's modulus of elasticity (E), upper and lower yield strength (f_y), ultimate tensile strength (f_u), and percentage elongation of structural reinforcing steel according to ASTM A615 / ASTM E8. Standardized parameter settings: 200.0, 400.0, 600.0, 800.0, 1000.0, 1200.0 µε.

01

Specimen Inspection & Gauge Marking

Inspect No. 5 (16 mm) rebar for surface defects; measure nominal diameter across ribs; mark 200 mm initial gauge length L_0 using center punch jig.

02

Cross-Sectional Area Determination

Weigh a 1000 mm length of rebar to compute mass per meter and determine nominal cross-sectional area A_0 according to ASTM A615.

03

UTM Clamping & Extensometer Mounting

Mount specimen into hydraulic wedge grips; ensure vertical coaxial alignment; clip electronic extensometer onto the central gauge section and zero software readings.

04

Monotonic Tensile Loading in Elastic Domain

Apply axial tension at a strain-controlled rate of 0.001 mm/mm/min; record simultaneous load P and strain ε across 200, 400, 600, 800, 1000, and 1200 µε.

05

Yield & Strain Hardening Progression

Continue loading through yield plateau; remove extensometer prior to necking to prevent sensor damage; record yield force P_y and ultimate force P_u.

06

Fracture Assembly & Ductility Characterization

Remove fractured bar halves; fit tightly together; measure final gauge length L_f to calculate percentage elongation and neck diameter d_f to determine reduction of area.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

Structural steel constitutes the tensile reinforcing backbone of modern reinforced and prestressed concrete infrastructure, providing ductility, energy absorption, and catastrophic collapse prevention. Standard specification ASTM A615 / ASTM E8 requires deformed reinforcing bars (rebars) to be tested in uniaxial tension on an automated Universal Testing Machine (UTM) to verify mechanical grade compliance. As uniaxial tensile force P increases on a coupon of initial gauge length L_0 and nominal cross-sectional area A_0, the bar responds through four classical behavioral regimes: (1) Linear elastic deformation governed by Hooke's law (σ = E · ε), where atomic iron bonds stretch reversibly; (2) Plastic yielding at yield strength f_y, characterized by the propagation of Lüders slip bands across the crystal lattice at nearly constant stress; (3) Strain hardening up to ultimate tensile strength f_u as dislocation tangles multiply and impede crystal slip; and (4) Localized necking followed by cup-and-cone shear ductile fracture. In this laboratory investigation, precision extensometers monitor strain in the linear elastic regime across microstrain levels from 200 to 1200 µε to calibrate Young's modulus E and evaluate Grade 60 (420 MPa) rebar properties.
