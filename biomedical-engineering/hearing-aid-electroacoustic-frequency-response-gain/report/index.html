1. Title and aim

Digital Hearing Aid Electroacoustic 2-cc Coupler Sound Pressure Gain. Determine the linear acoustic gain and input-output transfer slope k_g of a digital hearing aid in an ANSI S3.22 standardized 2-cc acoustic coupler across input sound pressure levels.

4. Theory and graph choice

Sensorineural hearing loss affects hundreds of millions of individuals worldwide, degrading speech intelligibility, cognitive function, and social interaction. Modern digital hearing aids are sophisticated audio-processing bio-acoustic computers that capture acoustic waves via MEMS microphones, digitize the signal with 24-bit audio converters, apply multi-channel dynamic range compression (WDRC) and acoustic feedback cancellation on a low-power digital signal processor (DSP), and deliver amplified sound into the external ear canal via a balanced armature receiver. To objectively evaluate the electroacoustic performance of hearing aids independent of individual patient ear canal anatomy, the American National Standards Institute established the standard specification ANSI/ASA S3.22 ('Specification of Hearing Aid Characteristics'). The international standard specifies measuring acoustic output inside an HA-1 or HA-2 standardized 2-cc acoustic coupler (which replicates the average 2.0 mL acoustic volume and impedance of an adult human ear canal occluded by an earmold). In the linear amplification region below the wide dynamic range compression knee-point (typically 50 to 75 dB SPL input), the output sound pressure level SPL_out scales linearly with input sound level SPL_in: SPL_out = k_g · SPL_in + Gain_0, where k_g is the input-output acoustic transfer slope (approximately 0.95 dB/dB in linear mode) and Gain_0 represents baseline acoustic gain (~28.5 dB at 1.0 kHz). In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented anechoic hearing aid test box evaluates a digital behind-the-ear (BTE) hearing aid across input sound pressure levels from 50 to 75 dB SPL.

SPL_out = k_g · SPL_in + Gain_0 = 0.9500 · SPL_in + 28.5000

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Acoustic input sound level SPL_in (dB SPL), and the dependent coordinate y represents 2-cc Coupler output sound level SPL_out (dB SPL). The gradient m directly determines Input-Output Acoustic Transfer Slope k_g, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Close the anechoic test box and execute automated sound field equalization to calibrate loudspeaker output across 100 Hz to 8000 Hz.
  2. Attach the BTE hearing aid sound tube securely to the HA-2 2-cc coupler using acoustic sealing putty; seat the reference microphone within 5 mm of the hearing aid inlet.
  3. Program hearing aid via wireless Noahlink interface into standardized ANSI S3.22 linear test mode (disable adaptive noise reduction and feedback suppression).
  4. Set pure-tone 1.0 kHz acoustic stimulus to input level SPL_in = 50.0 dB SPL and allow 5 seconds for output stabilization.
  5. Record the RMS sound pressure level SPL_out inside the 2-cc coupler using the calibrated laboratory condenser microphone.
  6. Increment input sound level through 55.0, 60.0, 65.0, 70.0, and 75.0 dB SPL, recording stabilized coupler output SPL_out at each step.
  7. Plot coupler output level SPL_out versus input level SPL_in, compute transfer slope k_g and baseline gain Gain_0 via linear regression, and calculate total acoustic gain.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Seal hearing aid tube into the 2-cc coupler with acoustic putty; microscopic acoustic leaks completely destroy low-frequency measurement accuracy.
  • Verify anechoic test box door is fully latched to maintain isolation from ambient room noise.
  • Position the reference microphone within 5 mm of the hearing aid sound inlet to ensure accurate closed-loop sound field leveling.
  • Ensure hearing aid digital adaptive features (directional microphones, noise reduction) are disabled during ANSI standard testing.
