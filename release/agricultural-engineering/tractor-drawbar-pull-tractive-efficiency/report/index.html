1. Title and aim

Tractor Drawbar Pull, Wheel Slip & Tractive Efficiency Determination. Determine tractor wheel slip, net traction coefficient, and tractive efficiency on agricultural soils across varying drawbar pull loads using an instrumented loading car and fifth-wheel tachometer.

3. Apparatus and fixed settings

  • Agricultural Research Tractor: Provides controlled PTO and axle torque with ballast weights establishing a static rear axle load of 35.0 kN.
  • Eddy-Current Dynamometer Car: Applies variable braking resistance to generate continuous, regulated drawbar pull loads.
  • Drawbar Strain-Gauge Load Cell: Measures horizontal draft force with 0.1 kN resolution and continuous telemetry logging.
  • Non-Contact Doppler Radar Sensor: Measures true tractor ground speed eliminating rolling tire slip errors.
  • Drive Wheel Optical Encoders: Monitors exact axle angular velocity and theoretical zero-slip forward travel velocity.
  • Soil Cone Penetrometer: Assesses pre-test topsoil cone index and moisture profile to ensure consistent field shear strength.
  • Test soil: Silt loam (moisture 18.2% d.b.)
  • Tractor static rear axle weight: 35.0 kN
  • Rear tire inflation: 110 kPa

4. Theory and graph choice

In agricultural mechanization and tractor mechanics, tractive efficiency defines the proportion of power delivered by the drive axles that is successfully converted into useful linear drawbar work. When a pneumatic drive tire develops forward thrust on deformable agricultural soil, micro-shearing and soil strain invariably induce wheel slip. In accordance with the classical ASABE D497 and Wismer-Luth traction models, the net traction ratio κ (defined as the drawbar pull force P divided by the dynamic vertical wheel load W) increases rapidly with slip in the initial deformation zone before asymptoting toward the ultimate soil shear strength envelope. Simultaneously, rolling resistance absorbs axle torque through soil rut compaction and tire carcass deflection. Identifying the optimum slip operating point (typically 8% to 15% on firm loam and 12% to 18% on tilled soil) ensures peak fuel economy, minimum tire wear, and maximum field tractive efficiency. In this investigation, an instrumented research tractor coupled with an eddy-current dynamometer loading car is evaluated across controlled drawbar pull increments while logging drive wheel revolutions and true ground speed via an optical fifth-wheel radar sensor.

κ = μ_t · s + κ_0 = 2.4000 · s + 0.0800

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents Slip ratio s, and the dependent axis y represents Net traction coefficient κ = P / W. The gradient m directly determines Traction Development Slope μ_t, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Operate the tractor over a surveyed 100-meter test course in neutral drawbar condition to record baseline drive wheel revolutions and establish theoretical rolling radius under zero net pull.
  2. Hitch the mobile loading car horizontally through the calibrated strain-gauge load cell, verifying that the hitch line is parallel to the ground surface to prevent spurious vertical forces.
  3. Set tractor engine throttle to rated speed (2200 RPM) in standard field gear (Gear B-2) to ensure constant input mechanical power to the transmission.
  4. Increment the eddy-current braking load on the dynamometer car in six distinct steps, allowing the tractor to reach steady-state velocity for at least 30 seconds per trial.
  5. Simultaneously log true ground velocity from the Doppler radar, wheel rotation counts from axle encoders, and drawbar draft force from the load cell over standard 50-meter runs.
  6. Calculate slip ratio s = (V_t - V_a)/V_t and net traction coefficient κ = P/W_d, plotting the traction curve and identifying peak tractive efficiency.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Maintain uniform hitch height across all trials to prevent erratic pitch moments on the rear axle.
  • Ensure constant engine governor throttle setting to avoid confounding engine torque droop with traction limits.
  • Perform all comparative slip trials along identical soil tracks with uniform bulk density and moisture content.
  • Verify zero-tare offset of the drawbar strain-gauge load cell before each loading increment.

The linear model applies strictly to the steady pre-saturation slip range (s ≤ 0.25) before catastrophic soil shear failure occurs. Linearized traction development model valid in the operational working range of 5% to 25% slip on settled silt loam soil. Dynamic weight transfer from the front axle to the rear drive axle is accounted for using moment equilibrium about the tire-soil contact patches.
