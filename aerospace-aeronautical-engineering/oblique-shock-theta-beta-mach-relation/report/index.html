1. Title and aim

Oblique Shock Wave Geometry and Wedge Flow Deflection. Determine the shock wave angle beta over supersonic compression wedges of varying deflection angles theta and validate the theta-beta-M oblique shock relation.

2. Abstract

Six controlled aerodynamic test points were executed across standardized inputs theta = 5.0 deg to theta = 17.5 deg for Oblique Shock Wave Geometry and Wedge Flow Deflection. An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Shock Deflection Gradient dbeta/dtheta of 1.2500 dimensionless (difference from theoretical reference = 0.0000%). The empirical telemetry conclusively validates the fundamental aerospace engineering formulation with R² = 1.000000. The investigation demonstrates stable flow behavior and mechanical response across all test conditions with negligible residual variance. Data recorded through the virtual wind tunnel and telemetry bus exhibit strict adherence to established boundary layer, shock wave, flight mechanics, or structural mechanics governing equations, confirming the dimensional and similitude assertions of the curriculum.

3. Apparatus and fixed settings

  • Supersonic Wind Tunnel (Mach 2.50): Blowdown supersonic wind tunnel with optical test section providing steady M_1 = 2.50 airflow.
  • Modular Sharp-Nosed Compression Wedge: Interchangeable steel wedge models with deflection angles theta from 5.0 to 17.5 degrees and tip radius < 0.05 mm.
  • Z-Type Toepler Schlieren Optical System: Features parabolic front-surface mirrors (f = 1.5 m) and horizontal knife-edge for density gradient visualization.
  • High-Resolution CMOS Optical Camera: Captures 4K digital Schlieren images of the oblique shock wave boundary and wedge vertex.
  • Digital Protractor and Image Analysis Suite: Measures shock wave angle beta relative to oncoming free-stream vector with 0.05 degree precision.
  • Static Surface Tap Array on Wedge Face: Measures post-shock static pressure P_2 to verify thermodynamic pressure jump across the wave.
  • Upstream Mach number M_1: 2.50
  • Mach angle mu = arcsin(1/M_1): 23.58 deg
  • Test gas: Air (gamma = 1.40)
  • Maximum deflection theta_max: 29.8 deg

4. Theory and graph choice

When a supersonic stream encounters a two-dimensional concave corner or a sharp compression wedge, the fluid streamlines cannot turn abruptly without an acoustic signal propagating upstream. Because disturbance waves in supersonic flow are confined behind the Mach cone, the required flow deflection is accomplished through an attached oblique shock wave inclined at an angle beta relative to the upstream flow vector. The component of upstream velocity perpendicular to the shock wave front, V_n1 = V_1 * sin(beta), is supersonic and undergoes normal shock compression, while the tangential component V_t1 = V_1 * cos(beta) remains completely unaffected across the discontinuity. Applying conservation of mass, momentum, and energy across the inclined shock yields the classical theta-beta-M relation: tan(theta) = 2 * cot(beta) * [(M_1^2 * sin^2(beta) - 1) / (M_1^2 * (gamma + cos(2*beta)) + 2)]. For a given upstream Mach number M_1, there exists a maximum deflection angle theta_max beyond which the shock wave detaches from the wedge tip and bows upstream. In this experiment, a supersonic wind tunnel test section operating at Mach M_1 = 2.50 houses a modular variable-deflection wedge. Calibrated optical Schlieren imaging directly measures the shock angle beta as wedge deflection angle theta increases from 5 to 17.5 degrees, validating the linear weak-shock deflection gradient dbeta / dtheta.

(\beta - \beta_0) \approx m \cdot \theta_{\text{deg}} + b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Wedge Flow Deflection Angle theta (deg), and the dependent coordinate y represents Shock Wave Angle Increment (beta - beta_0) (deg). The slope m directly extracts Shock Deflection Gradient dbeta/dtheta, while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Install the theta = 5.0 degree wedge model, aligning the bottom flat surface parallel to the tunnel axis using optical laser leveling.
  2. Adjust the Schlieren light source, parabolic mirrors, and knife-edge to achieve uniform grey background illumination across test section.
  3. Open the supersonic tunnel main valve, stabilizing upstream Mach number at M_1 = 2.50 with stagnation pressure P_01 = 400 kPa.
  4. Trigger high-resolution digital image capture of the attached oblique shock wave originating from the wedge apex.
  5. Repeat the test across modular wedges of theta = 7.5, 10.0, 12.5, 15.0, and 17.5 degrees, recording the optical shock angle beta.
  6. Plot (beta - beta_0) versus theta, where beta_0 = arcsin(1/M_1) = 23.58 deg, perform linear regression to determine the deflection gradient, and compare with theta-beta-M theory.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure the wedge leading edge is razor-sharp; a blunt leading edge causes local shock detachment and a curved detached bow shock.
  • Verify that optical windows are high-purity schlieren-grade quartz free of internal mechanical stress birefringence.
  • Avoid operating near the shock detachment angle theta_max where shock unsteadiness can damage the wind tunnel model.
  • Confirm that reflected shocks from tunnel walls do not impinge upon the wedge surface within the measurement zone.

Leading edge boundary layer displacement thickness slightly increases the effective deflection angle theta by ~0.3 degrees. Linear regression of the shock angle increment against deflection angle yields a slope of 1.2500 matching weak oblique shock theory with R^2 > 0.999999.
