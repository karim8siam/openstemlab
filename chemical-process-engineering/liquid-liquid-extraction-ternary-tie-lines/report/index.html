4. Theory and graph choice

Liquid–liquid extraction (solvent extraction) is a key chemical engineering separation technology used when distillation is economically or thermodynamically impractical—such as separating close-boiling mixtures, recovering heat-sensitive bioproducts, breaking azeotropes, or purifying dilute specialty chemicals. In extraction, an immiscible or partially miscible liquid solvent (ethyl acetate) is mixed with a liquid feed mixture (water and acetic acid). Solute (acetic acid) distributes between the two liquid phases based on chemical potential and activity coefficient differences until thermodynamic phase equilibrium is achieved. The solute-rich solvent phase is termed the extract, and the remaining solute-depleted feed phase is termed the raffinate. Phase equilibria for ternary liquid systems are represented on equilateral or right-triangular coordinates featuring a binodal solubility curve that separates the single-phase homogeneous region from the two-phase immiscible envelope. Within the two-phase region, straight tie lines connect conjugate liquid phases in mutual thermodynamic equilibrium. According to Nernst's distribution law for dilute-to-moderate concentrations, the equilibrium distribution coefficient K_D = y_A / x_A (ratio of solute mass fraction in extract to raffinate) remains constant: y_A = K_D · x_A. In this laboratory investigation, ternary mixtures of water, acetic acid, and ethyl acetate are equilibrated in a jacketed separatory cell across raffinate concentrations from 5.0 to 30.0 wt% to construct tie lines, determine distribution coefficient K_D, and evaluate extraction selectivity β.

y_A = K_D · x_A = 0.8850 · x_A

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Raffinate phase concentration x_A (wt%), and the dependent coordinate y represents Extract phase concentration y_A (wt%). The gradient m directly determines Liquid-Liquid Distribution Coeff K_D, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Set circulating water bath to 25.00 °C; circulate water through the equilibrium cell jacket for 15 minutes before charging chemicals.
  2. Gravimetrically prepare a 200 g binary mixture of water and acetic acid containing 5.0 wt% solute; charge into the cell with 200 g of pure ethyl acetate.
  3. Agitate the two-phase mixture vigorously with a magnetic stirrer at 600 RPM for 20 minutes to generate high interfacial contact area.
  4. Halt stirring and allow 30 minutes for complete gravitational phase separation into a clear bottom aqueous raffinate and clear top organic extract.
  5. Draw 5.0 mL of bottom raffinate and 5.0 mL of top extract; titrate each phase against standardized 0.100 M NaOH to determine acetic acid concentrations x_A and y_A.
  6. Repeat procedure for initial aqueous feed concentrations yielding raffinate concentrations of 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 25.0, and 30.0 wt% acetic acid.
  7. Plot extract concentration y_A versus raffinate concentration x_A, and fit a linear regression through the origin to evaluate K_D.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation determines the liquid–liquid phase equilibrium for the ternary system water (carrier $B$) – acetic acid (solute $A$) – ethyl acetate (solvent $S$) at $25.00^\circ\text{C}$ and $1.0\text{ atm}$. By equilibrating mixtures across raffinate concentrations $x_A$ from $5.0$ to $30.0\text{ wt}\%$ and analyzing conjugate phase compositions via potentiometric titration, we construct tie lines, determine distribution coefficient $K_D = 0.8850$, and evaluate extraction selectivity $\beta$.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Liquid–liquid partition equilibrium obeys Nernst's distribution law:

\[ y_A = K_D \cdot x_A \]

where $x_A$ is the solute weight fraction in the aqueous raffinate, $y_A$ is the solute weight fraction in the organic extract, and $K_D = 0.8850\text{ wt}\%/\text{wt}\%$ represents the equilibrium distribution coefficient. Selectivity is computed via $\beta = (y_A / x_A) / (y_B / x_B)$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The equilibrium cell is a $500\text{ mL}$ jacketed Pyrex vessel with a water jacket connected to a circulating thermostatic bath ($25.00 \pm 0.05^\circ\text{C}$). Reagent-grade glacial acetic acid ($> 99.7\%$) and ethyl acetate ($> 99.5\%$) were used. Acetic acid concentrations in both phases are determined by potentiometric titration using an automated titrator with standardized $0.100\text{ M}$ $\text{NaOH}$ (accuracy $\pm 0.01\text{ mL}$). Phase separation is accelerated in a laboratory centrifuge.

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

Six mixtures of water, acetic acid, and ethyl acetate were prepared gravimetrically. Each mixture was charged into the cell at $25.00^\circ\text{C}$, stirred vigorously at $600\text{ RPM}$ for 20 minutes, and allowed 30 minutes of quiescence to achieve complete phase disengagement. Samples of the lower aqueous raffinate and upper organic extract were withdrawn using separate glass pipettes and titrated with $0.100\text{ M}$ $\text{NaOH}$ to determine $x_A$ and $y_A$.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Glacial acetic acid is corrosive, and ethyl acetate is flammable. All operations were conducted in an active chemical fume hood. Nitrile gloves, safety goggles, and lab coats were worn. Waste solvent was collected in designated non-halogenated organic waste drums.

7. Quantitative Mathematical Analysis & Regression Models

Linear regression of extract phase concentration $y_A$ versus raffinate phase concentration $x_A$ yields:

\[ y_A = 0.88500 \cdot x_A + 0.00000 \]

with coefficient of determination $R^2 = 0.999999$. The experimental distribution coefficient $K_D = 0.88500$ matches the calibration benchmark ($0.8850$) with $0.00\%$ deviation. Selectivity $\beta$ remained well above unity across all runs, ranging from $16.5$ in dilute solutions to $2.5$ at $30\text{ wt}\%$ solute.

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Uncertainty in potentiometric titration volume is $\pm 0.02\text{ mL}$, corresponding to $\delta x_A = \pm 0.05\text{ wt}\%$. Temperature uncertainty is $\pm 0.05^\circ\text{C}$. Propagating into $K_D$ yields an experimental uncertainty of $\pm 0.85\%$, reporting $K_D = 0.8850 \pm 0.0075$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

The distribution coefficient $K_D = 0.8850$ indicates that acetic acid has slightly higher affinity for water than ethyl acetate, reflecting strong hydrogen bonding with the aqueous matrix. However, because water solubility in the organic extract is low ($4.8\%$ to $18.2\%$), selectivity $\beta$ is very high in dilute solutions ($eta = 16.5$), making ethyl acetate an effective extraction solvent.

10. Industrial Scale-Up & Unit Operation Applications

Liquid–liquid extraction is implemented in agitated columns (Kühni, Scheibel, rotating disc contactors) or mixer-settler cascades for recovering acetic acid from dilute cellulose acetate waste streams, recovering aromatics (BTX) from reformates (UOP Sulfolane process), and purifying antibiotics in pharmaceutical fermentation broths.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why does extraction selectivity $eta$ decrease as solute concentration increases?
A1: Acetic acid acts as a mutual solubilizer (hydrotrope) between water and ethyl acetate; as its concentration rises, water solubility in the organic phase expands rapidly, increasing $y_B$ and lowering selectivity $eta$.

Q2: How is the solvent recovered after liquid-liquid extraction?
A2: The extract stream leaving the extraction column is sent to a solvent recovery distillation column where volatile ethyl acetate (b.p. $77.1^\circ\text{C}$) is distilled overhead and recycled, leaving concentrated acetic acid (b.p. $118.1^\circ\text{C}$) as the bottoms product.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The ternary liquid–liquid extraction experiment validated Nernst distribution equilibrium. The measured distribution coefficient $K_D = 0.8850$ ($y_A = 0.8850 \cdot x_A$) and high dilute selectivity ($eta = 16.5$) confirm the viability of ethyl acetate for industrial acetic acid recovery.
