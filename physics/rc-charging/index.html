Simulation assumptions and limits

The model uses E = 5.000 V, R = 10,000 Ω and C = 100 μF, with zero initial voltage, instantaneous source switching, exact elapsed times and no leakage, equivalent series resistance, dielectric absorption or meter loading. Vc is rounded to 0.001 V. Each requested observation starts an identical transient. The unweighted logarithmic fit treats times as exact and does not model the voltage-dependent uncertainty introduced by transformation. The generator calculates Vc = 5[1 − exp(−t/1.000)] V and rounds to 0.001 V. Analysis uses x = t and y = ln[5/(5 − Vc)], estimating τ = 1/gradient. The reference 1.000 s is the product of the fixed model R and C, not a measurement of actual components.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the charging time constant of an RC circuit from capacitor-voltage observations and a logarithmic graph. Suggested independent settings: 0.2, 0.6, 1, 1.8, 2.6 s.

1

Prepare the discharged state

With a physical source disconnected, discharge through a suitable resistor and verify near-zero capacitor voltage. Check component polarity and ratings before wiring.

Why this step matters

The model solution requires Vc(0) = 0 and safe physical handling requires controlled discharge.

2

Set the source and timing reference

Use E = 5.000 V and identify t = 0 as the start of the applied voltage step. Choose a recording method suited to a roughly one-second transient.

Why this step matters

A time-origin error changes the transformed intercept.

3

Read the charging voltage

Choose an elapsed time in the 0.2–3.0 s range and request the capacitor-voltage observation. Record Vc in volts and time in seconds.

Why this step matters

The measured quantity is capacitor voltage, not resistor voltage.

4

Gather distinct times

Collect at least five distinct times across the range. Each model observation restarts the same initially discharged transient; real repeat runs require a verified discharge first.

Why this step matters

A common initial condition makes measurements comparable.

5

Transform and plot

Check that Vc is below E. Calculate E − Vc, then the dimensionless ratio E/(E − Vc), then its natural logarithm. Fit this logarithm against time.

Why this step matters

The positive logarithm yields a positive slope equal to 1/τ.

6

Calculate and assess

Use τ = 1/m in seconds. Compare with RC = 1.000 s, inspect the intercept and residuals, and discuss voltage-dependent transformed uncertainty and timing calibration.

Why this step matters

Agreement with model components is a consistency check rather than independent proof of their accuracy.
