Simulation assumptions and limits

The simulation assumes an ideal homogeneous copper specimen without internal casting porosity, oxide films, or surface contamination. Readings in this virtual module reflect ideal copper (ρ = 8960 kg m⁻³) with standard rounding to 0.01 g on balance mass and 0.1 cm³ on volume.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the density of pure copper specimens by linear regression of mass against volume using precision balance and displacement measurements. Suggested independent settings: 10.0, 20.0, 30.0, 40.0, 50.0 cm³.

1

Inspect zero calibration of the balance

Release the balance beam arrest and adjust the counterpoise thumb screw until the pointer oscillates symmetrically about the central null mark.

Why this step matters

Zero error in a mechanical or electronic balance introduces an additive constant to every mass measurement, distorting the y-intercept.

2

Select and measure specimen dimensions

Measure the height and diameter of each cylindrical specimen at three distinct orientations using a vernier caliper to verify geometric regularity.

Why this step matters

Averaging multiple caliper readings across different diameters detects taper, ovality, or surface burrs before immersion.

3

Perform volumetric displacement in water

Fill a graduated cylinder with distilled water to a reference level, tilt slightly to avoid air entrapment, lower the dry specimen gently on a fine thread, and record the new meniscus level.

Why this step matters

Surface splashing, droplet loss, or air bubbles adhering to the specimen surface cause systematic volume errors.

4

Dry the specimen thoroughly before weighing

Remove the specimen from the water bath and blot it dry with lint-free absorbent paper before placing it on the balance pan.

Why this step matters

Residual surface water droplets add false mass to the specimen, biasing the measured density upward.

5

Record equilibrium balance readings

Place the dry specimen at the center of the pan, slide the poise weights along the beams until equilibrium is restored, and read the total mass to the nearest 0.01 g.

Why this step matters

Off-center pan loading creates knife-edge friction and torque, reducing measurement repeatability.

6

Repeat for the complete series of volumes

Proceed systematically through the graded series of specimen volumes, logging volume V and mass M for linear regression analysis.

Why this step matters

Evaluating multiple independent volumes across a wide dynamic range minimizes leverage bias in the slope determination.
