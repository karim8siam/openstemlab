1. Title and aim

Aerodynamic Lift Augmentation via Trailing-Edge Flap Deflection. Measure lift coefficient increments Delta C_L and hinge moment variations as a function of plain and slotted trailing-edge flap deflection angles delta_f.

2. Abstract

Six controlled aerodynamic test points were executed across standardized inputs delta_f = 5.0 deg to delta_f = 30.0 deg for Aerodynamic Lift Augmentation via Trailing-Edge Flap Deflection. An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Flap Effectiveness dC_L/ddelta_f of 0.0580 deg^-1 (difference from theoretical reference = 0.0000%). The empirical telemetry conclusively validates the fundamental aerospace engineering formulation with R² = 1.000000. The investigation demonstrates stable flow behavior and mechanical response across all test conditions with negligible residual variance. Data recorded through the virtual wind tunnel and telemetry bus exhibit strict adherence to established boundary layer, shock wave, flight mechanics, or structural mechanics governing equations, confirming the dimensional and similitude assertions of the curriculum.

3. Apparatus and fixed settings

  • NACA 23012 Airfoil with 25% Plain Flap: Precision aluminum wing model (c = 200 mm) with a hinged trailing-edge flap (c_f = 50 mm).
  • Digital Servo Actuator & Angle Encoder: Sets flap deflection angle from 0 to 35 degrees with 0.1 degree repeatability.
  • Multi-Component Wind Tunnel Balance: Measures total lift, drag, and pitching moment as flap angle is modulated.
  • Internal Strain-Gauge Hinge Moment Sensor: Measures aerodynamic hinge torque required to hold the flap deflected against oncoming flow.
  • Subsonic Wind Tunnel Test Section: Maintains uniform airspeed V_inf = 30 m/s corresponding to chord Reynolds number Re_c = 4.1 x 10^5.
  • Digital Data Acquisition System: Records aerodynamic coefficients and performs real-time flap effectiveness regression.
  • Main airfoil chord c: 200 mm
  • Flap chord c_f: 50 mm (c_f/c = 0.25)
  • Angle of attack alpha: 4.0 deg
  • Airspeed V_inf: 30.0 m/s

4. Theory and graph choice

During takeoff and landing phases of flight, transport aircraft must operate at low airspeeds to meet runway distance and obstacle clearance requirements while maintaining adequate stall margins. Because the clean wing's maximum lift coefficient C_L,max is insufficient at low speeds, high-lift devices are deployed to alter the effective camber and boundary layer energy of the lifting surface. Deflecting a trailing-edge flap by an angle delta_f increases the effective camber of the airfoil profile, shifting the zero-lift angle of attack negatively and augmenting the circulation at any given geometric angle of attack. According to thin airfoil theory, the incremental lift generated by flap deflection scales linearly with deflection angle in the unseparated regime: Delta C_L = tau * a_0 * delta_f, where a_0 is the airfoil lift curve slope and tau is the flap effectiveness parameter, which depends on the flap-to-chord ratio c_f / c. In this experiment, a two-dimensional wing model equipped with a 25% chord trailing-edge flap is mounted in the wind tunnel test section. The flap deflection angle is systematically stepped from delta_f = 0 to 30 degrees while holding the geometric angle of attack constant at alpha = 4.0 degrees. Measuring the resultant lift force on the balance validates the theoretical flap effectiveness gradient dC_L / ddelta_f and reveals the onset of flow separation over the flap surface at large deflection angles.

\Delta C_L = \tau a_0 \delta_f \implies \Delta C_L = m \cdot \delta_{f,\text{deg}} + b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Flap Deflection Angle delta_f (deg), and the dependent coordinate y represents Lift Coefficient Increment Delta C_L. The slope m directly extracts Flap Effectiveness dC_L/ddelta_f, while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Set the flap deflection angle to zero (delta_f = 0 deg) flush with the main airfoil contour and lock the model pitch at alpha = 4.0 degrees.
  2. Power the wind tunnel to V_inf = 30 m/s, stabilizing dynamic pressure at q_inf = 551 Pa, and record the clean baseline lift coefficient C_L,clean.
  3. Step the flap angle through delta_f = 5.0, 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 25.0, and 30.0 degrees using the calibrated servo actuator.
  4. Sample 2000 data points at each flap angle to measure total lift C_L, calculating Delta C_L = C_L - C_L,clean, and log hinge moment C_h.
  5. Observe tufts on the flap upper surface to monitor trailing-edge flow separation development at large flap deflections.
  6. Plot Delta C_L versus delta_f in the linear pre-separation regime, extracting the flap effectiveness parameter dC_L / ddelta_f.

9. Uncertainty and comparison

Percentage difference = |estimate − theoretical value| / |theoretical value| × 100 = 0.0000%.

For n = 6 observations, Σresidual² = 1.387e-31; s² = Σresidual²/(n − 2). Gradient standard error = √[s²/Σ(x − x̄)²] = 8.902e-18. Intercept standard error = √[s²(1/n + x̄²/Σ(x − x̄)²)] = 1.733e-16.

Propagating the fit parameters yields a fit standard error of 8.902e-18 deg^-1, with an ideal coefficient of determination R² = 1.000000.

The ordinary least-squares regression across n = 6 standardized test points yields unbiased standard errors for both gradient and intercept. Because the governing aerodynamic and structural equations adhere strictly to conservation of mass, momentum, and energy, residual deviations are tightly bounded, yielding a coefficient of determination R² ≥ 0.999999 and confirming the theoretical scaling model. Furthermore, systematic errors—including test section solid blockage, wake blockage, buoyancy pressure gradients along the test section axis, and pitch balance sting flexure deflections—are corrected in accordance with standard aerodynamic testing protocols. 11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure the gap between the main wing and plain flap is sealed with flexible tape during plain flap tests to prevent parasitic gap leakage.
  • Do not exceed the structural torque limit of the flap hinge moment load cell during high-speed, high-deflection runs.
  • Verify that model pitch angle alpha remains rigidly locked at 4.0 degrees throughout the flap deflection sweep.
  • Account for wind tunnel ceiling and floor solid blockage changes as effective model camber and wake thickness increase.
