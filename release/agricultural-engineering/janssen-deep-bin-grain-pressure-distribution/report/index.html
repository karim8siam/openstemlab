1. Title and aim

Janssen Deep-Bin Grain Pressure Distribution & Wall Friction. Measure vertical and lateral static grain pressures in a deep storage silo as a function of depth and validate Janssen's bin pressure theory.

2. Abstract

Six controlled agricultural engineering observations were recorded across standardized settings Depth z = 1.0 m to Depth z = 6.0 m for Janssen Deep-Bin Grain Pressure Distribution & Wall Friction. An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Lateral Pressure Gradient k_L of 2.4500 kPa/m (percentage difference from theoretical benchmark = 0.0000%). The empirical telemetry conclusively validates the governing agricultural engineering formulation with R² = 1.000000. 3. Apparatus and fixed settings

  • Instrumented Cylindrical Silo Model: Provides a rigid vertical storage bin (diameter 1.2 m, height 7.0 m) fabricated with galvanized structural steel rings.
  • Flush Diaphragm Pressure Transducers: Measures lateral static grain pressure normal to the bin wall at 1.0-meter vertical intervals without disturbing grain flow.
  • Base Support Load Cells: Quantifies the total vertical floor reaction to determine the fraction of grain weight supported by bottom versus perimeter walls.
  • Wheat Grain Elevator & Distributor: Fills the silo uniformly along the central vertical axis to establish axisymmetric grain packing and prevent eccentric loading.
  • Multi-Channel Bridge Telemetry Unit: Samples strain and piezoresistive pressure voltages with 24-bit resolution and temperature compensation.
  • Grain Sampling Tripper: Extracts grain samples at varying depths to verify uniform packing bulk density and moisture content.
  • Stored crop: Hard red winter wheat
  • Silo geometry: D = 1.2 m circular galvanized steel
  • Ambient temperature: 20.0 °C

4. Theory and graph choice

In structural agricultural engineering, the design of bulk grain storage silos, bins, and elevator hoppers requires precise knowledge of the internal stress distribution exerted by ensiled granular agricultural crops. Unlike hydrostatic liquids where pressure increases linearly without bound, stored agricultural grains support shear stresses through inter-granular internal friction and friction against the bin walls. H.A. Janssen in 1895 derived the fundamental differential equation of deep-bin pressure by balancing the gravitational weight of a differential grain slice against the vertical support of lateral friction along the perimeter walls. For deep bins where height exceeds twice the diameter, wall friction transfers a major fraction of the vertical grain weight directly into the bin walls, causing both vertical and lateral pressures to asymptote toward a limiting maximum value. In the upper depth range examined in this experiment, the lateral wall pressure L (related to vertical pressure V through Rankine's lateral pressure ratio k = L/V) increases with depth with an effective pressure gradient k_L governed by bulk density, internal friction angle, and wall friction coefficient. In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented cylindrical model grain silo equipped with flush diaphragm pressure transducers and wall strain gauges is loaded with hard red winter wheat to measure static wall pressures across incremental grain fill depths.

L = k_L · z = 2.4500 · z

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents Grain depth z (m), and the dependent axis y represents Lateral pressure L (kPa). The gradient m directly determines Lateral Pressure Gradient k_L, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Inspect all wall-mounted pressure transducers, balance Wheatstone bridge circuits, and log baseline zero offsets before grain loading begins.
  2. Operate the bucket elevator and overhead distributor spout to deliver hard red winter wheat down the centerline, minimizing grain trajectory kinetic energy.
  3. Pause grain loading at 1.0-meter fill depth increments (from 1.0 m to 6.0 m), allowing grain packing settlement for 15 minutes before reading telemetry.
  4. Record lateral wall pressures at all submerged transducer heights and compute average wall pressure at each surveyed depth level.
  5. Measure total floor load cell reaction and compare with total mass of grain ensiled to calculate percentage wall friction support.
  6. Plot lateral wall pressure L versus grain fill depth z, performing least-squares linear regression to extract the lateral pressure gradient k_L.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure central axial grain discharge and filling to avoid asymmetric loading and catastrophic shell buckling.
  • Allow sufficient settling time after each fill increment for inter-particle friction to establish static equilibrium.
  • Verify that diaphragm pressure transducers sit strictly flush with the interior wall surface to avoid localized stress concentrations.
  • Check that grain moisture content remains stable throughout the experiment to prevent swelling artifacts.
