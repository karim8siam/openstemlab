FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine uncalibrated radiocarbon age (BP) and calibrated calendar age ranges from residual 14C activity ratios using radioactive decay kinetics. Standardized parameter settings: 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0 half-lives.

  1. Apparatus Calibration & Baseline Standard Verification: Inspect all optical, electrical, and radiometric sensors. Verify instrument calibration against certified reference materials and perform electronic zero tare.
  2. Specimen Conditioning & Matrix Preparation: Clean, dry, or prepare the archaeological sample according to standardized micro-analytical protocols to eliminate modern surface contamination.
  3. Sensor Alignment & Baseline Datum Acquisition: Mount the specimen securely within the sensor housing or establish spatial grid coordinates. Verify alignment of the decay interval (half-lives) with primary excavation datum.
  4. Systematic Parameter Variation & Acquisition: Increment the decay interval (half-lives) systematically across the designated domain (0.5 to 3.0 half-lives), permitting full signal equilibration at each measurement station.
  5. Multi-Channel Telemetry Recording & Signal Integration: Record the primary dependent variable (Radiocarbon Age Before Present (BP) t) alongside auxiliary environmental monitors. Validate signal stability and confirm low noise thresholds.
  6. Post-Test Verification & Digital Archive Export: Cycle the sensor through a verification standard to confirm zero baseline drift, de-energize primary drives, and export all digital telemetry observations to the persistent laboratory database.

Question 4: Why is sample pre-treatment essential in archaeological laboratory analysis?
