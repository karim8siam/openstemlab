1. Title and aim

Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) Spin-Lattice Relaxation T₁ Mapping. Determine the longitudinal spin-lattice relaxation rate constant k_t and calculate T₁ relaxation time of a biological tissue phantom using an MRI inversion recovery pulse sequence.

4. Theory and graph choice

Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) is a non-invasive diagnostic imaging modality that provides exquisite soft-tissue anatomical and functional contrast based on the nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) properties of hydrogen protons (¹H) in water and lipids. In a strong static magnetic field B_0, proton nuclear spins align with the field, establishing a net macroscopic equilibrium longitudinal magnetization M_0. When excited by radiofrequency (RF) pulses, proton spins absorb energy and perturb away from equilibrium. The recovery of longitudinal magnetization back toward thermal equilibrium along the z-axis is governed by the Bloch equation for spin-lattice (T₁) relaxation: dM_z/dt = -(M_z - M_0) / T_1, where T_1 is the longitudinal relaxation time representing energy dissipation from spinning nuclei to the surrounding thermal molecular lattice. The gold-standard pulse sequence for measuring T_1 is the Inversion Recovery (IR) sequence: a 180° RF pulse inverts longitudinal magnetization to -M_0, followed after an inversion time delay TI by a 90° readout pulse that tips remaining M_z into the transverse plane for signal detection. The theoretical recovery equation is M_z(TI) / M_0 = 1 - 2 · exp(-TI / T_1). For short-to-intermediate inversion times (TI ≪ T_1), the exponential recovery expands via Taylor series into a linear relation: M_z(TI) / M_0 ≈ -1 + (2 / T_1) · TI = k_t · TI - 0.9500, where k_t = 2 / T_1 is the initial recovery slope (ms⁻¹). In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented benchtop NMR/MRI spectrometer evaluates an aqueous copper sulfate biological tissue phantom across inversion times from 100 to 600 ms to map T_1.

M_z/M₀ = k_t · TI - 0.9500 = 0.00145 · TI - 0.9500

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Inversion time TI (ms), and the dependent coordinate y represents Normalized longitudinal magnetization M_z/M₀ (ratio). The gradient m directly determines Inversion Recovery Initial Slope k_t, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Power on permanent magnet temperature controller, allow 30 minutes for thermal stabilization, and adjust active gradient shim coils to achieve linewidth < 5 Hz.
  2. Execute nutation pulse sweep to determine exact RF pulse widths for 90° excitation (typically 6.5 μs) and 180° inversion (13.0 μs).
  3. Insert 5 mm NMR sample tube containing aqueous CuSO₄ phantom into probe coil and allow 10 minutes for thermal equilibration at 22.0 °C.
  4. Set repetition time TR = 5000 ms (> 3 · T_1) and apply 180° inversion pulse; after inversion time delay TI = 100 ms, apply 90° readout pulse.
  5. Digitize the free induction decay (FID) signal, perform phase correction, and record initial FID amplitude as proportional to M_z(TI).
  6. Increment inversion delay TI through 200, 300, 400, 500, and 600 ms, capturing normalized longitudinal magnetization M_z/M_0 at each step.
  7. Plot normalized magnetization M_z/M_0 versus inversion time TI, extract initial slope k_t, and compute spin-lattice relaxation time T_1 = 2 / k_t.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure repetition time TR is at least 5000 ms to guarantee full longitudinal relaxation recovery between successive inversion cycles.
  • Calibrate the 180° inversion RF pulse width accurately; incomplete inversion leaves residual transverse magnetization that corrupts T_1.
  • Maintain constant magnet temperature; thermal drift in permanent magnets shifts B_0 and detunes the RF resonance frequency.
  • Perform phase-sensitive detection so that inverted signals are correctly recorded as negative amplitudes.

The linear approximation applies to initial inversion times (TI ≤ 600 ms); full recovery curves require three-parameter non-linear fitting.
