Agricultural and physical foundations of Soil Moisture Retention pF.

The soil water retention characteristic curve (also termed the soil water characteristic curve, or pF curve) describes the thermodynamic relationship between matric suction (energy potential with which water is held by soil capillary and adsorptive forces) and volumetric water content θ. It is the defining constitutive property used to evaluate Field Capacity (FC, typically -33 kPa or pF 2.54), Permanent Wilting Point (PWP, -1500 kPa or pF 4.18), and Available Water Capacity (AWC = FC - PWP) for irrigation water management. Saturated porous ceramic plates inside sealed pressure chambers allow positive pneumatic air pressure to be applied to soil cores resting on water-saturated ceramic discs. The applied air pressure forces water out through microscopic ceramic pores into an atmospheric drain until matric potential inside the soil exactly balances the applied pneumatic chamber pressure. In the intermediate desaturation zone between air-entry suction and permanent wilting, the retention curve follows a linear semi-logarithmic relationship θ = -λ_ret·ln(ψ) + θ_0 in accordance with the Brooks-Corey model.

θ = -λ_ret · ln(ψ) + θ_0 = -0.0650 · ln(ψ) + 0.4800

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

Define Field Capacity (FC) and Permanent Wilting Point (PWP).

Field Capacity is the volumetric water content retained in soil after rapid gravitational drainage has ceased (typically at matric suction of -10 to -33 kPa). Permanent Wilting Point is the soil moisture content at -1500 kPa suction where plant roots can no longer extract water, causing permanent wilting.

What is the pF scale for expressing soil matric suction?

The pF scale, introduced by Schofield, is the common logarithm of matric suction expressed in centimeters of water column head: pF = log10(h_cm). For example, 1000 cm head (~100 kPa) corresponds to pF 3.0.

How does soil texture influence the shape of the pF curve?

Sandy soils have large uniform macropores that drain rapidly at low suction, producing a steep drop in water content. Clay soils have tiny micropores and high surface area, retaining significant water even at very high suction with a gentle, flatter retention curve.

What is Available Water Capacity (AWC)?

Available Water Capacity is the total volume of water held in the crop root zone between Field Capacity and Permanent Wilting Point (AWC = θ_FC - θ_PWP), representing the soil water reservoir available for crop transpiration.

Why does soil water retention exhibit hysteresis during wetting versus drying?

Hysteresis (different moisture contents at the same suction during wetting vs drying) is caused by the 'ink-bottle' pore neck effect, trapped air during wetting, contact angle differences during advancing vs receding water menisci, and clay swelling/shrinkage.

What is the air-entry value (bubbling pressure) of a porous ceramic plate?

The air-entry value is the critical pneumatic pressure at which air can force water out of the largest pores of the ceramic plate and break through. Ceramic plates must operate below their air-entry rating to maintain a continuous water bridge.

Experimental precautions and agricultural field safety

  • Ensure ceramic plates are fully saturated before placing soil cores; dry spots prevent capillary drainage.
  • Verify that the outflow drain tube remains submerged in a water vial to prevent air back-diffusion into the drain line.
  • Depressurize and clamp drain tubes immediately when stopping the test to prevent back-absorption of water into the soil cores.
  • Maintain constant laboratory temperature to avoid thermal expansion and evaporation errors during multi-day equilibration.

Theoretical assumptions & biological limits

  • Pressure plate extraction requires several days to reach true hydraulic equilibrium at high suctions.
  • Hysteresis is not captured when testing only the primary desorption (drying) branch.
  • Pneumatic pressure in the laboratory simulates matric suction but does not reproduce natural mechanical overburden stress present in deep subsoils.
  • Solute concentration differences in soil water can induce secondary osmotic potentials not measured by ceramic plates.
