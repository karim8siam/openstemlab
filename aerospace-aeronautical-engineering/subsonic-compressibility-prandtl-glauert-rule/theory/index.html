Aerodynamic and physical foundations of Prandtl-Glauert Correction.

As an aircraft accelerates into high subsonic cruise speeds (Mach numbers between 0.3 and 0.7), the assumption of incompressible flow breaks down as air density variations become substantial. Fluid passing over the suction surface of a wing accelerates, expanding locally and lowering air density. This density drop forces the fluid to accelerate even faster than it would in an incompressible fluid, intensifying the local suction peak and augmenting the aerodynamic lift. Ludwig Prandtl and Hermann Glauert independently solved the linearized small-disturbance compressible potential flow equation, establishing the famous Prandtl-Glauert compressibility correction rule. For two-dimensional subsonic flow over thin airfoils at small angles of attack, the compressible pressure coefficient C_p and lift coefficient C_L scale inversely with the Prandtl-Glauert factor beta_PG = sqrt(1 - M_inf^2): C_p(M) = C_p0 / sqrt(1 - M_inf^2) and C_L(M) = C_L0 / sqrt(1 - M_inf^2), where C_p0 and C_L0 denote the incompressible values at low speeds. In this experiment, an instrumented NACA 0012 airfoil is tested in a high-speed subsonic wind tunnel across free-stream Mach numbers from M_inf = 0.20 to 0.71. Plotting measured suction peak magnitude |C_p| against the Prandtl-Glauert amplification parameter 1 / sqrt(1 - M_inf^2) demonstrates a strictly linear relationship whose gradient reveals the baseline incompressible pressure coefficient C_p0.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

Why does compressibility amplify aerodynamic lift and pressure suction at high subsonic speeds?

In compressible subsonic flow, fluid flowing over the curved suction surface experiences both acceleration and expansion (density reduction). By continuity, lower density forces the fluid to accelerate to an even higher velocity than in incompressible flow, generating a deeper pressure suction peak and higher lift.

How is the Prandtl-Glauert rule formulated mathematically?

The Prandtl-Glauert rule scales incompressible pressure coefficients C_p0, lift coefficients C_L0, and pitching moments C_m0 by the factor beta_PG = sqrt(1 - M_inf^2): C_p = C_p0 / sqrt(1 - M_inf^2) and C_L = C_L0 / sqrt(1 - M_inf^2).

What is the critical Mach number M_crit of an airfoil?

The critical Mach number M_crit is the free-stream Mach number at which sonic speed (local Mach M_local = 1.0) is first achieved at any point on the airfoil surface (usually at the minimum pressure suction peak on the upper surface).

How does the Karman-Tsien compressibility correction compare to the Prandtl-Glauert rule?

The Karman-Tsien rule uses a hodograph transformation with a tangent-gas approximation to account for higher-order non-linear density changes: C_p = C_p0 / [sqrt(1 - M_inf^2) + (M_inf^2 / (1 + sqrt(1 - M_inf^2))) * (C_p0 / 2)]. It provides superior accuracy closer to M_crit.

Why does linearized Prandtl-Glauert theory fail as Mach number approaches 1.0?

As M_inf approaches 1.0, the factor sqrt(1 - M_inf^2) approaches zero, causing the predicted pressure coefficient to diverge to infinity. In reality, non-linear transonic shock waves form, and the small-disturbance approximation is violated.

How does wing sweep reduce compressibility effects on high-subsonic commercial jetliners?

Sweeping a wing backward by an angle Lambda makes only the normal component of velocity V_inf * cos(Lambda) aerodynamically effective in creating pressure gradients. The effective Mach number becomes M_eff = M_inf * cos(Lambda), which delays M_crit and wave drag divergence to much higher flight airspeeds.

Experimental precautions and wind tunnel safety

  • Do not exceed the critical Mach number M_crit during the linear sweep to avoid local supersonic pocket and shock formation.
  • Verify that wind tunnel solid blockage corrections are applied to account for compressible streamtube constriction.
  • Ensure pressure transducers are temperature-compensated against wind tunnel stagnation temperature variations.
  • Inspect the airfoil model leading edge for surface roughness to maintain clean attached boundary layer flow.

Theoretical assumptions & flow limits

  • Linearized Prandtl-Glauert theory assumes small perturbations; errors increase for thick airfoils (> 15% thickness) or large alpha.
  • Fails completely in the transonic regime (M > M_crit) where non-linear mixed subsonic-supersonic equations govern.
  • More advanced non-linear rules (Karman-Tsien and Laitone) provide higher accuracy near M_crit.
  • Assumes two-dimensional flow across the span without three-dimensional relief effects.
