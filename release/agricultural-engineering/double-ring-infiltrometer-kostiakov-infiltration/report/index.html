1. Title and aim

Double-Ring Infiltrometer Soil Infiltration Dynamics & Kostiakov Model. Measure the cumulative water infiltration and infiltration rate of an agricultural soil using a double-ring infiltrometer and determine Kostiakov model parameters.

4. Theory and graph choice

Infiltration is the physical process by which water on the ground surface enters the soil matrix under the combined influence of gravity and matric suction gradients. Accurately determining infiltration capacity is essential for designing surface irrigation systems (furrows and borders), predicting sprinkler runoff, sizing watershed drainage structures, and calculating groundwater recharge. Field infiltration is universally measured using a double-ring infiltrometer consisting of two concentric metal cylinders driven vertically into the soil. The outer buffer ring restricts horizontal lateral seepage beneath the inner ring, forcing water from the inner ring to infiltrate vertically downward in a 1D column. The empirical Kostiakov equation I = c·t^α (or its linearized logarithmic form ln I = α·ln t + ln c) describes cumulative infiltration I over time t, where α is the time exponent (typically between 0.3 and 0.8 depending on soil structure) and c is a measure of initial soil sorptivity. Differentiating yields the transient infiltration rate i = dI/dt = α·c·t^(α-1), which asymptotically approaches the basic steady-state infiltration rate i_b governed by saturated hydraulic conductivity.

ln(I) = α · ln(t) + ln(c) = 0.6500 · ln(t) + 1.2500

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents ln(Time t in min), and the dependent axis y represents ln(Cumulative infiltration I in mm). The gradient m directly determines Kostiakov Infiltration Exponent α, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Select a representative, level site free of large stones and surface cracks. Place the double rings concentrically and drive them vertically into the soil to exactly 10 cm depth using the driving plate and hammer.
  2. Place a thin burlap fabric or perforated plastic sheet inside the inner ring to prevent soil surface puddling, detachment, and slaking when pouring water.
  3. Fill the outer ring with water first to approximately 5 cm depth to establish an outer saturated buffer zone and prevent lateral capillary spread.
  4. Rapidly fill the inner ring to exactly 5 cm depth, remove the burlap, connect the Mariotte syphon or hook gauge, and start the master stopwatch at t = 0.
  5. Record hook gauge water level drops at standardized elapsed intervals: 10, 30, 50, 70, 90, and 120 minutes, replenishing the water level to maintain steady head.
  6. Transform cumulative infiltration I (mm) and elapsed time t (min) into natural logarithms, plot ln I versus ln t, and perform linear regression to evaluate α and c.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Drive rings straight down without rocking; tilting or rocking creates annular voids along the metal wall that cause artificial edge leakage.
  • Maintain equal water levels in both inner and outer rings at all times to eliminate horizontal hydraulic gradients between rings.
  • Ensure water used is from the local irrigation source to match natural soil-water chemical and salinity equilibrium.
  • Never walk within 1.5 meters of the infiltrometer rings during testing to prevent soil compaction and surface disturbance.

Double-ring measurements evaluate positive ponding head, whereas sprinkler and rainfall infiltration occur under zero-ponding negative pressure. Evaluated on undisturbed agricultural silt loam soil at 14% initial moisture. Cumulative infiltration data is linearized via natural logarithm transformation to extract α and c.
