1. Title and aim

Hydraulic Spray Nozzle Discharge, Droplet VMD & Patternation Uniformity. Measure the liquid discharge rate and patternation uniformity of an agricultural hydraulic flat-fan spray nozzle across varying operating pressures using a patterned patternator table.

4. Theory and graph choice

Agricultural chemical application via tractor-mounted boom sprayers is critical for crop protection, weed management, and foliar fertilization. Hydraulic flat-fan spray nozzles convert pressurized liquid into a sheet of liquid that atomizes into millions of airborne droplets through aerodynamic surface wave instability. The flow rate through an orifice nozzle adheres strictly to the Torricelli orifice discharge law, scaling linearly with the square root of pressure: Q = C_n·√P. Simultaneously, pressure governs atomization quality: increasing operating pressure reduces the Volume Median Diameter (VMD or D_v0.5) of the droplets, shifting the spectrum toward fine mists (< 150 μm) that are highly susceptible to airborne spray drift. Conversely, insufficient pressure produces coarse droplets and narrow spray fan angles that cause streaking and non-uniform chemical deposition. Evaluating the transversal distribution pattern on a grooved patternator table allows calculation of Christiansen's Uniformity Coefficient (CU) and determination of the optimum boom height and nozzle overlap.

Q = C_n · √P = 0.8500 · √P

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents Square root of pressure √P (bar^0.5), and the dependent axis y represents Liquid discharge Q (L/min). The gradient m directly determines Nozzle Flow Coefficient C_n, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Mount the ISO 110-02 nozzle on the test boom at exactly 50 cm height above the patternator table, aligning the spray fan at a 5° angle to the boom axis to prevent interference.
  2. Start the diaphragm pump, bleeding all air from the boom line, and adjust the pressure relief valve to initial pressure P = 1.00 bar (√P = 1.00 bar^0.5).
  3. Collect the full nozzle discharge in a graduated cylinder over exactly 60 seconds, recording the total volume Q (L/min) on the digital balance.
  4. Allow spray to drain into the patternator graduated tubes for 2.0 minutes, recording the liquid height in each channel to plot the transverse distribution profile.
  5. Increase operating pressure across five additional increments up to 6.25 bar (√P = 1.3, 1.6, 1.9, 2.2, 2.5 bar^0.5), repeating discharge and droplet VMD scans.
  6. Plot discharge Q against √P, perform linear regression to determine nozzle flow coefficient C_n, and evaluate the pressure-droplet size trade-off.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Operate spray test equipment exclusively with clean water; never use hazardous crop protection chemicals in laboratory teaching patternators.
  • Inspect nozzle orifice under an optical microscope for erosion, scratches, or burrs prior to testing.
  • Ensure pressure transducer is mounted directly at the nozzle check-valve body to avoid boom friction pressure drops.
  • Wear protective safety goggles and operate exhaust ventilation during high-pressure mist trials.

Water calibration curves differ slightly from commercial pesticide tank mixes containing surfactants, adjuvants, or crop oils that alter surface tension and viscosity. Evaluated using standard ISO 110-02 flat-fan brass nozzle spraying clean water at 20°C across pressures from 1.0 to 6.25 bar (√P from 1.0 to 2.5 bar^0.5).
