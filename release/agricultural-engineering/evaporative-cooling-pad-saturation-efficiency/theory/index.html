Agricultural and physical foundations of Evaporative Pad Cooling.

In agricultural environmental control, evaporative cooling pads coupled with negative-pressure exhaust fans provide the primary method for moderating extreme summer temperatures inside commercial greenhouses and intensive poultry or swine housing. Evaporative cooling operates on the psychrometric principle of adiabatic saturation: warm, unsaturated ambient air passes through a cross-fluted cellulose pad continuously wetted with recirculating water. Sensible heat from the airstream is absorbed as latent heat of vaporization, converting liquid water into water vapor, which lowers the dry-bulb air temperature toward the thermodynamic wet-bulb temperature while increasing relative humidity. Sizing water storage reservoirs, recirculation pumps, and bleed-off rates requires accurate knowledge of the water evaporation rate m_dot_w as a function of face air velocity v_pad. At higher face velocities, increased volumetric airflow drives a proportional increase in total moisture uptake across the pad face area. In this experiment, an instrumented wind tunnel test section containing a 100 mm thick cross-fluted cellulose pad is tested across varying approach velocities while monitoring psychrometric properties, airflow rates, and water mass depletion.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

How is evaporative cooling saturation efficiency defined mathematically?

Cooling efficiency (saturation effectiveness) is defined as η = (T_dry_in - T_dry_out) / (T_dry_in - T_wet_in) × 100%, where T_wet_in represents the theoretical thermodynamic limit of cooling achievable via adiabatic saturation.

Why does cooling efficiency decline slightly as face air velocity increases?

At higher face velocities, air residence time inside the wetted flutes decreases, giving sensible heat less contact time to transfer to the liquid boundary layer and reach thermal equilibrium.

What is the function of the bleed-off water discharge in recirculating evaporative systems?

Continuous evaporation leaves dissolved minerals and salts behind; bleed-off discharges a fraction of circulating water (typically 5% to 10%) to prevent scale accumulation, clogging, and algae growth on the pad media.

Explain the significance of the 45°/15° unequal angle flute geometry in modern cellulose pads.

The steeper 45° angle directs water toward the air-inlet face where evaporation demand is highest, flushing dirt outward, while the flatter 15° angle ensures adequate air contact and low static pressure drop.

Under what ambient climate conditions is evaporative pad cooling most effective?

Evaporative cooling is most effective in hot, arid climates where the wet-bulb depression (T_dry - T_wet) is large (often 10 °C to 18 °C), yielding substantial temperature drops.

What causes water carry-over (drift) in agricultural evaporative cooling systems?

Face velocities exceeding 2.0 to 2.5 m/s exert aerodynamic drag forces that strip droplets from the pad face, blowing liquid mist into the greenhouse or barn.

Experimental precautions and agricultural field safety

  • Verify uniform water flow along the distribution pipe header to prevent localized dry streaks on the pad.
  • Ensure steady ambient inlet dry-bulb and wet-bulb temperatures throughout the test duration.
  • Shield psychrometer wick temperature sensors from radiant heat and ensure pure distilled water supply to wicks.
  • Zero and tare the electronic sump balance before each 15-minute gravimetric test interval.

Theoretical assumptions & biological limits

  • The linear evaporation model is valid for the design operational velocity range of 0.5 to 2.0 m/s.
  • Assumes constant ambient entering air enthalpy and moisture content during the testing sequence.
  • Does not account for calcium scale deposition effects present in long-term field operation.
  • Applies to clean, un-degraded cellulose media without biological biofilm fouling.
