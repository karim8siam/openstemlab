Biomedical assumptions and boundary condition limits

The linear clearance-flow model applies across standard clinical blood flows (150 to 400 mL/min); at extreme flows (> 500 mL/min), clearance plateaus asymptotically. FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Evaluate small-solute (urea) clearance K_u of a clinical hollow-fiber hemodialyzer across varying blood flow rates Q_b at fixed dialysate flow rate Q_d. Standardized parameter settings: 150.0, 200.0, 250.0, 300.0, 350.0, 400.0 mL/min.

01

Dialyzer Priming and Air Purging

Mount dialyzer vertically, prime blood side with 1 L heparinized saline at 150 mL/min to displace air, and invert to purge dialysate compartment.

02

Dialysate Temperature and Flow Stabilization

Set dialysate delivery to 500 mL/min at 37.0 °C with bicarbonate conductivity 14.0 mS/cm and verify zero ultrafiltration transmembrane pressure.

03

Blood Reservoir Urea Standardization

Prepare synthetic blood reservoir with urea concentration C_bi = 25.0 mmol/L (150 mg/dL BUN) at 37.0 °C under gentle magnetic stirring.

04

Blood Flow Step Initialization

Set blood pump to delivery rate Q_b = 150 mL/min, circulate counter-current to dialysate, and allow 5 minutes for concentration steady state.

05

Paired Blood Sampling and Urea Assay

Simultaneously sample inlet port (C_bi) and dialyzer outlet port (C_bo), assaying urea concentrations via spectrophotometric enzymatic assay to calculate clearance K_u.

06

Blood Flow Progression and Regression Analysis

Repeat sampling at Q_b = 200, 250, 300, 350, and 400 mL/min, plot urea clearance K_u versus Q_b, and determine flow sensitivity slope m_c.

07

Mass Transfer-Area Coefficient K_0·A Calculation

Verify dialyzer performance against the theoretical logarithmic mean mass transfer relation K_0·A = [Q_b·Q_d / (Q_d - Q_b)] · ln[(1 - K/Q_d)/(1 - K/Q_b)].
