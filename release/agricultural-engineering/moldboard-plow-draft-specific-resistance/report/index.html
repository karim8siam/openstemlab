1. Title and aim

Moldboard Plow Draft Force & Specific Soil Resistance Calibration. Measure the horizontal draft force of a multi-bottom moldboard plow as a function of furrow cross-sectional area and evaluate specific soil resistance.

Six controlled agricultural engineering observations were recorded across standardized settings Area A = 0.04 m² to Area A = 0.14 m² for Moldboard Plow Draft Force & Specific Soil Resistance Calibration. 4. Theory and graph choice

Primary tillage via moldboard plowing represents one of the most energy-intensive operations in agricultural crop production. The moldboard plow functions by cutting a rectangular prism of soil (furrow slice) with the horizontal share and vertical coulter, lifting it along the curved shin and breast, and inverting it across the moldboard wing to bury crop residues and aerate the root zone. The total horizontal draft resistance D is governed by soil mechanical properties (cohesion, internal friction, soil-metal adhesion), cutting geometry, and operating speed. In classical tillage mechanics (ASABE D497 and Gorjatschkin's rational equation), draft is partitioned into three terms: static cutting and friction resistance proportional to furrow cross-sectional area (A = w·d), soil acceleration and throwing resistance proportional to velocity squared, and parasitic landside friction. At steady field speed, specific soil resistance k_s = D / A (expressed in kPa or N/cm²) provides an objective benchmark of soil workability and structural state.

D = k_s · A = 48.5000 · A

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents Furrow area A (m²), and the dependent axis y represents Draft force D (kN). The gradient m directly determines Specific Soil Resistance k_s, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Set tractor wheel tread to match furrow bottom width and adjust tractor three-point top link and leveling rod so the plow frame sits parallel to the ground in both lateral and longitudinal directions.
  2. Sample soil moisture and bulk density at 5 cm depth increments across the test plot to verify uniform pre-tillage soil mechanical conditions.
  3. Set the gage wheel to initial shallow depth (d = 12 cm, A = 0.042 m²) and verify full furrow slice inversion without scouring failure.
  4. Operate the tractor at constant 5.5 km/h over a 60-meter measuring strip, recording three-point hitch draft load cell channels continuously.
  5. Systematically lower the plow depth in five discrete steps from 12 cm to 28 cm, measuring furrow width and depth with the profiler for each run.
  6. Calculate furrow cross-sectional area A = width × depth, plot draft force versus area, and compute the slope representing specific soil resistance k_s.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Scour all moldboard surfaces thoroughly with abrasive pads before testing to eliminate rust and establish steady soil-metal friction.
  • Ensure tractor right wheels run inside the previous furrow wall at constant offset to prevent variation in first-bottom width.
  • Check that draft load cells are properly temperature-compensated to prevent zero drift under field solar radiation.
  • Disengage the differential lock only after entering the straight test run to maintain true axial alignment.

Linear proportionality D = k_s·A holds strictly at constant operating speed; speed variations introduce quadratic dynamic errors.
