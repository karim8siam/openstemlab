Biomedical and physiological foundations of ECG Powerline Notch Filter.

In clinical electrophysiology, 50 Hz or 60 Hz powerline interference is the most pervasive electromagnetic contaminant obscuring biopotential telemetry (ECG, EEG, EMG). Radiated by AC power cabling, fluorescent lighting ballasts, and hospital equipment, powerline interference manifests as a sharp sinusoidal artifact superimposed on microvolt biopotentials. While differential instrumentation amplifiers and Right Leg Drive circuits attenuate a large fraction of common-mode hum, residual common-mode-to-differential conversion caused by electrode impedance imbalances necessitates digital filtering in the digital signal processor (DSP). To eliminate 60 Hz hum without distorting critical clinical features—such as the sharp high-frequency QRS peak or subtle low-frequency ST-segment elevations diagnostic of acute myocardial infarction—a specialized second-order Infinite Impulse Response (IIR) digital notch filter is synthesized via bilinear transformation. The transfer function places conjugate complex zeros on the unit circle at z = exp(±j · 2π · f_0 / f_s) and matching poles at r · exp(±j · 2π · f_0 / f_s) with radius r = 1 - (π · BW / f_s), establishing a sharp notch (Q = f_0 / BW = 30). In this laboratory investigation, synthesized ECG signals corrupted by controlled 60 Hz interference are processed through the digital notch filter to quantify power attenuation and verify linear power suppression.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

How is a digital notch filter transfer function constructed using z-plane poles and zeros?

The filter places a pair of complex-conjugate zeros directly on the unit circle at angle ω_0 = 2π·f_0/f_s to create infinite theoretical attenuation at the notch frequency. Matching poles are placed at the same angle just inside the unit circle at radius r (e.g., r = 0.985) to cancel attenuation at neighboring frequencies, creating a sharp, narrow notch.

What is the Quality Factor (Q) of a notch filter and why is high Q desirable?

The Quality Factor is defined as Q = f_0 / BW, where BW is the 3 dB notch bandwidth. A high Q (e.g., Q = 30) produces an extremely narrow notch (BW = 2 Hz), which removes the 60 Hz interference while preserving adjacent physiological ECG spectral energy at 58 Hz and 62 Hz.

What distortion can an IIR notch filter introduce into an ECG waveform?

Because IIR filters have non-linear phase response, frequency components near the notch frequency experience different time delays. This can cause phase ringing (oscillation tails) immediately following sharp QRS complexes and can distort the clinical ST-segment, potentially mimicking or masking myocardial ischemia.

How does an FIR notch filter compare with an IIR notch filter?

An FIR filter can achieve perfectly linear phase (zero phase distortion when filtered forward and backward), but requires hundreds of filter taps to achieve a sharp notch, creating significant computational latency; an IIR filter achieves a sharp notch with only 2nd-order complexity.

Why is powerline interference 60 Hz in the Americas and 50 Hz in Europe and Asia?

Electrical grid standards differ by region: North America and parts of South America and Japan use 60 Hz AC electrical distribution, whereas Europe, Asia, Africa, and Australia standardize on 50 Hz AC. Digital medical devices often feature auto-detecting notch filters.

Why should analog filtering not be solely relied upon for powerline rejection?

High-Q analog notch filters using operational amplifiers and capacitors suffer from component tolerance drift (resistor and capacitor aging and temperature drift), causing the notch center frequency to drift away from exact 60.0 Hz; digital DSP filters have stable, drift-free center frequencies.

Experimental precautions and patient safety

  • Verify that sampling frequency f_s is strictly synchronous and stable to prevent filter center frequency drift.
  • Ensure floating-point arithmetic uses 64-bit precision to prevent numerical quantization limit cycles in the IIR feedback loop.
  • Allow the filter to settle for 500 samples before measuring output power to eliminate initial transient step ringing.
  • Verify input signal does not clip or saturate the integer analog-to-digital converter dynamic range.

Theoretical assumptions & biological limits

  • The linear power transmission model applies across the tested linear range (P_in ≤ 1.1 mV²).
  • Does not remove harmonic powerline frequencies (120 Hz, 180 Hz); harmonic rejection requires comb filters.
  • Phase non-linearity can introduce ringing artifacts on fast spikes like pacemaker pulses.
  • Assumes stationary 60.0 Hz hum; rapid frequency fluctuations on unstable backup generators cause leakage.
