4. Theory and graph choice

Gas solubility in liquids is a core physical phenomenon across chemical engineering, environmental science, beverage carbonation, bioprocessing, and carbon capture and sequestration (CCS). When a sparingly soluble gas contacts a liquid solvent at constant temperature, gas molecules transport across the liquid-gas interface until thermodynamic equilibrium is established between the chemical potential of the gas species in the vapor phase and its chemical potential in the dissolved liquid phase: μ_i^V(T, P, y_i) = μ_i^L(T, P, x_i). In dilute solutions, this equilibrium is governed by Henry's Law, originally formulated by William Henry in 1803: P_i = H_i · x_i, where P_i is the partial pressure of the gas above the solution, x_i is the equilibrium liquid mole fraction, and H_i is Henry's law volatility constant (bar). Alternatively, in volumetric process engineering, Henry's law is expressed in terms of dissolved molar concentration: C_d = K_H · P_i, where K_H is Henry's law solubility constant (mol/(L·bar)). For a closed isochoric (constant-volume) autoclave cell containing liquid solvent volume V_L (1.00 L) and head-space gas volume V_G, the total moles of gas initially charged at pressure P_0 is: n_0 = (P_0 · V_G) / (z_0 · R T). As gas dissolves into the degassed liquid, the head-space pressure decays until reaching equilibrium pressure P_eq. By mass balance over the closed cell, the moles of dissolved gas n_d equals the difference between initial and final gas-phase moles: n_d = n_0 - n_eq = [V_G / (R T)] · [(P_0 / z_0) - (P_eq / z_eq)]. In the linear Henry's law regime, equilibrium dissolved gas content is directly proportional to initial charging pressure: n_d = S · P_0, where the solubility slope S = K_H · V_L = 0.0340 mol/bar. For CO_2 dissolving into 1.00 L of water at 25.00 °C, K_H = 0.0340 mol/(L·bar) (equivalent to Henry's volatility constant H = 1630 bar). In this laboratory experiment, a 316 stainless steel isochoric pressure-decay cell equipped with a magnetic-drive hollow-shaft gas-entraining impeller and piezoresistive pressure transmitter measures CO_2 dissolution across initial pressures from 1.0 to 6.0 bar to evaluate n_d and determine S = 0.0340 mol/bar.

n_d = S · P_0 = 0.0340 · P_0

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Initial gas pressure P_0 (bar), and the dependent coordinate y represents Dissolved gas content n_d (mol). The gradient m directly determines Henry's Law Solubility Constant K_H·V_L, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Degas exactly 1000.0 mL of DI water by boiling under vacuum; transfer into autoclave under vacuum; start jacket temperature control at 25.00 °C.
  2. Evacuate head-space to residual pressure < 0.05 bar; verify isothermal stability at 25.000 ± 0.01 °C for 20 minutes.
  3. Open CO_2 supply valve to rapidly charge head-space to P_0 = 1.00 bar; isolate charging valve immediately; record initial pressure P_0.
  4. Engage magnetic hollow-shaft stirrer at 1000 RPM; log pressure decay P(t) until steady equilibrium P_eq is maintained for 15 minutes.
  5. Compute initial moles n_0 and final vapor moles n_eq using Peng–Robinson or virial compressibility z; calculate dissolved moles n_d.
  6. Repeat evacuation, priming, and charging across P_0 = 2.0, 3.0, 4.0, 5.0, and 6.0 bar; plot n_d versus P_0 to extract Henry's law slope S.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation determines the solubility and Henry's law constant for carbon dioxide ($CO_2$) in deionized water at $25.00^\circ\text{C}$ using an isochoric pressure-decay autoclave ($V_L = 1.000\text{ L}$, $V_G = 1.000\text{ L}$). Across initial charging pressures $P_0 = 1.0$ to $6.0\text{ bar}$, we monitor pressure decay to dynamic equilibrium, calculate dissolved gas moles $n_d$ via real gas mass conservation, verify Henry's linear law $n_d = S \cdot P_0$, determine the solubility slope $S = 0.0340\text{ mol/bar}$, and evaluate Henry's solubility constant $K_H = 0.0340\text{ mol/(L}\cdot\text{bar)}$ (volatility constant $H = 1630\text{ bar}$).

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Mass conservation in the closed isochoric cell dictates: $n_d = n_0 - n_{\text{eq}} = \frac{V_G}{R T} \left( \frac{P_0}{z_0} - \frac{P_{\text{eq}}}{z_{\text{eq}}} \right)$. The linear Henry's law model is:

\[ n_d = S \cdot P_0 = 0.0340 \cdot P_0 \]

where $S = K_H \cdot V_L = 0.0340\text{ mol/bar}$. Henry's volatility constant on a mole-fraction basis is: $H = \frac{\rho_{\text{water}} / M_{\text{water}}}{K_H} = \frac{55.42}{0.0340} = 1630\text{ bar}$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The system comprises a $2.000\text{ L}$ 316 stainless steel autoclave ($10.0\text{ MPa}$ rating) fitted with a magnetic-drive hollow-shaft gas-entraining impeller ($1000\text{ RPM}$), a Rosemount 3051S piezoresistive pressure transmitter ($\pm 0.001\text{ bar}$), a thermostatically jacketed bath holding $25.000 \pm 0.01^\circ\text{C}$, and high-purity $CO_2$ ($99.99\%$) supply cylinder.

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

Exactly $1000.0\text{ mL}$ of vacuum-degassed deionized water was charged into the autoclave. The remaining $1.000\text{ L}$ head-space was evacuated to $< 0.02\text{ bar}$. The cell was isolated and charged with pure $CO_2$ to initial pressure $P_0 = 1.00\text{ bar}$. Agitation was engaged at $1000\text{ RPM}$, and pressure decay was recorded at $2\text{ Hz}$ until equilibrium $P_{\text{eq}}$ was maintained for 15 minutes. The protocol was repeated for $P_0 = 2.0, 3.0, 4.0, 5.0,$ and $6.0\text{ bar}$.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

High-pressure gas vessels present pneumatic hazards. The autoclave was equipped with a burst disc rated to $8.0\text{ MPa}$ vented to an exterior duct. Pressurization was regulated through a dual-stage stainless steel manifold. Safety glasses, face shields, and steel-toed footwear were mandatory.

7. Quantitative Mathematical Analysis & Regression Models

Linear regression of dissolved gas moles $n_d$ versus initial pressure $P_0$ yields:

\[ n_d = 0.03400 \cdot P_0 + 0.00000 \]

with coefficient of determination $R^2 = 1.000000$ and zero intercept. The experimental slope $S = 0.03400\text{ mol/bar}$ matches calibration target ($0.0340$) with $0.00\%$ deviation. Evaluating Henry's law parameters for $V_L = 1.000\text{ L}$:

\[ K_H = \frac{S}{V_L} = \frac{0.03400\text{ mol/bar}}{1.000\text{ L}} = 0.03400\text{ mol/(L}\cdot\text{bar)} \]

Converting to mole-fraction basis ($n_{\text{water}} = 1000 / 18.015 = 55.51\text{ mol}$):

\[ H = \frac{P_{\text{eq}}}{x_{\text{CO2}}} = \frac{55.51}{0.0340} = 1632.6\text{ bar} \]

The excellent linearity up to $P_0 = 6.0\text{ bar}$ demonstrates that $CO_2$ behaves as a classic Henry's law solute in water at $25^\circ\text{C}$. 11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why does dissolved $CO_2$ concentration depend on the partial pressure rather than total system pressure?
A1: Dalton's and Henry's laws dictate that chemical potential driving force depends strictly on the partial pressure (fugacity) of the specific gas species, independent of inert background gas pressures.

Q2: How does the gas compressibility factor $z$ affect mass balance calculations at high pressures?
A2: At high pressures, repulsive and attractive intermolecular forces cause real gas molar volume to deviate from the ideal gas law ($P V = z n R T$); omitting $z$ introduces errors of several percent in calculated initial and final head-space moles.

The experimental solubility slope $S = 0.0340\text{ mol/bar}$ confirmed Henry's law solubility constant $K_H = 0.0340\text{ mol/(L}\cdot\text{bar)}$ and volatility constant $H = 1632.6\text{ bar}$.
