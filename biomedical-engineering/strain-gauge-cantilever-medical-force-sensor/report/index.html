1. Title and aim

Strain-Gauge Cantilever Beam Force Sensor & Sensitivity Calibration. Measure the differential voltage output of a full-bridge foil strain gauge on a medical force sensor as a function of applied tip load to evaluate gauge factor and flexural sensitivity.

2. Abstract

Six controlled biomedical engineering observations were evaluated across standardized settings Force F = 1.0 N to Force F = 6.0 N for Strain-Gauge Cantilever Beam Force Sensor & Sensitivity Calibration. An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Force Sensor Sensitivity S_f of 2.4500 mV/N (difference from theoretical reference = 0.0000%). The empirical telemetry conclusively validates the fundamental biomedical engineering formulation with R² = 1.000000. The investigation demonstrates stable physiological response, transducer linearity, and reproducible telemetry across all clinical test conditions with negligible residual variance. Data acquired through the virtual biomedical workbench exhibit strict adherence to established biopotential, constitutive biomechanical, hemodynamic, or diagnostic imaging governing equations, confirming the dimensional and similitude assertions of the curriculum.

3. Apparatus and fixed settings

  • Instrumented Cantilever Load Cell Beam: Provides a precision-machined aluminum alloy flexure beam with four bonded 350 Ω foil strain gauges.
  • Full Wheatstone Bridge Conditioning Board: Arranges two upper tensile and two lower compressive gauges in push-pull bridge configuration.
  • Class F1 Precision Calibration Deadweight Set: Applies traceable vertical calibration forces from 1.0 N to 6.0 N (±0.01% tolerance).
  • Stable Low-Noise Bridge Power Supply: Provides highly regulated 10.000 V DC excitation with < 50 μV ripple.
  • Precision Instrumentation Amplifier & DMM: Amplifies microvolt bridge output and digitizes differential signals with 6.5-digit precision.
  • Rigid Clamped Mounting Fixture: Prevents base rotation or compliance slippage during tip load application.
  • Beam material: 6061-T6 Aluminum alloy (E = 70 GPa)
  • Bridge configuration: 4-gauge full bridge (350 Ω each, GF = 2.10)
  • Excitation: 10.000 V DC regulated

4. Theory and graph choice

Accurate force transduction is essential across biomedical applications, including surgical robotic haptic feedback, infusion syringe pump occlusion pressure monitoring, biomechanical gait analysis, and prosthetic limb load-bearing controls. The fundamental principle of a resistive strain gauge relies on piezoresistive foil deformation: when bonded to an elastic substrate subjected to mechanical strain ε, the fractional change in electrical resistance is governed by the gauge factor GF: ΔR / R = GF · ε. For a prismatic cantilever beam of length L, width b, and thickness h loaded at its distal tip by a force F, elementary Euler-Bernoulli beam theory dictates that the surface bending strain at distance x from the load is ε = 6·F·x / (E·b·h²). By mounting four identical active strain gauges in a full Wheatstone bridge configuration (two in tension on the top surface, two in compression on the bottom surface), the bridge sensitivity is quadrupled compared to a quarter-bridge while providing complete intrinsic common-mode cancellation of ambient thermal drift. In this experiment, a calibrated biomedical cantilever beam load cell is subjected to precision static deadweights to quantify output linearity, establish voltage sensitivity S_f, and verify transducer mechanics.

V_out = S_f · F = 2.4500 · F

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Applied force F (N), and the dependent coordinate y represents Differential output V_out (mV). The gradient m directly determines Force Sensor Sensitivity S_f, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Secure the cantilever base into the rigid clamping block and adjust the bridge balance circuit until unloaded differential output reads 0.0000 mV.
  2. Measure bridge input excitation voltage at terminals with calibrated DMM; confirm steady 10.000 V reference.
  3. Gently place the 1.000 N calibration weight onto the knife-edge tip loading hanger without dynamic shock.
  4. Allow 15 seconds for viscoelastic relaxation and mechanical vibrations to settle; record stabilized differential bridge voltage V_out in millivolts.
  5. Progressively add calibration masses to achieve loads of 2.0, 3.0, 4.0, 5.0, and 6.0 N, logging stabilized output voltage at each load.
  6. Plot differential voltage V_out against applied force F, performing least-squares linear regression to determine force sensitivity S_f.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Apply calibration deadweights gently along the beam centerline to avoid dynamic impact and torsional loading.
  • Verify that the cantilever mounting clamp is torqued evenly to prevent false compliance at the root.
  • Ensure strain gauge lead wires are dressed and secured with strain relief to avoid parasitic mechanical pulling forces.
  • Shield bridge wiring from ambient electrical noise and verify zero tare offset before each trial.
