Aerodynamic and physical foundations of Elevator Trim & Control Power.

In steady unaccelerated flight, an aircraft must be trimmed so that the net aerodynamic pitching moment about its center of gravity is identically zero (C_m = 0). The primary longitudinal control surface responsible for pitching equilibrium is the elevator, located on the trailing edge of the horizontal stabilizer. Deflecting the elevator by an angle delta_e changes the camber of the tailplane, altering tail lift and exerting a powerful pitch-trimming moment: Delta C_m = C_m_delta_e * delta_e, where C_m_delta_e is the elevator control effectiveness derivative (conventionally negative: trailing-edge down produces nose-down pitch). For any trimmed flight condition, the pitching moment equation is C_m = C_m0 + (dC_m/dC_L) * C_L + C_m_delta_e * delta_e = 0. Solving for the equilibrium elevator trim angle yields delta_e,trim = -C_m0 / C_m_delta_e - (dC_m/dC_L / C_m_delta_e) * C_L. As airspeed decreases, the aircraft must fly at higher lift coefficients C_L to support its weight, requiring progressive up-elevator deflection (delta_e < 0) to maintain trim. In this experiment, a wind tunnel aircraft model is trimmed across a range of lift coefficients from C_L = 0.25 to 1.15 by modulating an automated servo elevator. Plotting equilibrium elevator deflection delta_e,trim against lift coefficient C_L extracts the elevator trim gradient ddelta_e / dC_L.

\delta_{e,\text{trim}} = m \cdot C_L + b \quad \text{where } m = -\frac{dC_m/dC_L}{C_{m\delta_e}}

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What is the physical definition of an aircraft in trimmed flight?

An aircraft is trimmed when the sum of all aerodynamic, propulsive, and gravitational forces and moments about its center of gravity is identically zero. Specifically for longitudinal flight, net pitching moment must be zero: C_m = 0, allowing the aircraft to maintain steady flight without control force from the pilot.

Why does an aircraft require more up-elevator deflection as flight speed decreases?

As flight speed decreases in level flight, the aircraft must operate at a higher angle of attack and higher lift coefficient C_L to support its weight (L = W). Because the aircraft is statically stable (dC_m/dC_L < 0), higher lift creates a nose-down moment, requiring increased up-elevator deflection (delta_e < 0) to provide the opposing nose-up trim moment.

How is the elevator control effectiveness derivative C_m_delta_e defined?

C_m_delta_e = dC_m / ddelta_e represents the change in aircraft pitching moment coefficient per unit deflection of the elevator. It is negative because deflecting the elevator downward (positive delta_e) increases tail lift, producing a nose-down (negative) pitching moment.

What is a trim tab and how does it relieve pilot control stick force?

A trim tab is a small auxiliary hinged surface on the trailing edge of the elevator. Deflecting the trim tab in the direction opposite to the elevator generates a local aerodynamic force that balances the elevator's hinge moment, holding the elevator deflected at the desired trim angle with zero stick force required from the pilot.

How does the elevator trim gradient ddelta_e / dC_L relate to the aircraft's Static Margin?

The elevator trim curve slope is directly proportional to the static margin: ddelta_e / dC_L = - (dC_m/dC_L) / C_m_delta_e = SM / |C_m_delta_e|. A steeper trim gradient indicates a more stable aircraft with a larger static margin, requiring larger elevator movements to change speed.

What is control reversal and when can it occur on high-speed aircraft?

Control reversal occurs when the aerodynamic force generated by a deflected control surface twists the flexible wing or stabilizer structure in the opposite direction. At high dynamic pressures (above the reversal speed V_rev), the structural aeroelastic twisting completely overpowers the control surface, causing the aircraft to respond in reverse.

Experimental precautions and wind tunnel safety

  • Allow the closed-loop auto-trimmer to fully settle at each pitch condition to ensure residual C_m is less than 0.0005.
  • Verify that elevator hinge gap seals are intact to avoid parasitic aerodynamic blowing through the control horn.
  • Check that elevator servo torque is adequate to overcome aerodynamic hinge moments without angular deflection drift.
  • Avoid elevator deflections beyond +/- 20 degrees where boundary layer separation on the elevator degrades linear effectiveness.

Theoretical assumptions & flow limits

  • Linear elevator control power assumes attached flow over the tailplane; high deflections cause non-linear saturation.
  • Assumes constant wing downwash gradient at the horizontal tail across the lift coefficient range.
  • Propeller slipstream effects over the tail are not simulated in this unpowered glider model test.
  • Sting interference can produce a slight nose-down pitching bias requiring tare calibration.
