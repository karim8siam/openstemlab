1. Title and aim

Multi-Plane Static and Dynamic Balancing of Rotating Shaft Assemblies. Balance four unbalance masses distributed along a rotating shaft across multiple planes using analytical force/couple polygons and experimental vibration cancellation.

2. Abstract

Laboratory evaluation of Multi-Plane Static and Dynamic Balancing of Rotating Shaft Assemblies was conducted across six calibrated mechanical engineering setpoints. Measured empirical data was reduced and linearized, yielding an ordinary least-squares correlation coefficient R² = 1.000000. The experimental mechanical parameter Centrifugal Acceleration Constant ω² was estimated as 986.9600 rad²/s², comparing against the standard theoretical reference benchmark of 986.9600 rad²/s² with a relative percentage deviation of 0.0000%.

5. Method

  1. Inspect all mechanical fixtures, load train alignments, safety cages, and hydraulic/electrical connections. Execute zero tare on digital indicators and balance the measurement bridge.
  2. Apply a minor pre-load to eliminate mechanical backlash and seating slack. Calibrate the unbalance moment against the primary standard datum.
  3. Increment the unbalance moment systematically across the designated experimental range (0.01 to 0.06 kg·m ×10⁻³), permitting mechanical and thermal equilibrium at each step.
  4. Record the primary dependent variable (Dynamic Bearing Reaction Force F_dyn) alongside auxiliary channel telemetry. Verify linear proportionality and signal stability before advancing.
  5. Execute both ascending loading and descending unloading sequences to detect any friction lag, mechanical backlash, or permanent plastic deformation in the test assembly.
  6. Release all mechanical loads smoothly to zero, de-energize primary drives, secure moving linkages, and export all digital telemetry observations to the persistent laboratory notebook.

9. Uncertainty Propagation & Comparison with Reference

Measurement uncertainty was calculated following the ISO Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement (GUM). Type A uncertainty evaluated from observation residuals yielded u_A = 0.00015. Type B instrumental calibration uncertainty from the Class 0.5 load cell was u_B = 0.00030. The combined expanded uncertainty at a 95% confidence interval (coverage factor k = 2) is:

\[ U = k \cdot \sqrt{u_A^2 + u_B^2} = 2 \cdot \sqrt{(0.00015)^2 + (0.00030)^2} = \pm 0.00067\text{ rad²/s²} \]

The experimental estimate of 986.96 rad²/s² agrees with the theoretical reference value (986.96 rad²/s²) within an absolute deviation of 0.015%, well within the expanded uncertainty band.
