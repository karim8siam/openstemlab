4. Theory and graph choice

Doppler ultrasound velocimetry is a non-invasive diagnostic technique in clinical vascular surgery, cardiology, and maternal-fetal medicine, enabling quantitative evaluation of carotid artery stenosis, deep vein thrombosis (DVT), and peripheral arterial occlusive disease. The technique exploits the classical Doppler effect: when ultrasound waves emitted by a piezoelectric crystal encounter moving acoustic reflectors (primarily circulating red blood cells), the frequency of the scattered echo is shifted relative to the transmitted carrier frequency f_0. Because the red blood cell acts first as a moving receiver and then as a moving emitter, the frequency shift occurs twice. The clinical Doppler equation is given by: Δf = [2 · f_0 · v · cos(θ)] / c = k_d · v, where Δf is the Doppler frequency shift (Hz), f_0 is the transmitted carrier frequency (typically 2 to 8 MHz), v is blood flow velocity, θ is the Doppler insonation angle between the ultrasound beam and the flow vector, c is the speed of sound in soft tissue (1540 m/s), and k_d is the Doppler sensitivity constant (Hz/(cm/s)). In clinical Doppler systems operating at f_0 = 4.0 MHz and a standard insonation angle θ = 60°, the Doppler shift falls precisely within the human audible spectrum (hundreds to thousands of Hertz), allowing clinicians to listen directly to arterial pulsatility and heart murmur turbulence. In this experiment, a calibrated Doppler string and fluid flow phantom evaluates Doppler frequency shifts across velocities from 10 to 60 cm/s.

Δf = k_d · v = 28.5000 · v (at f₀ = 4.0 MHz, θ = 60°)

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Flow velocity v (cm/s), and the dependent coordinate y represents Doppler frequency shift Δf (Hz). The gradient m directly determines Doppler Velocity Sensitivity Slope k_d, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Fill the flow phantom with certified blood-mimicking fluid (nylon scattering particles, density 1050 kg/m³, c = 1540 m/s) and purge all air bubbles.
  2. Mount the 4.0 MHz CW Doppler probe on the goniometric stage and position probe at precisely θ = 60.0° relative to the flow channel axis.
  3. With the perfusion pump halted, verify zero Doppler audio frequency shift (Δf = 0.0 Hz) and null baseline wall-filter noise.
  4. Engage the calibrated pump driver to produce steady centerline flow velocity v = 10.0 cm/s and allow 60 seconds for steady laminar flow.
  5. Execute real-time 1024-point FFT spectral analysis on the demodulated Doppler audio stream, averaging 20 spectra to extract peak frequency shift Δf (Hz).
  6. Increment flow delivery through 20.0, 30.0, 40.0, 50.0, and 60.0 cm/s, logging peak Doppler frequency shift Δf at each velocity step.
  7. Plot Doppler frequency shift Δf versus flow velocity v and determine sensitivity slope k_d via linear regression through the origin.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Maintain probe insonation angle at exactly 60.0°; angle misalignment introduces severe cosine scaling errors.
  • Verify acoustic coupling with generous ultrasound transmission gel; air gaps completely reflect the 4.0 MHz acoustic wave.
  • Set the electronic wall filter (high-pass filter) to low (~50 Hz) to eliminate vessel wall motion artifacts without clipping low-velocity blood signals.
  • Ensure blood-mimicking fluid particles remain fully suspended without gravitational settling.

The linear Doppler relation applies across laminar velocities; severe turbulent flow creates broad spectral broadening rather than a single discrete shift.
