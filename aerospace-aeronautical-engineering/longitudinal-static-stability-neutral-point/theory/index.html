Aerodynamic and physical foundations of Longitudinal Static Stability.

Longitudinal static stability is the fundamental property ensuring an aircraft naturally tends to return to its original trimmed angle of attack following an uncommanded pitch disturbance (such as an atmospheric gust). For static pitch stability, two necessary criteria must be satisfied: (1) positive trim capability (C_m0 > 0), and (2) a negative pitching moment curve slope with respect to angle of attack and lift coefficient: dC_m / dC_L < 0. If a gust increases the angle of attack, a stable aircraft must generate an automatic nose-down restoring moment (Delta C_m < 0) to pitch back down toward equilibrium. According to classical flight mechanics, the pitching moment slope depends directly on the distance between the aircraft center of gravity x_cg and the stick-fixed aerodynamic neutral point x_np: dC_m / dC_L = (x_cg - x_np) / c = x_cg / c - x_np / c. The distance between the neutral point and center of gravity, non-dimensionalized by the mean aerodynamic chord c, is defined as the Static Margin: SM = (x_np - x_cg) / c. When the center of gravity is positioned ahead of the neutral point (x_cg < x_np), the static margin is positive and the aircraft is statically stable. When x_cg moves aft to x_np, the static margin becomes zero (neutral stability). In this experiment, a complete wind tunnel model is tested with center-of-gravity positions moved systematically aft from x_cg / c = 0.20 to 0.35, plotting dC_m / dC_L versus x_cg / c to locate the neutral point x_np / c.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What are the two necessary mathematical criteria for longitudinal static stability?

1. Negative pitching moment slope: dC_m / dC_L < 0 (or dC_m / dalpha < 0), ensuring that an increase in angle of attack produces a nose-down restoring moment. 2. Positive zero-lift pitching moment: C_m0 > 0, ensuring that the aircraft can achieve a positive trim lift coefficient (C_L,trim > 0).

What is the physical meaning of the stick-fixed neutral point x_np?

The neutral point x_np is the aerodynamic center of the entire aircraft (including wing, fuselage, and tail). When the center of gravity is located precisely at x_np, the pitching moment derivative vanishes (dC_m / dC_L = 0). The aircraft possesses neutral stability—it neither resists nor amplifies pitch disturbances.

What is Static Margin and what is a typical value for transport aircraft?

Static Margin is the non-dimensional distance between the neutral point and center of gravity: SM = (x_np - x_cg) / c = -dC_m / dC_L. For commercial airliners, a positive static margin of 5% to 15% (0.05 to 0.15c) is standard, providing positive stability and comfortable control stick forces.

How does the horizontal tail contribute to longitudinal stability?

The horizontal tail is located far aft of the center of gravity on a long moment arm l_t. When the aircraft pitches up by Delta alpha, the tail generates an upward lift increment Delta L_t that exerts a powerful nose-down restoring torque about the CG, acting as the primary stabilizing element.

Why do modern fly-by-wire fighter aircraft (such as the F-16) fly with negative static margins?

Fighter aircraft deliberately operate with relaxed static stability (negative static margin, x_cg aft of x_np). An unstable airframe initiates rapid turns instantaneously without needing large control surface deflections to overcome stability, providing extreme agility. High-speed digital flight computers provide artificial stability.

What is downwash lag and how does it influence dynamic pitch damping?

Vortices shed from the wing take a finite time Delta t = l_t / V to travel back to the horizontal tail. When the aircraft is pitching, the tail experiences downwash from a previous moment, creating a phase lag that provides positive pitch rate damping (derivative C_m_q + C_m_alphadot < 0).

Experimental precautions and wind tunnel safety

  • Accurately record model wind-off weight tares at each CG location to eliminate gravitational pitch moment bias.
  • Verify that the horizontal stabilizer is rigidly mounted to prevent aeroelastic tail incidence twist under aerodynamic load.
  • Ensure the test section downwash field from the wing correctly washes over the tail without ceiling boundary layer interference.
  • Avoid operating in the non-linear high-lift regime (alpha > 8 deg) during linear stability derivative evaluation.

Theoretical assumptions & flow limits

  • Linear stability derivative dC_m/dC_L assumes attached flow and constant wing downwash gradient depsilon/dalpha.
  • Assumes stick-fixed conditions (elevator rigidly locked at delta_e = 0); stick-free stability requires hinge moment tracking.
  • Propeller slipstream or jet exhaust induction over the horizontal tail can alter the neutral point location.
  • Wind tunnel sting support induces slight tail clearance blockage requiring tare corrections.
