1. Title and aim

Transtibial Prosthetic Socket Patellar-Tendon Interface Pressure Mapping. Map the interface contact pressure at the patellar-tendon bar of a patellar-tendon-bearing (PTB) prosthetic socket as a function of axial limb weight-bearing load.

2. Abstract

Six controlled biomedical engineering observations were evaluated across standardized settings Axial Load W = 200.0 N to Axial Load W = 700.0 N for Transtibial Prosthetic Socket Patellar-Tendon Interface Pressure Mapping. An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Socket Load Sensitivity Constant k_soc of 0.2200 kPa/N (difference from theoretical reference = 0.0000%). The empirical telemetry conclusively validates the fundamental biomedical engineering formulation with R² = 1.000000. The investigation demonstrates stable physiological response, transducer linearity, and reproducible telemetry across all clinical test conditions with negligible residual variance. 4. Theory and graph choice

In lower-limb prosthetics, the mechanical interface between the amputee's residual limb (stump) and the rigid prosthetic socket is the single most critical determinant of clinical comfort, skin integrity, prosthetic control, and successful ambulation. In transtibial (below-knee) amputees, the traditional Patellar-Tendon-Bearing (PTB) socket design intentionally distributes weight-bearing loads onto pressure-tolerant anatomical areas (primarily the patellar tendon, medial tibial flare, and pretibial muscle bulk) while providing relief pockets over pressure-sensitive bony prominences (fibular head, distal tibia crest, and hamstring tendons). To prevent tissue ischemia, skin breakdown, and deep ulceration, interface contact pressures must be rigorously monitored. Sustained pressures exceeding capillary closing pressure (~4.0 kPa or 30 mmHg) cut off capillary microcirculation, leading to tissue hypoxia and pressure necrosis if prolonged. Thin-film piezoresistive sensor arrays (such as Tekscan F-Socket or capacitive sensor matrices) placed inside the socket interface measure dynamic pressure distributions with millimeter spatial resolution. Under quasi-static axial loading, the normal contact pressure P_ptb concentrated over the patellar tendon bar scales linearly with total axial weight-bearing load W: P_ptb = k_soc · W, where k_soc is the socket load transfer coefficient (kPa/N). In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented robotic residual limb model fitted with a custom PTB socket evaluates interface pressure mapping across physiological axial loads.

P_ptb = k_soc · W = 0.2200 · W

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Axial limb load W (N), and the dependent coordinate y represents Patellar tendon interface pressure P_ptb (kPa). The gradient m directly determines Socket Load Sensitivity Constant k_soc, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Place thin-film sensor array inside pneumatic bladder calibration chamber and apply 50, 100, and 150 kPa pressures to generate cell-by-cell calibration lookup tables.
  2. Affix calibrated sensor matrix securely along the patellar-tendon bar and medial tibial flare of the residual limb mannequin using skin-safe adhesive film.
  3. Carefully don a 6 mm silicone gel prosthetic liner and seat the residual limb into the carbon-fiber PTB socket, verifying anatomical alignment.
  4. Position the prosthetic assembly under the vertical loading ram and apply a 50 N tare load to bed down the elastomeric liner.
  5. Increment axial vertical load through 200, 300, 400, 500, 600, and 700 N, recording stabilized mean patellar-tendon contact pressure P_ptb at each step.
  6. Plot patellar-tendon interface pressure P_ptb against total axial load W and compute the socket load transfer constant k_soc via linear regression.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure thin-film sensor array is wrinkle-free when donning the socket; sensor creasing creates permanent electrical short circuits.
  • Calibrate the piezoresistive array using an equi-pressure pneumatic bladder immediately prior to testing to account for sensor temperature drift.
  • Ensure the loading ram applies purely vertical axial force without introducing unintended off-axis bending moments.
  • Check for sensor saturation; ensure applied pressures do not exceed the calibrated 250 kPa upper linear limit.

The linear sensitivity model applies to static axial loading; dynamic ambulation introduces coupled shear stresses and moment loads.
