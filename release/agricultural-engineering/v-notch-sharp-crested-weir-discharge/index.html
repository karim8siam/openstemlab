Agricultural assumptions and boundary condition limits

The 5/2 power law applies strictly for heads H > 0.05 m; below 5 cm, water surface tension and fluid viscosity dominate. Evaluated using a precision brass 90° V-notch plate mounted in a rectangular flume across water heads from 0.12 m to 0.28 m (H^2.5 from 0.005 to 0.045 m^2.5).

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Calibrate a 90° V-notch sharp-crested weir in an open irrigation flume and evaluate the weir discharge coefficient C_d and proportionality constant C_w. Standardized parameter settings: 0.005, 0.013, 0.021, 0.029, 0.037, 0.045 m^2.5.

01

Weir Zero Datum Setting

Fill the flume until water is exactly level with the vertex of the V-notch. Lower the vernier point gauge until its tip touches the meniscus at the notch vertex and record the zero datum reading H_0.

02

Calming Screen Alignment

Place mesh calming baffles 3.0 meters upstream of the weir to dissipate surface waves and establish uniform approach velocity.

03

Initial Flow Stabilization

Start the supply pump to establish an upstream head H = 0.120 m (H^2.5 = 0.0050 m^2.5), verifying that the nappe springs clear of the crest and is fully aerated.

04

Timed Volumetric Collection

Divert the weir discharge into the gravimetric weigh tank for exactly 60.0 seconds, measuring collected mass to calculate volumetric flow rate Q (m³/s).

05

Head Incrementation

Systematically increase pump flow across five additional stages up to H = 0.280 m (H^2.5 = 0.0450 m^2.5), logging H and Q for each trial.

06

Weir Coefficient Regression

Plot measured discharge Q against H^2.5, perform linear regression through the origin, and determine the Thomson weir discharge coefficient C_w and C_d.
