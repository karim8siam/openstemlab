FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Measure differential pressure head across a convergent-divergent Venturi constriction as a function of volumetric flow rate, verify the mechanical energy balance (Bernoulli equation), and determine the discharge coefficient C_d. Standardized parameter settings: 10.0, 20.0, 30.0, 40.0, 50.0, 60.0 L/min.

01

System Priming & Bubble Purging

Start the centrifugal pump at moderate speed and vent all air pockets from the piezometer tubes and pressure tapping manifolds until identical static levels are observed under zero flow.

02

Zero-Flow Datum Verification

Close the downstream control valve completely; verify that upstream and throat manometer levels equalize to within 0.5 mm on the metric scale.

03

Initial Flow Rate Establishment

Open the needle valve until reference volumetric flow rate Q reaches 10.0 L/min; allow flow conditions and manometric columns to achieve steady state.

04

Differential Head Logging

Record upstream piezometric level h_1 and throat level h_2, calculating differential pressure head Δh = h_1 - h_2 in centimeters of water column.

05

Flow Progression & Manometer Reading

Increment volumetric flow rate through 20.0, 30.0, 40.0, 50.0, and 60.0 L/min, recording steady-state manometric head differentials at each operational plateau.

06

Discharge Coefficient Regression Analysis

Calculate sqrt(Δh) for each observation, plot sqrt(Δh) versus Q, and extract the flow sensitivity slope S to evaluate discharge coefficient C_d.

THEORY AND UNIT OPERATIONS CONTEXT

Process engineering background & physical principles.

In chemical process engineering and fluid transport systems, closed-conduit volumetric metering is indispensable for mass balancing, reactor stoichiometry, and heat exchanger control. The classical Venturi flowmeter utilizes a gradual convergent entrance section that accelerates the flowing fluid into a cylindrical throat, followed by a gently tapered divergent diffuser designed to maximize kinetic energy recovery. Applying the incompressible Bernoulli equation with continuity between upstream pipe (diameter D_1) and throat (diameter D_2) yields theoretical volumetric flow rate Q_th = [A_2 / sqrt(1 - β^4)] · sqrt(2 · g · Δh), where β = D_2 / D_1 and Δh is differential piezometric head. Because real fluid flow experiences wall friction and viscous boundary layer dissipation, the actual flow rate Q is related to theoretical flow by the discharge coefficient C_d: Q = C_d · Q_th. For well-engineered classical Venturi meters operating at pipe Reynolds numbers above 10^4, boundary layer separation is suppressed by the gradual 5° to 7° divergent cone, yielding a nearly constant discharge coefficient between 0.96 and 0.99. In this laboratory, differential manometric head Δh is measured across a calibrated Venturi tube over a wide range of flow rates to establish flow sensitivity and evaluate discharge coefficient C_d.
