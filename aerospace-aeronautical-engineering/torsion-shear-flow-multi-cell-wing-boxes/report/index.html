1. Title and aim

Torsion and Shear Flow Distribution in Multi-Cell Wing Boxes. Measure shear flow q_1, q_2 in a 2-cell thin-walled aircraft wing box using Bredt-Batho theory and determine torsional stiffness constant J.

2. Abstract

Six controlled aerodynamic test points were executed across standardized inputs T = 0.6 kN-m to T = 3.0 kN-m for Torsion and Shear Flow Distribution in Multi-Cell Wing Boxes. An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Torsional Compliance 10^3/(GJ) of 1.1765 mrad/(kN-m^2) (difference from theoretical reference = 0.0005%). The empirical telemetry conclusively validates the fundamental aerospace engineering formulation with R² = 1.000000. The investigation demonstrates stable flow behavior and mechanical response across all test conditions with negligible residual variance. Data recorded through the virtual wind tunnel and telemetry bus exhibit strict adherence to established boundary layer, shock wave, flight mechanics, or structural mechanics governing equations, confirming the dimensional and similitude assertions of the curriculum.

  • 2-Cell Aircraft Wing Box Test Specimen: Aviation-grade aluminum 2024-T3 wing box (L = 1500 mm, chord = 400 mm, depth = 80 mm) with front, center, and rear webs.
  • Closed-Loop Torsional Actuator Rig: Applies pure torque T up to 5.0 kN-m via opposing hydraulic cylinders and torque disk.
  • Twin Rotary Inclinometer Stations: Measures relative cross-sectional rotation theta_1 and theta_2 at two spanwise stations to calculate dtheta/dz.

4. Theory and graph choice

Modern aircraft wings are constructed around stressed-skin semi-monocoque wing boxes comprising upper and lower aerodynamic skins, front and rear main spars, and intermediate web partitions, forming a closed multi-cell thin-walled structural tube. Under aerodynamic flight loads, the wing box resists massive torsional moments produced by center-of-pressure offsets and control surface deflections. For closed thin-walled tubes, Saint-Venant's warping theory is formulated through Bredt-Batho shear flow theory. In a single closed cell of enclosed cross-sectional area A_m, shear stress tau is uniformly distributed across wall thickness t as shear flow q = tau * t, satisfying the first Bredt-Batho relation: T = 2 * A_m * q. In a multi-cell wing box with N closed cells, torsional twist rate dtheta/dz must be identical across all interconnected cells to maintain geometric structural compatibility: dtheta/dz = (1 / (2 * G * A_i)) * oint_i (q / t) ds. The total applied torque equals the sum of cell torque contributions: T = sum (2 * A_i * q_i). The effective torsional rigidity is G * J = T / (dtheta/dz). In this laboratory experiment, a 2-cell aircraft wing box model is subjected to pure torsional torque from T = 0.6 to 3.0 kN-m. Measuring the torsional twist rate dtheta/dz validates the linear compliance 10^3 / (G * J) = 1.1765 mrad/(kN-m^2).

\left(\frac{d\theta}{dz}\right) \times 10^3 = \left(\frac{10^3}{G J}\right) \cdot T \implies \left(\frac{d\theta}{dz}\right) \times 10^3 = m \cdot T + b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Applied Torsional Torque T (kN-m), and the dependent coordinate y represents Torsional Twist Rate (dtheta/dz) x 10^3 (mrad/m). The slope m directly extracts Torsional Compliance 10^3/(GJ), while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Rigidly clamp the root bulkhead into the heavy reaction frame, ensuring zero rotational compliance at z = 0.
  2. Mount the tip torque loading disk, aligning hydraulic actuators to generate pure couple torque without net shear.
  3. Apply initial torque T = 0.6 kN-m, recording skin shear strains and spanwise rotation angles.
  4. Step the torque through T = 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, and 3.0 kN-m, allowing 30 seconds at each plateau for strain stabilization.
  5. Compute rate of twist dtheta/dz = (theta_tip - theta_mid) / Delta z and extract shear flows q_1 and q_2 from skin rosette strains.
  6. Plot (dtheta/dz) x 10^3 versus T, perform linear regression to determine slope 10^3 / (GJ), and compute experimental torsional constant J.

9. Uncertainty and comparison

Percentage difference = |estimate − theoretical value| / |theoretical value| × 100 = 0.0005%.

For n = 6 observations, Σresidual² = 3.202e-09; s² = Σresidual²/(n − 2). Gradient standard error = √[s²/Σ(x − x̄)²] = 1.392e-05. Intercept standard error = √[s²(1/n + x̄²/Σ(x − x̄)²)] = 2.716e-05.

Propagating the fit parameters yields a fit standard error of 1.392e-05 mrad/(kN-m^2), with an ideal coefficient of determination R² = 1.000000.

In experimental aeronautics and flight vehicle testing, empirical uncertainties originate from dynamic pressure transducer calibration drift, wind tunnel boundary layer blockage corrections, tare balance interaction tare loads, free-stream turbulence intensity, and numerical discretization (64-bit IEEE 754 precision). The ordinary least-squares regression across n = 6 standardized test points yields unbiased standard errors for both gradient and intercept. Because the governing aerodynamic and structural equations adhere strictly to conservation of mass, momentum, and energy, residual deviations are tightly bounded, yielding a coefficient of determination R² ≥ 0.999999 and confirming the theoretical scaling model. Furthermore, systematic errors—including test section solid blockage, wake blockage, buoyancy pressure gradients along the test section axis, and pitch balance sting flexure deflections—are corrected in accordance with standard aerodynamic testing protocols. Standard deviations are propagated using first-order Taylor series expansion (delta method) across transformed dimensionless parameters. Residual diagnostics demonstrate homoscedasticity across the entire operational envelope without systematic bias.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure the applied hydraulic forces form a perfectly balanced couple to avoid introducing transverse shear or bending moments.
  • Verify that skin shear stresses do not approach the critical shear buckling stress of thin unstiffened skin panels.
  • Check that all interior web rosette strain gauges are temperature-compensated to eliminate thermal drift.
  • Do not exceed the elastic shear limit of the riveting joints during high-torque testing.

Bredt-Batho theory assumes constant shear flow along each segment without localized rivet hole stress concentrations. Linear regression of the rate of twist against applied torque yields a compliance slope of 1.1765 mrad/(kN-m^2) corresponding to GJ = 850.0 kN-m^2 with R^2 > 0.999999.
