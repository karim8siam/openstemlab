Agricultural and physical foundations of Bekker Terramechanics Sinkage.

Terramechanics—the study of terrain-vehicle mechanics and off-road mobility—provides the theoretical foundation for sizing agricultural tractor drive tires, predicting wheel sinkage ruts, and minimizing motion resistance on deformable agricultural soils. Dr. M.G. Bekker in his foundational works formulated the classical semi-empirical pressure-sinkage relationship governing soils under footing loads. Under Bekker's theory, the normal pressure p exerted by a circular or rectangular bearing plate penetrating into soil is related to sinkage depth z_s by the power-law equation: p = (k_c / b + k_phi) · z_s^n, where k_c is the cohesive modulus of soil deformation, k_phi is the frictional modulus of deformation, b is the smaller dimension (width or radius) of the contact patch, and n is the dimensionless soil sinkage exponent. In logarithmic space, this fundamental relation linearizes into: ln(p) = n · ln(z_s) + ln(k), where the gradient corresponds directly to the sinkage exponent n. When n < 1, the soil displays strain-softening or typical agricultural loam behavior where incremental resistance diminishes with depth; when n > 1, the soil compacts into a denser strain-hardening state. In this experiment, a bevameter test apparatus equipped with circular penetration plates is hydraulically driven into agricultural tilled soil to record pressure-sinkage curves and extract Bekker terramechanic parameters.

ln(p) = n · ln(z_s) + ln(k) = 0.8500 · ln(z_s) + 2.1000

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What is the physical meaning of Bekker's sinkage exponent n?

The exponent n describes the rate of soil resistance buildup with sinkage: n = 1 represents perfectly linear elastic soil; n < 1 represents typical agricultural soils where resistance increases sub-linearly (strain-softening); n > 1 represents highly compressible, dry muskeg or loose sandy soils that strain-harden as they compact.

How are the cohesive modulus k_c and frictional modulus k_phi separated experimentally?

By performing pressure-sinkage tests with two circular plates of different radii (b1 and b2). Plotting the apparent modulus k against 1/b yields a straight line where the slope equals k_c and the vertical intercept equals k_phi.

How does Bekker's theory predict tractor wheel rolling resistance?

Bekker integrated soil pressure across the circumference of a sinking rigid wheel, showing that compaction rolling resistance equals R_c = b · ∫ p · z · dθ, which scales as R_c ∝ W^((2n+2)/(2n+1)) / (D^(1/(2n+1))), demonstrating that larger diameter wheels dramatically reduce sinkage and resistance.

What is a bevameter in terramechanics research?

A bevameter is a field instrument invented by M.G. Bekker that performs two tests: a plate penetration test to determine normal pressure-sinkage parameters (k_c, k_phi, n) and a shear ring/plate test to measure soil shear strength (c and phi).

How does soil compaction from tractor traffic affect crop yield?

Excessive wheel sinkage compacts subsoil macro-pores, increasing bulk density, reducing aeration and saturated hydraulic conductivity, and impeding root development, leading to stunted growth and 10% to 25% yield penalties in wheel ruts.

What is the difference between a rigid wheel and a pneumatic tire in terramechanic models?

A rigid wheel does not deflect, forcing all deformation into the soil; a pneumatic tire deflects elastically, creating an elliptical contact patch that lowers peak ground pressure when tire inflation pressure is less than critical soil ground contact pressure.

Experimental precautions and agricultural field safety

  • Ensure steady vertical penetration rate (10 mm/s) to prevent dynamic inertial or rapid pore-pressure effects.
  • Verify the reaction truck is sufficiently ballasted so it does not lift during high-load plate sinkage.
  • Conduct tests away from prior vehicle wheel tracks to test pristine, un-compacted tilled soil.
  • Ensure test plates are perfectly perpendicular to the vertical loading axis to eliminate moment errors.

Theoretical assumptions & biological limits

  • The power-law relationship applies to vertical sinkage up to 6.0 cm; at extreme sinkage, lateral soil flow causes blowout.
  • Assumes homogeneous topsoil without encountering hard stones or subsurface plow pans.
  • Neglects dynamic lug churning and slip-induced sinkage present in driven tractor drive wheels.
  • Applies to cohesive-frictional loam; saturated mud exhibits viscous hydrodynamic resistance.
