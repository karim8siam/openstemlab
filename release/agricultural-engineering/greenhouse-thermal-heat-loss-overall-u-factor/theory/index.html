Agricultural and physical foundations of Greenhouse Thermal Loss.

Controlled environment agriculture (CEA) relies heavily on greenhouses to cultivate high-value horticultural crops outside standard growing seasons. However, the high solar transmittance of greenhouse glazing materials (single or double polyethylene, polycarbonate, or glass) is accompanied by substantial thermal conductance and long-wave infrared radiation loss during cold nighttime hours. Sizing heating systems and modeling heating energy budgets require an accurate determination of the overall heat transmission coefficient U_ov (U-factor). In thermal engineering of agricultural structures, steady-state heat loss per unit envelope surface area q_h is governed by Fourier's law and boundary layer convection: q_h = U_ov · (T_in - T_out) = U_ov · ΔT. The overall coefficient U_ov integrates interior natural convection, thermal conduction through glazing and structural framing, and exterior forced convection driven by ambient wind speed. In this laboratory investigation, an experimental research greenhouse bay with double-layer inflated polyethylene film is instrumented with calibrated thermopile heat flux plates, aspirated psychrometers, and precision electrical resistance heaters to evaluate steady-state thermal loss across controlled temperature differentials.

q_h = U_ov · ΔT = 5.8000 · ΔT

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What is the physical meaning of the overall heat transfer coefficient (U-factor)?

The U-factor represents the steady-state thermal transmission rate per unit surface area per degree temperature difference across the envelope (expressed in W/(m²·°C) or W/(m²·K)). A lower U-factor indicates superior thermal insulating performance.

How does double-layer inflated polyethylene reduce greenhouse heat loss compared to single glazing?

The 5 to 10 cm dead-air space maintained between the inflated films acts as a stagnant insulating boundary layer, substantially lowering conductive-convective heat transfer and cutting heating demand by 30% to 40% compared to single glass or single poly.

How does exterior wind speed influence greenhouse U-factors?

Higher exterior wind speeds increase the external convective heat transfer coefficient (h_o) by thinning the laminar boundary layer on the outer glazing surface, significantly increasing the overall U-factor.

What is thermal radiation trapping (the greenhouse effect)?

Glazing materials are transparent to shortwave solar radiation (0.3 to 3.0 μm) which heats indoor surfaces, but largely opaque to longwave thermal infrared radiation (3.0 to 50 μm) emitted by interior surfaces, trapping heat inside the structure.

Why is structural frame heat loss significant in commercial greenhouses?

Aluminum and steel structural mullions have thermal conductivities thousands of times higher than glazing air spaces, acting as thermal bridges that can account for 10% to 20% of total envelope heat loss.

How is structural infiltration heat loss distinguished from envelope transmission loss?

Envelope transmission occurs via conduction, convection, and radiation through solid surfaces, whereas infiltration represents sensible heat lost through physical air exchange through cracks, laps, and vents, quantified as Q_inf = V_dot · ρ · C_p · ΔT.

Experimental precautions and agricultural field safety

  • Conduct all testing during nocturnal periods to prevent shortwave solar radiation and thermal lag errors.
  • Ensure greenhouse ventilation louvers and perimeter access doors are fully sealed with weatherstripping.
  • Maintain uniform air circulation with low-velocity horizontal air flow (HAF) fans to eliminate thermal stratification.
  • Avoid tests during sudden wind gusts or precipitation events that alter exterior convection dynamics.

Theoretical assumptions & biological limits

  • The linear relation assumes a constant external convective boundary layer valid under calm ambient wind (≤ 2.0 m/s).
