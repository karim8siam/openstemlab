Mathematical foundations of Laplace & Poisson Relaxation.

Elliptic partial differential equations govern static equilibrium, steady-state diffusion, and potential fields throughout physics and applied mathematics. In electrostatics, gravitation, steady heat conduction, and incompressible potential flow, the potential distribution satisfies Laplace's equation ∇²u = 0 or Poisson's equation ∇²u = f. Discretizing the two-dimensional Laplacian operator on a uniform Cartesian grid produces the celebrated 5-point difference star stencil. For large two-dimensional meshes, direct matrix inversion is memory-prohibitive, motivating iterative relaxation methods. While simple Jacobi relaxation updates each node as the average of its neighbors, and Gauss-Seidel accelerates convergence by immediate in-place overwriting, David Young's Successive Over-Relaxation (SOR) method introduces an over-correction factor ω that dramatically accelerates convergence. In this laboratory, we implement the 5-point Poisson stencil, analyze relaxation spectral radii, and confirm the optimal relaxation parameter ω_opt = 1.729.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What is the physical interpretation of Laplace's and Poisson's equations?

Laplace's equation ∇²u = 0 models steady-state conservative potential fields in source-free media: electrostatic potentials, steady thermal equilibria, irrotational fluid velocity potentials, and gravitational fields. Poisson's equation ∇²u = f incorporates localized sources or charge distributions f(x, y).

How is the 5-point discrete Laplacian stencil derived in two dimensions?

Applying second-order central difference approximations to both second partial derivatives yields ∂²u/∂x² ≈ (u_{i+1,j} - 2u_{i,j} + u_{i-1,j})/h² and ∂²u/∂y² ≈ (u_{i,j+1} - 2u_{i,j} + u_{i,j-1})/h². Summing these gives the classic 5-point star: ∇²u ≈ (u_{i+1,j} + u_{i-1,j} + u_{i,j+1} + u_{i,j-1} - 4u_{i,j})/h² + O(h²).

What is the mean value property of harmonic functions and how does it manifest in Jacobi relaxation?

Harmonic functions (solutions to ∇²u = 0) satisfy the Gauss Mean Value Theorem: the potential at any point equals the average of its values on any surrounding circle. In Jacobi relaxation for Laplace's equation, each interior grid point is updated simply as the arithmetic average of its four nearest neighbors: u_{i,j}^{k+1} = (u_{i+1,j}^k + u_{i-1,j}^k + u_{i,j+1}^k + u_{i,j-1}^k) / 4.

How did David Young derive the formula for the optimal SOR relaxation parameter ω_opt?

In his 1950 doctoral dissertation, David Young proved that for matrices possessing 'Property A' (such as 5-point stencils with red-black ordering), the eigenvalues μ of the Jacobi iteration matrix and λ of the SOR iteration matrix satisfy (λ + ω - 1)² = ω² μ² λ. Minimizing the spectral radius max |λ| with respect to ω yields Young's celebrated formula: ω_opt = 2 / (1 + √(1 - ρ_J²)).

What is the asymptotic convergence speedup of SOR over Gauss-Seidel for fine grids (h ≪ 1)?

For grid spacing h, the Jacobi spectral radius is ρ_J ≈ 1 - (π² h²)/2. Gauss-Seidel converges at rate -ln(ρ_{GS}) ≈ π² h², requiring O(N²) iterations. For optimal SOR, the spectral radius is ρ_{SOR} = ω_opt - 1 ≈ 1 - 2π h, requiring only O(N) iterations—an order of magnitude reduction in computational time.

What is Red-Black (checkerboard) Gauss-Seidel ordering and why is it important?

Standard Gauss-Seidel updates nodes in raster-scan order, creating serial dependencies. Red-Black ordering colors grid points like a chessboard: all 'red' points depend only on 'black' points, and vice versa. This allows all red points to be updated simultaneously in parallel, followed by all black points, enabling vectorization on GPUs and multicore CPUs.

Why are multigrid methods even faster than SOR for huge Poisson problems?

SOR relaxes local high-frequency error components quickly, but smooth, low-frequency error modes take thousands of iterations to propagate across the domain. Multigrid methods project residual errors onto a hierarchy of coarser grids where low frequencies appear high-frequency, solving the Poisson equation in optimal O(N) total operations.

Algorithm precautions and stability rules

  • Always verify that relaxation parameter ω lies strictly within the stable interval 0 < ω < 2 to prevent catastrophic divergence.
  • Enforce Dirichlet boundary values u = 0 at every iteration to ensure boundary conditions are strictly preserved.
  • Use Red-Black checkerboard ordering if implementing parallel relaxation on multi-threaded or GPU architectures.
  • Ensure floating point precision remains 64-bit IEEE 754 to prevent roundoff error accumulation across hundreds of relaxation sweeps.
  • Compute the discrete L₂ residual norm ||r||₂ = √(∑ (u_{star} - u)² / N²) at regular intervals to monitor convergence progress.
  • Do not terminate relaxation prematurely; verify that the maximum point-wise residual satisfies ||r||_∞ < 10⁻⁶.

Theoretical limitations

  • Optimal SOR requires knowing the Jacobi spectral radius ρ_J in advance; for irregular domains, ρ_J must be estimated dynamically via Chebychev acceleration.
  • On very fine grids (e.g. N > 1000), even SOR becomes slow, requiring geometric or algebraic Multigrid methods.
  • The 5-point Laplacian stencil is second-order accurate O(h²); higher-order stencils (such as the 9-point Mehrstellen stencil O(h⁴)) require more complex coupling.
