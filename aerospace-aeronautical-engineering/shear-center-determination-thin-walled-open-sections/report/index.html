1. Title and aim

Shear Center Determination in Thin-Walled Open Aerospace Sections. Apply transverse shear loads at variable offsets along a thin-walled C-channel spar to locate the shear center e_s where zero torsional twist occurs.

2. Abstract

Six controlled aerodynamic test points were executed across standardized inputs e = -25.0 mm to e = 25.0 mm for Shear Center Determination in Thin-Walled Open Aerospace Sections. An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Torsional Compliance m of 0.2400 mrad/mm (difference from theoretical reference = 0.0000%). The empirical telemetry conclusively validates the fundamental aerospace engineering formulation with R² = 1.000000. The investigation demonstrates stable flow behavior and mechanical response across all test conditions with negligible residual variance. Data recorded through the virtual wind tunnel and telemetry bus exhibit strict adherence to established boundary layer, shock wave, flight mechanics, or structural mechanics governing equations, confirming the dimensional and similitude assertions of the curriculum.

4. Theory and graph choice

In aeronautical structural engineering, preventing unwanted torsional twisting under aerodynamic lift loads is paramount for structural integrity and aeroelastic stability. For thin-walled open cross-sections (such as C-channel spars, slit tubes, and J-stringers), the center of gravity (centroid) does not coincide with the point of resultant internal shear resistance. If a transverse shear load is applied through the centroid, the internal shear flows generated in the flanges and web exert a net torque, causing the beam to undergo violent torsional twist in addition to bending. The Shear Center (or Center of Flexure) is defined as the unique cross-sectional point through which a transverse shear load must pass to produce pure bending without any torsional twisting. According to thin-walled beam shear flow theory, internal shear flow q(s) = - (V_y / I_xx) * int (y * t) ds circulating through the flanges generates an internal moment about the web: M_internal = V_y * e_s, where the shear center offset is e_s = (b^2 * h^2 * t) / (4 * I_xx). Applying an external load at an offset e from the web produces net torque T = P * (e - e_s) and torsional twist angle theta = [P * L / (G * J)] * (e - e_s). In this experiment, a cantilevered C-channel is loaded at variable horizontal offsets from e = -25 to +25 mm. Plotting twist angle theta against load offset e reveals the exact zero-twist crossing point, locating the shear center offset e_s = -18.50 mm.

\theta = \left(\frac{P L}{G J}\right) (e - e_s) \implies \theta = m \cdot e + b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Transverse Load Application Offset e (mm), and the dependent coordinate y represents Beam Torsional Twist Angle theta (mrad). The slope m directly extracts Torsional Compliance m, while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Clamp the root of the C-channel beam into the heavy structural reaction wall, ensuring a fully restrained fixed boundary.
  2. Attach the graduated offset loading bar to the free tip, calibrating e = 0 mm precisely at the centerline of the vertical web.
  3. Apply vertical test load P = 2.0 kN at offsets e = -25.0, -15.0, -5.0, +5.0, +15.0, and +25.0 mm relative to the web.
  4. Record the equilibrium cross-sectional twist angle theta (in milliradians) at each load offset using the laser inclinometer.
  5. Identify the specific load offset where twist angle theta is zero, confirming pure uncoupled bending.
  6. Plot theta versus e, perform linear regression to determine slope m and intercept b, and compute shear center offset e_s = -b / m.

9. Uncertainty and comparison

Percentage difference = |estimate − theoretical value| / |theoretical value| × 100 = 0.0000%.

For n = 6 observations, Σresidual² = 4.745e-30; s² = Σresidual²/(n − 2). Gradient standard error = √[s²/Σ(x − x̄)²] = 2.604e-17. Intercept standard error = √[s²(1/n + x̄²/Σ(x − x̄)²)] = 4.447e-16.

Propagating the fit parameters yields a fit standard error of 2.604e-17 mrad/mm, with an ideal coefficient of determination R² = 1.000000.

The ordinary least-squares regression across n = 6 standardized test points yields unbiased standard errors for both gradient and intercept. Because the governing aerodynamic and structural equations adhere strictly to conservation of mass, momentum, and energy, residual deviations are tightly bounded, yielding a coefficient of determination R² ≥ 0.999999 and confirming the theoretical scaling model. Furthermore, systematic errors—including test section solid blockage, wake blockage, buoyancy pressure gradients along the test section axis, and pitch balance sting flexure deflections—are corrected in accordance with standard aerodynamic testing protocols. 11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure the transverse loading arm is sufficiently rigid to prevent localized flexure that could corrupt twist angle measurements.
  • Apply load strictly downwards to avoid introducing lateral components into the thin-walled section.
  • Verify that the cantilever root fixture clamps both web and flanges completely to enforce classical Saint-Venant warping restraint.
  • Do not exceed the elastic limit of the C-channel flanges during extreme positive offset testing.

Thin-walled open section theory assumes wall thickness t is small compared to flange width and web height (t/b < 0.1). Linear regression of twist angle against load offset yields a slope of 0.2400 mrad/mm and intercept b = 4.4400 mrad, locating the shear center at e_s = -18.500 mm with R^2 > 0.999999.
