Simulation assumptions and limits

The simulated wire is exactly uniform and 100.0 cm long. End resistances, jockey resistance, heating, thermoelectric offsets and galvanometer sensitivity are omitted. The unknown resistor is fixed at 5 Ω and known resistance settings are exact. Balance lengths are rounded to 0.1 cm; resulting fit scatter reflects quantization rather than a complete measurement uncertainty. The model places unknown R = 5 Ω on the left and known S on the right. It computes l = 100R/(R + S), rounded to 0.1 cm. The fitted graph uses x = S and y = (100 − l)/l, so resistance is 1/gradient. Range endpoints are available for sensitivity exploration; real measurements should favor central balances.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine an unknown resistance using balance lengths on a metre bridge and a straight-line graph. Suggested independent settings: 2.5, 4, 5, 7.5, 10 Ω.

1

Fix the orientation

Connect unknown R in the left gap and known S in the right gap. Mark the wire zero at the unknown side and use L = 100.0 cm.

Why this step matters

The resistance-length equation depends on the side assignment.

2

Set a known resistance

Select S, starting near 5 Ω in this model to place the expected null near the midpoint.

Why this step matters

Central balance points are less sensitive to length reading error.

3

Find the null

Close the cell key and touch the jockey lightly at trial positions until galvanometer deflection passes through zero. In this simulation request the model balance reading.

Why this step matters

A null compares potentials without drawing detector current at balance.

4

Record and repeat

Record S and l to the shown resolution. Collect at least five distinct S values, preferably keeping physical balances around the central portion.

Why this step matters

Several balances support a graph and allow comparison of separate R estimates.

5

Calculate graph variables

For each reading calculate L − l and y = (L − l)/l. Fit y against S with a free intercept.

Why this step matters

This transformation makes the reciprocal unknown resistance the gradient.

6

Interpret the result

Calculate R = 1/m in Ω and compare individual values S l/(L − l). In real apparatus interchange the gap resistors and use the reversed formula, then discuss end effects and uncertainties.

Why this step matters

Consistency across settings and orientations provides a stronger check than one balance.
