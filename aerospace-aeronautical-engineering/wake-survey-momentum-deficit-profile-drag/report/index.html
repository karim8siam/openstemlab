1. Title and aim

Wake Survey and Profile Drag Determination by Momentum Deficit. Determine section profile drag coefficient c_d of an airfoil using a downstream total-head wake survey rake and the Jones-Betz momentum deficit integral.

4. Theory and graph choice

Direct mechanical balance measurements of drag are frequently contaminated by model support strut interference and wingtip end-effects. To measure section profile drag c_d (the sum of skin friction and form drag) with unmatched precision, aerodynamicists utilize the wake survey momentum deficit method. When an airfoil generates drag, viscous shear within its boundary layers creates a downstream velocity deficit—a wake where total pressure P_0w is substantially lower than the free-stream total pressure P_0_inf. Applying the momentum theorem to a fixed control volume enclosing the airfoil reveals that the net profile drag per unit span equals the total momentum deficit convected downstream across the wake. B. M. Jones formulated an exact integral relating the section drag coefficient c_d to total and static pressure measurements across the wake: c_d = 2 * int [sqrt(q_w / q_inf) * (1 - sqrt(q_w / q_inf))] d(y/c). In this laboratory experiment, a multi-tube total-pressure wake rake is positioned one chord downstream of the trailing edge of an airfoil model. By recording total pressure profiles across the viscous wake deficit for various angles of attack, students perform numerical quadrature of the Jones integral to extract the profile drag polar, observing how viscous wake widening correlates with boundary layer thickening and trailing-edge separation.

c_d = 2 \int \sqrt{\frac{q_w}{q_\infty}} \left(1 - \sqrt{\frac{q_w}{q_\infty}}\right) d\left(\frac{y}{c}\right) \implies c_d = m \cdot (\text{Deficit Ratio}) + b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Peak Dynamic Head Deficit Ratio Delta q / q_inf, and the dependent coordinate y represents Section Profile Drag Coefficient c_d. The slope m directly extracts Section Profile Drag c_d, while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Mount the wake rake exactly 1.0 chord length downstream of the model trailing edge, centered on the model wake axis.
  2. Bring wind tunnel airspeed to V_inf = 35 m/s, stabilizing free-stream dynamic pressure at q_inf = 750 Pa.
  3. Set airfoil angle of attack to alpha = 0 deg and record baseline wake total pressure deficit across all 48 channels.
  4. Adjust model incidence to achieve peak wake deficit ratios Delta q / q_inf of 0.10, 0.20, 0.30, 0.40, 0.50, and 0.60.
  5. Compute local wake dynamic pressure ratio q_w / q_inf and local velocity ratio u_w / U_inf for every wake probe.
  6. Integrate the Jones momentum deficit integrand across the wake width to calculate the section profile drag coefficient c_d.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure the wake rake span covers the entire viscous wake so that outer probes record 100% of free-stream total pressure.
  • Account for static pressure recovery in the near wake by incorporating the Betz static pressure correction if surveying x < 1.0c.
  • Verify all rake total pressure tubes are purged and free from dust or aerosol contamination prior to testing.
  • Avoid surveying deep post-stall separated wakes where massive reverse flow invalidates the unidirectional Jones equation.

The Jones formula assumes static pressure across the wake survey plane has recovered to ambient free-stream static pressure. The ordinary least-squares fit across wake deficit samples yields an empirical section drag coefficient c_d = 0.0240 matching the integrated momentum deficit with R^2 > 0.999999.
