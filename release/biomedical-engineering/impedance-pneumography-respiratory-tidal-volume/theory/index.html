Biomedical and physiological foundations of Impedance Pneumography.

Continuous non-invasive monitoring of respiratory rate and tidal volume is essential in intensive care units, neonatal incubators, sleep medicine polysomnography, and post-anesthesia recovery rooms. Transthoracic electrical impedance pneumography provides an elegant bedside method to record respiration without requiring invasive face masks or mouthpieces. The physiological principle exploits the dramatic electrical conductivity difference between air and thoracic tissues. De-aerated lung tissue and thoracic blood have relatively high electrical conductivity (saline and blood ~0.7 S/m), whereas inhaled atmospheric air has zero electrical conductivity. During inspiration, the lungs inflate with air, increasing the non-conductive alveolar gas volume and lengthening electrical current streamlines across the ribcage, which causes baseline transthoracic electrical impedance to rise. Under a four-electrode (tetrapolar) configuration, a safe, high-frequency constant AC carrier current (typically 50 kHz, 100 μA RMS, far above the neuromuscular excitation threshold) is injected through outer drive electrodes, while inner pickup electrodes detect the resulting surface voltage drop. Across normal physiological tidal volumes V_t (0.5 to 3.0 L), the peak-to-peak respiratory impedance change ΔZ scales in direct linear proportion: ΔZ = k_v · V_t. In this experiment, an instrumented impedance pneumograph is calibrated simultaneously against a digital clinical spirometer.

ΔZ = k_v · V_t = 1.3500 · V_t

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

Why is a four-electrode (tetrapolar) arrangement used instead of two electrodes in impedance pneumography?

A two-electrode setup measures the huge, variable contact impedances of the skin-electrode interfaces in series with the tissue. In a four-electrode arrangement, current is injected through outer electrodes while inner voltage-sensing electrodes draw virtually zero current, eliminating contact impedance artifacts and isolating true deep thoracic impedance.

Why is a high-frequency AC carrier (e.g., 50 kHz) used rather than DC or low-frequency current?

At 50 kHz, the current easily bypasses the capacitive impedance of the skin's stratum corneum without stinging, and high frequency prevents any risk of depolarizing cardiac or skeletal muscle membranes (avoiding ventricular fibrillation hazards).

What is the cardiogenic impedance artifact and how is it filtered?

With each cardiac cycle, blood ejected from the ventricles into the pulmonary circulation and aorta alters regional thoracic conductivity, producing small rhythmic impedance pulses (0.1 to 0.3 Ω). Because their frequency matches the heart rate (1 to 2 Hz), digital notch filtering or R-wave triggered averaging removes them.

How does lung tissue conductivity change during inhalation?

Inhalation fills the alveolar sacs with air (conductivity ≈ 0 S/m), displacing conductive blood and interstitial fluid, lengthening electrical conduction pathways through the thoracic cage, and causing total electrical impedance to increase.

What clinical limitation affects impedance pneumography in detecting obstructive sleep apnea?

In obstructive sleep apnea, the upper airway is blocked, but the patient continues to make vigorous thoracic and abdominal respiratory efforts against the obstruction. The chest expands, producing an apparent impedance signal despite zero actual airflow, creating a false-negative apnea detection.

What is Electrical Impedance Tomography (EIT)?

EIT is an advanced extension of impedance pneumography that uses a ring of 16 to 32 electrodes around the chest to reconstruct cross-sectional 2D dynamic images of regional lung ventilation and perfusion in real time.

Experimental precautions and patient safety

  • Instruct subject to breathe steadily without torso twisting or posture shifts that alter baseline ribcage geometry.
  • Ensure outer current-drive electrodes are spaced at least 5 cm away from inner voltage-pickup electrodes.
  • Verify that spirometer turbine calibration is zeroed and calibrated with a certified 3.00 L calibration syringe.
  • Verify carrier current remains under IEC 60601-1 patient safety auxiliary current limits (under 500 μA at 50 kHz).

Theoretical assumptions & biological limits

  • The linear sensitivity model applies across normal physiological tidal volumes (0.5 to 3.0 L).
  • Calibrations shift significantly if patient changes posture (e.g., transitioning from supine to upright sitting).
  • Does not differentiate between ribcage expansion and abdominal diaphragm excursions without dual-band montage.
  • Severe pulmonary edema (fluid in the lungs) lowers baseline impedance and attenuates respiratory swings.
