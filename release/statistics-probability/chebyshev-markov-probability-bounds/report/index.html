1. Title and aim

Empirical Verification of Markov's and Chebyshev's Inequalities. Evaluate empirical tail probabilities across diverse probability distributions, compare empirical tail frequencies against Markov's and Chebyshev's upper bounds, and verify the inverse-square power law.

4. Theory and graph choice

Concentration inequalities provide rigorous mathematical bounds on the probability that a random variable deviates from its expected value. Markov's inequality establishes a fundamental bound for non-negative random variables using first moments, while Chebyshev's inequality uses the second moment (variance) to bound two-sided tail probabilities by 1/k², regardless of the distribution's shape or symmetry. In this laboratory, we generate large-scale random samples, measure empirical tail frequencies across multiple distribution families, verify the non-parametric validity of Chebyshev's bound, and quantitatively extract the inverse-square power law exponent α = -2.000. In mathematical statistics, empirical data science, and modern computational engineering, understanding the asymptotic sampling properties and theoretical behavior of Empirical Verification of Markov's and Chebyshev's Inequalities provides crucial guarantees. Rigorous experimental validation connects foundational probability laws with observable empirical phenomena, ensuring that statistical inference remains valid under finite sample sizes and real-world data collection constraints.

ln(P_bound) = -2.0000 · ln(k)

The statistical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent variable x represents Natural Log of Deviation Multiple ln(k), and the dependent variable y represents Natural Log of Upper Bound ln(1/k²). The slope m directly extracts Chebyshev Power Exponent α, while the intercept b accounts for residual baseline offsets. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver bias. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights distribution scale factors and rate parameters directly as slope gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential outlier leverage across the experimental domain, verifying that the empirical variance matches theoretical bounds.

5. Method

  1. Select a continuous distribution with verified finite variance (e.g., standard normal N(0, 1) or standardized Exponential) and draw N = 100,000 observations.
  2. For each sample observation, compute the standardized absolute deviation Z_i = |X_i - μ| / σ and sort the array to facilitate rapid tail searching.
  3. Set k = 1.5. Calculate the theoretical Chebyshev upper bound P_bound = 1 / k² = 1 / (1.5)^2 = 0.4444. Count the empirical fraction of observations exceeding 1.5σ.
  4. Confirm that applying Markov's inequality to the non-negative variable Y = (X - μ)² yields P(Y ≥ k²σ²) ≤ E[Y] / (k²σ²) = σ² / (k²σ²) = 1/k², deriving Chebyshev's inequality.
  5. Increment k across standardized values k ∈ {1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5, 4.0}, recording the steep decay of both empirical tail frequencies and the theoretical 1/k² bound.
  6. Fit a linear regression model to the coordinates (x = ln(k), y = ln(1/k²)). Confirm that the empirical gradient m = -2.0000 exactly verifies the inverse-square concentration scaling.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure the sample variance is positive and non-zero before standardizing deviation scores.
  • Use large samples (N ≥ 50,000) when estimating empirical tail frequencies at large k to avoid counting noise.
  • Note that Chebyshev's bound is two-sided; for one-sided bounds, Cantelli's inequality 1/(1+k^2) is sharper.
  • Perform replication across multiple independent pseudorandom seeds to verify that estimator variance and bias bounds are invariant to initial generator states.

Chebyshev's bound is conservative for normal data: at 3σ, normal tail is 0.27% while Chebyshev gives 11.11%. The empirical regression of ln(P_bound) versus ln(k) confirms the theoretical inverse-square concentration scaling α = -2.000 with R² = 1.000000.
