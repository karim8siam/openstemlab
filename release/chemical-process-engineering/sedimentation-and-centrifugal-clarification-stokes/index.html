FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Investigate batch hindered sedimentation kinetics for concentrated calcium carbonate (CaCO_3) slurries across suspension concentrations C_s from 10.0 to 60.0 g/L in a 1.0-meter graduated settling column, track interface subsidence rates, determine the Kynch hindered settling velocity slope S, evaluate free Stokes settling velocity v_0, and apply the Coe–Clevenger method to size an industrial continuous gravity thickener. Standardized parameter settings: 10.0, 20.0, 30.0, 40.0, 50.0, 60.0 g/L.

01

Slurry Stock Preparation & Dispersion

Weigh pure precipitated CaCO_3 (d_p = 25.0 μm, ρ_p = 2710 kg/m^3); disperse in DI water in homogenization tank at 1200 RPM for 15 minutes.

02

Initial Concentration Batch Charging (C_s = 10.0 g/L)

Charge the 1.0 m settling column with 10.0 g/L slurry; invert column 5 times to establish uniform initial suspension throughout height z_0 = 100.0 cm.

03

Interface Subsidence Tracking & Constant-Rate Velocity

Place column in vertical stand; initiate optical interface logging; record interface height z(t) versus elapsed time t at 5 s intervals.

04

Hindered Velocity Extraction

Extract constant settling velocity v_s from the initial linear slope of the subsidence curve: v_s = -dz/dt.

05

Concentration Progression Across Slurries

Repeat homogenization, charging, and subsidence logging across C_s = 20.0, 30.0, 40.0, 50.0, and 60.0 g/L, recording v_s for each batch.

06

Kynch Settling Kinetics & Clarifier Sizing

Plot v_s versus solids concentration C_s; fit linear regression to evaluate slope S and v_0; calculate Coe–Clevenger thickener area.

THEORY AND UNIT OPERATIONS CONTEXT

Process engineering background & physical principles.

Sedimentation and gravity clarification are foundational mechanical separation unit operations utilized extensively in chemical manufacturing, mineral beneficiation, hydrometallurgy, water clarification, and municipal wastewater treatment. In dilute suspensions (solids volume fraction φ < 0.005), individual spherical particles settle independently without hydrodynamic particle-particle interaction according to Stokes' law: v_0 = g · d_p^2 · (ρ_p - ρ_f) / (18 · μ). However, in concentrated industrial slurries (φ > 0.05), particles settle as a collective cloud or blanket with a sharp, visible interface between the descending clarified supernatant and the concentrated suspension—a phenomenon known as hindered settling or zone settling. According to the classical sedimentation theory formulated by George J. Kynch in 1952, the local settling velocity v_s of particles in a suspension depends solely on the local solids volume concentration: v_s = f(C_s). As solids concentration increases, the upward displacement velocity of displaced fluid through the narrowing interstitial pores increases, and viscous drag is amplified by surrounding particles, causing settling velocity to decrease sharply. In the moderate concentration regime (C_s = 10.0 to 60.0 g/L), the Kynch hindered settling velocity decreases linearly with solids concentration: v_s = v_0 - S · C_s, where v_0 = 120.0000 cm/min is the unhindered terminal velocity intercept and S = 1.8500 (cm/min)/(g/L) is the Kynch hindered settling slope. Alternatively, the non-linear relationship across wide concentration ranges is described by the Richardson–Zaki correlation: v_s = v_0 · ε^n = v_0 · (1 - φ)^n, where ε is suspension voidage and n is the Richardson–Zaki exponent (~4.65 in laminar creeping flow). In continuous industrial gravity thickeners, the solid flux method (Coe & Clevenger / Yoshioka analysis) utilizes these batch settling velocity curves to identify the critical solids handling capacity (minimum flux G_min) and determine the required cross-sectional clarifier area A = F_s / G_min. In this laboratory experiment, a 1.0-meter precision glass settling column equipped with automated optical array interface detection tracks suspension interface subsidence across CaCO_3 concentrations from 10.0 to 60.0 g/L to evaluate v_s, determine S = 1.8500 (cm/min)/(g/L), and calculate continuous thickener dimensions.
