Agricultural assumptions and boundary condition limits

Page's model is purely empirical; its parameters n_p and k_p do not directly represent fundamental physical transport properties like Fickian diffusivity. Evaluated using rough paddy rice (initial moisture 24.5% d.b., equilibrium moisture 9.2% d.b.) dried under forced air at 50°C and 1.2 m/s face velocity.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Investigate the forced-air thin-layer drying kinetics of agricultural cereal grain, determine moisture ratio decay over time, and evaluate Page model drying parameters. Standardized parameter settings: 2.3026, 2.7081, 3.2189, 3.6889, 4.0943, 4.4998 ln(min).

01

Drying Tunnel Warm-Up & Stabilization

Turn on the tunnel blower and PID heater, setting airflow to 1.2 m/s and air temperature to 50.0°C, allowing the empty duct to reach steady thermal equilibrium for 20 minutes.

02

Initial Moisture & Tare Setup

Determine initial grain moisture content M_0 via rapid moisture tester (24.5% d.b.). Tare the suspended perforated tray on the continuous balance.

03

Monolayer Grain Loading

Evenly spread 300.0 g of moist paddy rice in a single kernel layer across the tray, immediately inserting it into the drying air stream and zeroing the timer at t = 0.

04

Continuous Mass Loss Acquisition

Log instantaneous tray mass m(t) at 10, 15, 25, 40, 60, and 90 minutes as moisture evaporates into the conditioned air stream.

05

Moisture Ratio Transformation

Convert instantaneous mass into dry-basis moisture M_t, calculate Moisture Ratio MR = (M_t - M_e) / (M_0 - M_e), and compute the double logarithm ln[-ln(MR)].

06

Page Model Regression

Plot ln[-ln(MR)] against ln(t), perform linear regression, and extract Page's drying exponent n_p and drying rate constant k_p.
