1. Title and aim

Paddy Rubber-Roll Husker Differential Speed & Head Rice Yield Recovery. Evaluate the dehusking efficiency, brown rice breakage percentage, and head rice recovery yield in an agricultural rubber-roll paddy husker as a function of roller speed differential.

4. Theory and graph choice

Paddy rice milling converts raw harvested paddy (rough rice) into polished white rice for human consumption through two sequential mechanical unit operations: dehusking (removal of the fibrous protective outer hull to produce brown rice) and whitening/polishing (removal of the bran layer and germ). The rubber-roll husker is the global industry standard machine for paddy dehusking, offering superior hulling efficiency with minimal grain breakage compared to obsolete steel hullers or emery stone discs. The machine consists of two resilient vulcanized rubber rollers rotating in opposite directions at different peripheral velocities (typically a fast roller at 10 to 12 m/s and a slow roller at 8 to 9 m/s, yielding a differential velocity ratio of 1:1.20 to 1:1.30). As paddy kernels enter the narrow clearance between the rollers, the slower roller retards one side of the husk while the faster roller strips the opposite side, exerting a pure shearing force that peels off the loose hull without crushing the fragile starch endosperm. If the speed differential Δv = v_fast - v_slow is too low, unhusked paddy passes through, lowering efficiency. If Δv is too high, excessive friction generates heat, wears the expensive rubber rolls rapidly, and fractures kernels, depressing Head Rice Yield (HRY). In this laboratory, an instrumented pilot rubber-roll husker calibrates hulling metrics across varying differential speeds.

η_h = b_h · Δv + η_0 = 12.5000 · Δv + 42.0000

5. Method

  1. Inspect rubber roller surfaces for even wear and hardness (Shore A 85 to 90 durometer). Set the roll clearance to exactly 0.8 mm using a thickness feeler gauge.
  2. Set slow roller peripheral velocity to v_slow = 8.0 m/s and fast roller to v_fast = 9.5 m/s, establishing an initial speed differential Δv = 1.5 m/s.
  3. Feed exactly 1000.0 g of clean, graded paddy rice through the vibrating hopper over 60 seconds into the husking nip.
  4. Collect the discharge from the aspirator column, separating the three fractions: separated husks, dehulled brown rice (m_brown), and unhusked paddy (m_unhusked).
  5. Calculate dehusking hulling efficiency η_h = [m_brown / (m_brown + m_unhusked)] × 100%, and screen brown rice through the slotted sieve to determine broken rice percentage.
  6. Increment fast roller speed across five additional stages to achieve Δv = 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5, and 4.0 m/s, plotting η_h versus Δv to extract sensitivity b_h.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Never operate rubber rolls without paddy feeding; contact between rotating rubber faces generates extreme friction that burns and melts the rubber within seconds.
  • Ensure paddy is free from stones, gravel, and metal by passing through a pre-cleaner destoner prior to husking.
  • Check roller parallelism across the entire width using feeler gauges; tapering clearance causes uneven hulling and localized roller grooving.
  • Maintain uniform paddy feed across the full 50 mm roll width to ensure uniform wear.

Rubber rolls soften as operational temperature rises (> 60°C), slightly shifting the effective friction coefficient and hulling efficiency during prolonged continuous runs. Evaluated using long-grain paddy rice (moisture 13.0% d.b.) at constant rubber roll clearance of 0.8 mm across differential speeds from 1.5 to 4.0 m/s.
