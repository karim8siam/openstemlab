FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Calibrate electronic distance measurement (EDM) phase-shift carrier waves against a multi-pillar national baseline, determine instrument-prism addition constant and scale factor according to ISO 17123-4. Standardized parameter settings: 50.0, 100.0, 150.0, 200.0, 250.0, 300.0 m.

01

Total Station Setup & Precise Forced Centering

Mount total station onto baseline Master Pillar P1 using forced-centering tribrach; level instrument using electronic dual-axis tilt compensator to < 3 arcseconds.

02

Atmospheric Parameter Acquisition & PPM Input

Measure ambient air temperature T, atmospheric pressure P, and humidity; compute atmospheric correction factor in parts per million (ppm); enter into total station software.

03

Target Prism Alignment at Successive Pillars

Mount corner-cube prism on successive calibrated pillars (P2 = 50.0 m, P3 = 100.0 m, P4 = 150.0 m, P5 = 200.0 m, P6 = 250.0 m, and P7 = 300.0 m); align optical sighting target directly toward instrument telescope.

04

EDM Distance Measurement (Face I & Face II)

Sight prism center; trigger fine-measurement EDM mode; record slope distance, horizontal distance, and vertical zenith angle in both Face Left and Face Right to eliminate index errors.

05

Multi-Pillar Distance Cycle Execution

Acquire five independent distance readings per pillar station; calculate mean measured distance D_meas for each baseline span.

06

Additive Constant & Scale Error Linear Regression

Plot measured distance D_meas against true baseline distance D_true; execute least-squares linear regression to determine additive constant k_0 (intercept) and scale factor s (slope - 1.0).

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

In civil infrastructure geomatics, boundary cadastral surveying, high-rise construction layout, and tunnel breakthrough alignment, electronic total stations combine digital theodolite angle measurement with electro-optical Electronic Distance Measurement (EDM). EDM instruments emit an amplitude-modulated near-infrared gallium arsenide (GaAs) laser or LED carrier wave (wavelength λ ≈ 650 to 850 nm) directed toward a retro-reflecting glass corner-cube prism positioned at the target point. The retro-prism reflects the incoming beam parallel to its entrance path back to the total station receiver telescope. An internal digital phase detector compares the transmitted and returned modulation waveforms, measuring the residual phase delay Δφ to calculate two-way travel distance: D = 0.5 · (n·λ + Δφ·λ/2π), where integer cycle ambiguity n is resolved by stepping across multiple modulation frequencies (e.g. 15 MHz, 150 kHz, 15 kHz). Real-world EDM observations are subject to systematic errors: (1) Zero-point additive constant (k_0), comprising internal instrument electro-optical center offset plus the mechanical-optical center offset of the glass prism (typically -30 mm or 0 mm); and (2) Scale factor error (s), caused by atmospheric refractivity variations (temperature, barometric pressure, relative humidity) altering the speed of light c = c_0 / n_air, as well as master quartz crystal oscillator clock drift. Standardized under ISO 17123-4, calibration along a multi-pillar baseline determines the additive constant and verifies scale factor linearity. In this laboratory investigation, measured EDM distances across baseline distances from 50.0 to 300.0 m are evaluated to calibrate scale factor slope = 1.0000 and additive prism offset = +0.0020 m (2.0 mm).
