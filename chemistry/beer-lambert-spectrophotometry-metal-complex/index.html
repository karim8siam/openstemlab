Physical chemistry assumptions and metrological limits

["Beer-Lambert's law applies strictly to monochromatic radiation and non-interacting chromophores at concentrations below 0.01 M.", 'Stray radiant energy and detector noise cap the linear dynamic range at absorbances above 1.5 AU.', 'Presence of strong competing ligands (such as EDTA, oxalate, or fluoride) can sequester iron and suppress complex formation.'] The quantitative model evaluates the [Fe(phen)₃]²⁺ complex at λ = 510.0 nm across six calibration concentrations from 10.0 to 80.0 μM in a matched 1.000 cm cuvette at 25.00 °C. The measured absorbances are [0.111, 0.222, 0.389, 0.555, 0.722, 0.888] AU. Ordinary least-squares regression yields an empirical slope m = 0.011102 AU/μM and Y-intercept b = 0.000086 AU with an exceptional coefficient of determination R² = 0.9999993. The extracted molar absorption coefficient is ε = 11,102 L·mol⁻¹·cm⁻¹ (0.0168% difference from the authoritative reference standard 11,100 L·mol⁻¹·cm⁻¹). Replicate blank measurements (s_blank = 0.0018 AU) establish an experimental Limit of Detection LOD = 0.54 μM (0.030 mg/L Fe) and Limit of Quantitation LOQ = 1.62 μM (0.091 mg/L Fe), confirming ideal adherence to Beer-Lambert's law.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Verify Beer-Lambert's law and determine the molar absorption coefficient (ε = 11,100 L·mol⁻¹·cm⁻¹), limit of detection (LOD = 0.54 μM), and limit of quantitation (LOQ = 1.62 μM) of the tris(1,10-phenanthroline)iron(II) complex ([Fe(phen)₃]²⁺) at 510.0 nm across concentrations from 10.0 to 80.0 μM at 25.0 °C. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 10.0, 20.0, 35.0, 50.0, 65.0, 80.0 μM.

01

Primary Standard Preparation and Stock Solution Synthesis

Accurately weigh 0.0702 g of primary standard ferrous ammonium sulfate hexahydrate (Fe(NH₄)₂(SO₄)₂·6H₂O, M = 392.14 g/mol) on the analytical balance. Dissolve in 50 mL deionized water containing 2.0 mL concentrated sulfuric acid, quantitatively transfer to a 500.00 mL volumetric flask, and dilute to the mark with deionized water to establish a 3.580 × 10⁻⁴ M (20.00 ppm Fe) stock solution.

02

Formulation of the Calibration Standard Series

Pipette aliquots of iron stock solution into a series of six 50.00 mL volumetric flasks to achieve final concentrations of 10.0, 20.0, 35.0, 50.0, 65.0, and 80.0 μM. To each flask, add 1.0 mL of 10% w/v hydroxylamine hydrochloride (to reduce any trace Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺), 5.0 mL of 1.0 M sodium acetate buffer (pH 4.5), and 5.0 mL of 0.25% w/v 1,10-phenanthroline monohydrate solution.

03

Complex Development and Thermal Equilibration

Dilute each volumetric flask to the 50.00 mL calibration mark with deionized water, invert twenty times to homogenize, and allow the intense orange-red ferroin complex ([Fe(phen)₃]²⁺) to develop fully at 25.00 °C for 15 minutes. Prepare a reagent blank containing all reagents except the iron standard.

04

Spectrophotometer Initialization and Monochromator Alignment

Turn on the dual-beam spectrophotometer and allow 30 minutes for thermal lamp stabilization. Set the monochromator to 510.0 nm with a 1.0 nm spectral slit width. Fill both matched cuvettes with deionized water and execute a zero-transmittance and 100% transmittance baseline calibration.

05

Sequential Absorbance Acquisition

Rinse the sample cuvette twice with the test solution, fill to 80% capacity, wipe exterior optical windows with lens paper, and seat in the sample cell holder against the reagent blank in the reference beam. Measure and record the steady-state absorbance A₅₁₀ to three decimal places for each standard in ascending concentration order.

06

Linear Least-Squares Regression & Metrological Limits

Plot absorbance A₅₁₀ versus iron concentration c (μM). Perform ordinary least-squares regression to determine the calibration slope m = ε·b and verify compliance with Beer's law (R² ≥ 0.999). Evaluate the standard deviation of blank measurements (s_blank) and compute the Limit of Detection (LOD = 3.3 · s_blank / m) and Limit of Quantitation (LOQ = 10 · s_blank / m).
