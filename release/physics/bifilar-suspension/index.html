Simulation assumptions and limits

The model assumes a rigid uniform slender rod, equal massless inextensible strings, symmetric attachment points, matching upper and lower separation, a horizontal equilibrium rod and small isolated torsional motion. It ignores string torsional stiffness, damping, attachment inertia, finite rod width, mode coupling and support movement. The reference g is 9.80665 m/s squared. Generated readings and accelerated animation cannot reproduce the alignment difficulties or measurement uncertainty of a physical suspension. A finite-width bar requires the appropriate inertia formula; the half-separation convention must be checked before comparing with any other apparatus. Equal, parallel strings are attached symmetrically about the centre of mass. The model uses the small-yaw relation T = 2π√[IL/(Mga²)]. It assumes negligible string mass, rod width, damping and support compliance. The simulated target inertia is Mℓ²/12. Angles above 5° cannot be recorded.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the moment of inertia of a uniform slender rod about its vertical centre-of-mass axis from bifilar oscillations at different string lengths. Suggested independent settings: 0.3, 0.45, 0.6, 0.75, 0.9 m.

1

Record rod properties

Record M = 0.500 kg, D = 0.500 m and theoretical I = MD squared/12. Identify the vertical axis through the centre.

Why this step matters

The theoretical comparison must refer to the same axis as the motion.

2

Align the suspension

Set equal string lengths L. Keep the rod horizontal, with upper and lower attachment separations both 0.200 m and centred about the rod midpoint.

Why this step matters

This establishes equal parallel strings with a = 0.100 m, matching the formula.

3

Excite one small mode

Twist the rod only a few degrees about its central vertical axis and release without a push. Avoid lateral swinging and rocking.

Why this step matters

The small-angle theory assumes predominantly torsional motion.

4

Time 20 full oscillations

Start at a chosen orientation moving in one direction and stop after 20 returns in the same direction. Record the generated time; repeat if available.

Why this step matters

Using a fixed phase prevents half-cycle counting and spreads endpoint timing uncertainty over many cycles.

5

Vary string length

Use at least five well-spaced values of L while retaining the same rod and attachment spacing. Recheck equal lengths and horizontality each time; calculate T = mean time/20 and T squared.

Why this step matters

Only L should vary when measuring the T squared versus L relationship.

6

Fit and compare

Fit T squared = mL + b. Calculate I = mMga squared/(4pi squared), inspect intercept and residuals, and compare with 0.0104167 kg m squared with suitable uncertainty discussion.

Why this step matters

The gradient supplies the measured-model estimate; a comparison needs assumptions and uncertainty to be meaningful.
