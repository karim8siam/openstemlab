Physical chemistry assumptions and metrological limits

['Conductometric solubility determination is limited to salts with solubilities between 10⁻³ M and 10⁻⁶ M; salts more soluble than 10⁻³ M require extended Debye-Hückel activity corrections, while salts less soluble than 10⁻⁶ M (such as AgI) produce signals below the solvent water baseline.', 'Trace ionic contamination in glassware or reagents produces large relative errors due to the extremely low ionic strength of the analyte solution.', 'Dissolution kinetics can become slow at lower temperatures (< 15 °C), requiring extended equilibration times to avoid undersaturation artifacts.'] The thermodynamics of sparingly soluble salt dissolution is governed by the van 't Hoff relationship: ln(K_sp) = -(ΔH°_sol / R)(1/T) + ΔS°_sol / R. For silver chloride, dissolution is endothermic (ΔH°_sol = +65.50 kJ·mol⁻¹) due to the high crystal lattice enthalpy (916 kJ/mol) relative to ion hydration enthalpies (-850 kJ/mol). Because the saturated solution is at virtually infinite dilution (ionic strength I ≈ 1.3 × 10⁻⁵ M, activity coefficient γ_± > 0.995), molar conductivity equals the limiting molar conductivity Λ_m°(T) = 138.25 [1 + 0.0220(T - 25.0)] S·cm²·mol⁻¹. Saturated solution conductivity κ_sat increases from 1.352 μS/cm at 20 °C to 6.148 μS/cm at 45 °C. After subtracting the ultrapure water background κ_w, solubility product values track the van 't Hoff equation with R² > 0.99999, yielding an experimental enthalpy of dissolution agreeing within 0.01% of the certified literature benchmark.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the solubility product constant (K_sp) and standard enthalpy of dissolution (ΔH°_sol) of silver chloride (AgCl) across a range of temperatures using high-precision electrolytic conductometry. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 20.0, 25.0, 30.0, 35.0, 40.0, 45.0 °C.

01

Precipitation & Extensive Washing of Silver Chloride

Precipitate silver chloride by slowly mixing stoichiometric amounts of 0.100 M AgNO₃ and 0.100 M KCl under dim red safety lighting to prevent photolytic decomposition. Wash the precipitate six consecutive times by centrifugation and decantation with boiling ultrapure water until the supernatant exhibits zero chloride conductivity.

02

Solvent Background Conductivity Calibration

Rinse the clean platinized conductivity cell thoroughly with ultrapure water. Fill the thermostatic reaction vessel with 100.0 mL of pure water and record the solvent background conductivity κ_w across the temperature range 20.0 °C to 45.0 °C (0.045 μS/cm to 0.070 μS/cm).

03

Saturated Slurry Preparation & Cell Assembly

Transfer the washed AgCl precipitate into the double-walled conduction cell containing 100.0 mL of ultrapure water. Insert the dip-type conductivity cell and calibrated thermistor probe, ensuring the electrode plates are completely submerged.

04

Thermostatic Equilibration at Each Temperature

Set the circulating water bath to the lowest test temperature (20.00 ± 0.02 °C). Engage magnetic stirring at 300 RPM for 20 minutes to achieve complete heterogeneous dissolution equilibrium. Stop stirring for 15 seconds to allow solid particles to settle.

05

Specific Conductivity Acquisition Across Series

Log the steady-state specific conductivity κ_sat on the 1.0 kHz bridge. Increment the bath temperature in steps of 5.0 °C (25.0 °C, 30.0 °C, 35.0 °C, 40.0 °C, 45.0 °C). At each plateau, equilibrate for 15 minutes and record the steady-state conductivity.

06

Solubility Product & Enthalpy Extraction

Subtract the water background (κ_AgCl = κ_sat - κ_w). Calculate molar solubility s = (1000 × κ_AgCl) / Λ_m°(T) and solubility product K_sp = s². Plot ln(K_sp) versus 1000/T, perform linear least-squares regression, and extract ΔH°_sol from the slope (-ΔH°_sol / 1000 R).
