FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine Manning's roughness coefficient (n) and Chezy's coefficient (C) for smooth and artificially roughened open channels under steady uniform flow conditions across variable bed slopes. Standardized parameter settings: 0.01, 0.015, 0.02, 0.025, 0.03, 0.035 m^(2/3).

01

Flume Bed Slope Setting & Datum Calibration

Adjust motorized flume jacks to target longitudinal bed slope S; verify slope with digital inclinometer; zero vernier depth gauges to flume bed.

02

Uniform Flow Establishment

Start recirculation pump; adjust discharge Q and tailwater gate until water surface is parallel to channel bed (constant depth y_0 at all stations).

03

Normal Depth & Cross-Sectional Geometry Logging

Measure normal depth y_0; compute wetted area A = B · y_0, wetted perimeter P = B + 2y_0, and hydraulic radius R = A / P.

04

Discharge & Mean Velocity Determination

Record steady discharge Q; compute cross-sectional mean velocity v = Q / A; calculate hydraulic parameter R^(2/3) · S^(1/2).

05

Systematic Slope and Flow Variation

Repeat procedure across six systematic hydraulic parameter levels (0.010, 0.015, 0.020, 0.025, 0.030, and 0.035 m^2/3).

06

Manning n & Chezy C Regression Computation

Plot v versus R^(2/3) · S^(1/2); determine linear slope 1/n; calculate Manning's n = 1 / slope; determine equivalent Chezy coefficient C = R^(1/6) / n.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

In open channel hydraulic design—governing storm drainage canals, aqueducts, river floodways, and natural waterways—the resistance to gravity-driven steady uniform flow is parameterized by empirical friction coefficients. Formulated by Irish engineer Robert Manning in 1889, the Manning equation expresses mean flow velocity as v = (1/n) · R^(2/3) · S^(1/2), where v is mean cross-sectional velocity (m/s), n is Manning's roughness coefficient (s/m^(1/3)), R = A / P is hydraulic radius (wetted flow area A divided by wetted perimeter P), and S is channel bed slope (equal to energy slope S_f in steady uniform flow). Manning's n embodies surface roughness of channel boundaries, form resistance from sand ripples or dunes, vegetation drag, and channel planform sinuosity. Smooth painted steel or planed timber exhibits n ≈ 0.010 to 0.012 s/m^(1/3); smooth troweled concrete has n ≈ 0.013 to 0.015 s/m^(1/3); gravel-bed mountain streams range from n = 0.030 to 0.050 s/m^(1/3); and heavily vegetated floodplains exceed n = 0.080 s/m^(1/3). In this laboratory flume experiment, uniform flow depth y_0 is maintained across varying bed slopes S and discharges Q. Plotting mean velocity v against the compound hydraulic geometric parameter R^(2/3) · S^(1/2) directly yields the reciprocal of Manning's roughness coefficient (1/n), enabling precise verification of concrete flume boundary resistance.
