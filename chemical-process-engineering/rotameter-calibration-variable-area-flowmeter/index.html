FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Calibrate a tapered glass tube variable-area rotameter using primary gravimetric catch-and-weigh standards, evaluate the linear float height response, and determine calibration constant K_rot and float discharge coefficient. Standardized parameter settings: 5.0, 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 25.0, 30.0 kg/min.

01

Vertical Alignment & Parallax Setup

Level the rotameter tube vertically using a precision bubble level; align the optical sensor with the float top metering edge to eliminate parallax errors.

02

Zero Datum & Valve Pre-Purge

Open the needle valve to flush air bubbles; close the valve and verify the float rests firmly at the zero datum mark (h = 5.0 mm).

03

Initial Flow Rate Throttling

Crack open the control valve until the float rises to approximately h = 37.5 mm; divert effluent into the gravimetric tank to establish m_dot = 5.0 kg/min.

04

Gravimetric Catch-and-Weigh Timing

Collect liquid mass over a synchronized 120-second interval, recording exact accumulated mass Δm and computing true mass flow rate m_dot = Δm / Δt.

05

Calibration Curve Step Progression

Increment flow rate to advance float height through nominal targets of 70.0, 102.5, 135.0, 167.5, and 200.0 mm, recording gravimetric flow rates at each step.

06

Linear Calibration Regression Analysis

Plot float height h versus gravimetric mass flow rate m_dot, fit a linear regression, and calculate calibration constant K_rot.

THEORY AND UNIT OPERATIONS CONTEXT

Process engineering background & physical principles.

In chemical pilot plants and industrial process skid packages, variable-area flowmeters (commonly known as rotameters) provide robust, self-indicating, and cost-effective volumetric flow metering without requiring external electrical power. A rotameter consists of a precision-bored, vertically oriented tapered glass or metal tube containing an internal free-floating plumb-bob or sphere. Fluid enters from the bottom, exerting upward hydrodynamic drag and buoyancy forces that lift the float until they exactly balance the downward gravitational weight of the float. Because the tube cross-sectional area expands linearly with elevation, the annular flow area between the float perimeter and the tube wall widens as the float rises: A_ann(h) ≈ π D_0 (h · tan α), where D_0 is the base diameter and α is the half-angle of taper. At equilibrium, the buoyant submerged weight of the float remains constant, meaning differential pressure across the float is virtually fixed regardless of flow rate, while the annular flow area adjusts proportionally to fluid throughput. Consequently, the equilibrium float height h displays an exceptionally linear response to volumetric flow rate Q. In this laboratory experiment, a precision glass rotameter is calibrated against primary gravimetric mass standards across 5 to 30 kg/min to determine the linear calibration factor K_rot and evaluate float drag dynamics.

h = K_rot · m_dot + h_0 = 6.5000 · m_dot + 5.0000
