FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the bulk specific gravity (oven-dry and saturated surface-dry), apparent specific gravity, and water absorption percentage of coarse aggregate according to ASTM C127 / BS EN 1097-6. Standardized parameter settings: 1000.0, 1400.0, 1800.0, 2200.0, 2600.0, 3000.0 g.

01

Sample Washing & Immersion Saturation

Wash approximately 3 kg of 20 mm aggregate to remove surface dust; immerse completely in water at 23.0 ± 2.0 °C for 24 ± 4 hours to saturate internal pores.

02

Submerged Buoyant Mass Determination

Transfer saturated aggregate into the wire basket; immerse into the water tank; shake gently to release air bubbles, and record submerged mass C to 0.1 g.

03

Saturated Surface-Dry (SSD) Conditioning

Remove aggregate from basket; roll in a large absorbent cloth until all visible surface water films are removed while particles retain damp interior; immediately record SSD mass B.

04

Oven Drying to Constant Mass

Transfer SSD aggregate to a shallow tray; dry in oven at 110 ± 5 °C for 24 hours; cool in a desiccator to room temperature.

05

Oven-Dry Mass Measurement

Weigh the bone-dry aggregate and record dry mass A to 0.1 g.

06

Specific Gravity & Absorption Calculation

Compute Bulk Specific Gravity (Dry) = A / (B - C), Bulk Specific Gravity (SSD) = B / (B - C), Apparent Specific Gravity = A / (A - C), and Absorption = [(B - A) / A] x 100%.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

Specific gravity and water absorption are fundamental physical properties of coarse aggregates directly governing concrete mix proportioning, yield calculations, and batch water corrections. Coarse aggregates possess permeable internal pores (voids accessible to water) and impermeable sealed pores. When aggregate is immersed in water for 24 ± 4 hours, capillary action saturates the accessible pore system. In the Saturated Surface-Dry (SSD) condition, all internal permeable pores are filled with water while the external particle surface is completely dry. If aggregate is batched into a mixer in a dry state, it absorbs water from the cement paste, drastically reducing fresh workability and effective water-cement ratio; conversely, wet aggregate with free surface moisture contributes excess water, lowering hardened compressive strength. Under ASTM C127, aggregate mass is weighed in three distinct moisture states: oven-dry mass A, saturated surface-dry mass B, and submerged buoyant mass C using a wire basket suspended in a water tank. In this laboratory experiment, coarse limestone aggregate is tested across sample sizes from 1000 to 3000 g to determine specific gravity metrics and quantify the water absorption coefficient.
