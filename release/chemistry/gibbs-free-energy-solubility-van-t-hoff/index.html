Thermodynamic Determination of Gibbs Free Energy, Enthalpy, and Entropy of Dissolution via van 't Hoff Solubility Analysis

Determine the standard molar Gibbs free energy (ΔG°), enthalpy (ΔH°), and entropy (ΔS°) of dissolution for borax (disodium tetraborate decahydrate) by measuring its temperature-dependent solubility product via titrimetric analysis with standard hydrochloric acid, linearizing the thermodynamic equilibrium with the van 't Hoff equation, and extracting the state functions using ordinary least-squares regression. Physical chemistry assumptions and metrological limits

The standard van 't Hoff linear model assumes that the molar enthalpy of dissolution ΔH° and entropy ΔS° remain constant over the 30–55 °C interval, and that the activity coefficient product γ_±³ remains close to unity. In reality, ionic strength increases from 0.17 M to 0.51 M across this temperature range, which causes a modest ~1.5% suppression of activity that is effectively averaged out by ordinary least-squares regression. The physical simulation model calculates the temperature-dependent solubility product K_sp and titrant volume V_HCl using the integrated van 't Hoff relation ln K_sp = −(ΔH° / R) · (1/T) + (ΔS° / R). Thermodynamic benchmark constants for borax dissolution: standard molar enthalpy of dissolution ΔH° = 110.00 kJ/mol (110000 J/mol); standard molar entropy of dissolution ΔS° = 330.00 J/(mol·K); universal gas constant R = 8.31446 J/(mol·K). Solubility relation: s = (K_sp / 4.0)^(1/3) in mol/L. Titrimetric calculation: a 5.00 mL (0.00500 L) aliquot contains n_borax = s · 0.00500 mol. Stoichiometric reaction with standardized 0.500 M HCl: 1 mol tetraborate consumes 2 mol HCl, so n_HCl = 2 · s · 0.00500 = 0.0100 · s mol. Required titrant volume: V_HCl = n_HCl / M_HCl = (0.0100 · s) / 0.500 L = 0.0200 · s L = 20.0 · s mL. Across equilibration temperatures from 30.0 °C to 55.0 °C (303.15 K to 328.15 K), V_HCl spans 3.37 mL to 10.22 mL. Scaled regression coordinates: x = 1000.0 / T (in K⁻¹), y = ln(K_sp). Theoretical regression slope: m_slope = −ΔH° / (1000 · R) = −110000 / 8314.46 = −13.230 K. Theoretical intercept: b = ΔS° / R = 330.0 / 8.31446 = 39.690. Ordinary least-squares linear regression isolates ΔH° = −m_slope · (1000 · R) with R² = 1.00000; pre-warmed filtering pipettes prevent premature crystallization in the pipette barrel.

Analytical chemistry apparatus & tools.

Constant-Temperature Circulating Water Bath (±0.05 °C)

Maintains a constant thermal plateau for the borax saturation flask with continuous magnetic stirring to establish true thermodynamic solid-liquid equilibrium.

Borax Saturation Flask and Magnetic Stirrer

Contains an aqueous slurry of disodium tetraborate decahydrate with substantial excess solid phase present, ensuring 100% saturation at all temperatures.

Pre-Warmed Volumetric Transfer Pipettes (5.00 ± 0.01 mL)

Equilibrated in a drying oven at the bath temperature and fitted with a cotton wool filter plug to sample clear saturated solution without drawing in solid crystals or inducing thermal shock precipitation.

Precision 50 mL Class-A Glass Burette (±0.05 mL)

Dispenses standardized 0.5000 M hydrochloric acid titrant dropwise to determine the exact neutralization volume of dissolved tetraborate ions.

NIST-Calibrated Digital Thermometer (±0.01 °C)

Directly measures the temperature of the saturated borax slurry inside the flask at the instant of aliquot extraction.

Bromocresol Green pH Indicator Solution

Provides a sharp, highly visible color transition from blue (basic borate) to yellow (acidic boric acid) at pH 4.0, signalling the stoichiometric titration endpoint.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the standard molar Gibbs free energy (ΔG°), enthalpy (ΔH°), and entropy (ΔS°) of dissolution for borax (disodium tetraborate decahydrate) by measuring its temperature-dependent solubility product via titrimetric analysis with standard hydrochloric acid, linearizing the thermodynamic equilibrium with the van 't Hoff equation, and extracting the state functions using ordinary least-squares regression. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 30.0, 35.0, 40.0, 45.0, 50.0, 55.0 °C.

01

Slurry Preparation and Thermal Equilibration

Prepare a saturated slurry by adding approximately 25 g of pure borax to 100 mL of deionized water in an Erlenmeyer flask. Immerse in the water bath set to the assigned temperature (30.0 °C). Stir vigorously for at least 15 minutes to establish solid-liquid phase equilibrium, verifying that excess solid remains at the bottom.

02

Stirring Interruption and Sediment Settling

Turn off the magnetic stirrer and allow the suspension to stand quietly for 2 minutes to permit dense undissolved borax crystals to settle to the bottom, leaving a clear supernatant liquid.

03

Filter Plugged Pipetting of Saturated Aliquot

Insert a small wad of clean surgical cotton wool into the tip of a clean 5.00 mL volumetric pipette that has been pre-warmed in a water bath to avoid premature crystallization. Rapidly draw up exactly 5.00 mL of clear supernatant liquid. Remove the cotton plug, wipe the exterior tip, and transfer into a 125 mL titration flask containing 25 mL deionized water.

04

Titration with Standardized Hydrochloric Acid

Add 3 drops of bromocresol green indicator to the aliquot, producing a distinct blue color. Fill the Class-A burette with standardized 0.5000 M HCl. Titrate dropwise under continuous swirling until the solution transitions through green to a permanent bright yellow endpoint (pH 4.0). Record the titrant volume V_HCl to ±0.01 mL.

05

Multi-Temperature Series Execution

Increase the water bath temperature to 35.0, 40.0, 45.0, 50.0, and 55.0 °C. At each plateau, equilibrate for 15 minutes, allow crystals to settle, filter a fresh 5.00 mL aliquot, and titrate in duplicate with standardized HCl.

06

van 't Hoff Regression and State Function Derivation

Compute molar solubility s = (0.500 · V_HCl) / (2 · 5.00) = 0.050 · V_HCl, and solubility product K_sp = 4 s³. Calculate 1000/T (K⁻¹) and ln(K_sp). Perform ordinary least-squares regression of ln(K_sp) vs 1000/T. Extract ΔH° = −m_slope · (1000·R), ΔS° = b · R, and compute ΔG° = ΔH° − T ΔS° at 298.15 K.
