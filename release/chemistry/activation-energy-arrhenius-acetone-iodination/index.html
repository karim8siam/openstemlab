Physical chemistry assumptions and metrological limits

The Arrhenius equation represents an empirical idealization assuming temperature-independent activation energy over modest thermal spans (ΔT ≤ 30 K). Over broader thermal intervals, transition state theory reveals that the pre-exponential factor possesses intrinsic temperature dependence (A = (e·k_B·T / h)·exp(ΔS‡ / R)), causing minor curvature (dE_a/dT = R = 8.314 J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹). At temperatures exceeding 50 °C, experimental error is exacerbated by volatile acetone evaporation (vapor pressure > 68 kPa) and competitive acid-catalyzed aldol condensation of acetone into diacetone alcohol and mesityl oxide, which slightly perturbs ketone activity. At temperatures below 15 °C, water condensation on optical cuvette windows and prolonged reaction times (> 4 minutes) enhance atmospheric oxygen oxidation of iodide back to triiodide, distorting the zero-order depletion endpoint. The physical simulation engine numerically integrates the multi-step kinetic differential equations governing the acid-catalyzed iodination of acetone across the experimental domain from 20.0 °C (293.15 K) to 45.0 °C (318.15 K). The true phenomenological Arrhenius parameters embedded within the model are activation energy E_a = 84.14 kJ·mol⁻¹ (84,140 J·mol⁻¹) and pre-exponential frequency factor A = 1.541094 × 10¹⁰ L·mol⁻¹·s⁻¹. Fixed baseline experimental concentrations: [acetone]₀ = 1.000 M, [H⁺]₀ = 0.500 M, and limiting molecular iodine [I₂]₀ = 0.00100 M (1.00 mM). Under these pseudo-zero-order conditions, the constant consumption rate is r₀ = −d[I₂]/dt = k(T)·[acetone]₀·[H⁺]₀ = 0.500·k(T). The optical decolorization time to baseline extinction (A ≤ 0.005 at 465 nm) satisfies Δt = [I₂]₀ / r₀ = 0.00200 / k(T). For bath temperatures spanning 20.0 °C, 25.0 °C, 30.0 °C, 35.0 °C, 40.0 °C, and 45.0 °C, the simulated second-order rate constants evaluate to 1.569 × 10⁻⁵, 2.800 × 10⁻⁵, 4.901 × 10⁻⁵, 8.425 × 10⁻⁵, 1.423 × 10⁻⁴, and 2.365 × 10⁻⁴ L·mol⁻¹·s⁻¹, generating stopwatch decolorization intervals of 127.44 s, 71.43 s, 40.81 s, 23.74 s, 14.05 s, and 8.46 s, respectively. Linear regression of ln(k) against reciprocal absolute temperature (1000/T in K⁻¹) yields an ideal slope m = −E_a / (1000·R) = −10.120 K and intercept ln(A) = 23.458 with R² ≥ 0.9999, isolating the activation energy with an analytical accuracy within 0.025% of the theoretical value.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

To determine the reaction orders with respect to acetone, hydronium ion, and molecular iodine, evaluate the rate constants across the temperature range from 20.0 °C to 45.0 °C, and calculate the Arrhenius activation energy (E_a) and pre-exponential factor (A) for the acid-catalyzed iodination of acetone using optical spectrophotometry and zero-order iodine decolorization timing. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 20.0, 25.0, 30.0, 35.0, 40.0, 45.0 °C.

01

Reagent Preparation and Standardization

Prepare and standardize stock solutions: 4.000 M acetone in deionized water, 1.000 M hydrochloric acid, and 0.00500 M molecular iodine in 0.050 M potassium iodide matrix.

02

Thermal Conditioning of Optical Cell Holder

Configure the circulating water bath and optical cell holder to the target thermal setpoint (20.0 °C) and allow 15 minutes for complete thermodynamic equilibration.

03

Reaction Cocktail Formulations

Dispense 2.50 mL of acetone stock, 5.00 mL of 1.00 M HCl stock, and 1.50 mL of deionized water into a clean, dry reaction cuvette (total volume 9.00 mL before iodine addition).

04

Thermal Verification and Stirring Activation

Insert the cuvette into the thermostatic holder, activate the Teflon magnetic micro-stirrer at 400 rpm, and verify thermal stability with the platinum RTD sensor.

05

Kinetic Triggering and Decolorization Timing

Rapidly inject 1.00 mL of 0.00500 M I₂ stock solution (final total volume 10.00 mL, [acetone]₀ = 1.00 M, [H⁺]₀ = 0.50 M, [I₂]₀ = 0.00100 M), simultaneously latching the digital timer.

06

Multi-Temperature Kinetic Profiling

Monitor absorbance at 465 nm until complete decolorization occurs (absorbance baseline A ≤ 0.005); record elapsed time Δt, clean cuvette, and repeat across 25.0 °C to 45.0 °C in 5.0 °C increments.
