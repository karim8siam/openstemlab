1. Title and aim

Surface Electromyography (sEMG) Root-Mean-Square & Muscle Fatigue Shift. Measure the median frequency shift and root-mean-square (RMS) amplitude of surface EMG signals during sustained isometric biceps brachii muscle contraction as a function of contraction duration.

4. Theory and graph choice

Surface electromyography (sEMG) records the spatial-temporal summation of motor unit action potential trains (MUAPTs) propagating along skeletal muscle sarcolemmas during voluntary muscle activation. In rehabilitation engineering, sports biomechanics, and ergonomic assessment, sEMG provides an objective non-invasive biomarker of localized muscle fatigue. When a muscle sustains a constant submaximal isometric contraction (e.g., holding 50% of maximum voluntary contraction, MVC), anaerobic metabolism leads to intramuscular lactic acid accumulation, intracellular H⁺ ion buildup, and extracellular K⁺ accumulation. These biochemical shifts impair sarcolemmal excitability and reduce the Muscle Fiber Conduction Velocity (MFCV) from approximately 4.5 m/s down to 2.5 m/s. Because the power spectral density (PSD) of the surface EMG signal scales proportionally with conduction velocity, muscle fatigue manifests as a progressive downward shift of the spectral energy toward lower frequencies. The median frequency (MDF)—the frequency that divides the power spectrum into two equal halves—exhibits a steady, linear decline across sustained contraction time t: MDF = -k_f · t + MDF_0. Concurrently, motor unit recruitment and synchronization increase, causing the root-mean-square (RMS) amplitude to rise. In this laboratory experiment, sEMG signals from the biceps brachii are analyzed via Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) during a 60-second isometric hold to evaluate the spectral fatigue rate k_f.

MDF = -k_f · t + MDF_0 = -0.6500 · t + 95.0000

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Contraction time t (s), and the dependent coordinate y represents Median frequency MDF (Hz). The gradient m directly determines Spectral Fatigue Rate k_f, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Shave and abrade skin over the biceps brachii belly with alcohol; place bipolar electrodes parallel to fiber orientation with 20 mm inter-electrode distance.
  2. Perform two 3-second maximal isometric contractions; record peak force to establish the 50% MVC target level.
  3. Instruct subject to maintain steady 50% MVC forearm flexion against the visual feedback load indicator for 60 seconds.
  4. Segment raw sEMG into 1.0-second epochs and compute power spectral density (PSD) using 1024-point Hanning-windowed FFT.
  5. Extract MDF at 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, and 60 seconds by finding the frequency where cumulative power equals 50% of total spectral power.
  6. Plot MDF versus contraction time t, fitting linear regression to calculate the spectral fatigue rate k_f in Hz/s.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Align electrodes strictly parallel to the longitudinal axis of muscle fibers to prevent spatial filtering distortion.
  • Ensure subject maintains strictly constant isometric force; force fluctuations alter motor unit recruitment.
  • Avoid placing electrodes over the muscle motor point (innervation zone) where bidirectional action potentials collide.
  • Allow at least 15 minutes of rest between trials to ensure full metabolic clearance of muscle metabolites.
