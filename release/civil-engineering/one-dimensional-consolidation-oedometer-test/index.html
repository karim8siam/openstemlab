FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the one-dimensional consolidation parameters—Compression Index (C_c), Recompression Index (C_r), Preconsolidation Pressure (σ'_p), and void ratio curve (e - log σ')—of saturated cohesive clay according to ASTM D2435 / BS 1377-5. Standardized parameter settings: 50.0, 100.0, 200.0, 400.0, 800.0 kPa.

01

Specimen Trimming & Initial Characterization

Carefully trim an undisturbed clay core into the greased brass ring using a soil lathe and wire saw; strike ends flush; weigh ring and moist soil to 0.01 g; record initial height H_0 = 20.0 mm.

02

Oedometer Cell Assembly

Place saturated lower porous stone and filter paper in the cell; seat ring with soil; place top filter paper, upper porous stone, and loading cap; center in loading frame.

03

Seating Load & Water Inundation

Apply an initial seating load of 5.0 kPa; fill cell with water to prevent specimen desiccation; allow thermal and swelling equilibration for 1 hour; zero LVDT.

04

Incremental 24-Hour Loading Schedule

Apply sequential load increments with LIR = 1.0 (50, 100, 200, 400, and 800 kPa); maintain each load for exactly 24 hours to achieve 100% primary consolidation.

05

Time-Settlement Acquisition

Log vertical compression ΔH at standardized time intervals (0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8, 15, 30 min, 1, 2, 4, 8, 24 hr) for each load step.

06

Dismantling & Final Void Ratio Calculation

Unload specimen; remove ring; dry soil in oven at 110 °C to determine dry mass M_s; compute height of solids H_s and equilibrium void ratio e at each pressure; plot e versus log10(σ') to extract C_c.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

When heavy civil structures (such as skyscrapers, bridge piers, and highway embankments) are constructed over saturated cohesive clay deposits, building loads are initially carried entirely by the incompressible pore water, generating excess pore water pressure Δu. Over months, years, or decades, this excess pore pressure dissipates as water slowly squeezes out through microscopic clay pores under hydraulic gradients, transferring stress to the solid mineral skeleton and causing time-dependent primary consolidation settlement. Formulated by Karl von Terzaghi in 1925, one-dimensional consolidation is evaluated in the laboratory using the oedometer (consolidometer) test (ASTM D2435). An undisturbed cylindrical clay specimen is confined inside a rigid brass floating ring (typically 50 to 75 mm diameter, 20 mm initial height) between porous stones that permit double drainage. The rigid ring imposes zero lateral strain (ε_x = ε_y = 0), forcing all volumetric strain into vertical settlement: ε_v = ΔH / H_0. Axial consolidation pressure σ' is applied in standard load-increment ratios (LIR = 1.0, doubling stress every 24 hours: 25, 50, 100, 200, 400, 800 kPa). Plotting equilibrium void ratio e against log10(σ') yields the classic e - log σ' curve, characterized by an initial flat recompression curve below the preconsolidation stress σ'_p and a steep linear Virgin Compression Line defined by the Compression Index C_c = -de / d(log10 σ'). In this laboratory, consolidation tests are conducted across stress levels up to 800 kPa to evaluate C_c and calculate foundation settlement.
