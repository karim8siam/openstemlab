Physical chemistry assumptions and metrological limits

['The moving boundary method requires a following electrolyte that satisfies both the Kohlrausch mobility criterion (u_lead > u_foll) and gravitational density stability.', 'Excessive electrical current (> 10 mA) induces thermal Joule heating, creating radial temperature gradients that produce parabolic convective boundary distortion.', 'Volume corrections for electrode reactions and solvent transport become significant at higher electrolyte concentrations (> 0.5 M), requiring dilatometric calibration.'] The moving boundary method operates under the Kohlrausch regulating condition: because the slower following cation (Cd²⁺) has lower mobility than the leading cation (H⁺), continuity of current establishes a steeper electric field in the following phase (E₂ > E₁), creating an active restoring force that continuously sharpens the boundary against molecular diffusion. The swept volume V of the capillary increases strictly linearly with elapsed time t: V = [60,000 · I · t_+ / (F · c)] · t. For 0.1000 M HCl at 25.00 °C under a constant current of 5.000 mA, the boundary sweeps volume at a steady rate of 0.02568 cm³/min (25.68 μL/min). Linear least-squares regression of swept volume against elapsed time yields an empirical cation transference number t_+ = 0.8260, confirming the extraordinary dominance of the Grotthuss proton-hopping mechanism in aqueous acid conduction.

Analytical chemistry apparatus & tools.

Moving Boundary Electrophoresis Tube

Vertical calibrated glass capillary (cross-sectional area A = 0.03142 cm², internal radius 1.00 mm) enclosed in a water jacket regulated at 25.00 ± 0.05 °C.

High-Precision Digital Galvanostat

Solid-state constant-current power source delivering an unvarying current of 5.000 ± 0.001 mA despite progressive changes in column electrical resistance.

In Situ Cadmium Generating Anode

High-purity metallic cadmium rod positioned at the base of the capillary to generate the slower following Cd²⁺ cation boundary (Cd → Cd²⁺ + 2e⁻).

Silver-Silver Chloride Non-Gassing Cathode

Ag/AgCl wire gauze electrode housed in the upper reservoir that absorbs current without generating hydrogen gas bubbles (AgCl + e⁻ → Ag + Cl⁻).

Precision Optical Cathetometer

Telescopic traveling microscope with calibrated reticle crosshairs and digital vernier scale (resolution ±0.01 mm) for tracking the refractive index boundary.

Digital Microsecond Chronometer

Precision quartz digital electronic timer interfaced to the galvanostat to measure elapsed migration time with ±0.01 second accuracy.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the cation transference number (t_+) of hydrogen ions (H⁺) in hydrochloric acid solutions as a function of current and concentration using the precision moving boundary method. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 3.0, 6.0, 9.0, 12.0, 15.0, 18.0 min.

01

Apparatus Cleaning & Cadmium Electrode Activation

Clean the precision bore capillary with warm chromic acid cleaning solution, followed by extensive rinsing with triple-distilled deionized water. Polish the cadmium anode mechanically with fine emery paper to remove oxide films and seat it firmly in the bottom electrode socket.

02

Column Filling & Air Bubble Exclusion

Fill the capillary carefully with standardized 0.1000 M HCl solution, ensuring no air bubbles adhere to the tube walls or the cadmium electrode face. Mount the Ag/AgCl cathode in the upper reservoir and seal the system.

03

Thermostatic Equilibration at 25.00 °C

Circulate constant-temperature water through the outer cooling jacket at 25.00 ± 0.05 °C for 15 minutes to dissipate Joule heating and establish steady radial temperature profiles.

04

Boundary Formation & Galvanostat Initiation

Engage the galvanostat at constant current I = 5.000 mA and start the chronometer. Anodic oxidation of cadmium immediately produces a dense, localized layer of CdCl₂. The refractive index boundary between colorless HCl and denser CdCl₂ becomes sharply defined within 60 seconds.

05

Cathetometer Tracking Across Time Increments

Align the crosshairs of the traveling cathetometer with the sharp boundary meniscus. Record the boundary position at 3.00-minute intervals (3.00, 6.00, 9.00, 12.00, 15.00, and 18.00 min), converting linear displacement into swept capillary volume V in μL and cm³.

06

Linear Regression & Transference Number Calculation

Plot swept volume V (cm³) against elapsed time t (min). Perform linear least-squares regression to extract slope dV/dt. Calculate the cation transference number t_+ = (slope × F × c) / (60000 × I) and evaluate the anion transference number t_- = 1 - t_+.
