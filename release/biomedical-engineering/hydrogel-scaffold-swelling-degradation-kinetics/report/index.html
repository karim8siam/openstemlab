1. Title and aim

Biodegradable Hydrogel Scaffold Hydrolytic Degradation Kinetics. Quantify the hydrolytic degradation rate constant k_d and mass loss kinetics of a poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) / PEG hydrogel tissue engineering scaffold in PBS at 37.0 °C.

4. Theory and graph choice

In regenerative medicine and tissue engineering, synthetic and natural biodegradable hydrogel scaffolds provide three-dimensional porous architectures that guide cell adhesion, proliferation, differentiation, and extracellular matrix deposition. An ideal tissue engineering scaffold must exhibit synchronized degradation kinetics: as implanted cells synthesize endogenous structural matrix (e.g., collagen and glycosaminoglycans), the artificial scaffold should degrade hydrolytically or enzymatically at an equivalent rate, transferring mechanical load seamlessly to the developing neotissue without inducing toxic degradation products or chronic foreign body reactions. Poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) and cross-linked poly(ethylene glycol) (PEG) hydrogels degrade primarily via non-enzymatic hydrolytic cleavage of ester bonds in their polymer backbone. In bulk-degrading hydrophilic hydrogel networks, water penetrates the matrix rapidly, causing uniform ester cleavage throughout the entire volume. During the initial linear erosion phase (days 1 to 14), the residual dry mass percentage M_res follows pseudo-zero-order degradation kinetics: M_res(t) = -k_d · t + M_0, where k_d is the daily degradation rate constant (%/day) and M_0 is initial effective dry mass. In this experiment, fabricated porous hydrogel scaffolds incubated in sterile phosphate buffered saline (pH 7.40) at physiological 37.0 °C are evaluated gravimetrically to quantify degradation kinetics and scaffold stability.

M_res = -k_d · t + M_0 = -3.2000 · t + 98.0000

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Incubation time t (days), and the dependent coordinate y represents Residual dry mass M_res (%). The gradient m directly determines Degradation Rate Constant k_d, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Fabricate porous PLGA-PEG scaffolds via solvent casting/particulate leaching, lyophilize for 24 hours, and record initial dry mass W_0 (~50.00 mg) on analytical balance.
  2. Prepare 10 mL sterile phosphate buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.40) aliquots in sealed centrifuge tubes and pre-warm to 37.0 °C in orbital incubator.
  3. Submerge individual scaffolds into PBS tubes and incubate at 37.0 °C with continuous orbital shaking at 50 RPM.
  4. At incubation intervals of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, and 12 days, harvest duplicate scaffold specimens and record media pH to detect acidic buildup.
  5. Gently rinse harvested scaffolds three times in deionized water to remove crystallized buffer salts, freeze at -80 °C, and lyophilize for 48 hours to complete dryness.
  6. Weigh residual dry mass W_t, calculate residual percentage M_res = (W_t / W_0) · 100%, plot M_res versus time t, and evaluate degradation rate k_d.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Change PBS incubation media weekly or when pH drops below 7.2 to avoid artificial accelerated autocatalytic degradation.
  • Ensure thorough deionized water rinsing to completely eliminate buffer salt crystallization artifacts.
  • Verify lyophilizer condenser operates below -45 °C and vacuum below 0.1 mbar to guarantee complete moisture sublimation.
  • Handle delicate degraded scaffolds with silicone-tipped tweezers to prevent mechanical matrix fragmentation.

The linear zero-order degradation model applies to the initial mass loss phase (first 14 days); late-stage fragmentation exhibits non-linear mass loss.
