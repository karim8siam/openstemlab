FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Perform constant-pressure vacuum filtration on an aqueous calcium carbonate suspension, record filtrate volume V as a function of time t, and determine the specific cake resistance α and filter medium resistance R_m using Ruth's parabolic filtration model. Standardized parameter settings: 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0 L.

01

Slurry Preparation & Homogenization

Prepare 10.0 L of 5.0 wt% calcium carbonate slurry in deionized water; agitate at 400 RPM for 20 minutes to achieve uniform particle dispersion.

02

Vacuum System Stabilization

Start the vacuum pump and adjust the vacuum regulator until receiver differential pressure reads exactly ΔP = 60.0 kPa.

03

Filter Leaf Pre-Wetting & Positioning

Pre-wet the polypropylene cloth with clean water; immerse the leaf vertically into the agitated slurry tank and simultaneously open the vacuum valve (t = 0).

04

Filtrate Volume & Time Logging

Record elapsed time t at cumulative filtrate volume intervals of 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, and 3.0 L on the graduated cylinder.

05

Cake Recovery & Moisture Determination

Extract the leaf from the slurry, apply dry vacuum for 60 seconds to dewater the cake, scrape the cake into a tared dish, and dry in an oven at 105 °C to determine dry mass c.

06

Ruth Parabolic Regression Analysis

Calculate t/V for each volume interval, plot t/V versus V, and extract slope S and intercept B to evaluate specific cake resistance α and medium resistance R_m.

THEORY AND UNIT OPERATIONS CONTEXT

Process engineering background & physical principles.

Solid–liquid filtration is an indispensable mechanical separation unit operation in chemical processing, mineral ore beneficiation, water clarification, and pharmaceutical crystallizer product recovery. In cake filtration, a slurry of insoluble solid particles is forced under pressure across a porous filter medium (woven cloth or membrane). While the medium initially captures particles, a porous permeable bed of accumulated solids—the filter cake—rapidly builds up on the cloth surface, becoming the dominant filtration medium. Flow of filtrate liquid through the growing porous cake obeys Darcy's law applied in series with the filter cloth. Burrows F. Ruth (1933) formulated the foundational parabolic filtration model for constant-pressure operation: dV/dt = [A^2 · ΔP] / [μ · (c · α · V + R_m · A)], where V is cumulative filtrate volume, A is filter area, ΔP is applied pressure drop, μ is filtrate dynamic viscosity, c is dry cake mass per unit filtrate volume, α is the average specific cake resistance (m/kg), and R_m is filter medium resistance (m^-1). Integrating this differential equation under constant differential pressure ΔP yields Ruth's celebrated linear relation: t / V = (K_p / 2) · V + B, where slope S = K_p / 2 = (μ · c · α) / (2 · A^2 · ΔP) and intercept B = (μ · R_m) / (A · ΔP). In this laboratory experiment, a constant vacuum (-60.0 kPa) is applied to filter an aqueous calcium carbonate slurry across a 150 mm circular filter leaf, logging t vs V across 0.5 to 3.0 L to evaluate slope S and determine specific cake resistance α.

t/V = S · V + B = 18.5000 · V + 4.2000

Evaluated using a 5.0 wt% CaCO_3 slurry (dry cake mass c = 52.6 g/L filtrate) filtered across a circular leaf filter (diameter 150 mm, area A = 0.01767 m^2) under constant vacuum ΔP = 60.0 kPa. Linear regression models t/V versus V to extract S = 18.5000 s/L^2 and B = 4.2000 s/L.
