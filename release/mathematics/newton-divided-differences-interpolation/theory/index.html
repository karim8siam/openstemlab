Mathematical foundations of Newton Divided Differences.

Newton's divided difference formulation provides an elegant, recursive alternative to the Lagrange interpolation representation. In scientific computing, experimental data often arrives sequentially in real-time streams; modifying a Lagrange interpolant to accommodate a newly acquired datum demands recalculating every cardinal basis function. In contrast, Newton's representation expresses the interpolant in terms of triangular basis polynomials with coefficients extracted from a divided difference tableau. Furthermore, the divided difference operator provides a direct discrete analogue to the continuous derivative through the Hermite-Genocchi identity. In this investigation, we construct the divided difference tableau for high-precision test functions and verify the theoretical invariance of the leading divided difference coefficient.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What is the key advantage of Newton's divided difference form over Lagrange interpolation?

Newton's form allows incremental updating: adding a new node (x_{n+1}, y_{n+1}) requires computing only one additional diagonal entry in O(n) time, whereas the Lagrange form requires recomputing all basis polynomials from scratch in O(n²) time.

How does the divided difference relate to the derivatives of a smooth function?

By the Genocchi-Hermite formula and generalized Rolle's theorem, if f ∈ C^n[a, b], there exists an intermediate point ξ ∈ (min x_i, max x_i) such that f[x_0, x_1, ..., x_n] = f^{(n)}(ξ) / n!.

Why is the divided difference operator symmetric under permutations of its arguments?

Because the unique interpolating polynomial P_n(x) is independent of the ordering of the nodes, its leading coefficient f[x_0, ..., x_n] is invariant under any permutation of {x_0, ..., x_n}.

What happens to the divided difference f[x_0, ..., x_n] when all nodes coalesce to a single point x*?

As all nodes x_i → x*, the divided difference converges smoothly to the scaled derivative f^{(n)}(x*) / n!, forming the basis of Hermite-Birkhoff osculatory interpolation.

How does Horner's nested scheme evaluate a Newton interpolating polynomial?

Newton's polynomial is factored as P_n(x) = c_0 + (x - x_0)[c_1 + (x - x_1)[c_2 + ... + (x - x_{n-1}) c_n]...], requiring only n multiplications and n additions.

What is the consequence of evaluating divided differences on very closely spaced nodes?

When |x_{i+1} - x_i| approaches machine precision, subtractive cancellation in (f[x_{i+1}] - f[x_i]) can cause severe loss of significance, requiring forward difference representations or analytical derivative substitution.

Why does the third divided difference of a cubic polynomial evaluate to a constant?

For any polynomial of degree n, the (n+1)-th derivative is identically zero, so the n-th derivative is a constant f^{(n)}(x) = n! · a_n. Thus f[x_0, ..., x_n] = a_n identically, which for f(x) = x³/6 gives c_3 = 1/6 ≈ 0.1667.

Algorithm precautions and stability rules

  • Never pass duplicate nodal abscissas into the divided difference tableau, as zero denominator division will produce NaN values.
  • Store the divided difference tableau in an in-place single array or lower-triangular matrix to conserve cache memory during large-scale simulations.
  • Ensure nodes are ordered to avoid subtractive cancellation between nearly identical adjacent values whenever possible.
  • When evaluating Newton polynomials, always work from the highest-order coefficient inward using Horner's nested sequence.
  • Verify that floating-point registers maintain 64-bit precision throughout recursive quotient division steps.
  • Monitor condition numbers when interpolating empirical data contaminated with measurement noise, as divided differences amplify high-frequency noise.

Theoretical limitations

  • Like all polynomial interpolation on equidistant nodes, high-degree Newton polynomials eventually diverge near boundaries due to Runge's phenomenon.
  • Divided difference quotients are sensitive to catastrophic floating-point cancellation when nodes are clustered closer than machine precision.
  • Newton interpolation is restricted to scalar or tensor-product domains; unstructured multi-dimensional interpolation requires radial basis functions or simplex meshes.
