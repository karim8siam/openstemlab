Agricultural assumptions and boundary condition limits

USLE predicts average sheet and rill erosion; it does not model gully erosion, stream bank collapse, or mass landslides. Evaluated using bare tilled sandy clay loam soil packed in a 9% slope erosion flume (LS = 1.0, C = 1.0, P = 1.0) under simulated rainfall intensities from 35 to 110 mm/h.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Measure surface runoff volume and soil sediment detachment under controlled artificial rainfall and calibrate the soil erodibility factor (K) in the Universal Soil Loss Equation (USLE). Standardized parameter settings: 1.0, 1.7, 2.4, 3.1, 3.8, 4.5 index/100.

01

Soil Flume Packing & Slope Setup

Pack the erosion flume with sieved agricultural soil in 5 cm lifts to achieve uniform bulk density (1.30 g/cm³), setting flume tilt to exactly 9% standard slope.

02

Soil Pre-Wetting Calibration

Pre-wet the soil bed with fine mist for 15 minutes to bring topsoil to consistent field capacity and eliminate antecedent moisture variability.

03

Simulator Intensity Setting

Set oscillating nozzle frequency and water pressure to deliver rainfall intensity I = 35 mm/h (corresponding to EI_30 / 100 = 1.00).

04

Runoff Hydrograph & Sediment Sampling

Engage rainfall for exactly 30 minutes, recording the time to runoff initiation and collecting 1-liter runoff-sediment samples every 5 minutes from the collection trough.

05

Sediment Separation & Gravimetry

Flocculate and centrifuge runoff samples, decant clear water, and dry sediment in the 105°C oven to compute total sediment yield A (kg/ha).

06

Erosivity Stepping & Erodibility Slope

Increment rainfall erosivity across five additional levels (EI_30 / 100 = 1.7, 2.4, 3.1, 3.8, 4.5), plot sediment yield A versus erosivity, and extract the erodibility slope K_usle.
