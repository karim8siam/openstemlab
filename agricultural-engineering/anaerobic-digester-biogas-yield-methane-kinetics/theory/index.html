Agricultural and physical foundations of Anaerobic Biogas Production.

Agricultural anaerobic digestion represents a vital bioenergy and waste management technology that converts livestock manure, agricultural residues, and food waste into renewable biogas while mitigating greenhouse gas emissions and producing nutrient-rich digestate fertilizer. Anaerobic biodegradation of organic waste proceeds through four consecutive biochemical stages mediated by diverse syntrophic bacterial and archaeal consortia: hydrolysis of complex polymers into monomers, acidogenesis into volatile fatty acids (VFAs), acetogenesis into acetate and hydrogen, and methanogenesis into methane (CH4) and carbon dioxide (CO2). Sizing on-farm anaerobic digester vessels, combined heat and power (CHP) generation units, and gas storage flares requires accurate determination of the specific biogas yield Y_vs (expressed in normal cubic meters of biogas produced per kilogram of volatile solids loaded, m³/kg-VS). Under steady mesophilic digestion conditions (37.0 °C) with stable hydraulic retention times, cumulative biogas generation V_g scales in direct linear proportion to the volatile organic solids mass VS fed to the microbial reactor. In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented pilot-scale continuously stirred tank reactor (CSTR) digester fed with dairy cattle manure slurry is monitored to quantify biogas yield, methane concentration, and chemical oxygen demand (COD) conversion kinetics.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What are the four sequential biochemical stages of anaerobic digestion?

The four stages are: (1) Hydrolysis (complex polymers broken into soluble monomers), (2) Acidogenesis (monomers converted to volatile fatty acids and alcohols), (3) Acetogenesis (VFAs converted to acetic acid, CO2, and H2), and (4) Methanogenesis (methanogenic archaea convert acetate and H2/CO2 into CH4 and CO2).

What is the typical composition of agricultural biogas produced from dairy manure?

Biogas typically consists of 55% to 65% methane (CH4), 35% to 45% carbon dioxide (CO2), with trace amounts of water vapor, nitrogen, hydrogen, and 500 to 3000 ppm hydrogen sulfide (H2S).

Why is methanogenesis the most sensitive stage in the anaerobic digestion process?

Methanogenic archaea have slow growth rates and are strictly obligate anaerobes with narrow pH tolerance (6.8 to 7.6); excessive organic loading can cause acid accumulation ('sour digester') which halts methanogenesis.

What is the difference between total solids (TS) and volatile solids (VS)?

Total solids (TS) is the dry matter remaining after heating at 105 °C. Volatile solids (VS) is the portion of TS that combusts when heated to 550 °C in a muffle furnace, representing the biodegradable organic fraction.

What are the optimal operating temperature ranges for mesophilic versus thermophilic digestion?

Mesophilic digestion operates optimally between 35 °C and 38 °C (typically 37 °C), while thermophilic digestion operates between 50 °C and 55 °C, offering faster reaction rates but requiring higher heating energy and greater operational stability.

Why must hydrogen sulfide (H2S) be scrubbed from biogas prior to engine combustion?

When burned in an engine, H2S forms sulfur dioxide and sulfuric acid (H2SO4), causing severe corrosive degradation of engine cylinder liners, exhaust valves, and heat exchangers.

Experimental precautions and agricultural field safety

  • Ensure strict exclusion of atmospheric oxygen; methanogenic archaea are obligate anaerobes damaged by air.
  • Monitor digester pH daily; add sodium bicarbonate buffer immediately if pH drops below 6.8.
  • Maintain constant digester mixing to prevent thermal stratification and crust formation at the slurry surface.
  • Verify biogas gas-tightness using bubble leak tests; biogas contains explosive methane and toxic H2S.

Theoretical assumptions & biological limits

  • The linear yield model applies under stable organic loading without substrate shock or VFA souring.
  • Assumes consistent dairy manure diet and manure composition across all test batches.
  • Applies to mesophilic CSTR operation; plug-flow and covered lagoon digesters exhibit different kinetics.
  • Neglects minor gas production lag during the initial 12-hour acclimation period following feeding.
