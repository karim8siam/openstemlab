Agricultural and physical foundations of Grain Terminal Velocity.

Aerodynamic properties of agricultural seeds and biological materials govern the design of pneumatic conveyors, aspirators, combine cleaning shoes, air-screen grain cleaners, and fluidized bed dryers. The terminal velocity (v_t) of a particle is defined as the steady vertical velocity of an upward airstream that suspends the particle in stationary equilibrium, where the upward aerodynamic drag force F_d exactly equals the downward gravitational force F_g minus buoyant force F_b: F_d = C_d·A_p·(ρ_a·v_t² / 2) = m·g·(1 - ρ_a / ρ_p). Because drag depends on particle projected area, density, shape, and surface roughness, agricultural seeds exhibit distinct terminal velocities that enable clean pneumatic separation from lighter weed seeds, chaff, straw pieces, and dust. For intermediate Reynolds numbers characteristic of cereal seed suspensions (Re ≈ 1000 to 4000), terminal velocity scales near-linearly with equivalent seed diameter D_p and the square root of particle density. In this laboratory experiment, a vertical transparent acrylic wind column elutriator with variable-speed blower and precision hot-wire anemometer determines the exact floating terminal velocity across six seed size fractions.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

Define terminal velocity in the context of agricultural pneumatic separation.

Terminal velocity is the upward air velocity required to balance the downward gravitational force acting on a seed, causing the particle to remain stationary in suspension (net vertical force = 0).

How is aerodynamic drag formulated for an irregularly shaped grain kernel?

Drag force is F_d = 0.5·C_d·ρ_a·A_p·v_t², where C_d is the drag coefficient (dependent on particle shape and Reynolds number), ρ_a is air density, A_p is projected cross-sectional area, and v_t is relative velocity.

Why do grain kernels tumble and rotate when suspended in an elutriator?

Seeds are asymmetric non-spherical bodies whose center of mass does not coincide with their center of aerodynamic pressure, producing aerodynamic pitching and yawing moments that cause continuous rotational tumbling in turbulent airflow.

Explain how an air-screen cleaner uses differences in terminal velocity.

An aspirator duct passes upward air through a falling curtain of grain. Air velocity is set above the terminal velocity of chaff (3 m/s) but below that of grain (9 m/s); chaff is entrained and carried away, while clean grain falls downward into the screen grading shoe.

How does moisture content affect seed terminal velocity?

Moist grain has higher mass and swells in volume, altering both density and projected area. Generally, increasing grain moisture increases terminal velocity, requiring higher air velocities in cleaning equipment.

What is the equivalent diameter D_p of a tri-axial ellipsoid seed?

Geometric equivalent diameter is D_p = (L·W·T)^(1/3), the geometric mean of length (L), width (W), and thickness (T), representing the diameter of an equivalent sphere with identical volume.

Experimental precautions and agricultural field safety

  • Ensure airflow in the vertical column is completely laminarized by honeycomb straighteners to prevent wall downdrafts.
  • Introduce kernels one fraction at a time to prevent inter-particle collisions that disrupt suspension equilibrium.
  • Record air barometric pressure and temperature to correct for air density variations.
  • Ensure anemometer probe is positioned exactly at the vertical floating height of the suspended seeds.

Theoretical assumptions & biological limits

  • Individual kernels within the same sieve fraction exhibit minor shape and density variations, producing a ±5% velocity band.
  • Wall proximity effects in 15 cm ducts slightly alter velocity profiles compared to open field airflow.
  • High-speed tumbling alters instantaneous projected area, requiring time-averaged velocity measurements.
  • Cannot separate weed seeds that share identical aerodynamic terminal velocity with the crop grain.
