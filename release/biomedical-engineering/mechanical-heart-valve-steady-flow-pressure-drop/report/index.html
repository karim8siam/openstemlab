Bileaflet Mechanical Heart Valve Steady-Flow Pressure Drop & Orifice Area. Measure the transvalvular pressure gradient ΔP across a 25 mm bileaflet mechanical aortic heart valve under steady forward flow and evaluate the quadratic Bernoulli loss coefficient k_v.

4. Theory and graph choice

Valvular heart disease (such as calcific aortic stenosis) impairs unidirectional intracardiac hemodynamics, requiring surgical replacement with prosthetic heart valves. Bileaflet mechanical heart valves (such as the St. Jude Medical or CarboMedics pyrolytic carbon designs) are widely implanted due to their exceptional structural durability and life expectancy (> 25 years). However, prosthetic valves introduce geometric flow restrictions: the valve sewing ring, leaflet pivot hinges, and central open leaflets create flow separation, turbulent dissipation, and elevated transvalvular pressure gradients. According to the Gorlin formula and modified Bernoulli equation commonly utilized in clinical cardiology and ISO 5840 testing, the steady-flow pressure drop ΔP across a stenotic orifice or prosthetic valve is governed by convective acceleration and turbulent kinetic energy dissipation: ΔP = (ρ / 2·C_d²) · v² = k_v · v², where ρ is blood fluid density, C_d is the discharge coefficient, v is the peak transvalvular jet velocity (m/s), and k_v is the quadratic pressure loss coefficient (mmHg/(m²/s²)). In clinical echocardiography, the simplified Bernoulli formula ΔP ≈ 4·v² (assuming blood density ρ ≈ 1060 kg/m³) is universally used to estimate peak valve gradients. In this laboratory investigation, a 25 mm pyrolytic carbon bileaflet aortic valve mounted in an ISO 5840 steady-flow testing chamber evaluates transvalvular pressure drop across velocities from 1.0 to 2.0 m/s to determine k_v and effective orifice area (EOA).

ΔP = k_v · v² = 3.8500 · v²

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Square of transvalvular velocity v² (m²/s²), and the dependent coordinate y represents Transvalvular pressure drop ΔP (mmHg). The gradient m directly determines Bernoulli Pressure Loss Coefficient k_v, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Mount 25 mm mechanical valve securely in acrylic test chamber, ensuring leaflets open symmetrically without sewing ring impingement.
  2. Fill system with 36% glycerol-water blood analog, purge all trapped air from pressure lines, and heat reservoir to 37.0 °C.
  3. Open transducer bypass valve at zero flow to establish precise 0.00 mmHg baseline differential pressure.
  4. Adjust centrifugal pump to establish transvalvular jet velocity v = 1.0 m/s (verified by flowmeter Q and effective orifice area) and record steady pressure drop ΔP.
  5. Increment jet velocity through 1.2, 1.4, 1.6, 1.8, and 2.0 m/s, recording stabilized pressure gradient ΔP over 30 seconds at each step.
  6. Plot transvalvular pressure drop ΔP against v², compute the slope k_v via linear regression through the origin, and calculate effective orifice area EOA.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Purge all air from differential pressure lines; bubbles act as capacitive dampers that severely distort pressure readings.
  • Position pressure taps strictly according to ISO 5840 (1 diameter upstream, 3 diameters downstream) to capture true net unrecovered loss.
  • Verify leaflet mobility before testing; ensure pyrolytic carbon leaflets pivot freely to their maximum 85° opening angle.
  • Keep fluid temperature precisely at 37.0 °C to stabilize blood-analog viscosity and density.

Steady forward flow does not simulate transient valve opening/closing leaflet dynamics or dynamic regurgitation phases.
