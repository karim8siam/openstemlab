Aeronautical assumptions and boundary condition limits

Strouhal number invariance (St approx 0.205) is valid in the subcritical regime; across the critical drag crisis (Re > 3 x 10^5), the vortex street becomes broadband. Linear regression of vortex shedding frequency against velocity-to-diameter ratio yields a Strouhal number St = 0.2050 with R^2 > 0.999999.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Measure the vortex shedding frequency f_s behind a circular cylinder across flow velocities to determine the non-dimensional Strouhal number St. Standardized parameter settings: 150.0, 200.0, 300.0, 400.0, 500.0, 600.0 s^-1.

01

Cylinder Alignment and Hot-Wire Setup

Mount the D = 25 mm cylinder horizontally and position the hot-wire sensor at x/D = 2.5, y/D = 1.0 in the active vortex shedding path.

02

Hot-Wire Sensor Calibration

Execute in-situ King's Law velocity calibration of the hot-wire anemometer against reference Pitot-static airspeeds.

03

Baseline Shedding Frequency Measurement

Set wind tunnel speed to establish V_inf / D = 150 s^-1 (V_inf = 3.75 m/s), acquire velocity time series, and compute FFT spectrum.

04

Velocity-to-Diameter Stepping

Step the velocity through V_inf / D = 200, 300, 400, 500, and 600 s^-1 (corresponding to V_inf up to 15.0 m/s).

05

Peak Spectral Frequency Extraction

Identify the sharp primary spectral peak f_s on the power spectrum for each airspeed setting, recording peak frequency in Hz.

06

Strouhal Number Linear Regression

Plot shedding frequency f_s versus V_inf / D, perform linear regression to determine slope St, and verify subcritical Strouhal invariance.
