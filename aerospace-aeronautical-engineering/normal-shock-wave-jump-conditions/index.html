Shock-wave boundary layer interaction (SWBLI) creates a lambda-foot near duct walls that slightly smears the idealized 1D jump. Linear regression of the pressure jump ratio against (M_1^2 - 1) yields a slope of 1.1667 matching the Rankine-Hugoniot relation with R^2 > 0.999999.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Measure static pressure jump P_2 / P_1 and total pressure ratio P_02 / P_01 across a stationary normal shock wave in a supersonic duct as upstream Mach M_1 varies. Standardized parameter settings: 0.8, 1.25, 1.89, 2.61, 3.41, 4.29 dimensionless.

01

Supersonic Flow Stabilization

Start the supersonic wind tunnel, establishing stable supersonic flow at nominal Mach M_1 = 1.34 in the test section.

02

Normal Shock Wave Positioning

Gradually close the downstream throttle valve until Schlieren optics confirm a stationary normal shock centered over tap station 12.

03

Upstream and Downstream Pressure Acquisition

Record upstream static pressure P_1, downstream static pressure P_2, and stagnation pressures P_01 and P_02.

04

Systematic Mach Number Stepping

Adjust the supersonic nozzle block to step upstream Mach M_1 through values corresponding to (M_1^2 - 1) = 0.80, 1.25, 1.89, 2.61, 3.41, and 4.29.

05

Rankine-Hugoniot Jump Calculation

Compute static pressure jump ratio (P_2/P_1 - 1) and total pressure ratio P_02 / P_01 at each Mach operating point.

06

Rankine-Hugoniot Linear Regression

Plot (P_2/P_1 - 1) versus (M_1^2 - 1), perform linear regression to determine slope 2*gamma/(gamma+1), and verify gamma = 1.4 for air.
