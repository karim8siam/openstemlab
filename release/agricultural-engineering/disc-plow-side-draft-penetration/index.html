Agricultural assumptions and boundary condition limits

Assumes consistent soil penetration resistance without hidden boulder strikes or subsoil rock shelves. Evaluated at constant disc angle of 42° and tilt angle of 20° on hard clay soil. Draft F represents the forward horizontal component of total soil resistance.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Investigate the relationship between plowing depth and total draft force for an agricultural disc plow and evaluate penetration resistance under varying disc angles. Standardized parameter settings: 10.0, 13.0, 16.0, 19.0, 22.0, 25.0 cm.

01

Disc Angle & Tilt Angle Calibration

Adjust the disc standard clamps to set disc angle to exactly 42° and tilt angle to 20° using the mechanical protractor and inclinometer.

02

Hitch Geometry & Sway Setup

Attach the plow to the tractor Category II three-point linkage, adjusting check chains to allow slight floating sway for the furrow wheel to seat against the furrow corner.

03

Zero-Load Sensor Balancing

Elevate the plow fully with the hydraulic lift and record tare offsets on all draft, vertical, and side load cell channels.

04

Shallow Penetration Run

Set the rear furrow wheel depth stop to d = 10.0 cm, engage tractor forward speed at 5.0 km/h, and log steady draft force over a 50-meter test course.

05

Sequential Depth Increments

Increment the working depth in five uniform stages up to 25.0 cm, checking furrow slice throw and soil pulverization consistency.

06

Penetration Gradient Analysis

Plot draft force F against depth d, perform ordinary least-squares regression to extract the depth draft gradient c_d, and calculate side force ratio.
