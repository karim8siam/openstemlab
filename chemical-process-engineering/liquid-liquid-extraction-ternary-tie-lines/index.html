FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the binodal solubility curve and conjugate liquid–liquid tie lines for the ternary system water – acetic acid – ethyl acetate, evaluate the distribution coefficient K_D and selectivity β, and verify Nernst partition equilibrium. Standardized parameter settings: 5.0, 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 25.0, 30.0 wt%.

01

Equilibrium Cell Thermostating

Set circulating water bath to 25.00 °C; circulate water through the equilibrium cell jacket for 15 minutes before charging chemicals.

02

Ternary Feed Preparation

Gravimetrically prepare a 200 g binary mixture of water and acetic acid containing 5.0 wt% solute; charge into the cell with 200 g of pure ethyl acetate.

03

Intense Agitation & Equilibration

Agitate the two-phase mixture vigorously with a magnetic stirrer at 600 RPM for 20 minutes to generate high interfacial contact area.

04

Phase Quiescence & Disengagement

Halt stirring and allow 30 minutes for complete gravitational phase separation into a clear bottom aqueous raffinate and clear top organic extract.

05

Conjugate Phase Sampling & Titration

Draw 5.0 mL of bottom raffinate and 5.0 mL of top extract; titrate each phase against standardized 0.100 M NaOH to determine acetic acid concentrations x_A and y_A.

06

Concentration Progression Protocol

Repeat procedure for initial aqueous feed concentrations yielding raffinate concentrations of 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 25.0, and 30.0 wt% acetic acid.

07

Distribution Coefficient Regression

Plot extract concentration y_A versus raffinate concentration x_A, and fit a linear regression through the origin to evaluate K_D.

THEORY AND UNIT OPERATIONS CONTEXT

Process engineering background & physical principles.

Liquid–liquid extraction (solvent extraction) is a key chemical engineering separation technology used when distillation is economically or thermodynamically impractical—such as separating close-boiling mixtures, recovering heat-sensitive bioproducts, breaking azeotropes, or purifying dilute specialty chemicals. In extraction, an immiscible or partially miscible liquid solvent (ethyl acetate) is mixed with a liquid feed mixture (water and acetic acid). Solute (acetic acid) distributes between the two liquid phases based on chemical potential and activity coefficient differences until thermodynamic phase equilibrium is achieved. The solute-rich solvent phase is termed the extract, and the remaining solute-depleted feed phase is termed the raffinate. Phase equilibria for ternary liquid systems are represented on equilateral or right-triangular coordinates featuring a binodal solubility curve that separates the single-phase homogeneous region from the two-phase immiscible envelope. Within the two-phase region, straight tie lines connect conjugate liquid phases in mutual thermodynamic equilibrium. According to Nernst's distribution law for dilute-to-moderate concentrations, the equilibrium distribution coefficient K_D = y_A / x_A (ratio of solute mass fraction in extract to raffinate) remains constant: y_A = K_D · x_A. In this laboratory investigation, ternary mixtures of water, acetic acid, and ethyl acetate are equilibrated in a jacketed separatory cell across raffinate concentrations from 5.0 to 30.0 wt% to construct tie lines, determine distribution coefficient K_D, and evaluate extraction selectivity β.
