1. Title and aim

Enzymatic Amperometric Glucose Biosensor Kinetics & Linearity. Evaluate the steady-state catalytic current of a glucose oxidase (GOx) platinum-carbon amperometric electrode across blood glucose concentrations to evaluate sensor sensitivity and linear dynamic range.

4. Theory and graph choice

In diabetes management and point-of-care clinical diagnostics, enzymatic electrochemical biosensors represent the most widespread commercial medical biosensing technology in the world, utilized daily by millions of diabetic patients in personal glucometers and continuous glucose monitors (CGMs). The classic first-generation glucose biosensor couples the biocatalytic enzyme Glucose Oxidase (GOx, immobilized in a glutaraldehyde or chitosan membrane matrix) with an amperometric platinum working electrode. GOx catalyzes the oxidation of β-D-glucose by dissolved molecular oxygen to produce D-glucono-1,5-lactone and hydrogen peroxide: Glucose + O2 + H2O —[GOx]→ Gluconic Acid + H2O2. The generated hydrogen peroxide diffuses to the polarized platinum anode (held at +0.65 V versus Ag/AgCl), where it undergoes stoichiometric electrocatalytic oxidation: H2O2 → O2 + 2 H⁺ + 2 e⁻. Each mole of glucose consumed transfers two electrons to the electrode. In accordance with Michaelis-Menten enzyme kinetics, when the substrate concentration C_glu is well below the apparent Michaelis constant (C_glu << K_m,app), the rate of enzymatic turnover is first-order, and the resulting steady-state anodic current I_a is directly proportional to glucose concentration: I_a = S_glu · C_glu. In this laboratory experiment, a screen-printed platinum-carbon GOx biosensor is tested in physiological buffer across clinical glycemic levels (2.0 to 12.0 mmol/L) to evaluate linear sensitivity S_glu.

I_a = S_glu · C_glu = 0.7200 · C_glu

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Glucose concentration C_glu (mmol/L), and the dependent coordinate y represents Catalytic current I_a (μA). The gradient m directly determines Biosensor Amperometric Sensitivity S_glu, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Fill the glass cell with 10.0 mL of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4) at 37.0 °C; insert the three-electrode sensor strip and start magnetic stirring at 250 RPM.
  2. Apply +0.650 V polarizing potential to the platinum working electrode; wait 10 minutes until background capacitive current stabilizes (< 0.05 μA).
  3. Inject calibrated volume of glucose stock to achieve 2.0 mmol/L (36 mg/dL); allow 30 seconds for enzymatic steady-state current plateau.
  4. Record steady-state anodic oxidation current I_a in microamperes over a 15-second integration window.
  5. Perform sequential spiked standard additions to achieve 4.0, 6.0, 8.0, 10.0, and 12.0 mmol/L (up to severe hyperglycemia ~216 mg/dL), logging I_a at each step.
  6. Plot catalytic current I_a against glucose concentration C_glu, performing linear regression through zero to determine biosensor sensitivity S_glu.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Maintain constant magnetic stirring velocity to ensure stable diffusion boundary layer thickness.
  • Control reaction temperature strictly at 37.0 °C; GOx enzyme turnover velocity is highly temperature-dependent.
  • Ensure glucose solutions mutarotate for 24 hours prior to use so the α/β anomer ratio reaches thermodynamic equilibrium.
  • Shield the electrochemical cell inside an aluminum Faraday enclosure to eliminate electrical line noise.

The linear kinetic model applies across the clinical range (2.0 to 12.0 mmol/L); higher levels enter enzyme saturation.
