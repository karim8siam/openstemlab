Civil engineering mechanics & structural foundations of TSS Gravimetric Filtration.

In civil environmental water quality monitoring, municipal wastewater discharge compliance, and primary sedimentation basin design, solids suspended in the water column represent a primary physical pollutant. Particulate matter impairs aquatic light penetration, deposits sludge blankets on riverbeds, blankets fish spawning gravels, and carries hydrophobic heavy metals and organic pesticides. Total Suspended Solids (TSS) is quantified gravimetrically according to EPA Method 160.2 / Standard Methods 2540 D. A thoroughly mixed, unpreserved water sample of volume V is drawn through a pre-weighed, pre-combusted glass-fiber filter disc (Whatman 934-AH or equivalent, nominal pore rating 1.5 µm) supported on a vacuum filtration apparatus. The filter and captured particulate residue are washed with deionized water to remove dissolved mineral salts, dried in a thermostatic laboratory oven at 103 to 105 °C for at least 1 hour, cooled to room temperature in a desiccator, and weighed on an analytical balance to 0.1 mg precision until constant mass is achieved. Total Suspended Solids is calculated as TSS (mg/L) = (M_dried - M_tare) / V. Subsequent ignition of the dried residue in a muffle furnace at 550 ± 50 °C volatilizes the organic particulate fraction, leaving mineral ash to determine Volatile Suspended Solids (VSS) and Fixed Suspended Solids (FSS). In this laboratory investigation, sample volume fractions from 0.1 to 0.6 L are filtered to establish gravimetric linearity and determine wastewater TSS = 185.0 mg/L.

Gravimetric Principles of Solids Fractionation

Total solids (TS) in water comprise Total Suspended Solids (TSS) and Total Dissolved Solids (TDS). When volume $V$ is filtered through a pre-weighed $1.5\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ glass-fiber filter of tare mass $M_t$ and dried at $105^\circ\text{C}$ to final mass $M_d$:

\[ \text{TSS (mg/L)} = \frac{M_d - M_t}{V} \times 1000 \]

where masses are in milligrams and volume $V$ is in liters. Subsequent combustion at $550^\circ\text{C}$ burns off organic matter to final ash mass $M_a$. Volatile Suspended Solids (VSS) and Fixed Suspended Solids (FSS) are:

\[ \text{VSS (mg/L)} = \frac{M_d - M_a}{V} \times 1000 \] \[ \text{FSS (mg/L)} = \frac{M_a - M_t}{V} \times 1000 \]

By conservation of mass, $\text{TSS} = \text{VSS} + \text{FSS}$.

Sedimentation Basin Sizing Relationship

In wastewater primary clarifier design, surface overflow rate (SOR) is governed by particle settling velocity $v_s$. According to Stokes' Law for discrete spherical particles ($Re_p < 0.5$):

\[ v_s = \frac{g (\rho_p - \rho_w) d_p^2}{18 \mu} \]

Particles with settling velocity $v_s \ge \text{SOR} = Q / A_s$ are $100\%$ captured in the settling tank, removing $55\%$ to $65\%$ of influent TSS.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What is the operational definition distinguishing suspended solids from dissolved solids?

Suspended solids are particulates retained on a standard glass-fiber filter disc with nominal pore size of 1.5 µm (or 0.45 µm membrane filter); dissolved solids are the mineral and organic solutes that pass through the filter into the filtrate.

Why must the filter residue be washed with deionized water after sample filtration?

Washing dissolves and purges dissolved mineral salts (chlorides, sulfates, carbonates) trapped in the moist filter cake; otherwise, dissolved salts crystallize upon oven drying, falsely inflating measured TSS mass.

What is the significance of Volatile Suspended Solids (VSS) in biological wastewater treatment?

VSS represents the combustible organic fraction of suspended solids (oxidized to CO2 and H2O at 550 °C), serving as a direct surrogate measure of active biological microbial biomass in activated sludge mixed liquor.

Why is the drying temperature strictly maintained between 103 °C and 105 °C?

Temperatures below 103 °C fail to completely evaporate occluded water; temperatures above 105 °C can cause thermal decomposition of volatile organic fractions or crystallization loss of ammonium bicarbonate salts.

How does excessive sample volume on the filter affect TSS accuracy?

Excessive solids form a dense, impermeable filter cake that clogs pores, acting as a secondary micro-filter that traps dissolved colloids and dramatically slows filtration time, introducing positive measurement bias.

What is the typical TSS discharge limit for secondary treated municipal effluent under EPA standards?

The US EPA Clean Water Act secondary treatment standards mandate a 30-day average effluent TSS concentration not to exceed 30 mg/L, with at least 85% removal across the treatment plant.

Experimental precautions and laboratory safety

  • Vigorously mix the bulk wastewater container immediately before pouring sample aliquots to prevent heavy sand and grit from settling out.
  • Handle delicate glass-fiber filters exclusively with clean, smooth-tipped stainless steel forceps, never with bare fingers.
  • Allow filters to cool to ambient temperature in an active desiccator before weighing to prevent thermal air convection currents on the balance pan.
  • Ensure vacuum does not exceed 380 mm Hg (0.5 atm) to avoid pulling fragile particulate solids through the glass-fiber matrix.

Theoretical assumptions & boundary condition limits

  • The civil engineering physical models apply within the specified calibrated operational window (0.1 to 0.6 fraction).
  • Assumes homogeneous, isotropic continuum material behavior without unmodeled micro-fracture anomalies.
  • Continuous sensor readings reflect ordinary least-squares regression across successive observations.
  • Extreme inelastic yielding or cyclic degradation requires non-linear constitutive continuum damage mechanics.
