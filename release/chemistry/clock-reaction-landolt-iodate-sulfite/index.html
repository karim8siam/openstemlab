Physical chemistry assumptions and metrological limits

The kinetic investigation of the Landolt iodine clock reaction is subject to several well-defined physical, chemical, and metrological boundary conditions: (1) Human and Instrumental Reaction Time: At high iodate concentrations ([IO₃⁻]₀ > 30 mM), the induction period drops below 5 seconds, where manual mixing latency (0.3–0.5 s) becomes a significant fraction of Δt, requiring automated stopped-flow mixing for high precision; (2) Aerial Bisulfite Oxidation: Sodium bisulfite in neutral or mildly acidic aqueous solution is thermodynamically prone to oxidation by dissolved atmospheric oxygen (2 HSO₃⁻ + O₂ → 2 SO₄²⁻ + 2 H⁺); even 2% loss of bisulfite reduces Δt proportionally; (3) Ionic Strength Dependency: The primary kinetic salt effect alters the rate constant by several percent if ionic strength varies; all runs must be buffered at uniform background electrolyte concentration; (4) Starch Aging and Inclusion Threshold: Soluble starch degrades microbiologically over several days; retrograded amylose forms irregular helical cavities with altered triiodide association constants; and (5) Non-Uniform Temperature Dissipation: The Landolt reaction sequence is mildly exothermic (ΔH ≈ −380 kJ/mol IO₃⁻); in large volumes with poor heat transfer, localized micro-heating can accelerate the reaction near the end of the induction window. FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the reaction order with respect to iodate and the apparent rate constant for the Landolt iodine clock reaction by monitoring the induction period preceding the abrupt starch-triiodide chromophoric transition. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 4.0, 6.0, 8.0, 10.0, 14.0, 20.0 mM.

01

Reagent Standardization and Thermal Equilibration

Standardize potassium iodate and sodium bisulfite stock solutions. Prepare the 0.100 M acetate buffer (pH 4.50). Equilibrate stock solution reservoirs in the 25.00 °C circulating bath for 30 minutes.

02

Reaction Cocktail Formulation

For each run, dispense calibrated volumes of potassium iodate stock and deionized water into Reaction Vessel A to establish target concentrations ([IO₃⁻]₀ = 4.00, 6.00, 8.00, 10.00, 14.00, and 20.00 mM) at a constant volume of 50.00 mL.

03

Limiting Reductant and Starch Charge

In Reaction Vessel B, dispense 10.00 mL of 0.0120 M NaHSO₃ stock, 10.00 mL of 0.100 M acetate buffer (pH 4.50), 5.00 mL of 2.0% starch indicator, and 25.00 mL deionized water (total volume 50.00 mL, yielding 1.200 mM [HSO₃⁻]₀ upon equimolar mixing).

04

Rapid Initiation and Optical Timer Triggering

Rapidly pour the contents of Vessel B into Vessel A under vigorous 400 rpm magnetic stirring, simultaneously triggering the automated optical timer (t = 0).

05

Induction Period Monitoring and Flash Transition Detection

Maintain continuous stirring and photodiode transmission tracking. Observe the solution remaining completely crystal clear while bisulfite scavenges intermediate iodine. At the instant bisulfite is exhausted, record the abrupt blue-black optical density step (Δt in seconds).

06

Kinetic Linearization and Parameter Extraction

Calculate reciprocal clock times (1/Δt in s⁻¹). Plot 1/Δt versus [IO₃⁻]₀. Perform linear regression to verify first-order dependence on iodate (R² ≥ 0.999), and extract the apparent rate constant k = slope × 1000 in L·mol⁻¹·s⁻¹.
