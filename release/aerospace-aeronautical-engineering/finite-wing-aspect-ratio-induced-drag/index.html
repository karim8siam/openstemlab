Aeronautical assumptions and boundary condition limits

Prandtl lifting-line theory assumes unswept, high-aspect-ratio wings (AR > 4.0); small non-linearities appear at AR = 2.0. Linear regression of C_Di against 1/AR yields a slope of 0.1348 matching the Prandtl lifting-line theoretical gradient with R^2 > 0.999999.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Investigate wingtip vortex downwash and quantify induced drag coefficient C_Di as a function of reciprocal aspect ratio 1/AR using Prandtl's classical lifting-line theory. Standardized parameter settings: 0.125, 0.167, 0.2, 0.25, 0.333, 0.5 dimensionless.

01

Wing Model Mounting and Taring

Mount the baseline AR = 8.0 wing on the external balance, perform wind-off weight tares, and verify aerodynamic zero.

02

Wind Tunnel Stabilization

Accelerate wind tunnel airflow to V_inf = 30 m/s and trim angle of attack to achieve C_L = 0.60.

03

Drag Subtraction for Induced Drag

Measure total drag C_D, subtract the profile drag C_D0 measured on the 2D airfoil model, isolating induced drag C_Di.

04

Aspect Ratio Variation Sweep

Repeat the test across modular wings with AR = 6.0, 5.0, 4.0, 3.0, and 2.0 (corresponding to 1/AR = 0.125 to 0.500).

05

Downwash Mapping

Traverse the 5-hole probe across the spanwise wake behind the AR = 4.0 wing to record local downwash angles.

06

Prandtl Lifting-Line Regression

Plot C_Di against 1/AR, perform linear regression to determine the slope m = C_L^2 / (pi * e), and extract Oswald efficiency factor e.
