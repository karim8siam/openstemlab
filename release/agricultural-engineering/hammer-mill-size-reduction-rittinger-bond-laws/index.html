Agricultural assumptions and boundary condition limits

Hammer mills produce a wide, polydisperse particle size distribution with substantial fines compared to roller mills. Evaluated using shelled feed corn (initial diameter d_initial = 7.5 mm) ground through hammer mill screens from 1.0 mm to 6.0 mm (1/√d_p from 0.5 to 2.0 mm^-0.5).

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the specific electrical grinding energy, particle size distribution, and fineness modulus of ground grain in a hammer mill, and calibrate Bond's comminution law. Standardized parameter settings: 0.5, 0.8, 1.1, 1.4, 1.7, 2.0 mm^-0.5.

01

Initial Screen Installation & Tare Power

Install the 6.0 mm perforated screen in the hammer mill. Run the mill empty at rated speed (3600 RPM) to record baseline idle mechanical friction and windage power (P_idle).

02

Batch Feeding & Energy Logging

Weigh exactly 10.0 kg of shelled corn, start the vibratory feeder to deliver grain at 100 kg/h, and integrate net active electrical energy (kWh) until the batch is completely ground.

03

Specific Energy Calculation

Calculate net specific grinding energy E_s = (E_total - E_idle) / Mass_batch (kWh/tonne).

04

Sieve Shaker Fineness Analysis

Take a representative 100 g sample of ground meal, place in the standard Ro-Tap sieve shaker for 10.0 minutes, weigh fractions on each sieve, and compute geometric mean diameter d_p in accordance with ASABE S319.

05

Screen Size Stepping

Repeat the grinding trial across screens of 4.5, 3.0, 2.0, 1.5, and 1.0 mm, recording specific energy E_s and particle diameter d_p for each trial.

06

Bond's Law Linear Regression

Calculate size parameter 1/√d_p (mm^-0.5), plot specific energy E_s versus 1/√d_p, and determine Bond's work scaling factor K_b via linear regression.
