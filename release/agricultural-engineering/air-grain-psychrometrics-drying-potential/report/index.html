1. Title and aim

Moist Air Psychrometrics & Evaporative Drying Potential Across Grain Beds. Determine the sensible heat transfer, moisture carrying capacity, and psychrometric drying potential of heated air across deep grain beds using psychrometric enthalpy balances.

4. Theory and graph choice

Psychrometrics—the thermodynamic study of moist air mixtures—governs all agricultural grain drying, greenhouse climatization, livestock barn ventilation, and cold storage operations. In a convection grain dryer, ambient air is heated sensibly before passing through the crop bed. During sensible heating, the dry-bulb temperature rises while absolute humidity ratio W (mass of water vapor per unit mass of dry air, g/kg) remains constant, causing relative humidity to drop dramatically and expanding the air's vapor pressure deficit. As this warm, dry air flows through moist grain, it undergoes an adiabatic evaporative cooling process along a constant wet-bulb (or constant enthalpy) line on the psychrometric chart: sensible heat from the air supplies the latent heat of vaporization (approx. 2400 kJ/kg H₂O), causing air dry-bulb temperature to fall while humidity ratio increases until the air reaches saturation (or equilibrium with grain moisture). The drying potential evaporative capacity ΔW = W_sat - W_in (g H₂O/kg dry air) represents the maximum water a kilogram of air can absorb in a single pass. In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented psychrometric duct evaluates evaporative drying potential across temperatures from 35°C to 70°C.

ΔW = s_dp · T_db + ΔW_0 = 0.4200 · T_db - 5.0000

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents Drying air temperature T_db (°C), and the dependent axis y represents Evaporative capacity ΔW (g H₂O/kg dry air). The gradient m directly determines Thermal Drying Potential Sensitivity s_dp, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Record ambient air dry-bulb temperature (20.0°C) and relative humidity (60.0%), calculating baseline humidity ratio W_in = 8.80 g H₂O/kg dry air from psychrometric relations.
  2. Set duct air velocity to 0.4 m/s through the grain column, powering the heating stage to achieve initial dry-bulb temperature T_db = 35.0°C.
  3. Verify that absolute humidity ratio W upstream of the grain bed matches ambient W_in, confirming pure sensible heating without moisture addition.
  4. Measure exhaust air wet-bulb and dry-bulb temperatures downstream of the grain bed, determining saturation humidity ratio W_sat at the adiabatic wet-bulb temperature.
  5. Increment heater temperature in five uniform stages to 42, 49, 56, 63, and 70°C, recording steady-state inlet and outlet psychrometric states for each run.
  6. Calculate evaporative capacity ΔW = W_sat - W_in for each temperature, plot ΔW versus T_db, and determine the thermal drying sensitivity gradient s_dp.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure wet-bulb wicks are clean, saturated with distilled water, and aspirated at air velocities > 3.5 m/s to prevent radiation errors.
  • Allow thermal equilibrium to stabilize for at least 15 minutes after adjusting electric heater settings.
  • Verify airtight duct seals; ambient air infiltration downstream of the heater skews psychrometric mass balances.
  • Do not exceed 70°C drying temperature to avoid scorch and thermal degradation of grain endosperm.
