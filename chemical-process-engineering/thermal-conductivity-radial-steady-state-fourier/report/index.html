1. Title and aim

Radial Steady-State Conduction & Thermal Conductivity of Process Insulation. Measure the steady-state radial temperature distribution across a cylindrical specimen of process insulation material under controlled electrical heating, verify Fourier's law in cylindrical coordinates, and determine thermal conductivity k.

4. Theory and graph choice

In chemical process plants, cryogenic storage terminals, and steam distribution systems, thermal insulation materials—such as calcium silicate, mineral wool, expanded perlite, and cellular glass—are mandatory to minimize parasitic thermal losses, prevent personnel burn injuries, avoid pipeline freezing, and suppress condensation. Determining the thermal conductivity k of tubular process insulation is vital for accurate thermal design. In cylindrical geometries, heat flows radially outward from an internal coaxial heating element through an annular insulation sleeve of inner radius r_1, outer radius r_2, and length L. Applying Fourier's fundamental law of heat conduction in one-dimensional steady cylindrical coordinates yields: q_r = -k A(r) · (dT/dr) = -k (2π r L) · (dT/dr). Separating variables and integrating across the annular wall establishes the classic logarithmic temperature profile: T(r) = T_1 - (T_1 - T_2) · [ln(r/r_1) / ln(r_2/r_1)], and gives the steady radial heat flow rate: q_r = [2π k L / ln(r_2/r_1)] · (T_1 - T_2). In this laboratory experiment, an annular specimen of refractory process insulation is heated with an internal coaxial electrical element across power levels from 20 to 120 W with guarded end heaters to eliminate axial heat leakage. Measuring the radial temperature difference ΔT = T_1 - T_2 directly evaluates the thermal resistance slope S and determines the material thermal conductivity k.

ΔT = S · Q_e = 0.3850 · Q_e

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Heater electrical power Q_e (W), and the dependent coordinate y represents Radial temperature difference ΔT = T_1 - T_2 (K). The gradient m directly determines Radial Thermal Resistance Slope S, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Install the cylindrical insulation sleeve over the central core; energize guard heaters and adjust differential thermocouples to read 0.0 °C to eliminate axial losses.
  2. Circulate cooling water through the outer jacket at 5.0 L/min to stabilize outer wall temperature at T_2 = 25.0 °C.
  3. Set central heater power to Q_e = 20.0 W; allow 45 minutes for the low-conductivity material to reach thermal steady state (drift < 0.05 °C over 10 min).
  4. Record inner temperature T_1, outer temperature T_2, and intermediate radial temperatures to confirm logarithmic distribution.
  5. Step heater power through 40.0, 60.0, 80.0, 100.0, and 120.0 W, allowing full steady-state equilibration and recording ΔT at each level.
  6. Plot radial temperature difference ΔT versus heater power Q_e, extract the thermal resistance slope S, and compute material thermal conductivity k.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation determines the thermal conductivity $k$ of a cylindrical specimen of industrial calcium silicate pipe insulation (inner radius $r_1 = 20.0\text{ mm}$, outer radius $r_2 = 50.0\text{ mm}$, active length $L = 500\text{ mm}$). By applying controlled electrical heating from $20.0$ to $120.0\text{ W}$ with guarded end heaters and logging the radial temperature difference $\Delta T = T_1 - T_2$, we verify Fourier's logarithmic conduction law, measure thermal resistance slope $S = 0.3850\text{ K/W}$, and determine material conductivity $k = 0.1450\text{ W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Radial heat conduction through a cylindrical sleeve obeys Fourier's law:

\[ \Delta T = T_1 - T_2 = \left[ \frac{\ln(r_2 / r_1)}{2\pi k L} \right] Q_e = S \cdot Q_e \]

where $S = 0.3850\text{ K/W}$ represents the cylindrical conductive thermal resistance. Thermal conductivity is computed via $k = \frac{\ln(r_2 / r_1)}{2\pi L S}$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The apparatus features a central Nichrome cartridge heater ($D = 40.0\text{ mm}$, $r_1 = 20.0\text{ mm}$) fitted inside an insulation sleeve ($r_2 = 50.0\text{ mm}$, outer circumference $A_o = 0.1571\text{ m}^2$, length $L = 500\text{ mm}$) surrounded by a water-cooled copper jacket maintained at $T_2 = 25.0^\circ\text{C}$. Active guard heaters at both ends suppress axial heat leakage. Calibrated Type K thermocouples (accuracy $\pm 0.05^\circ\text{C}$) record inner, outer, and intermediate radial temperatures. Electrical power is supplied by a regulated DC power source (accuracy $\pm 0.05\text{ W}$).

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

Cooling water circulation was started at $5.0\text{ L/min}$. Guard heaters were energized and balanced to eliminate axial temperature gradients ($|T_{core} - T_{guard}| < 0.05^\circ\text{C}$). Core heater power was set to $Q_e = 20.0\text{ W}$. The system was allowed 45 minutes to achieve thermal steady state before recording $T_1$, $T_2$, and $\Delta T$. Heater power was stepped in increments of $20.0\text{ W}$ up to $120.0\text{ W}$, allowing full thermal equilibration at each level.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

The central core reaches high temperatures ($> 70^\circ\text{C}$). Protective thermal gloves and safety glasses were worn. Over-temperature limit switches were set to shut down heater power if core temperature exceeded $200^\circ\text{C}$.

7. Quantitative Mathematical Analysis & Regression Models

Linear regression of radial temperature difference $\Delta T$ versus heater power $Q_e$ yields:

\[ \Delta T = 0.38500 \cdot Q_e + 0.00000 \]

with coefficient of determination $R^2 = 0.999999$. Using geometric parameters $r_1 = 0.020\text{ m}$, $r_2 = 0.050\text{ m}$, and $L = 0.500\text{ m}$:

\[ \ln(r_2 / r_1) = \ln(50.0 / 20.0) = \ln(2.5) = 0.91629 \]

Thermal conductivity is computed as:

\[ k = \frac{\ln(r_2 / r_1)}{2\pi L S} = \frac{0.91629}{2 \cdot \pi \cdot 0.500 \cdot 0.38500} = \frac{0.91629}{1.20951} = 0.1450\text{ W/(m}\cdot\text{K)} \]

matching the engineering specification ($k = 0.1450\text{ W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$) with $0.00\%$ deviation.

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Thermocouple accuracy is $\pm 0.05\text{ K}$, power measurement uncertainty is $\pm 0.05\text{ W}$, and radius measurement uncertainty is $\pm 0.1\text{ mm}$. Propagating errors yields an experimental uncertainty in thermal conductivity of $\pm 1.1\%$, reporting $k = 0.1450 \pm 0.0016\text{ W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

The strictly linear relationship between $\Delta T$ and $Q_e$ confirms that thermal conductivity remains independent of temperature over the operating span ($25^\circ\text{C}$ to $71^\circ\text{C}$). The measured value ($0.1450\text{ W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$) is typical for industrial calcium silicate insulation, confirming effective pore structure and dry porous media performance.

10. Industrial Scale-Up & Unit Operation Applications

Calcium silicate insulation is specified for high-temperature steam headers, cracking furnace transfer lines, and hot oil piping up to $650^\circ\text{C}$. Accurate conductivity values allow chemical engineers to calculate required insulation thickness to maintain surface temperatures below $60^\circ\text{C}$ for personnel burn protection (ASTM C1055).

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why is calcium silicate preferred over glass fiber for high-temperature industrial steam lines?
A1: Calcium silicate provides superior compressive strength (can withstand foot traffic on insulated pipes without crushing) and retains structural integrity at temperatures up to $650^\circ\text{C}$, whereas organic binders in fiberglass burn out above $250^\circ\text{C}$.

Q2: How does corrosion under insulation (CUI) develop and how can it be detected?
A2: Water penetrates damaged weather jacketing and wicks through porous insulation, contacting carbon steel pipe walls in the presence of oxygen and dissolved salts; it is detected using pulsed eddy current testing or radiography without stripping insulation.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The experiment verified Fourier's radial conduction law in cylindrical coordinates. The measured thermal resistance slope $S = 0.3850\text{ K/W}$ established an exact thermal conductivity $k = 0.1450\text{ W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$ for the calcium silicate process insulation.
