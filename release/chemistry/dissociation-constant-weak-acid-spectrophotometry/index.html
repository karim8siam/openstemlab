Physical chemistry assumptions and metrological limits

["Spectrophotometric pKa determination assumes strict adherence to Beer-Lambert's law and ideal two-state chemical equilibrium.", 'Total indicator concentration must remain in the range 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁴ M to prevent self-buffering and dye dimerization.', 'Secondary ionic strength variations modulate activity coefficients; constant ionic strength (I = 0.100 M) is required to maintain constant apparent pKa.'] The spectrophotometric model evaluates Bromothymol Blue (BTB) at λ = 616 nm across six buffer solutions from pH 6.20 to 7.70 at 25.00 °C. The limiting absorbances are A_acid = 0.040 AU (in 0.10 M HCl) and A_base = 0.960 AU (in 0.10 M NaOH). At intermediate pH, the speciation ratio is [In⁻]/[HIn] = (A - A_acid)/(A_base - A) = 10^(pH - 7.100). The measured absorbances are [0.143, 0.225, 0.347, 0.500, 0.653, 0.775] AU, corresponding to log ratios [-0.899, -0.599, -0.300, 0.000, 0.300, 0.599]. Linear least-squares regression yields an empirical slope m = 1.00124 (unitless) and Y-intercept b = pKa = 7.10002 with R² = 0.9999997, isolating an experimental pKa = 7.100 ± 0.005 and Ka = 7.94 × 10⁻⁸ M (difference = 0.0003% from literature benchmark 7.100), with standard Gibbs free energy of dissociation ΔG° = +40.53 kJ·mol⁻¹.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the thermodynamic acid dissociation constant (pKa) and standard Gibbs free energy of ionization of Bromothymol Blue indicator by precision spectrophotometric absorbance measurements at 616 nm across buffered solutions from pH 6.20 to 7.70 at 25.0 °C. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 6.2, 6.5, 6.8, 7.1, 7.4, 7.7 pH.

01

Spectrophotometer Initialization and Wavelength Calibration

Power on the dual-beam UV-Vis spectrophotometer and allow deuterium/tungsten lamp sources to warm up for 30 minutes to achieve photometric baseline stability. Set the monochromator to 616.0 nm with a 1.0 nm spectral bandwidth. Verify wavelength calibration using the 656.1 nm deuterium emission line.

02

Preparation of Extreme Limiting Reference Solutions

Prepare strongly acidic (0.10 M HCl) and strongly alkaline (0.10 M NaOH) standard solutions containing identical total indicator concentration (4.00 × 10⁻⁵ M BTB). Measure the limiting absorbance of pure protonated yellow acid (A_acid = 0.040 AU at pH 1.0) and pure deprotonated blue base (A_base = 0.960 AU at pH 12.0) at 616.0 nm against a deionized water blank.

03

Buffer Series Formulation and Precision pH Measurement

Formulate a series of six 0.100 M phosphate buffer solutions spanning the pH transition range (pH 6.20, 6.50, 6.80, 7.10, 7.40, and 7.70). Calibrate the combined glass electrode with certified pH 4.01 and 7.00 buffers at 25.00 °C. Measure and record the exact equilibrium pH of each buffer solution to ±0.01 pH units.

04

Quantitative Indicator Dosing and Thermal Equilibration

Transfer exactly 2.00 mL of 1.00 × 10⁻³ M stock Bromothymol Blue into each 50.00 mL volumetric flask, dilute to the mark with the respective phosphate buffer, and invert twenty times to ensure thorough homogenization. Place the flasks in the 25.00 °C water bath for 15 minutes to establish complete thermodynamic equilibrium.

05

Cuvette Loading and Absorbance Acquisition

Rinse the sample quartz cuvette twice with the buffered indicator solution, then fill to 80% capacity. Wipe the exterior optical windows with lint-free lens paper, inspect for microbubbles or dust, and insert into the sample beam holder. Insert the matched buffer blank into the reference beam and record the steady-state absorbance A₆₁₆ to three decimal places.

06

Multi-Point Absorbance Acquisition and Linear Regression

Repeat the spectrophotometric measurement across all six buffered indicator solutions in ascending order of pH. Compute the ionization ratio [In⁻]/[HIn] = (A - A_acid)/(A_base - A) and its logarithm for each solution. Perform ordinary least-squares regression of pH versus log([In⁻]/[HIn]) to extract the acid dissociation constant pKa from the intercept and verify theoretical unity slope.
