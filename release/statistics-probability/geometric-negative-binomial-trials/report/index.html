4. Theory and graph choice

The Geometric and Negative Binomial distributions govern stopping times and waiting intervals in sequential Bernoulli trials. Characterized by the discrete memoryless property in the geometric case and generalized to multi-success quotas under the Negative Binomial model, these distributions form the theoretical core of reliability testing, sequential clinical trials, and overdispersed count modeling. In this laboratory, we simulate stopping-time sequences, verify memorylessness, evaluate quota scaling, and confirm the linear expectation gradient m = 2.000. In mathematical statistics, empirical data science, and modern computational engineering, understanding the asymptotic sampling properties and theoretical behavior of Geometric Distribution, Memoryless Property, and Negative Binomial Stopping Times provides crucial guarantees. Rigorous experimental validation connects foundational probability laws with observable empirical phenomena, ensuring that statistical inference remains valid under finite sample sizes and real-world data collection constraints.

5. Method

  1. Set the Bernoulli success probability to p = 0.50 on the simulation dashboard, noting that the theoretical expected trials until first success is 1/p = 2.000.
  2. Simulate 10,000 runs of the experiment with stopping quota r = 1. Record the distribution of total trials required to obtain the initial success.
  3. Compute conditional frequencies P(X > s + t | X > s) for s = 2 and t = 1, verifying agreement with the unconditional survival probability P(X > t) = (1-p)^t = 0.50.
  4. Configure the stopping engine to halt only when exactly r successes have accumulated, observing that the Negative Binomial variable is the sum of r independent Geometric variables.
  5. Increment the target quota across standardized settings r ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}, recording the empirical mean total trials E[X] for each setting.
  6. Fit a linear regression model y = mx to the coordinate pairs (x = r, y = E[X]). Confirm that the empirical gradient m = 2.0000 matches theoretical expectation E[X] = r/p.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure the termination condition is checked after every single trial to avoid over-counting stopping times.
  • Maintain separate accumulator registers for successful trials and failure trials to avoid parameter confusion.
  • Verify that p is strictly bounded in (0, 1) to prevent infinite loops (at p = 0) or degenerate single-step trials (at p = 1).
  • Use sufficiently large Monte Carlo sample sizes (N ≥ 10,000) when estimating higher-order Negative Binomial moments.

The memoryless property fails if the success probability p shifts or drifts across sequential trials. The empirical regression of expected trial count against success quota r yields a gradient of m = 2.0000, confirming theoretical waiting time scaling with R² = 1.000000.
