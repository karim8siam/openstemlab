4. Theory and graph choice

In end-stage renal disease (ESRD), loss of native renal function leads to fatal accumulation of uremic toxins, fluid overload, and electrolyte disturbances. Hemodialysis serves as life-sustaining renal replacement therapy by passing the patient's blood through an artificial kidney (hollow-fiber hemodialyzer) counter-current to an aqueous dialysate solution. A clinical dialyzer contains 10,000 to 15,000 semipermeable capillary fibers (inner diameter ~200 μm, wall thickness ~30 μm, effective surface area 1.5 to 2.0 m²) composed of synthetic polysulfone or polyethersulfone polymers. Solute removal is driven by diffusion across the membrane pore network down concentration gradients. The dialyzer clearance K of a solute (such as urea, MW 60 Da) is defined as the volume of blood completely cleared of solute per unit time: K = Q_bi · (C_bi - C_bo) / C_bi + Q_f · (C_bo / C_bi), where Q_bi is blood inlet flow, C_bi and C_bo are inlet and outlet solute concentrations, and Q_f is ultrafiltration rate. In the absence of ultrafiltration (pure diffusion), clearance depends on membrane mass transfer-area coefficient K_0·A, dialysate flow Q_d, and blood flow Q_b. Over typical clinical blood flows (150 to 400 mL/min) with high dialysate flow (Q_d = 500 mL/min), urea clearance scales linearly with blood pump delivery: K_u = m_c · Q_b + K_u0, where m_c is the flow sensitivity coefficient. In this laboratory experiment, a clinical dialysis machine test station evaluates hollow-fiber dialyzer urea mass transfer across blood flows from 150 to 400 mL/min.

K_u = m_c · Q_b + K_u0 = 0.5800 · Q_b + 42.0000

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Blood flow rate Q_b (mL/min), and the dependent coordinate y represents Urea clearance K_u (mL/min). The gradient m directly determines Clearance-Flow Sensitivity Slope m_c, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Mount dialyzer vertically, prime blood side with 1 L heparinized saline at 150 mL/min to displace air, and invert to purge dialysate compartment.
  2. Set dialysate delivery to 500 mL/min at 37.0 °C with bicarbonate conductivity 14.0 mS/cm and verify zero ultrafiltration transmembrane pressure.
  3. Prepare synthetic blood reservoir with urea concentration C_bi = 25.0 mmol/L (150 mg/dL BUN) at 37.0 °C under gentle magnetic stirring.
  4. Set blood pump to delivery rate Q_b = 150 mL/min, circulate counter-current to dialysate, and allow 5 minutes for concentration steady state.
  5. Simultaneously sample inlet port (C_bi) and dialyzer outlet port (C_bo), assaying urea concentrations via spectrophotometric enzymatic assay to calculate clearance K_u.
  6. Repeat sampling at Q_b = 200, 250, 300, 350, and 400 mL/min, plot urea clearance K_u versus Q_b, and determine flow sensitivity slope m_c.
  7. Verify dialyzer performance against the theoretical logarithmic mean mass transfer relation K_0·A = [Q_b·Q_d / (Q_d - Q_b)] · ln[(1 - K/Q_d)/(1 - K/Q_b)].

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure dialysate is thoroughly degassed; air bubbles trapped in the fiber bundle reduce effective surface area and impair clearance.
  • Verify zero net ultrafiltration (Q_f = 0) so that measured solute clearance reflects pure diffusive mass transfer.
  • Calibrate blood pump roller tubing occlusion; under-occlusion causes internal back-leakage and false blood delivery rate readings.
  • Draw blood inlet and outlet samples simultaneously to account for any gradual drawdown in the recirculation reservoir.

The linear clearance-flow model applies across standard clinical blood flows (150 to 400 mL/min); at extreme flows (> 500 mL/min), clearance plateaus asymptotically.
