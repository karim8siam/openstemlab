1. Title and aim

Fruit & Vegetable Respiration Heat Rate under Controlled Atmosphere. Measure the carbon dioxide evolution rate and respiration heat production of stored agricultural produce across storage temperatures using closed-system gas analysis.

3. Apparatus and fixed settings

  • Hermetic Acrylic Respiration Chamber: Provides an airtight 50-liter test enclosure with internal gas circulation fan and sampling ports.
  • Refrigerated Environmental Test Chamber: Regulates ambient chamber temperature from 0 °C to 25 °C within ±0.1 °C stability.
  • Non-Dispersive Infrared (NDIR) CO2 Analyzer: Monitors carbon dioxide concentration rise with 1 ppm resolution over discrete sampling intervals.
  • Galvanic Fuel-Cell Oxygen Sensor: Measures oxygen depletion rate to compute the respiratory quotient (RQ = CO2 produced / O2 consumed).
  • Pulp Core Thermistor Probes: Monitors internal flesh temperature of representative fruit specimens to verify core thermal equilibrium.
  • Precision Platform Balance: Measures total fresh produce test mass to normalize gas evolution rates to milligrams per kilogram-hour.
  • Commodity: Gala apples (freshly harvested)
  • Chamber volume: 50.0 L hermetic acrylic
  • Baseline gas: Standard air (21% O2, 400 ppm CO2)

4. Theory and graph choice

Fresh agricultural horticultural produce (fruits, vegetables, and ornamentals) remains metabolically active living tissue following post-harvest harvest. Cellular respiration consumes stored carbohydrates (primarily glucose) and oxygen while generating carbon dioxide, water vapor, and vital metabolic heat: C6H12O6 + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + 2870 kJ/mol. In cold storage design and refrigeration load calculations, respiration heat represents a dynamic, internal thermal load that must be continuously extracted by the evaporator coils to prevent temperature rise, quality degradation, and senescent breakdown. In accordance with Arrhenius and van 't Hoff biological temperature kinetics, respiration rate accelerates markedly with increasing pulp temperature, approximately doubling or tripling for each 10 °C temperature increase (Q10 coefficient). Within the commercial refrigerated storage window of 2 °C to 22 °C, the carbon dioxide evolution rate R_CO2 displays a predictable linear sensitivity k_r to storage temperature. In this laboratory experiment, a batch of fresh Gala apples is sealed inside a temperature-controlled hermetic respiration chamber equipped with non-dispersive infrared (NDIR) CO2 gas analyzers and oxygen sensors to measure respiration kinetics and determine refrigeration heat loads.

R_CO2 = k_r · T + R_0 = 1.4500 · T + 3.2000

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents Temperature T (°C), and the dependent axis y represents CO2 evolution rate R_CO2 (mg/(kg·h)). The gradient m directly determines Thermal Respiration Sensitivity k_r, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Weigh exactly 10.00 kg of uniform, blemish-free Gala apples and insert core thermistors into two representative reference fruits.
  2. Place the produce in the environmental chamber at 2.0 °C for 6 hours until fruit pulp core reaches within 0.2 °C of setpoint.
  3. Seal the respiration chamber lid and flush briefly with fresh air to establish standard 400 ppm baseline atmospheric CO2.
  4. Isolate the chamber and record NDIR CO2 accumulation over a 60-minute incubation period, ensuring CO2 remains below 10,000 ppm.
  5. Repeat thermal equilibration and 60-minute CO2 accumulation trials at 6.0, 10.0, 14.0, 18.0, and 22.0 °C.
  6. Calculate R_CO2 in mg/(kg·h) and plot against temperature T, fitting a linear regression to determine thermal respiration sensitivity k_r.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure produce pulp core temperature reaches true thermal equilibrium before initiating gas collection.
  • Verify airtight seal integrity with pressure decay test prior to each gas accumulation run.
  • Limit measurement duration so that O2 depletion remains under 1.5% and CO2 buildup does not exceed 1.0%.
  • Calibrate the NDIR CO2 analyzer with certified zero and span gases before testing.
