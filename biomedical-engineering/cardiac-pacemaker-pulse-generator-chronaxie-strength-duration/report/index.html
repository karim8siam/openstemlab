4. Theory and graph choice

Implantable cardiac pacemakers are life-sustaining electronic medical devices that treat symptomatic bradyarrhythmias, high-grade atrioventricular (AV) blocks, and sick sinus syndrome by delivering timed electrical stimulation pulses to myocardial tissue. The electrical excitability of excitable cardiac cell membranes is governed by the classic neurostimulation principles formulated by Louis Lapicque and Georges Weiss. To trigger an action potential that propagates across the myocardium and initiates coordinated ventricular contraction ('capture'), the delivered electrical pulse must depolarize the cell membrane from its resting potential (-85 mV) to its threshold potential (-65 mV). The minimum stimulation voltage V_th required for capture depends on pulse duration t_p according to the Lapicque strength-duration relationship: V_th = V_rh · (1 + t_ch / t_p) = (V_rh · t_ch) · (1 / t_p) + V_rh = m_ch · (1 / t_p) + V_rh, where V_rh is the rheobase voltage (the asymptotic minimum voltage threshold at infinitely long pulse width), t_ch is the chronaxie time (the pulse duration required to achieve capture at exactly twice the rheobase voltage: V_th = 2 · V_rh), and m_ch = V_rh · t_ch is the Lapicque slope constant (V·ms). In clinical pacemaker programming, setting pulse width near the chronaxie (typically 0.4 to 0.5 ms for modern steroid-eluting steroid collar leads) optimizes battery longevity by minimizing delivered pacing energy (E = V² · t_p / R). In this experiment, an instrumented cardiac electrophysiology simulator evaluates pacemaker threshold voltages across pulse durations from 0.2 to 1.0 ms.

V_th = m_ch · (1/t_p) + V_rh = 0.3200 · (1/t_p) + 0.6500

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Inverse pulse duration 1/t_p (ms⁻¹), and the dependent coordinate y represents Threshold voltage V_th (V). The gradient m directly determines Lapicque Slope Parameter m_ch, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Submerge bipolar pacing lead tip in 37.0 °C saline, connect to pacing simulator, and verify pacing impedance R = 550 ± 20 Ω via telemetry.
  2. Position wireless programming wand over pacemaker, establish bidirectional telemetry link, and select automated capture threshold testing mode.
  3. Program pacemaker pulse duration to t_p = 0.20 ms (inverse width 1/t_p = 5.00 ms⁻¹).
  4. Step pacing voltage downward from 3.0 V in 0.05 V increments until evoked electrical response ceases, recording last successful capture voltage as V_th.
  5. Increment pulse width through 0.25 ms (1/t_p = 4.00), 0.333 ms (3.00), 0.50 ms (2.00), 0.667 ms (1.50), and 1.00 ms (1.00 ms⁻¹), measuring capture threshold V_th at each setting.
  6. Plot threshold voltage V_th versus inverse pulse width 1/t_p, extract slope m_ch and y-intercept V_rh, and calculate chronaxie time t_ch = m_ch / V_rh.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Always maintain a clinical safety margin (typically programming output voltage to at least 2 times capture threshold or 3 times safety margin) to prevent loss of capture.
  • Ensure pacemaker lead tip is fully submerged in physiological saline to prevent electrode polarization artifacts.
  • Verify oscilloscope input impedance is > 1 MΩ to avoid loading the pacemaker output circuit.
  • Allow 10 pacing cycles at each voltage step during threshold testing to ensure 100% reliable myocardial capture.

The Lapicque-Weiss model assumes a simple parallel R-C membrane circuit, neglecting active voltage-gated sodium channel reactivation dynamics. Evaluated on a Medtronic clinical cardiac pulse generator interfaced with a steroid-eluting active-fixation bipolar endocardial pacing lead (tip area 1.2 mm², pacing impedance R = 550 Ω) immersed in 37.0 °C physiological saline. Rheobase voltage V_rh = 0.6500 V, chronaxie time t_ch = 0.4923 ms, yielding Lapicque slope m_ch = 0.3200 V·ms across pulse widths t_p from 0.20 to 1.00 ms (1/t_p from 5.0 to 1.0 ms⁻¹).
