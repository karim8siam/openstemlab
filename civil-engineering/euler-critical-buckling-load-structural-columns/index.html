FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the critical elastic buckling load (P_cr) of slender structural columns under pinned-pinned, pinned-fixed, and fixed-fixed end constraints, and verify Euler's column buckling formula according to ASTM E9 / AISC 360. Standardized parameter settings: 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5 m^-2.

01

Specimen Cross-Sectional Geometry Verification

Measure column thickness t at five points with micrometer to 0.01 mm; measure width b and clear unsupported length L; compute minimum moment of inertia I_min = b t^3 / 12.

02

End Fixture Installation & Alignment

Install desired boundary fixtures (pinned-pinned K=1.0, pinned-fixed K=0.7, fixed-fixed K=0.5); insert column into end sockets ensuring strict vertical alignment.

03

Lateral Dial Indicator Setup

Mount digital lateral displacement indicator at column mid-height; zero indicator at zero axial load.

04

Axial Compressive Loading

Apply axial compressive load slowly and steadily using the loading handwheel; monitor load P and lateral deflection v_mid simultaneously.

05

Bifurcation Point & Critical Load Identification

Observe onset of lateral bowing; log the critical buckling load P_cr where lateral deflection accelerates rapidly under virtually zero incremental load.

06

Constraint Sweep & Regression Verification

Repeat testing across variable effective lengths 1/(KL)^2 by altering boundary restraints and column lengths; plot P_cr versus 1/(KL)^2 to determine stiffness slope π^2·EI.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

In structural and civil engineering, columns and compression members fail not by material yielding or crushing, but by sudden out-of-plane elastic buckling instability. Discovered by Swiss mathematician Leonhard Euler in 1757, buckling occurs when an axially compressed slender column transitions from stable straight equilibrium to an unstable bifurcated curved equilibrium state. The differential equation governing column lateral deflection v(x) under axial compressive load P is E I (d^2v/dx^2) + P v = 0. Solving this eigenvalue boundary-value problem yields the famous Euler buckling formula: P_cr = π^2 E I / (K L)^2, where E is Young's modulus, I is the minimum second moment of area about the weak buckling axis, L is physical unsupported column length, and K is the effective length factor governed by end boundary restraints. Under classical boundary conditions: K = 1.0 for pinned-pinned ends, K = 0.707 for pinned-fixed ends, K = 0.50 for fixed-fixed ends, and K = 2.0 for a cantilever column with free top. Slender columns with slenderness ratio λ = K L / r > 120 buckle purely elastically at compressive stresses well below material yield strength f_y. In this laboratory investigation, slender structural columns are tested under variable end constraints and effective lengths to calibrate Euler stiffness π^2·EI across slenderness parameters.
