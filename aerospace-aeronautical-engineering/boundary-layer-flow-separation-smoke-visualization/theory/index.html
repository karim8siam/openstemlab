Aerodynamic and physical foundations of Smoke Flow Separation.

Aerodynamic stall represents the catastrophic breakdown of attached flow over a lifting surface, resulting in abrupt loss of lift, massive increases in pressure drag, and violent unsteady aerodynamic buffeting. When an airfoil operates at high angles of attack, fluid particles negotiating the upper surface must overcome an intense adverse pressure gradient (dP/dx > 0) as flow decelerates from the leading-edge suction peak toward the trailing-edge ambient pressure. Viscous friction continuously saps kinetic energy from the near-wall boundary layer. Eventually, the near-wall velocity gradient drops to zero ((du/dy)_w = 0)—the mathematical definition of the separation point. Downstream of this point, reverse flow sets in, the boundary layer breaks away from the surface, and a broad turbulent recirculation zone forms. In this laboratory experiment, a smoke-wire flow visualization system is installed upstream of a NACA 0012 airfoil. A thin nichrome wire coated with mineral oil is pulsed with electrical current, releasing tightly spaced streaklines that trace the streamline contours. High-speed digital imaging tracks the chordwise location of flow separation x_sep / c across angles of attack from attached flow (alpha = 6 deg) through deep stall (alpha = 18 deg). Plotting the non-dimensional separated chord fraction (1 - x_sep / c) against angle of attack demonstrates the linear forward migration of the turbulent separation point prior to full stall.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What is the fluid mechanical definition of the flow separation point on a solid wall?

The flow separation point is defined as the location where the wall-normal gradient of tangential velocity vanishes: (du/dy)_w = 0. This implies that the local wall shear stress drops to zero: tau_w = mu * (du/dy)_w = 0. Downstream of this point, reverse flow occurs.

Why does an adverse pressure gradient cause a boundary layer to separate?

In an adverse pressure gradient (dP/dx > 0), static pressure increases in the direction of flow, exerting a net decelerating force on fluid particles. Because particles in the near-wall boundary layer have already lost kinetic energy to viscous friction, they lack sufficient momentum to overcome this pressure hill, come to a standstill, and reverse direction.

What is the difference between trailing-edge stall and leading-edge stall?

Trailing-edge stall occurs on thick, cambered airfoils where turbulent separation initiates near the trailing edge and creeps forward progressively as alpha increases, producing a gentle, gradual loss of lift. Leading-edge stall occurs on thin airfoils where a laminar separation bubble bursts abruptly near the leading edge, causing sudden, violent stall.

How does a laminar separation bubble form on an airfoil?

A laminar boundary layer encounters a steep local adverse pressure gradient and separates. In the separated shear layer, transition to turbulence occurs rapidly due to Kelvin-Helmholtz instability. The turbulent flow entrains high-energy fluid from the free stream, causing the flow to reattach downstream, enclosing a laminar separation bubble.

What devices are used on aircraft wings to delay boundary layer separation?

Passive devices include vortex generators (which mix high-momentum free-stream fluid into the sluggish boundary layer), leading-edge slats, and wing turbulators. Active flow control devices include synthetic jet actuators, boundary layer suction, and tangential blowing.

How does Reynolds number affect the stall angle and maximum lift coefficient of an airfoil?

Higher Reynolds numbers thin the boundary layer and increase its momentum, making it more resilient against adverse pressure gradients. Consequently, higher Reynolds numbers delay flow separation, increasing both the stall angle alpha_stall and maximum lift coefficient C_L,max.

Experimental precautions and wind tunnel safety

  • Operate smoke pulse for no longer than 2.0 seconds per run to prevent excessive oil accumulation on optical windows.
  • Maintain adequate laboratory ventilation and exhaust filtration to disperse paraffin aerosol vapor.
  • Keep the test section airspeed at or below 15 m/s during smoke-wire runs; higher speeds induce turbulent dissipation of smoke streaklines.
  • Verify that the nichrome wire tension is sufficient to prevent aerodynamic bowing under wind-on conditions.

Theoretical assumptions & flow limits

  • Smoke-wire flow visualization is restricted to low Reynolds numbers (Re_c < 2.0 x 10^5) to maintain distinct streaklines.
  • Oil droplets on wire introduce slight periodic three-dimensional disturbances into oncoming streamlines.
  • Visual identification of separation point possesses an optical uncertainty of approximately +/- 2% chord.
  • Two-dimensional observation neglects spanwise stall cell structures (stall eggs) that form during deep post-stall.
