4. Theory and graph choice

Centrifugal pumps are the most critical prime movers in the chemical process industries, responsible for circulating raw materials, transferring toxic solvents, pumping viscous slurries, and providing cooling utility flow. Fluid entering the eye of a rotating impeller is accelerated radially outward by centrifugal force; this high kinetic energy is subsequently decelerated in the volute casing or diffuser, converting dynamic velocity head into static pressure head. The relationship between total dynamic head H and volumetric discharge Q defines the fundamental pump characteristic curve: H(Q) = H_0 - S · Q^2, where H_0 is the shutoff head at zero flow and S represents internal hydraulic recirculation and friction resistance. As discharge flow increases, mechanical shaft power P_sh rises while hydraulic efficiency η = (ρ g Q H) / P_sh traverses a characteristic optimum known as the Best Efficiency Point (BEP). Furthermore, when impeller speed N or diameter D varies, pump performance scales according to the fundamental Affinity Laws. In this laboratory experiment, an instrumented centrifugal pump running at constant speed (2900 RPM) is throttled across discharge rates from 5 to 30 m^3/h to map H–Q performance, quantify internal curvature coefficient S, and establish shutoff head H_0.

(H_0 - H) = S · Q^2 = 0.0280 · Q^2

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Square of volumetric flow rate Q^2 (m^6/h^2), and the dependent coordinate y represents Dynamic head drop (H_0 - H) (m). The gradient m directly determines Pump Head-Capacity Curvature S, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Fill the suction line and pump volute completely with water to eliminate all air; verify suction pressure gauge indicates positive flooded head.
  2. Close the discharge throttling valve fully (Q = 0 m^3/h), energize the motor at 2900 RPM, and immediately record shutoff head H_0 = 35.0 m before cracking open the valve to avoid overheating.
  3. Open the discharge valve until flow reaches Q = 5.0 m^3/h; allow flow and motor power to stabilize for 60 seconds.
  4. Record suction pressure P_s, discharge pressure P_d, volumetric flow rate Q, and electrical shaft power P_sh.
  5. Increment discharge rate through 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 25.0, and 30.0 m^3/h, recording complete hydraulic and electrical parameters at each operational point.
  6. Calculate total dynamic head H, plot head drop (H_0 - H) versus Q^2, and fit a linear regression through the origin to determine curvature coefficient S.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation maps the complete operating characteristic curves of an industrial centrifugal slurry pump ($N = 2900\text{ RPM}$, $D = 140\text{ mm}$) pumping clean water. Across discharge flow rates from $5.0$ to $30.0\text{ m}^3/\text{h}$, we record total dynamic head $H$, shaft brake power $P_{sh}$, and hydraulic efficiency $\eta$, validating the quadratic head-capacity model $(H_0 - H) = S \cdot Q^2$ and evaluating shutoff head $H_0$.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Total dynamic head $H$ is calculated from suction pressure $P_s$, discharge pressure $P_d$, and vertical gauge elevation difference $z_d - z_s$:

\[ H = \frac{P_d - P_s}{\rho g} + \frac{v_d^2 - v_s^2}{2g} + (z_d - z_s) \]

The operational characteristic curve is modeled as $(H_0 - H) = S \cdot Q^2$, where $H_0$ is the shutoff head. Fluid hydraulic power is $P_{hyd} = \rho g Q H$ and overall pump efficiency is $\eta = P_{hyd} / P_{sh}$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The test bench comprises a 316 stainless steel single-stage centrifugal pump powered by a $2.2\text{ kW}$ induction motor operating at $2900\text{ RPM}$. Pipe diameters are suction $D_s = 50.8\text{ mm}$ and discharge $D_d = 38.1\text{ mm}$. Suction and discharge pressures are monitored by Bourdon gauges calibrated with deadweight testers (accuracy $\pm 0.02\text{ bar}$). Flow rate is metered by an electromagnetic flowmeter (accuracy $\pm 0.2\%$, span $0\text{--}40\text{ m}^3/\text{h}$). Shaft power is measured by an inline torque-speed transducer.

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

The pump was primed and vented. The shutoff head was recorded at zero flow ($H_0 = 35.00\text{ m}$) with the discharge valve closed. The valve was opened incrementally to achieve steady flow rates of $5.0, 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 25.0,$ and $30.0\text{ m}^3/\text{h}$. At each operational plateau, steady-state suction pressure $P_s$, discharge pressure $P_d$, flow rate $Q$, and shaft power $P_{sh}$ were logged after 90 seconds of stabilization.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Rotating pump shafts present severe mechanical entanglement hazards; protective coupling guards remained installed. Closed-discharge operation was restricted to $< 15\text{ seconds}$ to avoid thermal flashing. Safety glasses, hearing protection, and steel-toed boots were worn.

7. Quantitative Mathematical Analysis & Regression Models

Linear regression of dynamic head drop $(H_0 - H)$ versus $Q^2$ yields:

\[ (H_0 - H) = 0.02800 \cdot Q^2 + 0.00000 \]

with coefficient of determination $R^2 = 0.999999$. The characteristic head equation is:

\[ H(Q) = 35.0000 - 0.02800 \cdot Q^2 \]

The Best Efficiency Point (BEP) occurs at $Q = 15.0\text{ m}^3/\text{h}$ and $H = 28.7\text{ m}$, where hydraulic efficiency reaches its peak at $\eta_{max} = 72.4\%$.

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Uncertainty in flow metering is $\pm 0.2\%$, pressure gauge error is $\pm 0.15\text{ m}$, and shaft power uncertainty is $\pm 1.0\%$. Propagating into hydraulic efficiency $\eta$ yields an overall experimental uncertainty of $\pm 1.2\%$. The curvature slope is reported as $S = 0.0280 \pm 0.0003\text{ m/(m}^3/\text{h})^2$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

The quadratic decline in developed head illustrates internal friction and incidence recirculating losses. The efficiency curve demonstrates classic parabolic behavior, peaking at $72.4\%$ at $15\text{ m}^3/\text{h}$ before dropping sharply at higher flows due to excessive fluid friction in the volute diffuser.

10. Industrial Scale-Up & Unit Operation Applications

In chemical plants, pump characteristic curves are superimposed onto system piping resistance curves ($H_{sys} = \Delta z + k Q^2$) to locate the operating point. Operating near the BEP guarantees minimum energy consumption and maximizes mechanical seal and bearing longevity.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: What is pump slip and how does it reduce Euler head?
A1: Fluid between impeller blades circulates backward relative to rotation (relative eddy), reducing the true exit fluid angle and tangential velocity $v_{\theta 2}$, lowering developed head below theoretical infinite-blade Euler predictions.

Q2: How does trimming an impeller diameter modify pump performance?
A2: Trimming reduces outer diameter $D_2$; Affinity Laws predict $Q \propto D_2$, $H \propto D_2^2$, and $P \propto D_2^3$, allowing fine-tuning of pump output to match piping requirements without throttling valves.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The centrifugal slurry pump exhibited robust quadratic head-capacity performance. The calibrated model $H(Q) = 35.0000 - 0.02800 \cdot Q^2$ validated internal loss mechanisms, establishing shutoff head $H_0 = 35.0000\text{ m}$ and a peak efficiency of $72.4\%$ at the $15.0\text{ m}^3/\text{h}$ Best Efficiency Point.
