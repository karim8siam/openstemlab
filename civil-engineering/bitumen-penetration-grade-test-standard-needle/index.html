FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Measure vertical depth of penetration of a standard 100 g loaded steel needle into bituminous asphalt binder at 25°C over 5 seconds to classify paving bitumen grade (e.g., 60/70, 80/100) according to ASTM D5 / EN 1426. Standardized parameter settings: 20.0, 23.0, 26.0, 29.0, 32.0, 35.0 °C.

01

Bitumen Melting & Bubble-Free Pouring

Heat bulk bitumen sample in oven at 140 °C until fluid; stir gently to homogenize without entraining air bubbles; pour into test container to depth ≥ 30 mm; cover with beaker to protect from dust.

02

Ambient Cooling & Water Bath Conditioning

Cool specimen in air at room temperature for 1.5 hours; transfer into thermostatic circulating water bath at target temperature (e.g. 25.0 ± 0.1 °C); condition for exactly 1.5 hours.

03

Submerged Transfer & Needle Alignment

Place sample into transfer dish filled with water from bath; position on penetrometer stage; clean needle with toluene; mount needle and attach 50 g weight to make 100.0 g total load.

04

Needle Tip Surface Contact Zeroing

Using micrometer adjustment screw and mirror light reflection, lower needle until its sharp tip just makes contact with the bitumen surface without indentation; zero digital dial gauge.

05

Automated Five-Second Release & Depth Measurement

Press start trigger; the automatic timer releases the needle assembly for exactly 5.0 seconds and clamps it; record penetration depth in decimillimeters (dmm).

06

Triplicate Testing Across Systematic Temperatures

Repeat determinations at three points spaced ≥ 10 mm from cup edge and each other; calculate mean penetration; repeat across temperatures from 20.0 to 35.0 °C to plot thermal susceptibility.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

In civil highway materials engineering and flexible pavement design, petroleum asphalt bitumen is the colloidal thermoplastic binder binding mineral aggregates into cohesive pavements. As a viscoelastic material derived from crude oil vacuum distillation residues, bitumen exhibits strong temperature susceptibility: it behaves as an elastic glass-like solid at freezing temperatures, a viscoelastic ductile solid at ambient temperatures, and a Newtonian fluid at elevated mixing temperatures (140 to 160 °C). The Standard Penetration Test (ASTM D5 / EN 1426) is the historical and foundational consistency test used worldwide to classify paving bitumen into standardized penetration grades (such as 40/50, 60/70, and 80/100). The test measures the distance in decimillimeters (1 dmm = 0.1 mm) that a standard polished stainless steel needle (100.0 g total moving assembly mass, 1.00 to 1.02 mm diameter, 3.2° tapered conical tip) penetrates vertically into a bitumen sample maintained in a thermostatic water bath at 25.0 ± 0.1 °C over exactly 5.0 ± 0.1 seconds. A softer bitumen permits deeper penetration (higher dmm), suitable for cold climatic regions to prevent thermal fracture; a harder bitumen yields lower penetration (e.g. 40/50 grade), specified for hot desert climates to resist heavy truck wheel rutting. In this laboratory experiment, penetration depth is evaluated across water bath temperatures from 20.0 to 35.0 °C to quantify thermal susceptibility slope = 3.8000 dmm/°C and classify 60/70 penetration grade binder (Pen = 70.0 dmm at 25 °C).
