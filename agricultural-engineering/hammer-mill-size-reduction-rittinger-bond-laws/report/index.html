1. Title and aim

Hammer Mill Size Reduction, Sieve Fineness Modulus & Bond's Comminution Law. Determine the specific electrical grinding energy, particle size distribution, and fineness modulus of ground grain in a hammer mill, and calibrate Bond's comminution law.

4. Theory and graph choice

Size reduction (comminution) of agricultural cereal grains (corn, barley, sorghum) is universally practiced in animal feed manufacturing to increase surface area, improve digestive enzyme accessibility, and enhance feed conversion efficiency in livestock and poultry. The hammer mill is the primary industrial comminution machine in feed mills, reducing particle size through high-speed impact between swinging hardened steel hammers (peripheral tip speeds 60 to 90 m/s) and grain kernels, supplemented by attrition against a perforated cylindrical screen. The energy required for mechanical size reduction is governed by classical comminution laws: Rittinger's law states that energy is proportional to the new surface area created (E ∝ 1/d_p); Kick's law states that energy is proportional to the reduction ratio (E ∝ ln(d_initial / d_p)); while Bond's third law of comminution states that energy scales inversely with the square root of particle diameter: E_s = 10·W_i·(1/√d_p - 1/√d_initial) ≈ K_b·(1/√d_p). The ground feed particle size distribution and uniform fineness modulus (FM) are evaluated using standard Tyler sieve shakers in accordance with ASABE Standard S319. In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented pilot hammer mill with digital power analyzer grinds corn through varying screen sizes to calibrate Bond's work factor.

E_s = K_b · (1 / √d_p) = 4.8000 · (1 / √d_p)

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents Size parameter 1/√d_p (mm^-0.5), and the dependent axis y represents Specific energy E_s (kWh/t). The gradient m directly determines Bond Work Index Scaling Factor K_b, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Install the 6.0 mm perforated screen in the hammer mill. Run the mill empty at rated speed (3600 RPM) to record baseline idle mechanical friction and windage power (P_idle).
  2. Weigh exactly 10.0 kg of shelled corn, start the vibratory feeder to deliver grain at 100 kg/h, and integrate net active electrical energy (kWh) until the batch is completely ground.
  3. Calculate net specific grinding energy E_s = (E_total - E_idle) / Mass_batch (kWh/tonne).
  4. Take a representative 100 g sample of ground meal, place in the standard Ro-Tap sieve shaker for 10.0 minutes, weigh fractions on each sieve, and compute geometric mean diameter d_p in accordance with ASABE S319.
  5. Repeat the grinding trial across screens of 4.5, 3.0, 2.0, 1.5, and 1.0 mm, recording specific energy E_s and particle diameter d_p for each trial.
  6. Calculate size parameter 1/√d_p (mm^-0.5), plot specific energy E_s versus 1/√d_p, and determine Bond's work scaling factor K_b via linear regression.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Install a strong permanent magnet at the feed hopper inlet to trap tramp metal (bolts, nails) that would cause sparks or explode in the mill.
  • Ensure ear defenders and dust respirator masks are worn during all hammer mill operating trials.
  • Never open the grinding chamber door until the rotor has come to a complete, verified stop.
  • Allow the motor to reach full operating speed before initiating grain feeding to prevent electrical stalling.

Hammer mills produce a wide, polydisperse particle size distribution with substantial fines compared to roller mills. Evaluated using shelled feed corn (initial diameter d_initial = 7.5 mm) ground through hammer mill screens from 1.0 mm to 6.0 mm (1/√d_p from 0.5 to 2.0 mm^-0.5).
