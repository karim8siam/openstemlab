FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Quantify localized minor energy losses (h_L) and loss coefficients (K_L) across 90° elbows, sudden pipe enlargements, sudden contractions, and globe valves in pressurized pipe flow. Standardized parameter settings: 0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.2, 0.25, 0.3 m.

01

Apparatus Priming & Air Bubble Purging

Start recirculating pump; open bleed valves on all piezometer tubes; purge all air until clear, bubble-free water columns appear in the manometer bank.

02

Zero Flow Static Datum Check

Close downstream control valve; verify that all piezometer tubes read identical horizontal levels corresponding to static datum.

03

Flow Adjustment to Initial Velocity Head

Adjust pump speed and throttle valve to establish initial velocity head v²/(2g) = 0.05 m in the reference 25 mm pipe.

04

Piezometric Head Drop Measurement

Record piezometric levels immediately upstream (h_u) and downstream (h_d) of the test fitting assembly; subtract continuous pipe friction between taps to isolate net minor head loss h_L.

05

Systematic Velocity Head Escalation

Increase flow across six systematic velocity head steps (0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25, and 0.30 m); log net head loss for each trial.

06

Minor Loss Coefficient Regression Calculation

Plot minor head loss h_L versus kinetic velocity head v²/(2g); determine linear slope to evaluate empirical loss coefficient K_L.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

In civil pipeline networks, water treatment piping galleries, building plumbing systems, and fire protection distribution mains, fluids encounter valves, elbows, tees, reducers, and sudden cross-sectional geometry changes that induce flow separation, recirculating vortices, and severe eddy turbulence. Although termed 'minor losses' in contrast to continuous pipe wall friction ('major losses'), these localized disruptions frequently dominate total system head loss in complex plant pipework. Localized minor head loss h_L is formulated in terms of kinetic velocity head: h_L = K_L · (v² / 2g), where K_L is the dimensionless minor loss coefficient. For a sudden enlargement from diameter D_1 to D_2, the Borda-Carnot momentum equation provides an exact analytical loss coefficient: K_L = [1 - (D_1/D_2)²]². For sudden contractions, loss occurs due to flow convergence into a vena contracta followed by turbulent re-expansion (K_L ≈ 0.4 to 0.5). For standard 90° elbows, flow separation along the inner radius and secondary swirling double-vortices produce K_L ≈ 0.75 to 0.90. Fully open globe valves introduce intense tortuous flow redirection (K_L ≈ 5.0 to 10.0), whereas gate valves offer minimal resistance (K_L ≈ 0.15). In this laboratory test rig, multi-port differential manometers record piezometric head drops across standardized pipe fittings across kinetic heads from 0.05 to 0.35 m to determine empirical loss coefficient K_L = 2.4500.
