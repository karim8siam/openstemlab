Biomedical and physiological foundations of LVDT Displacement Sensor.

Accurate linear displacement transduction is vital in medical diagnostic systems, including respiratory chest-wall plethysmography, prosthetic knee joint articulation tracking, spinal distraction monitoring, and precision mechanical organ tissue biomechanics. The Linear Variable Differential Transformer (LVDT) is an electromechanical inductive sensor consisting of a central primary coil flanked symmetrically by two identical secondary coils wound on a cylindrical bobbin, with a high-permeability ferromagnetic nickel-iron core moving axially inside the bore. When energized with a carrier AC excitation voltage (typically 2.5 kHz to 5 kHz), the primary magnetic flux couples mutual inductances M1 and M2 into the two secondary windings, which are connected in series-opposition (differential pairing). At the magnetic null center position, the induced secondary voltages are equal and out of phase, yielding zero net differential output. As the core displaces by distance x, the magnetic coupling increases in one secondary and decreases in the other. Following phase-sensitive demodulation and low-pass filtering, the resulting DC output voltage V_dc is directly proportional to axial core displacement: V_dc = S_x · x. In this experiment, a medical-grade LVDT mounted on a micrometer calibration stage is tested to evaluate linearity, zero null symmetry, and displacement sensitivity S_x.

V_dc = S_x · x = 0.8500 · x

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

How does an LVDT eliminate physical friction and mechanical wear?

There is no physical contact between the movable high-permeability magnetic core and the coil bore walls. Because the core floats freely in air or fluid inside the coil tube, there is virtually zero mechanical friction, no hysteresis, and an essentially infinite operational lifespan.

Why are the two secondary coils connected in series-opposition?

Series-opposition connection means their induced AC voltages subtract: V_sec = V_s1 - V_s2. At the center null position, V_s1 = V_s2, producing zero net voltage and allowing the polarity (phase) of the output to indicate the direction of core movement.

What is the function of phase-sensitive demodulation in an LVDT signal conditioner?

The raw differential secondary AC voltage only provides magnitude. A phase-sensitive demodulator compares the phase of the secondary signal with the primary carrier reference to assign a positive sign for displacement in one direction and a negative sign for the opposite direction.

What causes residual 'null voltage' at the magnetic zero position?

Residual null voltage (typically a few millivolts) is caused by small harmonic distortions in the primary excitation, slight capacitive coupling between primary and secondary windings, and minute metallurgical impurities in the magnetic core.

How is an LVDT utilized in respiratory chest wall displacement monitoring?

The LVDT core is connected to an elastic thoracic circumference harness; cyclic expansion and contraction of the ribcage during inhalation and exhalation moves the core, translating chest excursion directly into proportional respiratory volume waveforms.

What are the advantages of LVDTs over resistive potentiometers in medical equipment?

Potentiometers suffer from mechanical wiper contact wear, contact electrical noise, and poor resolution. LVDTs offer frictionless operation, infinite theoretical resolution, superior isolation, and immune hermetic sealing capability.

Experimental precautions and patient safety

  • Use non-magnetic fixtures (brass, aluminum, plastic) to mount the LVDT; ferrous metals distort flux pathways.
  • Ensure the micrometer spindle advances axially without tilting or applying transverse radial loads to the core.
  • Verify carrier excitation voltage and frequency remain strictly constant throughout calibration.
  • Keep the sensor cable well-shielded to prevent capacitive pickup from external power wiring.

Theoretical assumptions & biological limits

  • The linear sensitivity model applies within the linear stroke envelope (x ≤ 6.0 mm for the tested unit).
  • Dynamic frequency response is limited by the carrier excitation frequency (typically bandwidth < 10% of carrier).
  • Temperature changes alter coil copper resistance; high-end signal conditioners use primary current regulation.
  • External strong stray magnetic fields can induce offset errors if ferromagnetic shielding is breached.
