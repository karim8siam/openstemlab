2. Theoretical Principles

Cognitive psychophysics and experimental psychology investigate mental processes through objective quantitative measurement of behavior under tightly constrained laboratory conditions. In Learning, Conditioning & Behavioral Decision Making, human information processing is modeled as a sequence of discrete neurocomputational stages: sensory detection, feature binding, central executive decision-making, and motor execution. By systematically perturbing the independent parameter—Stake across a calibrated span from 10.0 to 60.0 $—this investigation measures corresponding variations in Compensating Certainty Disutility (utils).

The empirical relationship is characterized by the governing regression equation: ΔU = 2.1500 · x. The slope parameter directly yields Loss Aversion Framing Coefficient (λ), with a theoretical reference value of 2.15 utils/$. By tracking response latencies, error frequencies, and signal detection metrics, this study evaluates the validity of formal information processing models against empirical human performance data.

3. Apparatus and Experimental Configuration

The experimental apparatus consists of a certified behavioral testing suite housed in a sound-attenuated, electromagnetically shielded testing chamber (ambient noise < 30 dBA, photopic luminance 50 cd/m²). Visual and auditory stimuli were rendered via a calibrated 240 Hz OLED stimulus monitor driven by dedicated real-time psychophysics runtime software with microsecond raster synchronization. Participant responses were acquired via an optical micro-switch response console featuring hardware-level debouncing and USB polling jitter under 0.1 ms. Eye position and pupil diameter were monitored using a 1000 Hz video-oculography infrared eye-tracking bar positioned beneath the display screen, with head placement stabilized via an ergonomic chin-and-forehead rest located at a calibrated viewing distance of 57.3 cm (ensuring 1 cm on screen subtends exactly 1.0 degree of visual angle).

4. Standard Operating Procedure

Prior to formal data collection, participants completed a demographic screening, visual acuity check (Snellen 20/20 or corrected), and informed consent protocol. Participants underwent a 10-minute dark or light adaptation period inside the testing booth followed by a block of 25 practice trials with immediate latency and accuracy feedback to stabilize motor response baselines. During formal acquisition, the independent variable was tested across 6 calibrated setpoints: 10.0, 20.0, 30.0, 40.0, 50.0, 60.0 $. Each setpoint was sampled across 30 randomized trials interspersed with central fixation intervals (500–800 ms jittered). Trials contaminated by eye blinks during critical stimulus epochs or anticipatory responses (<150 ms) were automatically flagged and repeated at the end of the testing block.

6. Quantitative Calculations and Uncertainty Analysis

Ordinary least-squares (OLS) linear regression was performed on the six paired observation coordinates. Sum of squares decomposition yielded: SS_tot = Σ(y_i - ȳ)², SS_res = Σ(y_i - ŷ_i)², and R² = 1 - (SS_res / SS_tot). The empirical slope was computed via: m = [N Σ(x_i y_i) - (Σx_i)(Σy_i)] / [N Σ(x_i²) - (Σx_i)²]. Standard errors for slope and intercept were determined using the residual variance: s² = SS_res / (N - 2), yielding SE_m = sqrt(s² / Σ(x_i - x̄)²). The resulting empirical parameter was compared against standard reference values via relative percentage deviation: % Dev = |m_obs - m_ref| / m_ref × 100%.

9. Sources of Experimental Error and Mitigation

Potential sources of variance in behavioral psychometrics include: (a) biological vigilance fluctuation and mental fatigue across extended trial blocks; (b) subtle variations in motor response force or microswitch contact bounce; (c) micrometer-level shifts in participant head posture relative to the chin rest altering effective visual angle; and (d) autonomic arousal shifts. These factors were systematically mitigated by: employing acoustic shielding, automated jittered trial scheduling, 60-second compulsory resting intervals, hardware-level sub-millisecond polling clocks, and continuous gaze tracking with fixation verification.
