4. Theory and graph choice

Longitudinal static stability is the fundamental property ensuring an aircraft naturally tends to return to its original trimmed angle of attack following an uncommanded pitch disturbance (such as an atmospheric gust). For static pitch stability, two necessary criteria must be satisfied: (1) positive trim capability (C_m0 > 0), and (2) a negative pitching moment curve slope with respect to angle of attack and lift coefficient: dC_m / dC_L < 0. If a gust increases the angle of attack, a stable aircraft must generate an automatic nose-down restoring moment (Delta C_m < 0) to pitch back down toward equilibrium. According to classical flight mechanics, the pitching moment slope depends directly on the distance between the aircraft center of gravity x_cg and the stick-fixed aerodynamic neutral point x_np: dC_m / dC_L = (x_cg - x_np) / c = x_cg / c - x_np / c. The distance between the neutral point and center of gravity, non-dimensionalized by the mean aerodynamic chord c, is defined as the Static Margin: SM = (x_np - x_cg) / c. When the center of gravity is positioned ahead of the neutral point (x_cg < x_np), the static margin is positive and the aircraft is statically stable. When x_cg moves aft to x_np, the static margin becomes zero (neutral stability). In this experiment, a complete wind tunnel model is tested with center-of-gravity positions moved systematically aft from x_cg / c = 0.20 to 0.35, plotting dC_m / dC_L versus x_cg / c to locate the neutral point x_np / c.

\frac{dC_m}{dC_L} = \frac{x_{cg}}{c} - \frac{x_{np}}{c} \implies \frac{dC_m}{dC_L} = m \cdot \left(\frac{x_{cg}}{c}\right) + b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Center of Gravity Location x_cg / c, and the dependent coordinate y represents Pitching Moment Derivative dC_m / dC_L. The slope m directly extracts Neutral Point Location x_np/c, while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Install the complete aircraft model onto the wind tunnel sting balance, zeroing pitching moments at zero aerodynamic load.
  2. Position the internal ballast slider to place center of gravity at the forward limit x_cg / c = 0.20.
  3. Run the wind tunnel to V_inf = 35 m/s, stabilizing dynamic pressure at q_inf = 750 Pa.
  4. Sweep model incidence through alpha = -2, 0, 2, 4, and 6 degrees, measuring C_m and C_L to extract slope dC_m / dC_L.
  5. Step the internal ballast aft through x_cg / c = 0.23, 0.26, 0.29, 0.32, and 0.35, repeating the pitch sweep at each station.
  6. Plot dC_m / dC_L versus x_cg / c, perform linear regression to verify unity slope m = 1.000, and find horizontal intercept where dC_m/dC_L = 0 to identify x_np / c.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Accurately record model wind-off weight tares at each CG location to eliminate gravitational pitch moment bias.
  • Verify that the horizontal stabilizer is rigidly mounted to prevent aeroelastic tail incidence twist under aerodynamic load.
  • Ensure the test section downwash field from the wing correctly washes over the tail without ceiling boundary layer interference.
  • Avoid operating in the non-linear high-lift regime (alpha > 8 deg) during linear stability derivative evaluation.

Linear stability derivative dC_m/dC_L assumes attached flow and constant wing downwash gradient depsilon/dalpha. Linear regression of dC_m/dC_L against x_cg/c yields a slope of 1.0000 and intercept b = -0.3600, locating the neutral point at x_np/c = 0.3600 with R^2 > 0.999999.
