Civil engineering mechanics & structural foundations of Concrete Modulus of Rupture.

Because concrete is composed of aggregate particles bound by a cementitious paste characterized by microscopic flaws, pores, and interfacial transition zones, its tensile strength is substantially lower than its compressive strength (typically 8% to 12% of f'c). In highway pavements, airport runways, and industrial ground slabs, flexural cracking under repetitive wheel loads represents the primary design governing criterion. The standard flexure test (ASTM C78) utilizes unreinforced prism specimens (150 x 150 x 500 mm) supported across a span L = 450 mm and subjected to symmetrical two-point loading at the third points (spaced L/3 = 150 mm apart). Third-point loading is superior to center-point loading because it establishes a pure bending zone over the central middle third where shear force is exactly zero and bending moment is constant and maximized (M = P L / 6). Consequently, fracture initiates at the weakest critical flaw within the middle third. In this laboratory experiment, concrete prisms are tested to ultimate flexural rupture under controlled loading rates to calculate the Modulus of Rupture f_r and evaluate empirical correlations with compressive strength.

f_r = 0.1333 · P

1. Flexural Mechanics & Elastic Beam Theory

Consider an unreinforced homogeneous concrete prism beam of rectangular cross-section (width $b$, depth $d$) supported on a frictionless simply supported span $L$. When subjected to two equal vertical line loads $P/2$ applied symmetrically at the third points (distance $L/3$ from each support), the beam is partitioned into three distinct structural zones.

In the outer thirds ($0 \le x < L/3$ and $2L/3 < x \le L$), vertical shear force is constant: $V(x) = P/2$, and bending moment increases linearly: $M(x) = (P/2) x$. In the central middle third ($L/3 \le x \le 2L/3$), vertical shear force vanishes identically:

\[ V(x) = \frac{P}{2} - \frac{P}{2} = 0 \]

and the internal bending moment is uniform, pure, and maximized across the entire middle span:

\[ M_{max} = \frac{P}{2} \left( \frac{L}{3} \right) = \frac{P L}{6} \]

2. Modulus of Rupture Derivation

According to the Navier–Euler–Bernoulli theory of pure flexure, longitudinal normal stress $\sigma(y)$ at distance $y$ from the neutral axis is evaluated from elastic section modulus $Z$:

\[ \sigma(y) = \frac{M \cdot y}{I} \]

For a rectangular section, moment of inertia about the centroidal neutral axis ($y_{max} = d/2$) is $I = b d^3 / 12$. The elastic section modulus $Z$ is:

\[ Z = \frac{I}{y_{max}} = \frac{b d^3 / 12}{d / 2} = \frac{b d^2}{6} \]

When the applied load reaches peak fracture load $P$, extreme outer fiber tensile stress reaches the Modulus of Rupture $f_r$:

\[ f_r = \frac{M_{max}}{Z} = \frac{P L / 6}{b d^2 / 6} = \frac{P L}{b d^2} \]

For standard test dimensions ($b = 150\text{ mm}$, $d = 150\text{ mm}$, $L = 450\text{ mm}$):

\[ \frac{f_r}{P} = \frac{450}{150 \times 150^2} = \frac{450}{3,375,000} = 0.00013333\text{ mm}^{-2} = 0.13333\text{ MPa/kN} \]

3. Fracture Mechanics & Size Effect in Concrete Tension

Unlike ductile metals that yield plastically, concrete behaves as a quasi-brittle material exhibiting strain-softening. Microcracking initiates in the interfacial transition zone (ITZ) between cement paste and aggregate particles. As load peaks, microcracks coalesce into a localized macrocrack within the middle third. Because the stress gradient in flexure restricts peak tensile strain to a thin outer boundary layer, apparent flexural strength $f_r$ exceeds true uniaxial tensile strength $f_t$ by a factor of $1.5$ to $1.8$, explained by Weibull statistical weakest-link theory and fracture process zone mechanics.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

Why is third-point loading preferred over center-point loading for evaluating modulus of rupture?

Third-point loading creates a zone of constant maximum bending moment and zero shear force across the entire middle third, allowing the specimen to seek out its weakest flaw, whereas center-point loading forces fracture to occur precisely under the single central roller.

Why is the specimen turned on its side relative to its as-cast orientation before testing?

The top as-cast surface is hand-troweled and may exhibit minor irregularities or bleeding channels; testing on the mold-formed sides ensures smooth, parallel bearing faces in contact with loading rollers.

What is the consequence if the fracture occurs outside the middle third of the span?

If fracture occurs outside the middle third by not more than 5% of span, a modified formula f_r = 3 P a / (b d^2) must be applied where a is distance from support; if > 5%, ASTM C78 mandates discarding the result.

How does moisture content on the tension face influence the measured flexural strength?

Surface drying produces differential drying shrinkage, generating residual tensile stresses on the outer fibers that prematurely trigger cracking, artificially reducing apparent modulus of rupture by up to 20%.

What empirical relationship links modulus of rupture f_r to compressive strength f'c in ACI 318?

ACI 318 specifies f_r = 0.62 sqrt(f'c) in MPa for normal-weight concrete, reflecting the square-root dependence of tensile capacity on matrix compressive strength.

Why is modulus of rupture f_r higher than splitting tensile strength f_ct for identical concrete?

In flexure, maximum tensile stress occurs only at extreme outer fibers with a steep linear strain gradient, allowing adjacent inner fibers to sustain load, whereas splitting tension subjects a larger volume to nearly uniform tension.

Experimental precautions and laboratory safety

  • Keep specimens saturated surface-dry; never allow prism surfaces to dry in ambient air prior to testing.
  • Ensure all four contact rollers make full, uniform contact across the entire 150 mm width to prevent torsional twisting.
  • Verify that bearing rollers are free to articulate and rotate slightly to accommodate elastic beam curvature without axial friction.
  • Discard any test where the fracture surface displays large void pockets or aggregate segregation exceeding 10% cross-section.

Theoretical assumptions & boundary condition limits

  • The civil engineering physical models apply within the specified calibrated operational window (15.0 to 40.0 kN).
  • Assumes homogeneous, isotropic continuum material behavior without unmodeled micro-fracture anomalies.
  • Continuous sensor readings reflect ordinary least-squares regression across successive observations.
  • Extreme inelastic yielding or cyclic degradation requires non-linear constitutive continuum damage mechanics.
