Aerodynamic and physical foundations of Normal Shock Wave Relations.

A normal shock wave is an extremely thin discontinuous compression front (thickness on the order of the molecular mean free path, ~10^-7 m) oriented perpendicular to oncoming supersonic flow. As supersonic gas (Mach M_1 > 1) traverses this microscopic zone, intense viscous dissipation and thermal conduction abruptly decelerate the flow to subsonic speed (M_2 < 1), causing violent irreversible thermodynamic changes. By applying the fundamental conservation equations of mass, linear momentum, and energy across the normal shock wave, the classical Rankine-Hugoniot jump relations are derived. For a calorically perfect gas with specific heat ratio gamma = 1.4, the static pressure ratio across the shock scales strictly with the upstream Mach number squared according to P_2 / P_1 = 1 + [2*gamma / (gamma+1)] * (M_1^2 - 1) = (7/6)*M_1^2 - 1/6. Crucially, the entropy generation across the irreversible shock causes a severe loss of total stagnation pressure (P_02 < P_01), while total temperature remains constant (T_02 = T_01). In this laboratory experiment, a supersonic wind tunnel test section equipped with a variable back-pressure throttle positions a stationary normal shock over a static pressure tap array. Upstream Pitot-static measurements determine M_1, while downstream taps record the static pressure jump and stagnation pressure deficit across Mach numbers from M_1 = 1.34 to 2.30, validating the Rankine-Hugoniot gradient.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

Why does static pressure increase while total pressure decreases across a normal shock wave?

Decelerating the supersonic flow from M_1 > 1 to M_2 < 1 converts kinetic energy into static pressure and internal thermal energy. However, the extreme viscous dissipation and thermal conduction inside the shock wave generate entropy (Delta s > 0). By the thermodynamic relation Delta s = -R * ln(P_02 / P_01), this entropy creation forces a loss in total stagnation pressure.

What is the downstream Mach number M_2 behind a normal shock wave?

The downstream Mach number is always subsonic (M_2 < 1). The normal shock relation yields M_2^2 = (1 + (gamma-1)/2 * M_1^2) / (gamma * M_1^2 - (gamma-1)/2). As M_1 approaches infinity, M_2 asymptotes to sqrt((gamma-1)/(2*gamma)) = 0.378 for air.

Why can a shock wave never form in a subsonic flow (M_1 < 1)?

A expansion shock (subsonic to supersonic with a pressure drop) would require an increase in total pressure and a decrease in entropy (Delta s < 0), which violates the Second Law of Thermodynamics. Therefore, stable shock waves can only form when upstream flow is supersonic (M_1 > 1).

How does total temperature T_0 behave across a stationary normal shock wave?

Total temperature T_0 remains perfectly constant across a normal shock wave (T_01 = T_02). Because the shock is stationary and adiabatic with no external heat transfer (q = 0) or shaft work (w = 0), energy conservation dictates that total enthalpy h_0 = c_p * T_0 is strictly conserved.

What is the Rankine-Hugoniot theoretical slope for air (gamma = 1.4)?

The Rankine-Hugoniot relation states (P_2/P_1 - 1) = [2*gamma / (gamma+1)] * (M_1^2 - 1). For air with gamma = 1.4, the theoretical slope is 2 * 1.4 / (1.4 + 1.0) = 2.8 / 2.4 = 7 / 6 = 1.1667.

What is a lambda shock wave and when does it appear in duct flows?

When a normal shock wave impinges on a viscous boundary layer on a solid wall, the severe adverse pressure gradient causes the boundary layer to separate ahead of the shock. The separation bubble generates an oblique shock that merges with the normal shock, forming a bifurcated Greek letter lambda (lambda) shock structure.

Experimental precautions and wind tunnel safety

  • Maintain strict throttle valve stability to prevent high-amplitude normal shock oscillation (shock buzz) inside the test section.
  • Verify that wall boundary layer separation does not induce a complex lambda-shock bifurcation near the static pressure taps.
  • Ensure the supersonic Pitot tube is aligned with the tunnel axis to within +/- 0.5 degrees.
  • Apply the Rayleigh supersonic Pitot formula when evaluating total pressure behind the detached bow shock of the probe.

Theoretical assumptions & flow limits

  • Shock-wave boundary layer interaction (SWBLI) creates a lambda-foot near duct walls that slightly smears the idealized 1D jump.
  • Calorically perfect gas assumption assumes specific heat ratio gamma remains exactly 1.40; high temperatures cause minor vibrational shifts.
  • Normal shock unsteadiness caused by turbulence introduces small RMS pressure fluctuations.
  • Assumes adiabatic duct walls with negligible heat transfer during the rapid blowdown testing window.
