Agricultural assumptions and boundary condition limits

The air-oven method is destructive and requires 19 to 72 hours, making it unsuitable for instant elevator grain receiving. Evaluated using standard 20.0 g samples of clean wheat (constant dry solid mass m_dry = 16.0 g). Dry basis moisture M_db scales linearly with evaporated moisture mass loss Δm.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the dry-basis and wet-basis moisture content of agricultural grains using the standard air-oven method and calibrate an electronic capacitance grain moisture meter. Standardized parameter settings: 1.0, 1.7, 2.4, 3.1, 3.8, 4.5 g.

01

Oven Pre-Heating & Dish Taring

Pre-heat the forced-convection oven to exactly 130°C. Clean, dry, and tare aluminum moisture dishes with lids, recording tare mass m_dish on the analytical balance.

02

Sample Preparation & Weighing

Weigh exactly 20.000 g of unground grain sample into each dish, spreading kernels in a uniform thin layer, and record initial wet sample mass m_initial.

03

Oven Thermal Desiccation

Place uncovered dishes into the 130°C oven (resting lids beside dishes), closing the door and initiating timer for exactly 19.0 hours in accordance with ASABE S352.

04

Desiccator Cooling

At 19 hours, quickly cover dishes with lids, transfer immediately into the silica gel desiccator, and allow dishes to cool to room temperature (30 to 45 minutes).

05

Dry Mass Weighing & Loss Calculation

Weigh cooled covered dishes to determine final bone-dry mass m_final, calculating moisture loss Δm = m_initial - m_final and dry-basis moisture M_db = (Δm / m_dry) × 100%.

06

Capacitance Meter Cross-Calibration

Repeat testing across six grain moisture levels, plot oven M_db versus Δm to confirm gradient k_m = 100 / m_dry, and plot electronic meter readings against true oven moisture.
