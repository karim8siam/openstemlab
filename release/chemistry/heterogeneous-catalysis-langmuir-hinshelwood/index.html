Physical chemistry assumptions and metrological limits

The classical Langmuir-Hinshelwood model makes idealized physical assumptions: (1) Energetic site uniformity: Real heterogeneous catalysts possess stepped facets, edges, corners, and defect sites with differing adsorption energies, whereas the Langmuir isotherm assumes an energetically homogeneous surface. (2) Absence of lateral adsorbate-adsorbate interactions: At high surface coverages, dipole-dipole repulsion or steric crowding between neighboring adsorbed molecules causes adsorption enthalpy to decrease with coverage (better described by Temkin or Freundlich isotherms). (3) Internal mass transfer: For large catalyst porous pellets, intraparticle Knudsen diffusion limitations (quantified by the Thiele modulus) can depress observed rates; fine powder slurries (< 50 μm) are used here to ensure effectiveness factors η ≈ 1.0. (4) Product inhibition: If decomposition products adsorb competitively on surface sites, an additional term (1 + K_P [P]) must be incorporated into the rate denominator. The Langmuir-Hinshelwood (L-H) mechanism describes heterogeneous catalytic surface reactions where reactant molecules first undergo rapid, reversible chemisorption onto active catalytic surface sites (S) to form an adsorbed monolayer intermediate (AS), followed by a rate-determining unimolecular or bimolecular transformation of the adsorbed species on the solid surface: A(aq) + S ⇄ AS (adsorption equilibrium constant K_A), followed by AS ⟶ Products + S (surface reaction rate constant k_r). Assuming ideal Langmuir chemisorption—where all surface active sites are energetically uniform, each site binds at most one substrate molecule, and lateral adsorbate-adsorbate interactions are negligible—the fractional surface coverage θ_A at steady-state adsorption equilibrium obeys θ_A = K_A [A] / (1 + K_A [A]). The rate of catalytic turnover r is directly proportional to fractional surface coverage: r = k_r · θ_A = (k_r · K_A · [A]) / (1 + K_A · [A]). At low substrate concentrations (K_A [A] ≪ 1), fractional coverage is linear (θ_A ≈ K_A [A]) and the reaction exhibits apparent first-order kinetics with rate r ≈ k_r K_A [A]. At high substrate concentrations (K_A [A] ≫ 1), the active catalyst surface reaches monolayer saturation (θ_A → 1) and the reaction collapses to zero-order kinetics independent of substrate concentration: r → k_r. Linearizing the Langmuir-Hinshelwood hyperbolic rate expression into double-reciprocal coordinates yields: 1/r₀ = [1 / (k_r · K_A)] · (1 / [A]) + 1 / k_r. Plotting 1/r₀ versus 1/[A] produces a straight line where the Y-intercept b = 1/k_r yields the maximum surface reaction rate constant k_r = 1/b, and the slope m = 1/(k_r · K_A) enables evaluation of the adsorption equilibrium constant K_A = b / m.

Analytical chemistry apparatus & tools.

Jacketed Three-Neck Borosilicate Slurry Reactor (100 mL)

Houses the solid catalyst suspension and liquid reaction mixture surrounded by an outer thermal jacket circulating water from a constant-temperature bath maintained at 298.15 ± 0.05 K.

High-Torque Overhead Digital Stirrer with Four-Blade PTFE Turbine

Agitates the solid catalyst slurry at 900 RPM to eliminate stagnant liquid boundary layers around catalyst particles, ensuring reaction rates are purely surface-reaction limited rather than mass-transfer controlled.

Mesoporous Supported Platinum Catalyst (5.0 wt% Pt/Al₂O₃ powder)

Provides highly dispersed active metallic platinum crystallites (mean particle diameter 2.8 nm) supported on high-surface-area gamma-alumina to catalyze substrate decomposition.

Digital Mass Flow / Micro-Manometric Gas Evolution Sensor

Measures the instantaneous rate of gaseous product evolution (CO₂ and H₂ or O₂) with high temporal resolution to calculate initial surface decomposition rates r₀.

Hermetic Septum Port and Microliter Substrate Injection Syringe

Allows instantaneous introduction of concentrated substrate stock solution into the pre-equilibrated catalyst slurry without opening the reactor to the atmosphere.

Analytical Electronic Balance and Ultrasonic Catalyst Dispersion Bath

Weighs the solid catalyst mass to ±0.1 mg and de-agglomerates particle aggregates in distilled water prior to reactor loading.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

To investigate the heterogeneous surface reaction kinetics of catalytic substrate decomposition on supported platinum nanoparticles, verify the Langmuir-Hinshelwood adsorption-reaction model, and determine the maximum surface rate constant k_r and adsorption equilibrium constant K_A at 298.15 K. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 10.0, 15.0, 25.0, 40.0, 60.0, 100.0 mM.

01

Catalyst Pre-treatment, Slurry Preparation, and Ultrasonic De-agglomeration

Accurately weigh exactly 50.0 mg of 5.0 wt% Pt/Al₂O₃ catalyst powder into a clean weighing boat. Transfer quantitatively into the jacketed reactor with 35.0 mL of Milli-Q water. Subject the suspension to 5 minutes of mild ultrasonic agitation to break up agglomerates into a uniform slurry.

02

Thermostatic Bath Equilibrium and Reactor Degassing

Connect the reactor water jacket to the circulating water bath set at 298.15 K (25.00 °C). Seal the reactor necks with ground-glass joints and purge the system with inert nitrogen gas for 10 minutes to remove dissolved oxygen and establish thermal equilibrium.

03

Overhead Agitator Calibration and Stirring Speed Verification

Insert the four-blade PTFE turbine agitator into the slurry, adjust the shaft clearance to 5 mm above the reactor floor, and engage rotation at 900 RPM. Verify that the vortex does not draw air bubbles into the liquid and that the catalyst remains completely suspended.

04

Substrate Injection and Real-Time Surface Reaction Logging

Connect the gas outlet to the calibrated micro-manometric sensor. Rapidly inject the designated volume of concentrated substrate stock solution through the septum port to establish the desired initial concentration (e.g., 10.0 mM to 100.0 mM in 50.0 mL total volume). Record gas evolution rates continuously from t = 0 to 180 s.

05

Initial Rate Extraction and Multi-Concentration Serial Runs

Determine the initial surface rate r₀ (mM/min) from the linear slope of product formation within the first 60 seconds post-injection. Clean and recharge the reactor, then repeat the trial for substrate concentrations of 10.0, 15.0, 25.0, 40.0, 60.0, and 100.0 mM.

06

Langmuir-Hinshelwood Double-Reciprocal Linearization and Parameter Evaluation

Calculate reciprocal concentrations 1/[A] (mM⁻¹) and reciprocal rates 1/r₀ (min·mM⁻¹). Construct the Langmuir-Hinshelwood plot of 1/r₀ versus 1/[A], perform ordinary linear regression, and calculate the maximum surface rate constant k_r = 1/intercept and adsorption equilibrium constant K_A = intercept / slope.
