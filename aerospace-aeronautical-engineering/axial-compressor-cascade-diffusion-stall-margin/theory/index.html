Aerodynamic and physical foundations of Compressor Cascade Aerodynamics.

The axial flow compressor is the most aerodynamically critical and aerodynamically delicate component of modern aircraft gas turbines. Operating against a severe adverse pressure gradient across multiple successive rotor and stator blade rows, air must be compressed from ambient atmospheric pressure to over 40 atmospheres without triggering aerodynamic blade stall or catastrophic compressor surge. To evaluate the aerodynamic loading limit of compressor blading, aerodynamicists utilize linear cascade wind tunnels that model a two-dimensional unwrapped blade row. Seymour Lieblein formulated the fundamental non-dimensional Diffusion Factor D, which quantifies the local boundary layer decelerating pressure gradient on the blade suction surface: D = (1 - V_2 / V_1) + Delta V_theta / (2 * sigma_solidity * V_1), where V_1 and V_2 are inlet and exit velocities, Delta V_theta is the tangential velocity turning, and sigma_solidity = c / s is blade solidity (chord to blade pitch spacing ratio). Lieblein proved that when D exceeds a critical threshold (typically D > 0.55 to 0.60), boundary layer separation occurs on the suction surface, causing wake momentum losses to skyrocket and triggering compressor blade stall. In this laboratory experiment, a linear compressor cascade of NACA 65-series blades is tested across flow turning angles from theta_t = 12 to 35 degrees, plotting the diffusion factor D against flow turning angle theta_t.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

Why is flow through an axial compressor inherently more prone to boundary layer separation than flow through a turbine?

In an axial compressor, flow travels in the direction of increasing static pressure (an adverse pressure gradient, dP/dx > 0) as kinetic energy is converted into pressure. Fluid particles continually decelerate, and near-wall boundary layers easily exhaust their momentum and separate. In contrast, a turbine operates in a favorable pressure gradient (dP/dx < 0), which naturally stabilizes and accelerates the boundary layer.

What is the physical meaning of the Lieblein Diffusion Factor D?

The Lieblein Diffusion Factor D measures the overall decelerating velocity gradient acting on the blade suction surface. It combines the main stream deceleration (1 - V_2/V_1) with the circulation-induced velocity peak on the suction surface (Delta V_theta / (2 * sigma * V_1)). Higher D values correlate directly with thicker trailing-edge boundary layers and imminent separation.

What happens when the Diffusion Factor D exceeds the critical threshold of 0.55 to 0.60?

When D exceeds approximately 0.58, the adverse pressure gradient on the suction surface becomes too severe for the turbulent boundary layer to remain attached. Widespread flow separation occurs, cascade total pressure loss coefficient omega skyrockets, flow turning collapses, and the compressor stage enters aerodynamic stall.

What is the difference between compressor rotating stall and catastrophic compressor surge?

Rotating stall is a localized aerodynamic phenomenon where one or more stall cells rotate around the compressor annulus at 30-70% of rotor speed, causing severe blade vibration and temperature rise. Compressor surge is a violent, global, system-wide breakdown of flow where the entire compressor chokes and backflows, causing massive explosive pressure reversals and flame expulsion out the inlet.

How do variable stator vanes (VSVs) and bleed valves prevent compressor stall at low engine RPM?

At off-design low RPM, early compressor stages encounter excessive incidence angles (stalling), while rear stages choke. Variable stator vanes automatically pivot closed at low RPM to reduce flow incidence angles and keep diffusion factors below D_crit. Intermediate bleed valves vent excess mass flow to prevent choking in the rear stages.

What is blade solidity sigma and how does it influence blade aerodynamic loading?

Solidity sigma = c / s is the ratio of blade chord c to circumferential pitch spacing s. High solidity (tightly spaced blades) shares the aerodynamic turning among more blades, reducing the individual circulation and diffusion factor D on each blade, but increases wetted surface skin friction drag.

Experimental precautions and wind tunnel safety

  • Verify cascade pitchwise periodicity: static pressure profiles across adjacent blade passages must match within +/- 1.0%.
  • Operate sidewall boundary layer suction slots to prevent corner stall separation from bridging the cascade passage.
  • Do not exceed the critical diffusion limit (D > 0.60) for extended durations to prevent severe blade unsteadiness and acoustic resonance.
  • Ensure the downstream 3-hole probe is properly nulled in yaw before recording exit velocity vectors.

Theoretical assumptions & flow limits

  • Linear 2D cascade neglects three-dimensional radial centrifugal forces, radial flow migration, and tip clearance leakage flows.
  • Assumes incompressible flow (inlet Mach M_1 approx 0.15); transonic compressor cascades develop shock-induced boundary layer separation.
  • Rotor-stator wake blade passing unsteadiness is not simulated in a stationary linear cascade.
  • Blade surface tap orifices introduce micro-roughness that can trip laminar boundary layers prematurely.
