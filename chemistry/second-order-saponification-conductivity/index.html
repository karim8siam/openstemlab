Physical chemistry assumptions and metrological limits

Conductometric reaction tracking assumes that atmospheric carbon dioxide is strictly excluded. In open air, ambient CO₂ dissolves and reacts with hydroxide to form carbonate ions (CO₂ + 2 OH⁻ → CO₃²⁻ + H₂O), which lowers [OH⁻] and alters the effective solution conductance. The reaction vessel must be fitted with a tight Teflon lid or purged with inert nitrogen. Furthermore, because electrolytic conductivity has a high temperature coefficient (~2.0% per °C), thermal fluctuations exceeding ±0.05 °C can distort derived rate constants by over 1.5%. The simulation engine models the second-order saponification kinetics of ethyl acetate at 25.00 ± 0.02 °C. Baseline setup: 50.0 mL of 0.0400 M NaOH is combined with 50.0 mL of 0.0400 M ethyl acetate, establishing equimolar initial concentrations c₀ = 0.0200 M (20.0 mM) in a 100.0 mL jacketed cell. The true second-order rate constant is k = 6.600 L·mol⁻¹·min⁻¹ (0.1100 L·mol⁻¹·s⁻¹), yielding an initial half-life t₁/₂ = 1 / (k·c₀) = 1 / (6.60 × 0.0200) = 7.576 minutes. The specific electrolytic conductivity is governed by Kohlrausch's law: κ_t = 10⁻³·[c_t·λ(OH⁻) + (c₀ − c_t)·λ(CH₃COO⁻) + c₀·λ(Na⁺)], with limiting conductivities λ(OH⁻) = 198.6, λ(Na⁺) = 50.11, and λ(CH₃COO⁻) = 40.9 S·cm²·mol⁻¹. Baseline conductivity at t = 0 is κ₀ = 4.974 mS/cm; infinity conductivity at complete reaction (t = ∞) is κ_∞ = 1.820 mS/cm (Δκ_max = 3.154 mS/cm). At reaction times of 3.0, 6.0, 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, and 30.0 minutes, the simulated specific conductivities evaluate to 4.079, 3.580, 3.179, 2.878, 2.686, and 2.456 mS/cm, yielding reactant concentrations c_t of 14.33, 11.16, 8.62, 6.71, 5.49, and 4.03 mM. The derived reciprocal concentrations 1/c_t evaluate to 69.81, 89.60, 116.04, 149.05, 182.10, and 247.96 L/mol. Ordinary least-squares regression of 1/c_t against t yields an ideal slope m = k = 6.5995 L·mol⁻¹·min⁻¹ and intercept b = 50.03 L/mol with R² = 0.999999, isolating k within 0.008% of the accepted literature value.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

To determine the second-order rate constant (k) and initial reaction half-life (t₁/₂) for the alkaline saponification of ethyl acetate at 25.00 ± 0.02 °C under equimolar initial conditions ([ester]₀ = [OH⁻]₀ = 0.0200 M) using continuous audio-frequency AC electrolytic conductometry, and verify Kohlrausch limiting ionic mobilities and second-order integrated rate kinetics. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 3.0, 6.0, 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 30.0 min.

01

Reagent Standardization and CO₂ Scavenging

Standardize stock solutions: 0.0400 M sodium hydroxide (standardized against potassium hydrogen phthalate) and 0.0400 M ethyl acetate in carbon-dioxide-free deionized water.

02

Conductivity Cell Calibration and Cell Constant Verification

Calibrate the dip-type conductivity cell against standard 0.0100 M potassium chloride (KCl) solution to verify cell constant θ = 1.000 cm⁻¹ at 25.00 °C.

03

Thermal Conditioning of Reactant Stocks

Thermostat 50.00 mL of 0.0400 M NaOH and 50.00 mL of 0.0400 M ethyl acetate in separate stoppered flasks inside the 25.00 ± 0.02 °C water bath for 20 minutes.

04

Reaction Initiation and Cell Immersion (t = 0)

Rapidly mix the two pre-equilibrated solutions in the jacketed reaction vessel, simultaneously starting the digital stopwatch (t = 0), and insert the conductivity cell.

05

Periodic Conductometric Telemetry Acquisition

Maintain gentle magnetic stirring (250 rpm) and record the specific electrolytic conductivity κ_t at scheduled elapsed intervals (3.0, 6.0, 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, and 30.0 minutes).

06

Infinity Reading Evaluation (κ_∞)

To evaluate κ_∞, heat a portion of the reaction mixture in a sealed tube at 60 °C for 20 minutes to achieve complete saponification, re-equilibrate to 25.00 °C, and measure κ_∞ (1.820 mS/cm).
