Agricultural assumptions and boundary condition limits

Laboratory small-cylinder bulk densities are slightly lower than deep silo densities where thousands of tons of overburden compact the grain. Evaluated using clean seed wheat at 12.5% moisture (true kernel density ρ_t = 1.340 g/mL, porosity ε = 41.4%) across standardized graduated cylinders.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the true density, bulk density, porosity, thousand-grain mass, and sphericity of cereal grains and verify the linear mass-volume relation in bulk storage. Standardized parameter settings: 100.0, 180.0, 260.0, 340.0, 420.0, 500.0 mL.

01

Funnel Hopper Standardization

Position the standard filling funnel 5.0 cm above the graduated cylinder, ensuring the discharge shutter is centered and level.

02

Initial 100 mL Bulk Filling

Pour wheat into the funnel, open the shutter to allow free-fall filling into the 100 mL cylinder until overflowing, and strike off excess grain level with the rim using a wooden straightedge.

03

Bulk Mass Determination

Weigh the filled cylinder, subtract tare mass, and record bulk grain mass m_b (g) for V_b = 100.0 mL.

04

Volume Incrementation

Repeat standardized filling across cylinders of 180, 260, 340, 420, and 500 mL, recording mass m_b for each volume.

05

True Density & Porosity Determination

Place 50 g of wheat into the helium pycnometer to measure true volume and calculate true density ρ_t, then compute porosity ε = (1 - ρ_b / ρ_t) × 100%.

06

Linear Bulk Density Regression

Plot bulk mass m_b versus volume V_b, perform linear regression through the origin, and determine the bulk density slope ρ_b.
