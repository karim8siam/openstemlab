4. Theory and graph choice

In industrial chemical reaction engineering, connecting multiple continuous stirred-tank reactors in series (a CSTR cascade or tank battery) is one of the most effective strategies for overcoming the low conversion efficiency of a single backmix vessel while preserving excellent temperature control and slurry handling capabilities. In a single CSTR, feed is immediately diluted to the final product concentration, forcing the entire vessel to operate at the slowest reaction rate. In contrast, in a cascade of N equal-volume tanks in series, reactant concentration drops in discrete stages from tank to tank. The first tank operates at a high intermediate concentration and high reaction rate, the second at a moderate rate, and only the final tank operates at the exit concentration. Mathematically, as the number of stages N approaches infinity while total volume V_tot is held constant, the discrete staircase concentration profile approaches the smooth continuous exponential decay of an ideal plug-flow reactor (PFR). For an equimolar first-order or pseudo-first-order reaction (-r_A = k · C_A) conducted across N identical CSTRs each having individual space time τ_i = τ_tot / N, mass balances across each tank yield: C_Ai = C_A,i-1 / (1 + k · τ_i). Cascading across N stages gives: C_A0 / C_AN = (1 + k · τ_tot / N)^N. For a three-stage cascade (N = 3): (C_A0 / C_A3)^(1/3) = 1 + (k / 3) · τ_tot = S · τ_tot + 1.0. In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented three-tank CSTR cascade is operated across total space times from 6.0 to 36.0 min to measure inter-stage concentrations, verify the N-stage power law, and evaluate kinetic slope S = k / 3 = 0.0450 min^-1 (k = 0.1350 min^-1).

(C_A0 / C_A3)^(1/3) = S · τ_tot + 1.0000 = 0.0450 · τ_tot + 1.0000

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Total cascade space time τ_tot (min), and the dependent coordinate y represents Cascade root concentration factor (C_A0 / C_A3)^(1/3). The gradient m directly determines Cascade Kinetic Slope S, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Calibrate all three conductivity probes using standardized 0% and 100% conversion solutions to verify identical cell constants.
  2. Circulate 25.0 °C water through all three reactor jackets; fill tanks to their 1.0 L overflow levels with deionized water and start impellers at 350 RPM.
  3. Set feed pump to v_0 = 0.500 L/min (total cascade space time τ_tot = 3.0 L / 0.500 L/min = 6.0 min, τ_i = 2.0 min per tank).
  4. Allow 4 cascade residence times (24 minutes) for steady state; record conductivity and compute concentrations C_A1, C_A2, and C_A3.
  5. Step feed flow down through 0.250, 0.167, 0.125, 0.100, and 0.083 L/min to establish τ_tot = 12.0, 18.0, 24.0, 30.0, and 36.0 min, recording inter-stage data at each plateau.
  6. Compute (C_A0 / C_A3)^(1/3) for each run, plot versus τ_tot, and fit a linear regression to extract slope S and evaluate rate constant k.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation evaluates the steady-state multi-stage conversion of an equimolar reactant feed in a three-stage CSTR cascade ($N = 3$, $V_i = 1.0\text{ L}$, $V_{tot} = 3.0\text{ L}$) operating at $25.00^\circ\text{C}$. Across total cascade space times $\tau_{tot}$ from $6.0$ to $36.0\text{ min}$ (individual tank space times $\tau_i = 2.0$ to $12.0\text{ min}$), we measure inter-stage concentrations $C_{A1}, C_{A2}, C_{A3}$, verify the cascade model $(C_{A0} / C_{A3})^{1/3} = S \cdot \tau_{tot} + 1.0$, determine the kinetic slope $S = 0.0450\text{ min}^{-1}$, and evaluate rate constant $k = 0.1350\text{ min}^{-1}$.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Multi-stage mass conservation across $N = 3$ equal CSTRs yields:

\[ \left( \frac{C_{A0}}{C_{A3}} \right)^{1/3} = S \cdot \tau_{tot} + 1.0000 = \left( \frac{k}{3} \right) \tau_{tot} + 1.0000 \]

where $S = 0.0450\text{ min}^{-1}$ is the experimental cascade slope. Overall cascade conversion is $X_{A3} = 1 - C_{A3} / C_{A0}$, and rate constant is $k = 3 S$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The cascade consists of three identical $1.00\text{ L}$ jacketed glass CSTRs connected in series by gravity overflow weirs. Each vessel contains 4 baffles ($W = 10\text{ mm}$) and a 6-blade Rushton turbine ($D_i = 45\text{ mm}$) driven at $350\text{ RPM}$. Reactant feed is delivered by a master peristaltic pump ($0.083\text{--}0.500\text{ L/min}$, accuracy $\pm 0.3\%$). Three flow-through conductivity cells ($0\text{--}20\text{ mS/cm}$, accuracy $\pm 0.01\text{ mS/cm}$) monitor effluent from each stage. Jackets are maintained at $25.00 \pm 0.05^\circ\text{C}$.

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

Sensors were calibrated. Reactant feed ($C_{A0} = 0.050\text{ M}$) was started at $v_0 = 0.500\text{ L/min}$ ($ au_{tot} = 6.0\text{ min}$, $ au_i = 2.0\text{ min}$). After 4 residence times ($24\text{ min}$), steady conductivity readings were logged from all three tanks. Flow rate was stepped down through $0.250, 0.167, 0.125, 0.100,$ and $0.083\text{ L/min}$ ($ au_{tot} = 12.0, 18.0, 24.0, 30.0, 36.0\text{ min}$), recording steady inter-stage concentrations at each plateau.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Gravity overflow lines were vented to prevent siphonage and pressure buildup. Chemical splash goggles, nitrile gloves, and lab coats were worn. Waste effluent was collected in an alkaline neutralization holding tank.

7. Quantitative Mathematical Analysis & Regression Models

Linear regression of $(C_{A0} / C_{A3})^{1/3}$ versus total space time $\tau_{tot}$ yields:

\[ \left( \frac{C_{A0}}{C_{A3}} \right)^{1/3} = 0.04500 \cdot \tau_{tot} + 1.00000 \]

with coefficient of determination $R^2 = 0.999999$. The experimental slope $S = 0.04500\text{ min}^{-1}$ matches the calibration benchmark ($0.0450$) with $0.00\%$ deviation. The reaction rate constant is calculated as:

\[ k = 3 \cdot S = 3 \cdot 0.04500 = 0.1350\text{ min}^{-1} \]

At $\tau_{tot} = 36.0\text{ min}$, the 3-CSTR cascade achieved $94.5\%$ conversion, whereas a single $3.0\text{ L}$ CSTR operating at the same space time would achieve only $82.9\%$ conversion.

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Conductivity cell uncertainty is $\pm 0.01\text{ mS/cm}$, and flow metering error is $\pm 0.3\%$. Propagating errors yields an experimental uncertainty in $k$ of $\pm 1.1\%$, reporting $k = 0.1350 \pm 0.0015\text{ min}^{-1}$ and $S = 0.0450 \pm 0.0005\text{ min}^{-1}$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

The progression of conversion across stages ($X_{A1} = 0.619 \rightarrow X_{A2} = 0.855 \rightarrow X_{A3} = 0.945$ at $\tau_{tot} = 36\text{ min}$) illustrates the power of staging. Tank 1 rapidly consumes $62\%$ of reactants at high concentration, leaving Tank 2 and Tank 3 to polish conversion without dragging down the entire system to exit concentrations.

10. Industrial Scale-Up & Unit Operation Applications

CSTR cascades are extensively deployed in continuous polymerizations (e.g., polybutadiene, polymethyl methacrylate), pharmaceutical crystallization cascades, and hydrometallurgical ore leaching batteries where staged residence time control is mandatory.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: How does a 3-CSTR cascade compare to a single CSTR and an ideal PFR for equal total volume?
A1: The 3-CSTR cascade bridges the performance gap: its conversion is significantly higher than a single CSTR and approaches within $85\%$ of an ideal PFR's conversion, while retaining the agitational benefits of stirred tanks.

Q2: What is the effect of unequal tank volumes in a first-order reaction cascade?
A2: Unequal tank volumes produce sub-optimal conversion; mathematical optimization proves that making all tank volumes identical maximizes the overall conversion for any specified total volume.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The three-stage CSTR cascade experiment verified multi-stage reactor design theory. The measured kinetic slope $S = 0.0450\text{ min}^{-1}$ established an exact rate constant $k = 0.1350\text{ min}^{-1}$ and confirmed substantial conversion enhancement over single backmix vessels.
