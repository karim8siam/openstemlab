Physical chemistry assumptions and metrological limits

['The simple Nernst and Gibbs-Helmholtz equations assume unit activity coefficients; in 1.0 M solutions, mean ionic activity coefficients (γ_± ≈ 0.04 to 0.15) cause minor deviations from infinite-dilution formal potentials.', 'Residual liquid junction potentials across the agar-KCl bridge, while minimized (typically < 0.5 mV), introduce a minor systematic offset that does not cancel perfectly if half-cell ionic strengths differ.', 'Slow inter-diffusion of copper ions through the salt bridge over extended experimental durations (> 48 hours) can lead to trace cementation on the zinc anode.'] For the Daniell cell reaction Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) ⇌ Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s) (n = 2), the standard potential at 298.15 K (25.0 °C) is E° = 1.1000 V (1100.0 mV). Because the tighter electrostrictive hydration shell of Zn²⁺ compared to Cu²⁺ produces a net increase in microscopic solvent ordering, the reaction entropy is negative (ΔS° = -21.47 J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹), yielding a characteristic negative temperature coefficient dE/dT = -0.1113 mV·K⁻¹. Ordinary least-squares regression of equilibrium EMF against absolute temperature T extracts dE/dT with exceptional precision (R² = 0.99999), yielding standard Gibbs free energy ΔG°(298.15 K) = -212.27 kJ·mol⁻¹ and reaction enthalpy ΔH° = -218.67 kJ·mol⁻¹, confirming the fundamental validity of the Gibbs-Helmholtz relation.

Analytical chemistry apparatus & tools.

High-Purity Zinc Electrode Half-Cell

High-purity metallic zinc cylinder immersed in standardized 1.000 M zinc sulfate (ZnSO₄) electrolyte acting as the negative anode where oxidation occurs (Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻).

Burnished Copper Electrode Half-Cell

Electrolytic-grade metallic copper plate immersed in standardized 1.000 M copper(II) sulfate (CuSO₄) electrolyte acting as the positive cathode where reduction occurs (Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu).

Agar-KCl Gel Electrolytic Salt Bridge

Inverted U-tube containing 3.0 M potassium chloride in 3.0% w/v purified agar gel with porous glass frits to maintain electroneutrality while minimizing liquid junction potential (E_j < 1.0 mV).

High-Input-Impedance Digital Electrometer

Ultra-high input impedance voltmeter (Z_in > 10¹² Ω) that measures true thermodynamic equilibrium open-circuit cell EMF without drawing net current (i < 10⁻¹² A).

Microprocessor Thermostatic Circulating Water Bath

Proportional-integral-derivative (PID) regulated thermostatic water bath maintaining cell temperature stability within ±0.02 °C across the 15.0 °C to 45.0 °C test envelope.

Calibrated NIST-Traceable Pt100 RTD Thermometer

Precision four-wire platinum resistance temperature detector (resolution ±0.01 °C) positioned adjacent to the electrode faces to measure actual solution temperature.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the standard electromotive force (EMF), temperature coefficient (dE/dT), standard Gibbs free energy (ΔG°), standard entropy change (ΔS°), and standard enthalpy of reaction (ΔH°) for the reversible Daniell cell across the temperature range 288.15 K to 313.15 K. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 15.0, 20.0, 25.0, 30.0, 35.0, 40.0 °C.

01

Electrode Burnishing & Chemical Activation

Burnish the zinc rod and copper plate with fine silicon carbide abrasive paper to expose clean, active metal surfaces. Degrease with acetone, rinse thoroughly with triple-distilled water, and immerse in 0.1 M nitric acid for 10 seconds before final rinsing.

02

Electrolyte Preparation & Cell Assembly

Transfer 100.0 mL of standardized 1.000 M ZnSO₄ into the left half-cell vessel and 100.0 mL of standardized 1.000 M CuSO₄ into the right vessel. Mount both vessels securely in the thermostatic water bath.

03

Salt Bridge Placement & Ionic Contact

Seat the freshly conditioned 3.0 M KCl agar salt bridge so that both porous sintered glass ends are fully submerged below the electrolyte surfaces, establishing ionic contact without convective bulk mixing.

04

Electrometer Interfacing & Shielding

Connect the zinc anode to the negative electrometer terminal and the copper cathode to the positive terminal using gold-plated, shielded low-noise coaxial cables to eliminate contact potentials.

05

Thermostatic Equilibration Across Temperature Coordinates

Set the thermostatic circulator sequentially to 15.0, 20.0, 25.0, 30.0, 35.0, and 40.0 °C. Allow 15 minutes of equilibration at each coordinate until the Pt100 RTD reading and cell EMF stabilize within ±0.01 mV.

06

EMF Acquisition & Thermodynamic Data Reduction

Record the stable open-circuit cell EMF at each temperature. Plot E against absolute temperature T in Kelvin, perform linear least-squares regression to extract dE/dT, and calculate ΔG°, ΔS°, and ΔH° using fundamental thermodynamic relations.
