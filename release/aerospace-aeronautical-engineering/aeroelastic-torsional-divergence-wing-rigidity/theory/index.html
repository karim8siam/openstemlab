Aerodynamic and physical foundations of Wing Torsional Divergence.

Aeroelasticity investigates the dangerous mutual interaction between aerodynamic forces, elastic structural flexibility, and inertial dynamics on flight vehicles. When an aerodynamic lifting surface deflects under air loads, the structural deformation alters the aerodynamic pressure distribution, creating a closed-loop feedback mechanism. One of the most catastrophic static aeroelastic instabilities is wing torsional divergence. On an unswept wing, the aerodynamic center (where lift acts, approx quarter-chord c/4) typically lies ahead of the structural elastic axis (shear center axis, approx mid-chord c/2) by an offset distance e. When the wing experiences an initial angle of attack alpha_0, aerodynamic lift generates a nose-up twisting torque about the elastic axis. This twists the wing elastically by an angle theta, increasing the local angle of attack to (alpha_0 + theta). This additional incidence generates extra lift, creating even more torque. The structural torsional restoring stiffness resists this twisting: M_elastic = G * J * (dtheta/dz). However, aerodynamic moment scales with dynamic pressure q = 0.5 * rho * V^2, whereas structural stiffness remains constant. At the critical divergence dynamic pressure q_div, aerodynamic twisting moment completely overpowers structural torsional rigidity, causing the twist amplification factor theta / alpha_0 = 1 / (1 - q / q_div) to diverge to infinity and snapping the wing off in flight. Inverting this relation yields the linear form (1 - alpha_0 / theta) = (1 / q_div) * q. In this experiment, a wind tunnel elastic wing model is tested across dynamic pressures from q = 0.30 to 1.80 kPa, verifying q_div = 3.20 kPa.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What is aeroelastic wing torsional divergence physically?

Torsional divergence is a static aeroelastic instability where aerodynamic twisting moments overcome structural torsional restoring moments. When lift acts ahead of the structural elastic axis, an initial twist increases angle of attack, generating more lift and more torque. Because aerodynamic torque scales with dynamic pressure q (airspeed squared) while structural stiffness remains constant, a critical speed V_div exists where structural restoring capability is overwhelmed and the wing twists to structural failure.

How is the critical divergence dynamic pressure q_div formulated mathematically?

For an unswept cantilever wing modeled by strip theory, divergence dynamic pressure is q_div = (pi^2 * G * J) / (4 * l^2 * e * c * a_0), where G*J is torsional rigidity, l is wing span, e is the aerodynamic center-to-elastic axis offset distance, c is chord, and a_0 is the lift curve slope.

Why are forward-swept wings (like the Grumman X-29 or Su-47) highly susceptible to aeroelastic divergence?

On a forward-swept wing, upward bending deformation naturally twists the wing leading edge upward, increasing the local angle of attack. This geometric cross-coupling dramatically exacerbates the aerodynamic twisting moment, driving divergence speeds dangerously low unless advanced composite aeroelastic tailoring is applied.

What is aeroelastic tailoring with composite materials?

Carbon-fiber composite laminates are anisotropic: their stiffness properties depend on ply fiber orientations. By orienting carbon fiber plies at calculated asymmetric angles, structural designers introduce directional bending-torsion coupling. An upward bending load can be engineered to twist the wing nose-down (washout), completely neutralizing aerodynamic divergence.

Where is the structural elastic axis (shear center axis) located on a typical aircraft wing?

The elastic axis is the spanwise locus of cross-sectional shear centers. On a conventional two-spar wing box, the elastic axis typically lies near 40% to 50% of the chord. Because the subsonic aerodynamic center is located at 25% chord, the aerodynamic center almost always lies ahead of the elastic axis on unswept wings.

How does aeroelastic wing twist modify the spanwise lift distribution in flight?

On a swept-back wing, upward bending twists the wing nose-down (aerodynamic washout), shedding lift from the flexible wingtips toward the rigid wing root. This reduces wing root bending moments in high-g maneuvers but can reduce roll control effectiveness from ailerons.

Experimental precautions and wind tunnel safety

  • Never operate the wind tunnel near the extrapolated divergence dynamic pressure q_div; test points must remain below 60% of q_div.
  • Verify that safety optical interlocks immediately kill fan drive power if twist angle exceeds 8.0 degrees.
  • Ensure the internal torsion rod is operating well within its elastic fatigue limit without plastic deformation.
  • Account for wind tunnel ceiling and floor aerodynamic interference when testing high-aspect-ratio aeroelastic models.

Theoretical assumptions & flow limits

  • Linear divergence theory assumes 2D strip aerodynamics without 3D wingtip vortex relief.
  • Aerodynamic stall (which occurs near alpha_total = 12 deg) limits actual flight twist; stall divergence can trigger severe limit cycle oscillations.
  • Forward-swept wings experience coupled bending-torsion divergence; this lab models an unswept straight wing.
  • Assumes isothermal structural temperature; aerodynamic kinetic heating softens structural stiffness on high-Mach vehicles.
