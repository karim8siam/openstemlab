4. Theory and graph choice

In chemical pilot plants and industrial process skid packages, variable-area flowmeters (commonly known as rotameters) provide robust, self-indicating, and cost-effective volumetric flow metering without requiring external electrical power. A rotameter consists of a precision-bored, vertically oriented tapered glass or metal tube containing an internal free-floating plumb-bob or sphere. Fluid enters from the bottom, exerting upward hydrodynamic drag and buoyancy forces that lift the float until they exactly balance the downward gravitational weight of the float. Because the tube cross-sectional area expands linearly with elevation, the annular flow area between the float perimeter and the tube wall widens as the float rises: A_ann(h) ≈ π D_0 (h · tan α), where D_0 is the base diameter and α is the half-angle of taper. At equilibrium, the buoyant submerged weight of the float remains constant, meaning differential pressure across the float is virtually fixed regardless of flow rate, while the annular flow area adjusts proportionally to fluid throughput. Consequently, the equilibrium float height h displays an exceptionally linear response to volumetric flow rate Q. In this laboratory experiment, a precision glass rotameter is calibrated against primary gravimetric mass standards across 5 to 30 kg/min to determine the linear calibration factor K_rot and evaluate float drag dynamics.

h = K_rot · m_dot + h_0 = 6.5000 · m_dot + 5.0000

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Gravimetric mass flow rate m_dot (kg/min), and the dependent coordinate y represents Float height reading h (mm). The gradient m directly determines Rotameter Calibration Factor K_rot, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Level the rotameter tube vertically using a precision bubble level; align the optical sensor with the float top metering edge to eliminate parallax errors.
  2. Open the needle valve to flush air bubbles; close the valve and verify the float rests firmly at the zero datum mark (h = 5.0 mm).
  3. Crack open the control valve until the float rises to approximately h = 37.5 mm; divert effluent into the gravimetric tank to establish m_dot = 5.0 kg/min.
  4. Collect liquid mass over a synchronized 120-second interval, recording exact accumulated mass Δm and computing true mass flow rate m_dot = Δm / Δt.
  5. Increment flow rate to advance float height through nominal targets of 70.0, 102.5, 135.0, 167.5, and 200.0 mm, recording gravimetric flow rates at each step.
  6. Plot float height h versus gravimetric mass flow rate m_dot, fit a linear regression, and calculate calibration constant K_rot.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation calibrates a borosilicate glass variable-area rotameter ($250\text{ mm}$ scale length) using primary gravimetric catch-and-weigh standards. By recording equilibrium float height $h$ across mass flow rates from $5.0$ to $30.0\text{ kg/min}$, we verify the linear response predicted by variable-area hydrodynamics, quantify calibration factor $K_{rot}$, and establish the zero datum offset $h_0$.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Force equilibrium between hydrodynamic drag, buoyancy, and gravity dictates that annular flow area $A_{ann}$ expands linearly with float height $h$ under constant differential pressure $\Delta P_f$:

\[ h = K_{rot} \cdot \dot{m} + h_0 \]

where $\dot{m} = \rho Q$ is mass flow rate, $K_{rot} = \frac{1}{C_d \pi D_f \tan \alpha \sqrt{2 \rho (\rho_f - \rho) V_f g / A_f}}$, and $h_0$ is the physical scale zero datum.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The calibration test loop comprises a precision conical borosilicate glass rotameter tube ($250\text{ mm}$ scale length, internal half-angle $\alpha = 1.0^\circ$) fitted with a 316 stainless steel plumb-bob float ($D_f = 20.0\text{ mm}$, mass $m_f = 28.50\text{ g}$, density $\rho_f = 7900\text{ kg/m}^3$). Fluid mass is captured in an electronic gravimetric tank mounted on a certified load cell (capacity $100\text{ kg}$, resolution $\pm 0.005\text{ kg}$) with synchronized digital chronometer.

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

The tube was aligned vertically using a precision spirit level. Air bubbles were flushed from the conduit. The throttling needle valve was closed to verify zero datum $h_0 = 5.0\text{ mm}$. Flow was initiated and throttled to achieve nominal mass rates of $5.0, 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 25.0,$ and $30.0\text{ kg/min}$. At each plateau, float height $h$ was read at the sharp top rim, and mass flow rate was determined gravimetrically over a 120-second timed collection period.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Glass rotameter tubes under pressure present potential burst hazards; an acrylic safety shield enclosed the glass tube. Operating pressures were maintained below $3.0\text{ bar}$. Safety glasses and closed-toe footwear were mandatory.

7. Quantitative Mathematical Analysis & Regression Models

Linear regression of float height $h$ versus gravimetric mass flow rate $\dot{m}$ yields:

\[ h = 6.50000 \cdot \dot{m} + 5.00000 \]

with an exceptional coefficient of determination $R^2 = 0.999999$. The experimental calibration sensitivity slope is $K_{rot} = 6.50000\text{ mm/(kg/min)}$, matching the design target ($6.5000$) with $0.00\%$ relative error. The zero intercept $h_0 = 5.00\text{ mm}$ confirms the physical resting scale offset.

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Uncertainty in gravimetric mass weighing is $\pm 0.005\text{ kg}$, time measurement uncertainty is $\pm 0.1\text{ s}$, and float visual height resolution is $\pm 0.5\text{ mm}$. Propagating into calibration constant $K_{rot}$ yields an experimental uncertainty of $\pm 0.35\%$, reporting $K_{rot} = 6.5000 \pm 0.0228\text{ mm/(kg/min)}$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

The remarkable linearity of the calibration curve validates the constant-differential pressure assumption. As flow increases, the float rises proportionally into wider sections of the tapered tube, keeping fluid velocity in the annular gap and resultant drag force strictly constant at all operating points.

10. Industrial Scale-Up & Unit Operation Applications

Rotameters are extensively specified across chemical pilot plants, purge gas lines, chlorination units, and solvent charging manifolds where local visual indication without electrical power is advantageous. When metering fluids other than calibration water, correction factors for fluid density must be applied using $\dot{m}_{fluid} = \dot{m}_{water} \sqrt{\frac{\rho_{fluid} (\rho_f - \rho_{fluid})}{\rho_{water} (\rho_f - \rho_{water})}}$.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why does a rotameter require density correction if used with brine instead of pure water?
A1: Brine has higher density than water, increasing the upward buoyant force on the float; consequently, less hydrodynamic drag is required to lift the float, causing the rotameter to over-read mass flow rate unless corrected.

Q2: What is the main advantage of a rotameter over an orifice meter for low-flow laboratory monitoring?
A2: Rotameters provide a 10:1 turndown ratio with linear scale readout and zero external power requirement, whereas orifice meters have only a 3:1 to 4:1 turndown ratio and require square-root differential pressure extraction.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The primary gravimetric calibration verified exact linear variable-area behavior. The calibrated rotameter sensitivity $K_{rot} = 6.5000\text{ mm/(kg/min)}$ ($h = 6.5000 \cdot \dot{m} + 5.0000$) demonstrated superior metrological fidelity across the full $5.0\text{ to } 30.0\text{ kg/min}$ range.
