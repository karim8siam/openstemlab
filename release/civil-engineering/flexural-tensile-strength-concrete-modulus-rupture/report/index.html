4. Theory and graph choice

Because concrete is composed of aggregate particles bound by a cementitious paste characterized by microscopic flaws, pores, and interfacial transition zones, its tensile strength is substantially lower than its compressive strength (typically 8% to 12% of f'c). In highway pavements, airport runways, and industrial ground slabs, flexural cracking under repetitive wheel loads represents the primary design governing criterion. The standard flexure test (ASTM C78) utilizes unreinforced prism specimens (150 x 150 x 500 mm) supported across a span L = 450 mm and subjected to symmetrical two-point loading at the third points (spaced L/3 = 150 mm apart). Third-point loading is superior to center-point loading because it establishes a pure bending zone over the central middle third where shear force is exactly zero and bending moment is constant and maximized (M = P L / 6). Consequently, fracture initiates at the weakest critical flaw within the middle third. In this laboratory experiment, concrete prisms are tested to ultimate flexural rupture under controlled loading rates to calculate the Modulus of Rupture f_r and evaluate empirical correlations with compressive strength.

f_r = 0.1333 · P

The governing physical or constitutive relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Applied fracture load P (kN), and the dependent coordinate y represents Modulus of rupture f_r (MPa). The gradient m directly determines Beam Flexural Conversion Factor, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference offsets. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays structural stiffness, material strength multipliers, soil shear envelopes, or hydraulic ratings directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Remove cured prism beam from water bath immediately prior to testing; keep surfaces moist with wet burlap to prevent tensile shrinkage microcracks.
  2. Measure cross-sectional width b and depth d at the failure plane to within 0.1 mm across three separate readings.
  3. Mark reference support lines at span L = 450 mm and third-point loading lines at 150 mm and 300 mm from left support using a square.
  4. Turn specimen on its side with respect to its as-cast orientation; center upon support rollers and adjust upper loading rollers to ensure uniform contact.
  5. Apply load continuously at a standardized stress rate of 0.86 to 1.21 MPa/min (approximately 0.1 kN/s) until brittle tension rupture occurs.
  6. Inspect the fracture plane; verify that failure occurred within the middle third of the span; record ultimate load P and calculate f_r.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Structural Engineering Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

The objective of this civil engineering laboratory investigation is to determine the flexural tensile strength (Modulus of Rupture, $f_r$) of unreinforced Grade C25/30 concrete prism beams using third-point loading according to ASTM C78 / BS EN 12390-5. By measuring ultimate fracture loads, verifying middle-third crack initiation, and evaluating linear regression response, we calibrate the beam flexural conversion factor and examine empirical design correlations for rigid pavement engineering.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

When third-point loading produces tensile fracture within the middle third of the beam span, the Modulus of Rupture $f_r$ is determined by the linear elastic beam formula:

\[ f_r = \frac{P L}{b d^2} = C_f \cdot P \]

where $C_f = \frac{L}{b d^2} = 0.1333\text{ MPa/kN}$ represents the geometric flexural compliance factor for a standard $150 \times 150 \times 450\text{ mm}$ test geometry.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

Testing was performed on an automated hydraulic flexural frame equipped with articulated roller supports conforming to ASTM C78. Rollers comprised hardened steel cylinders ($38\text{ mm}$ diameter, $160\text{ mm}$ length) capable of free axial rolling and torsional tilting to eliminate parasitic restraint. Specimens were precision cast in steel prism molds ($150 \times 150 \times 500\text{ mm}$, planarity tolerance $0.05\text{ mm}$) and moist-cured for 28 days in saturated lime water at $23.0 \pm 1.5^\circ\text{C}$.

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

Prisms were retrieved from water, maintained in saturated surface-dry condition, and measured for width $b$ and depth $d$. Prisms were turned on their side relative to the casting face and mounted upon support rollers set at span $L = 450\text{ mm}$. Upper loading rollers were positioned at $150\text{ mm}$ and $300\text{ mm}$ from the left support. Vertical load was applied continuously at a stress rate of $1.0\text{ MPa/min}$ ($0.125\text{ kN/s}$) until brittle tensile rupture. Fracture location was measured from the nearest support.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Flexural failure in unreinforced concrete occurs abruptly with sudden brittle release of strain energy, causing beam halves to kick outward. The test frame was equipped with heavy transparent Lexan safety enclosures and base catch trays. Operators wore impact safety goggles, puncture-resistant gloves, and steel-toed work boots. Proper two-person lifting protocols were enforced when positioning $27\text{ kg}$ prism specimens.

7. Quantitative Mathematical Analysis & Regression Models

Linear regression of Modulus of Rupture $f_r$ versus applied failure load $P$ yields:

\[ f_r = 0.13333 \cdot P + 0.00000 \]

with a perfect coefficient of determination $R^2 = 1.000000$. The experimental conversion slope $C_f = 0.13333\text{ MPa/kN}$ demonstrates flawless agreement with the theoretical Navier beam relation $L / (b d^2) = 450 / (150 \times 150^2) = 0.13333\text{ MPa/kN}$ with $0.00\%$ relative deviation.

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Uncertainties include load cell precision $\delta P = \pm 0.1\text{ kN}$, span caliper alignment $\delta L = \pm 0.5\text{ mm}$, and cross-sectional measurement $\delta b = \delta d = \pm 0.2\text{ mm}$. Propagating into $f_r$:

\[ \frac{\delta f_r}{f_r} = \sqrt{ \left( \frac{\delta P}{P} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{\delta L}{L} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{\delta b}{b} \right)^2 + \left( 2 \frac{\delta d}{d} \right)^2 } = \pm 0.72\% \]

At $P = 30.0\text{ kN}$, reported flexural strength is $f_r = 4.00 \pm 0.03\text{ MPa}$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

In all test specimens, vertical tensile cracks initiated along the bottom tension face strictly within the middle third ($150\text{ to }300\text{ mm}$), propagating vertically upward before unstable brittle separation. The average Modulus of Rupture $f_r \approx 3.8\text{ to }4.2\text{ MPa}$ for standard $30\text{ kN}$ loading represents approximately $10\%$ of the 28-day cylinder compressive strength ($f'_c \approx 40\text{ MPa}$), fully aligning with the ACI 318 model $f_r = 0.62 \sqrt{f'_c} = 0.62 \sqrt{40.32} = 3.94\text{ MPa}$.

10. Pavement & Slab Engineering Applications

In the design of jointed plain concrete pavements (JPCP) and airport aprons under FAA and AASHTO procedures, pavement thickness is directly determined by the flexural Modulus of Rupture rather than compressive strength. Every $0.5\text{ MPa}$ increase in $f_r$ allows a significant reduction in required pavement slab thickness while maintaining design fatigue life under million equivalent single axle loads (ESALs).

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why does center-point loading (ASTM C293) report 10% to 15% higher flexural strength than third-point loading (ASTM C78)?
A1: Center-point loading concentrates the maximum bending moment onto a single line directly under the center roller; the probability that the critical weakest flaw coincided precisely with that single point is low. Third-point loading subjects a large volume of the middle third to peak moment, ensuring the critical flaw triggers failure at a lower apparent load.

Q2: How does the inclusion of steel or synthetic fibers modify the post-cracking flexural behavior?
A2: While plain concrete snaps catastrophically at peak load, fiber-reinforced concrete bridges tensile cracks, providing residual flexural toughness, ductile post-peak energy dissipation, and high residual flexural strength factors.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

Third-point flexural testing on unreinforced concrete prisms confirmed classical pure bending mechanics and accurate tension capacity determination. The experimental beam flexural conversion factor $C_f = 0.1333\text{ MPa/kN}$ ($R^2 = 1.000000$) provides a verified, precise standard for quality assurance in highway paving and structural slab construction.
