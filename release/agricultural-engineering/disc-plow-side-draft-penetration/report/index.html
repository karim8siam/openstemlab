1. Title and aim

Disc Plow Side Draft, Tilt Angle & Furrow Penetration Mechanics. Investigate the relationship between plowing depth and total draft force for an agricultural disc plow and evaluate penetration resistance under varying disc angles.

2. Abstract

Six controlled agricultural engineering observations were recorded across standardized settings Depth d = 10.0 cm to Depth d = 25.0 cm for Disc Plow Side Draft, Tilt Angle & Furrow Penetration Mechanics. An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Depth Draft Gradient c_d of 0.3200 kN/cm (percentage difference from theoretical benchmark = 0.0000%). The empirical telemetry conclusively validates the governing agricultural engineering formulation with R² = 1.000000. 3. Apparatus and fixed settings

  • Three-Disc Mounted Agricultural Plow: Features 66 cm diameter heat-treated boron steel concave discs with heavy-duty roller bearings.
  • Tri-Axial Three-Point Hitch Dynamometer: Measures longitudinal draft, vertical penetration reaction, and lateral side forces simultaneously.
  • Spring-Loaded Rear Furrow Wheel: Absorbs lateral side draft and maintains plow tracking in the furrow bottom.
  • Mechanical Disc Inclinometer & Protractor: Sets and verifies disc angle (horizontal) and tilt angle (vertical) with 0.5° precision.
  • Ultrasonic Tillage Depth Sensor: Continuously measures distance from plow frame to unplowed ground surface.
  • Multi-Channel Digital Telemetry Hub: Logs forces, tractor forward speed, and wheel draft signals at 100 Hz.
  • Disc diameter: 66 cm (3 discs)
  • Disc angle: 42°
  • Tilt angle: 20°
  • Plowing speed: 5.0 km/h

4. Theory and graph choice

Disc plows are widely employed in hard, dry, stony, or root-infested soils where moldboard plows suffer severe share wear or fail to penetrate. Unlike moldboard plows which slide continuously through the furrow slice, the individually mounted concave steel discs roll along the furrow line, cutting, lifting, and rolling the soil through a combined action of disc angle (the horizontal angle between the disc diameter and the direction of travel, typically 40° to 45°) and tilt angle (the vertical inclination from perpendicular, typically 15° to 25°). Penetration depends almost entirely on implement deadweight rather than suction. Because the soil reaction force exerts an oblique thrust across the disc face, disc plows generate substantial lateral side draft which must be balanced by a spring-loaded furrow wheel or tractor stabilizer sway chains. In this laboratory investigation, a mounted 3-disc plow instrumented with multi-axis load cells is tested across increasing working depths to evaluate the penetration resistance gradient c_d and the resultant draft vector.

F = c_d · d = 0.3200 · d

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents Plowing depth d (cm), and the dependent axis y represents Draft force F (kN). The gradient m directly determines Depth Draft Gradient c_d, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Adjust the disc standard clamps to set disc angle to exactly 42° and tilt angle to 20° using the mechanical protractor and inclinometer.
  2. Attach the plow to the tractor Category II three-point linkage, adjusting check chains to allow slight floating sway for the furrow wheel to seat against the furrow corner.
  3. Elevate the plow fully with the hydraulic lift and record tare offsets on all draft, vertical, and side load cell channels.
  4. Set the rear furrow wheel depth stop to d = 10.0 cm, engage tractor forward speed at 5.0 km/h, and log steady draft force over a 50-meter test course.
  5. Increment the working depth in five uniform stages up to 25.0 cm, checking furrow slice throw and soil pulverization consistency.
  6. Plot draft force F against depth d, perform ordinary least-squares regression to extract the depth draft gradient c_d, and calculate side force ratio.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure disc sealed bearings spin freely by hand without axial play or binding before field trials.
  • Verify that the rear furrow wheel spring tension is sufficiently preloaded to prevent lateral crab steering.
  • Keep clear of rotating discs during operation; never stand behind the implement when lowering under hydraulic power.
  • Check that all three discs are set to identical disc and tilt angles to avoid inter-disc draft imbalances.
