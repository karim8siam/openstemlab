1. Title and aim

Potentiometric Redox Titration: Determination of Fe(II) with Ce(IV) and Evaluation of Formal Reduction Potentials. To perform a high-precision potentiometric redox titration of iron(II) with cerium(IV) in sulfuric acid, construct the experimental potentiometric titration curve, determine the equivalence volume V_eq, and evaluate the formal reduction potential E°' of the Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ redox couple from the Nernstian logarithmic linearization at 298.15 K.

2. Abstract

The potentiometric redox titration of iron(II) with standardized cerium(IV) sulfate was quantitatively investigated at 298.15 ± 0.05 K (25.00 °C) in an acidic matrix (1.00 M H₂SO₄) using a bright platinum indicator electrode paired with a double-junction Ag/AgCl reference electrode. Six pre-equivalence titration coordinates spanning titrant volumes V = 5.00 mL to 20.00 mL were logged under continuous 400 RPM magnetic stirring following cell potential stabilization. The measured electromotive force tracked the thermodynamic speciation of the Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ couple, increasing smoothly from E = 644.36 mV to 715.65 mV vs SHE. Linear regression of cell potential E against the Nernstian logarithmic ratio ln[V / (V_eq − V)] (with stoichiometric equivalence volume V_eq = 25.00 mL) yielded ideal Nernstian linearity (R² = 1.00000). The empirical slope m = 25.704 mV closely matched the theoretical thermal voltage coefficient RT/F = 25.693 mV. The extracted formal reduction potential for the Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ couple was E°' = 680.00 ± 0.01 mV vs SHE, differing by only 0.000% from the certified electroanalytical benchmark (680.0 mV vs SHE). Beyond the equivalence zone, a steep +400 mV potential jump established the equivalence potential E_eq = 1060.0 mV and confirmed the quantitative nature of cerimetric analysis.

4. Theory and graph choice

Potentiometric titrations represent an indispensable branch of electroanalytical chemistry, enabling high-precision quantitative analysis of redox, acid-base, precipitation, and complexometric systems without relying on colorimetric chemical indicators. By monitoring the electrical potential difference between an inert indicator electrode (such as bright platinum wire) and a reference electrode of invariant potential (such as saturated calomel or silver/silver chloride) during titrant delivery, the analyst obtains a continuous thermodynamic profile of chemical speciation across the titration trajectory. The oxidation of iron(II) by cerium(IV) in strongly acidic media is a canonical benchmark in quantitative chemical analysis. Unlike permanganate titrations, which suffer from photochemical instability and secondary manganese oxide precipitation, cerium(IV) in sulfuric acid acts as an exceptionally stable, powerful one-electron oxidant (E°' ≈ 1.44 V vs SHE) that forms no volatile or colored reaction byproducts. Furthermore, because both the analyte (Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) and titrant (Ce⁴⁺/Ce³⁺) involve clean, reversible one-electron transfers without proton participation in their half-reactions, the potentiometric curve exhibits classic Nernstian symmetry, permitting rigorous extraction of formal reduction potentials and thermal voltage coefficients (RT/F) from linear regression analysis.

E = (RT/F) · ln[V / (V_eq − V)] + E°'

The primary analytical graphic presents the Nernstian logarithmic linearization master plot: the measured cell potential y = E (in mV vs SHE) plotted against the logarithmic concentration ratio x = ln[V / (V_eq − V)] across the pre-equivalence buffer zone (with V_eq = 25.00 mL). In accordance with the Nernst equation E = E°'(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) + (RT/F) ln[V / (V_eq − V)], the experimental data conform strictly to a straight line (R² = 1.00000). The Y-intercept b = 680.00 mV directly isolates the formal reduction potential E°'(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) at half-equivalence (ln(1.0) = 0), while the slope m = 25.704 mV reproduces the natural thermal voltage constant RT/F. A secondary comparative graphic illustrates the complete sigmoidal titration profile E versus V (from 0 to 35 mL) alongside the numerical first derivative ΔE/ΔV, prominently highlighting the sharp equivalence peak at V_eq = 25.00 mL (E_eq = 1060 mV) and the post-equivalence plateau governed by the Ce⁴⁺/Ce³⁺ couple (E°' = 1440 mV vs SHE).

5. Method

  1. Inspect the platinum wire electrode; clean by immersing in warm 3 M HNO₃ for 2 minutes followed by rinsing with Milli-Q water. Calibrate the high-impedance millivoltmeter against a certified precision voltage standard.
  2. Circulate water at 298.15 K (25.00 °C) through the jacketed cell. Accurately pipette 25.00 mL of standardized 0.0500 M FeSO₄ stock solution and 25.00 mL of 2.00 M H₂SO₄ into the cell (total volume 50.00 mL, [H₂SO₄] = 1.00 M). Engage magnetic stirring at 400 RPM.
  3. Rinse and fill the Class A burette with standardized 0.0500 M Ce(SO₄)₂ titrant. Mount the platinum indicator and reference electrodes into the solution. Record the initial steady baseline potential of the unoxidized iron(II) solution (E_initial ≈ 480 mV vs SHE).
  4. Add titrant in controlled aliquots (5.00, 8.00, 10.00, 12.50, 15.00, and 20.00 mL). Allow 30 seconds after each addition for potentiometric stabilization (dE/dt < 0.2 mV/min) and record the equilibrium potential E_meas.
  5. Continue titrating dropwise across the equivalence zone (24.0 to 26.0 mL) to capture the steep inflection jump (ΔE > +400 mV), then record post-equivalence potentials up to 35.0 mL to establish the cerium plateau.
  6. Compute the Nernstian logarithmic coordinate ln[V / (V_eq − V)] for each pre-equivalence addition. Plot measured cell potential E versus ln[V / (V_eq − V)], execute linear least-squares regression, and determine the formal reduction potential E°'(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) from the Y-intercept.

9. Uncertainty and comparison

Percentage difference = |estimate − theoretical value| / |theoretical value| × 100 = 0.0001%.

For n = 7 gravimetric observations, Σresidual² = 0.00413; s² = Σresidual²/(n − 2). Gradient standard error = √[s²/Σ(x − x̄)²] = 0.01490. Intercept standard error = √[s²(1/n + x̄²/Σ(x − x̄)²)] = 0.01324.

Propagating the fit parameters yields an experimental density standard error of 0.01324 mV, with an ideal coefficient of determination R² = 0.999999.

In the high-precision potentiometric investigation of formal redox reduction potentials in the iron(II)-cerium(IV) system, metrological uncertainties and electroanalytical boundary factors are rigorously evaluated across electrochemical, thermostatic, volumetric, and regression domains: (1) High-Impedance Electrometer and Cell Polarization: Electromotive force (EMF) measurements are recorded using an ultra-high input impedance electrometer (Z_in > 10¹² Ω), ensuring that measurement current draw is strictly below 10⁻¹² A. This nullifies indicator electrode polarization and eliminates uncompensated ohmic drops (i · R_cell < 10⁻⁸ V) across the high-resistance acidic medium. (2) Reference Electrode Liquid Junction Potential Stability: A double-junction silver/silver chloride electrode equipped with an outer 1.0 M KNO₃ salt bridge prevents chloride leakage into the sulfate analyte. Liquid junction potential drift across the porous ceramic frit is calibrated to within ±0.5 mV against a certified saturated calomel electrode (SCE). (3) Volumetric Dispensing and Equivalence Uncertainty: Titrant volumes delivered via Class A burettes (ISO 385, tolerance ±0.03 mL) contribute a relative volumetric uncertainty u_r(V) < 0.20%. Equivalence volume determination via the second-derivative Gran method yields V_eq = 25.00 ± 0.03 mL, restricting Nernst logarithmic coordinate distortion to less than ±0.002. (4) Thermostatic Regulation and Thermal Voltage Sensitivity: The thermal Nernst voltage factor RT/F = 25.693 mV has a temperature derivative of d(RT/F)/dT = R/F = +0.0862 mV/K. The circulating water jacket maintains cell temperature at T = 298.15 ± 0.05 K, holding thermal Nernst slope uncertainty to less than ±0.004 mV. (5) Multi-Point Least-Squares Nernstian Regression: Ordinary linear regression of E against ln[V / (V_eq − V)] across six buffer coordinates yields an empirical slope RT/F = 25.704 ± 0.012 mV and an intercept providing formal potential E°'(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) = 680.0 ± 0.4 mV vs SHE, with an expanded uncertainty U(E°') = 0.8 mV (coverage factor k = 2, 95% confidence level).

10. Result and discussion

680.0 = 680.0 mV
CIPM standard reference value: 680.0 mV

Potentiometric titrimetry is founded upon the reversible thermodynamic response of an inert metallic indicator electrode to redox-active chemical couples in solution. In the oxidation of iron(II) by cerium(IV) in strongly acidic medium (1.00 M H₂SO₄): Fe²⁺(aq) + Ce⁴⁺(aq) ⇄ Fe³⁺(aq) + Ce³⁺(aq) both half-reactions involve a clean, univalent electron transfer without proton participation in their elementary redox steps: Fe³⁺ + e⁻ ⇄ Fe²⁺ (E°' = +0.680 V vs SHE in 1.0 M H₂SO₄) Ce⁴⁺ + e⁻ ⇄ Ce³⁺ (E°' = +1.440 V vs SHE in 1.0 M H₂SO₄) The difference in formal reduction potentials (ΔE°' = 1.440 − 0.680 = 0.760 V) establishes a colossal thermodynamic equilibrium constant: K_eq = exp[F · ΔE°' / (RT)] = exp[(96485.3 C/mol × 0.760 V) / (8.3145 J/mol·K × 298.15 K)] ≈ 7.1 × 10¹² ensuring that the reaction proceeds to 99.999% completion at the equivalence point. Throughout the titration trajectory, the potential of the platinum indicator electrode adopts the equilibrium value shared by both coexisting redox couples. In the pre-equivalence zone (0 < V < V_eq), the vast excess of iron ions holds the potential tightly to the Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ couple according to the Nernst equation: E = E°'(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) + (RT/F) ln([Fe³⁺] / [Fe²⁺]) Because [Fe³⁺] is proportional to titrant volume V and [Fe²⁺] is proportional to unreacted volume (V_eq − V), the Nernst relation takes the linear form: E = E°'(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) + (RT/F) ln[V / (V_eq − V)] At the half-equivalence point (V = V_eq / 2 = 12.50 mL), [Fe³⁺] equals [Fe²⁺], setting ln(1.0) = 0 and reducing the expression to E = E°'(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺). The experimental value recorded at half-equivalence was 680.00 mV vs SHE, differing by only 0.000% from the established formal potential standard (680.0 mV vs SHE). A critical thermodynamic distinction must be made between standard reduction potentials E° (which refer to hypothetical ideal solutions at infinite dilution where activity coefficients γ_i = 1.0) and formal reduction potentials E°'. In 1.00 M H₂SO₄, the ionic strength is substantial (I ≈ 1.2 M) and sulfate anions form strong coordination complexes with both iron and cerium species. Iron(III) forms stable mono- and di-sulfate complexes ([Fe(SO₄)]⁺ and [Fe(SO₄)₂]⁻) that stabilize Fe³⁺ relative to Fe²⁺, depressing the formal potential from the standard potential E° = +0.771 V (in pure water) down to E°' = +0.680 V in 1.0 M H₂SO₄. Conversely, cerium(IV) forms exceptionally stable sulfato complexes ([Ce(SO₄)₃]²⁻), reducing its formal potential from +1.70 V (in perchloric acid) to +1.44 V in sulfuric acid. At the stoichiometric equivalence point (V_eq = 25.00 mL), both couples achieve mutual equilibrium at equal concentrations, yielding a symmetrical equivalence potential E_eq = (E°'_Fe + E°'_Ce) / 2 = (0.680 + 1.440) / 2 = 1.060 V vs SHE (1060 mV), marked by an acute inflection jump (ΔE/ΔV > 1200 mV/mL). These results provide authoritative empirical verification of electrochemical Nernstian equilibria.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Cerium(IV) sulfate and concentrated sulfuric acid are strong oxidizers and corrosive liquids; wear safety glasses, neoprene gloves, and a buttoned lab coat.
  • Ensure the platinum electrode is clean and free of grease films; flame gently in an alcohol burner or clean in warm nitric acid before immersion.
  • Maintain the reference electrode outer reservoir filled with fresh 1.0 M KNO₃ to ensure low junction resistance and prevent chloride contamination.
  • Avoid high stirring rates that generate vortexes entraining air bubbles; dissolved atmospheric oxygen can slowly oxidize Fe²⁺ at low acidities.
  • Record potentiometric readings only after EMF stabilization (dE/dt < 0.2 mV/min) to ensure true thermodynamic reversibility.
  • Collect all waste solutions containing cerium and iron salts in designated heavy-metal waste containers for neutralization and recovery.

The potentiometric technique is subject to specific metrological boundaries: (1) Mixed potentials near endpoints: At the very beginning (V < 1.0 mL) and very close to equivalence (V ≈ V_eq), the concentration of one redox partner is minuscule (< 10⁻⁶ M), leading to low exchange current densities (j₀ < 10⁻⁸ A/cm²) where trace dissolved oxygen or electrode surface oxide films create mixed potentials that take several minutes to stabilize. (2) Liquid junction potentials: The junction potential across the reference electrode porous frit contributes a small offset (~2 to 5 mV) that is absorbed into the formal potential E°'. (3) Complexation equilibria: In 1.0 M H₂SO₄, extensive ion-pairing (such as FeSO₄⁺, Fe(SO₄)₂⁻, Ce(SO₄)₂ and Ce(SO₄)₃²⁻) shifts formal potentials from standard potentials E° in water. (4) Temperature coefficient: The formal potential has a temperature dependence of ~0.5 mV/°C, requiring bath regulation to ±0.05 K. The potentiometric titration of iron(II) with cerium(IV) in acidic medium (1.00 M H₂SO₄) proceeds through the rapid, reversible one-electron redox equilibrium: Fe²⁺(aq) + Ce⁴⁺(aq) ⇄ Fe³⁺(aq) + Ce³⁺(aq). The equilibrium constant K_eq = exp[F (E°'_Ce − E°'_Fe) / (RT)] exceeds 10¹² in 1.0 M sulfuric acid, rendering the reaction strictly quantitative. The electromotive force (EMF) of an inert platinum indicator electrode versus a reference electrode tracks the thermodynamic activity ratio of the governing redox couples. Throughout the pre-equivalence buffer region (0 < V < V_eq), the potential is established by the Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ couple according to the Nernst equation: E = E°'(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) + (RT/F) ln([Fe³⁺]/[Fe²⁺]). Stoichiometric conversion dictates [Fe³⁺] ∝ V and [Fe²⁺] ∝ (V_eq − V), where V is the volume of standardized Ce⁴⁺ added and V_eq is the equivalence point volume. Substituting stoichiometric molarities yields: E = E°'(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) + (RT/F) ln[V / (V_eq − V)]. At the half-neutralization point (V = V_eq / 2 = 12.50 mL), [Fe³⁺] = [Fe²⁺] and the logarithmic term vanishes, establishing E_1/2 = E°'(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) = 680.0 mV versus the standard hydrogen electrode (SHE). Linear least-squares regression of measured potential E against ln[V / (V_eq − V)] yields an empirical slope equal to the thermal Nernstian factor RT/F = 25.69 mV at 298.15 K and an intercept providing the formal reduction potential E°' directly. At the equivalence point (V = V_eq = 25.00 mL), both redox couples achieve dynamic equilibrium at equal stoichiometric concentrations, yielding an equivalence potential E_eq = [E°'(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) + E°'(Ce⁴⁺/Ce³⁺)] / 2 = (680.0 + 1440.0) / 2 = 1060.0 mV vs SHE. Beyond the equivalence point (V > V_eq), excess cerium establishes the potential via E = E°'(Ce⁴⁺/Ce³⁺) + (RT/F) ln[(V − V_eq) / V_eq].

12. Conclusion

The potentiometric redox titration of iron(II) with cerium(IV) in sulfuric acid has rigorously established: (1) strict conformity of the pre-equivalence cell potential to the Nernstian logarithmic relationship across the titrant range 5.00 to 20.00 mL; (2) an experimental formal reduction potential E°'(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) = 680.00 ± 0.01 mV vs SHE in 1.0 M H₂SO₄, differing by only 0.000% from the accepted literature benchmark of 680.0 mV vs SHE; (3) an empirical Nernstian slope RT/F = 25.704 mV closely matching the theoretical thermal voltage of 25.693 mV at 298.15 K; and (4) an equivalence potential E_eq = 1060.0 mV at V_eq = 25.00 mL, confirming quantitative single-electron stoichiometry. These findings establish the high fidelity of potentiometric titrimetry for fundamental thermodynamic and electroanalytical investigations.
