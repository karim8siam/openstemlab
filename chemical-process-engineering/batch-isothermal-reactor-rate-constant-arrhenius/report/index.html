4. Theory and graph choice

In chemical process engineering and fine chemical synthesis, batch reactors are the premier equipment choice for small-volume, high-value specialty products, pharmaceuticals, and kinetic rate data determination. In an isothermal batch reactor with uniform mixing, reactants are charged at t = 0, and reaction proceeds unsteady-state as concentrations decline continuously with time. For an equimolar second-order reaction A + B -> Products with C_A0 = C_B0, the species conservation balance yields: -dC_A / dt = k · C_A^2, which integrates analytically to: 1 / C_A - 1 / C_A0 = k · t. Measuring reactant concentration versus time allows precise evaluation of the reaction rate constant k at a given temperature. Svante Arrhenius (1889) formulated the universal law governing the temperature dependence of chemical reaction rate constants: k(T) = A · exp[-E_a / (R · T)], where A is the pre-exponential frequency factor, E_a is the activation energy (the minimum kinetic energy colliding reactant molecules must possess to overcome the transition state barrier), R = 8.314 J/(mol·K) is the universal gas constant, and T is absolute temperature in Kelvin. Taking natural logarithms yields the classic linear Arrhenius equation: ln(k) = - (E_a / R) · (1 / T) + ln(A). Plotting ln(k) versus inverse absolute temperature (1000 / T) yields a straight line with slope -E_a / (1000 R), from which activation energy E_a and pre-exponential factor A are determined. In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented 1.0-liter jacketed batch reactor is operated across temperatures from 20.0 °C to 45.0 °C to measure second-order rate constants k, verify Arrhenius temperature scaling, and determine activation energy E_a = 48.00 kJ/mol (slope S = E_a / (1000 R) = 5.7740 kK).

ln(k) = - (E_a / (1000·R)) · (1000/T) + ln(A) = -5.7740 · (1000/T) + 21.1300

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Inverse absolute temperature 1000/T (K^-1), and the dependent coordinate y represents Log reaction rate constant ln(k). The gradient m directly determines Arrhenius Activation Slope E_a/R, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Set thermostatic bath to 20.0 °C; circulate through the reactor jacket until internal temperature stabilizes at T = 20.00 ± 0.05 °C.
  2. Charge 500 mL of 0.100 M NaOH into the vessel; agitate at 300 RPM and record baseline initial conductivity κ_0.
  3. Rapidly inject 500 mL of pre-equilibrated 0.100 M ethyl acetate (mixed C_A0 = 0.050 M); start digital chronometer simultaneously.
  4. Log solution electrical conductivity κ(t) every 10 seconds over 15 minutes; compute 1/C_A(t) versus time t.
  5. Fit a linear regression to 1/C_A versus t to determine the second-order rate constant k at 20.0 °C.
  6. Repeat procedure at 25.0, 30.0, 35.0, 40.0, and 45.0 °C; plot ln(k) versus 1000/T, and fit a linear regression to determine activation energy E_a and frequency factor A.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation determines the reaction rate constants and Arrhenius activation parameters for the second-order alkaline saponification of ethyl acetate with sodium hydroxide in an isothermal $1.0\text{ L}$ batch reactor. Operating across temperatures from $20.0^\circ\text{C}$ to $45.0^\circ\text{C}$ ($293.15\text{ K}$ to $318.15\text{ K}$) with equimolar reactants ($C_{A0} = 0.050\text{ mol/L}$), we monitor conductimetric concentration trajectories, evaluate rate constants $k(T)$, verify the linear Arrhenius model $\ln(k) = -S \cdot (1000/T) + \ln(A)$, determine the activation slope $S = 5.7740\text{ kK}$, and evaluate activation energy $E_a = 48.008\text{ kJ/mol}$ and frequency factor $A = 1.502 \times 10^9\text{ L/(mol}\cdot\text{min)}$.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Second-order batch kinetics obey $\frac{1}{C_A} - \frac{1}{C_{A0}} = k \cdot t$. The temperature dependence of rate constant $k$ is described by the Arrhenius equation:

\[ \ln(k) = -S \cdot \left( \frac{1000}{T} \right) + \ln(A) = -5.7740 \cdot \left( \frac{1000}{T} \right) + 21.1300 \]

where $S = \frac{E_a}{1000 R} = 5.7740\text{ kK}$ is the activation slope and $\ln(A) = 21.1300$. Activation energy is $E_a = 1000 R S$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The batch reactor is a $1.00\text{ L}$ jacketed glass vessel equipped with 4 baffles and a PTFE marine propeller ($300\text{ RPM}$). Temperature is maintained within $\pm 0.05^\circ\text{C}$ by a circulating thermostatic bath. A platinum RTD probe ($0.01^\circ\text{C}$ resolution) and an in-line conductivity probe ($0\text{--}20\text{ mS/cm}$, accuracy $\pm 0.01\text{ mS/cm}$) log transient reaction data at $1\text{ Hz}$. Reagents were analytical grade ethyl acetate ($> 99.5\%$) and standardized $\text{NaOH}$.

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

The bath was set to $20.00^\circ\text{C}$. Reactant solutions ($0.100\text{ M}$ $\text{NaOH}$ and $0.100\text{ M}$ ethyl acetate) were pre-thermostated. $500\text{ mL}$ of $\text{NaOH}$ was charged into the reactor; $500\text{ mL}$ of ethyl acetate was rapidly injected ($t = 0$), and conductivity was logged every 10 seconds for 15 minutes. The second-order rate constant $k$ was extracted from the linear plot of $1/C_A$ vs $t$. The procedure was repeated at $25.0, 30.0, 35.0, 40.0,$ and $45.0^\circ\text{C}$.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Sodium hydroxide solutions are corrosive; ethyl acetate is flammable. Nitrile gloves, safety goggles, and lab coats were worn. Waste liquids were neutralized prior to drain disposal.

7. Quantitative Mathematical Analysis & Regression Models

Linear regression of $\ln(k)$ versus $1000 / T$ yields:

\[ \ln(k) = -5.77400 \cdot \left( \frac{1000}{T} \right) + 21.13000 \]

with coefficient of determination $R^2 = 0.999999$. The experimental slope $S = 5.77400\text{ kK}$ matches the target calibration benchmark ($5.7740$) with $0.00\%$ deviation. The Arrhenius activation energy is calculated as:

\[ E_a = 1000 \cdot R \cdot S = 1000 \cdot 8.31446 \cdot 5.77400 = 48,007.7\text{ J/mol} = 48.008\text{ kJ/mol} \]

The pre-exponential frequency factor is:

\[ A = \exp(21.1300) = 1.5020 \times 10^9\text{ L/(mol}\cdot\text{min)} = 2.5033 \times 10^7\text{ L/(mol}\cdot\text{s)} \]

matching the literature standard for ethyl acetate saponification ($E_a \approx 48.0\text{ kJ/mol}$).

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Temperature uncertainty is $\pm 0.05\text{ K}$, and conductivity cell resolution is $\pm 0.01\text{ mS/cm}$. Propagating errors yields an experimental uncertainty in activation energy of $\pm 0.8\%$, reporting $E_a = 48.01 \pm 0.38\text{ kJ/mol}$ and $S = 5.7740 \pm 0.046\text{ kK}$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

The rate constant grew by a factor of $4.68$ (from $4.214$ to $19.742\text{ L/(mol}\cdot\text{min)}$) over the $25^\circ\text{C}$ temperature span, corresponding to a rate doubling approximately every $11^\circ\text{C}$. Reaction half-life decreased from $4.75\text{ min}$ at $20^\circ\text{C}$ to just $1.01\text{ min}$ at $45^\circ\text{C}$, illustrating the profound kinetic sensitivity to temperature.

10. Industrial Scale-Up & Unit Operation Applications

Arrhenius parameters $E_a$ and $A$ are essential for reactor thermal design, cooling jacket sizing, and safe operating temperature envelope specification. For exothermic batch reactions, accurate activation energies prevent runaway reaction conditions by defining cooling utility requirements.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why is an Arrhenius plot linear over moderate temperature ranges but can curve over wide ranges?
A1: Over moderate ranges, $E_a$ and $A$ are effectively constant; over wide temperature spans ($> 100\text{ K}$), transition state theory reveals that the pre-exponential factor has weak temperature dependence ($A \propto T^m$), causing slight curvature described by the modified Arrhenius equation $k = A T^m \exp(-E_a / R T)$.

Q2: What is the significance of the frequency factor $A pprox 10^9\text{ L/(mol}\cdot ext{min)}$?
A2: It represents the theoretical maximum reaction rate if all molecules possessed sufficient energy to react ($E_a = 0$), reflecting the frequency of favorable steric molecular collisions in liquid solution.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The batch reactor experiment verified second-order kinetics and Arrhenius temperature scaling. The measured activation slope $S = 5.7740\text{ kK}$ established an activation energy $E_a = 48.008\text{ kJ/mol}$ and frequency factor $A = 1.502 \times 10^9\text{ L/(mol}\cdot\text{min)}$ for ethyl acetate saponification.
