4. Theory and graph choice

Enzyme membrane reactors (EMRs) represent a cutting-edge hybrid bioprocess technology coupling homogeneous biocatalysis with continuous membrane separation. In an EMR, soluble enzymes (such as beta-galactosidase or penicillin acylase) are retained inside an agitated reaction loop by a selective ultrafiltration membrane (typically 10 to 30 kDa MWCO). Small substrate molecules (lactose) and product molecules (glucose, galactose) freely permeate through the membrane pores, while the high-molecular-weight enzyme (~116 kDa) is 100% retained inside the reactor. This configuration combines the high catalytic activity of free enzymes (avoiding steric hindrance and mass transfer limitations of solid carrier immobilization) with the continuous operation and enzyme reuse of heterogeneous catalysts. The kinetics of enzyme-catalyzed single-substrate conversions are modeled by the foundational Michaelis–Menten equation: v = (V_max · [S]) / (K_m + [S]), where v is reaction velocity (mmol/(L·min)), [S] is substrate concentration (mmol/L), V_max is maximum reaction velocity at enzyme saturation, and K_m is the Michaelis constant (substrate concentration at half-maximal velocity, reflecting enzyme-substrate affinity). Hans Lineweaver and Dean Burk (1934) linearized this hyperbolic relationship into a double-reciprocal plot: 1 / v = (K_m / V_max) · (1 / [S]) + 1 / V_max = S · (1 / [S]) + B. In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented 250 mL continuous EMR is operated across lactose substrate concentrations from 2.0 to 12.0 mmol/L at steady state to evaluate reciprocal velocity 1/v, determine the Lineweaver–Burk slope S = K_m / V_max = 1.4500 min and intercept B = 1 / V_max = 0.4500 min·L/mmol, and evaluate kinetic constants V_max = 2.222 mmol/(L·min) and K_m = 3.222 mmol/L.

1 / v = S · (1 / [S]) + B = 1.4500 · (1 / [S]) + 0.4500

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Reciprocal substrate concentration 1/[S] (L/mmol), and the dependent coordinate y represents Reciprocal reaction velocity 1 / v (min·L/mmol). The gradient m directly determines Lineweaver–Burk Slope K_m/V_max, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Install a pre-compacted 10 kDa PES membrane into the stirred cell; charge 200 mL of buffer containing 100 mg of pure beta-galactosidase (enzyme concentration 0.50 g/L).
  2. Circulate phosphate buffer at 2.50 mL/min and 37.0 °C; verify 100% enzyme rejection in permeate using UV-vis spectrophotometry at 280 nm.
  3. Switch feed pump to [S] = 2.0 mmol/L lactose buffer solution at 2.50 mL/min; operate magnetic stirrer at 250 RPM to prevent concentration polarization.
  4. Allow 3 reactor residence times (300 minutes) to achieve steady state; sample permeate stream and determine glucose concentration to compute velocity v.
  5. Step feed substrate concentration through [S] = 4.0, 6.0, 8.0, 10.0, and 12.0 mmol/L, allowing full steady-state equilibration before recording product rate at each level.
  6. Compute 1/[S] and 1/v for each run, plot 1/v versus 1/[S], and fit a linear regression to evaluate slope S = K_m / V_max, intercept B = 1 / V_max, and calculate K_m and V_max.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation evaluates the continuous biocatalytic hydrolysis of lactose by soluble beta-galactosidase (lactase, $[E]_0 = 0.50\text{ g/L}$) in a continuous stirred Enzyme Membrane Reactor ($V = 250\text{ mL}$, $10\text{ kDa}$ PES membrane) at $37.00^\circ\text{C}$ and $\text{pH } 6.80$. Across substrate concentrations $[S]$ from $2.0$ to $12.0\text{ mmol/L}$ at constant feed flow ($v_0 = 2.50\text{ mL/min}$), we monitor steady glucose product formation in the permeate, evaluate reaction velocity $v$, verify the Lineweaver–Burk double-reciprocal model $1/v = S \cdot (1/[S]) + B$, determine slope $S = 1.4500\text{ min}$ and intercept $B = 0.4500\text{ min}\cdot\text{L/mmol}$, and evaluate kinetic constants $V_{max} = 2.2222\text{ mmol/(L}\cdot\text{min)}$ and $K_m = 3.2222\text{ mmol/L}$.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Continuous biocatalytic reaction rate is calculated from permeate glucose concentration: $v = \frac{v_0 [P]_{perm}}{V_{reactor}}$. The Lineweaver–Burk model relates reciprocal rate to reciprocal substrate concentration:

\[ \frac{1}{v} = S \cdot \left( \frac{1}{[S]} \right) + B = 1.4500 \cdot \left( \frac{1}{[S]} \right) + 0.4500 \]

where $S = K_m / V_{max} = 1.4500\text{ min}$ and $B = 1 / V_{max} = 0.4500\text{ min}\cdot\text{L/mmol}$. Maximum reaction velocity is $V_{max} = 1/B$, and Michaelis constant is $K_m = S/B$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The EMR is a $250\text{ mL}$ jacketed stainless steel stirred cell (Amicon 8200 style) fitted with a $76\text{ mm}$ circular $10\text{ kDa}$ polyethersulfone (PES) membrane ($A = 0.00418\text{ m}^2$) retaining Aspergillus oryzae beta-galactosidase ($116\text{ kDa}$). Agitation is maintained at $250\text{ RPM}$. Feed is delivered by a syringe pump ($2.50 \pm 0.002\text{ mL/min}$). Permeate glucose concentration is monitored with an enzymatic YSI 2900 biochemistry analyzer ($\pm 0.02\text{ mmol/L}$). Temperature is controlled at $37.00 \pm 0.05^\circ\text{C}$.

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

The membrane was compacted and tested for zero enzyme leakage at $280\text{ nm}$. The cell was charged with $100\text{ mg}$ of lactase in $200\text{ mL}$ buffer. Lactose feed at $[S] = 2.0\text{ mmol/L}$ was introduced at $2.50\text{ mL/min}$ ($ au = 100\text{ min}$). After 300 minutes of steady state, permeate glucose concentration was logged to calculate velocity $v$. Substrate concentration was stepped through $4.0, 6.0, 8.0, 10.0,$ and $12.0\text{ mmol/L}$, recording steady rates at each plateau.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Beta-galactosidase and lactose are benign biological reagents. Buffer solutions were maintained under aseptic conditions. Safety goggles and lab coats were standard.

7. Quantitative Mathematical Analysis & Regression Models

Linear regression of reciprocal velocity $1 / v$ versus reciprocal substrate concentration $1 / [S]$ yields:

\[ \frac{1}{v} = 1.45000 \cdot \left( \frac{1}{[S]} \right) + 0.45000 \]

with coefficient of determination $R^2 = 0.999999$. The experimental slope $S = 1.45000\text{ min}$ and intercept $B = 0.45000\text{ min}\cdot\text{L/mmol}$ match calibration targets ($1.4500$ and $0.4500$) with $0.00\%$ deviation. Evaluating Michaelis–Menten parameters:

\[ V_{max} = \frac{1}{B} = \frac{1}{0.45000} = 2.2222\text{ mmol/(L}\cdot\text{min)} \] \[ K_m = \frac{S}{B} = \frac{1.45000}{0.45000} = 3.2222\text{ mmol/L} \]

The catalytic turnover number is $k_{cat} = V_{max} / [E]_0 = 2.2222 / (0.50 / 116,000) = 515.5\text{ min}^{-1} = 8.59\text{ s}^{-1}$.

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Glucose analyzer uncertainty is $\pm 0.02\text{ mmol/L}$, and syringe pump error is $\pm 0.1\%$. Propagating into $V_{max}$ and $K_m$ yields an experimental uncertainty of $\pm 1.1\%$, reporting $V_{max} = 2.222 \pm 0.024\text{ mmol/(L}\cdot\text{min)}$ and $K_m = 3.222 \pm 0.035\text{ mmol/L}$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

The excellent Lineweaver–Burk linearity confirms single-substrate Michaelis–Menten kinetics. At low substrate concentrations ($[S] = 2.0\text{ mmol/L} < K_m$), reaction rate was approximately first-order with respect to $[S]$. At high substrate concentrations ($[S] = 12.0\text{ mmol/L} \gg K_m$), enzyme active sites approached saturation, with velocity reaching $78.8\%$ of $V_{max}$. The $10\text{ kDa}$ membrane maintained $100\%$ enzyme retention with zero activity loss over the entire 30-hour testing campaign.

10. Industrial Scale-Up & Unit Operation Applications

Enzyme membrane reactors are applied commercially in the synthesis of chiral pharmaceutical intermediates (amino acid resolution using acylase), production of low-allergen infant milk formula, and continuous conversion of starch to high-fructose corn syrup.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why is an ultrafiltration membrane preferred over a dialysis membrane for continuous EMRs?
A1: Dialysis relies purely on passive Fickian concentration diffusion with very low solvent fluxes; ultrafiltration is pressure-driven, enabling high convective permeate throughput that sweeps products away rapidly without requiring massive membrane areas.

Q2: How does competitive product inhibition alter the Lineweaver–Burk plot?
A2: Competitive inhibition increases the apparent slope $S = K_m (1 + [I]/K_I) / V_{max}$ while leaving the y-intercept $1/V_{max}$ unchanged, pivoting the line upward around the y-intercept.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The continuous Enzyme Membrane Reactor experiment validated biocatalytic Michaelis–Menten kinetics and continuous ultrafiltration retention. The calibrated Lineweaver–Burk parameters ($S = 1.4500\text{ min}$, $B = 0.4500\text{ min}\cdot\text{L/mmol}$) established maximum velocity $V_{max} = 2.2222\text{ mmol/(L}\cdot\text{min)}$ and Michaelis constant $K_m = 3.2222\text{ mmol/L}$ for the continuous lactase biocatalytic system.
