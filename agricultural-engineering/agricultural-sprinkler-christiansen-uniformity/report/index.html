1. Title and aim

Agricultural Impact Sprinkler Christiansen Uniformity Coefficient (CU) Catch-Can Analysis. Determine the radial water distribution profile, Christiansen Uniformity Coefficient (CU), and overlapping pattern efficiency of an agricultural impact sprinkler nozzle across varying operating pressures.

An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Uniformity Pressure Sensitivity s_p of 0.0550 %/kPa (percentage difference from theoretical benchmark = 0.0000%). 4. Theory and graph choice

Sprinkler irrigation applies water by spraying high-velocity droplets through air under pressure, simulating natural rainfall across field crops, pastures, and orchards. An agricultural impact sprinkler utilizes an oscillating spring-loaded drive arm that repeatedly deflects into the water jet, intermittently rotating the nozzle body around its vertical axis. The spatial distribution of water caught on the ground is governed by nozzle pressure, nozzle orifice diameter, jet trajectory angle (typically 23° to 27°), and ambient wind speed. In accordance with ASABE S398, sprinkler water distribution is quantified using a radial grid of catch cans, and uniformity across overlapping sprinkler spacings (rectangular or triangular layouts) is calculated using the Christiansen Uniformity Coefficient: CU = 100·[1 - (Σ|X_i - X_bar|) / (n·X_bar)], where X_i is the water depth caught in each can and X_bar is the mean catch depth. Operating at sub-optimal low pressure causes 'doughnut' patterns where water falls in an outer ring while the center remains dry, producing poor CU (< 70%). Increasing pressure breaks the jet into fine droplets that fill the inner radius, boosting CU above 85% until excessive misting and wind drift emerge. In this indoor laboratory trial, a 100-catch-can grid records sprinkler precipitation profiles.

CU = s_p · P + CU_0 = 0.0550 · P + 64.0000

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents Operating pressure P (kPa), and the dependent axis y represents Christiansen Uniformity CU (%). The gradient m directly determines Uniformity Pressure Sensitivity s_p, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Lay out the catch cans in a square 1.5 m × 1.5 m matrix across the indoor test hall, ensuring all can rims sit level and at uniform elevation.
  2. Mount the impact sprinkler on the central 1.2 m vertical riser, setting the pressure regulator to initial low pressure P = 150.0 kPa.
  3. Start the sprinkler pump and allow the sprinkler to rotate continuously for exactly 30.0 minutes, distributing water across the catch-can grid.
  4. Measure and record the depth of water caught in each can X_i (mm), computing the mean depth X_bar and the mean absolute deviation Σ|X_i - X_bar| / n.
  5. Simulate a 12 m × 12 m overlapping sprinkler layout and calculate the Christiansen Uniformity Coefficient CU = 100·[1 - Σ|X_i - X_bar| / (n·X_bar)].
  6. Repeat the procedure at 200, 250, 300, 350, and 400 kPa, plotting CU versus pressure to extract the sensitivity slope s_p.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Perform all sprinkler patternation testing indoors or in zero-wind (< 1.0 m/s) conditions to prevent wind drift distortion.
  • Verify that catch cans are clean and dry before each run; check that can lips are sharp and burr-free.
  • Ensure the sprinkler riser is perfectly plumb; any vertical tilt skews water distribution into an asymmetric pattern.
  • Do not stand in the spray path during rotation to avoid intercepting water before it reaches catch cans.

Indoor tests eliminate wind drift and atmospheric evaporative droplet losses occurring in open fields. Evaluated using a single 4.8 mm brass agricultural impact sprinkler mounted on a 1.2 m riser in zero-wind indoor catch-can grid with 12 m × 12 m simulated overlap.
