Chemical engineering transport & equilibrium foundations of Crossflow Ultrafiltration.

Membrane separation processes—including microfiltration, ultrafiltration, nanofiltration, and reverse osmosis—are premier pressure-driven unit operations in biotechnology, water desalination, dairy processing, and wastewater reclamation. In crossflow ultrafiltration (UF), pressurized feed slurry recirculates tangentially across the membrane surface at high velocity, while pure solvent permeates perpendicularly through the porous polymeric or ceramic skin (pore size 1 to 100 nm, molecular weight cut-off MWCO 1,000 to 100,000 Da). Retained macromolecular solutes (e.g., proteins, polysaccharides, colloids) accumulate at the membrane boundary layer, forming a concentrated solute layer termed concentration polarization. At low transmembrane pressures (TMP = (P_feed + P_ret - 2 P_perm) / 2), permeate flux J obeys Darcy's law: J = TMP / [μ · R_m] = L_p · TMP, where R_m is clean membrane hydraulic resistance and L_p is pure water permeability. However, as TMP increases, the solute concentration at the membrane surface reaches a critical gel concentration C_g, forming a viscous gel layer that adds dynamic resistance R_g. Beyond this threshold, permeate flux enters the pressure-independent (limiting flux) regime governed by convective-diffusive back-transport: J_lim = k_bl · ln(C_g / C_b), where k_bl is the boundary layer mass transfer coefficient. In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented crossflow flat-sheet ultrafiltration cell with a 30 kDa polyethersulfone (PES) membrane is operated across TMPs from 0.5 to 3.0 bar to measure clean water permeability L_p = 42.5000 L/(m^2·h·bar) and observe the onset of gel polarization.

J = L_p · TMP = 42.5000 · TMP

1. Principles of Crossflow Membrane Ultrafiltration

Ultrafiltration (UF) is a pressure-driven membrane separation process utilizing semi-permeable asymmetric polymeric or ceramic membranes with pore diameters of $1$ to $100\text{ nm}$. It effectively retains macromolecules, proteins, viruses, and colloidal silica while permitting solvent and small dissolved salts to pass freely.

2. Darcy's Law & Clean Water Permeability

In clean water filtration, fluid transport across the porous membrane matrix is governed by Darcy's law:

\[ J = \frac{\Delta P}{\mu R_m} = L_p \cdot \text{TMP} \]

where $J$ is permeate flux ($\text{L/(m}^2\cdot\text{h)}$), $\text{TMP}$ is transmembrane pressure, $\mu$ is permeate viscosity, $R_m$ is intrinsic membrane hydraulic resistance ($\text{m}^{-1}$), and $L_p = 1 / (\mu R_m)$ is the pure water permeability coefficient in $\text{L/(m}^2\cdot\text{h}\cdot\text{bar)}$.

3. Concentration Polarization & Film Theory

During macromolecular filtration, rejected solute accumulates at the membrane surface. At steady state, convective solute transport toward the membrane is balanced by Fickian diffusion back into the bulk crossflow stream:

\[ J C - D \frac{dC}{dy} = 0 \]

Integrating across boundary layer thickness $\delta$ with boundary conditions $C(0) = C_w$ (wall concentration) and $C(\delta) = C_b$ (bulk concentration):

\[ J = k_{bl} \ln\left( \frac{C_w}{C_b} \right) \]

where $k_{bl} = D / \delta$ is the mass transfer coefficient. When wall concentration reaches saturation gel concentration $C_g$, a viscous gel layer forms ($R_g$), and flux plateaus at the limiting flux:

\[ J_{lim} = k_{bl} \ln\left( \frac{C_g}{C_b} \right) \]

In the pre-gel linear Darcy regime, plotting pure water flux $J$ versus $\text{TMP}$ yields a straight line through the origin whose slope directly provides membrane permeability $L_p$:

\[ J = L_p \cdot \text{TMP} \]

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What is the difference between dead-end filtration and crossflow filtration?

In dead-end filtration, slurry flow is perpendicular to the membrane, causing all retained solids to accumulate in a rapidly thickening cake that blinds the filter; in crossflow filtration, fluid sweeps tangentially across the membrane at high velocity, continuously scouring away accumulated foulants and maintaining high steady flux.

How does concentration polarization differ from membrane fouling?

Concentration polarization is a reversible thermodynamic accumulation of rejected solute in the liquid boundary layer that dissipates immediately when flow or pressure stops; membrane fouling is an irreversible deposition (pore blocking, adsorption, gel formation) requiring chemical cleaning to restore permeability.

What is Molecular Weight Cut-Off (MWCO) in membrane specification?

MWCO is the molecular weight of a globular solute (in Daltons) that is 90% rejected by the membrane; a 30 kDa membrane rejects > 90% of molecules larger than 30,000 g/mol (such as BSA, MW ≈ 66.5 kDa).

Why does permeate flux become independent of transmembrane pressure at high TMP?

At high TMP, convective solute transport toward the membrane balances back-diffusion, forcing surface solute concentration to reach its maximum packing limit (gel concentration C_g); further pressure increases merely compress this gel layer, adding hydraulic resistance that exactly cancels the increased pressure driving force.

How does crossflow velocity affect the limiting permeate flux J_lim?

Higher crossflow velocity enhances turbulence and thins the boundary layer, increasing the mass transfer coefficient k_bl; according to the film model J_lim = k_bl ln(C_g / C_b), limiting flux scales with shear rate (v^0.8 in turbulent flow).

How are fouled polymeric membranes restored in industrial bioprocessing?

Membranes undergo Clean-in-Place (CIP) sequences consisting of forward flush, alkaline wash (0.1 M NaOH with sodium hypochlorite to hydrolyze proteins), acid wash (citric or phosphoric acid to dissolve inorganic scale), and enzymatic detergent cleaning.

Experimental precautions and process safety

  • Compact new membranes at 4.0 bar prior to acquiring calibration data to eliminate permanent polymer deformation artifacts.
  • Maintain feed temperature strictly at 25.0 ± 0.1 °C, as water viscosity drops by ~2.5% per °C, causing apparent flux drift.
  • Ensure feed is pre-filtered through 5 μm cartridge filters to prevent particulate scoring of the membrane surface.
  • Do not allow membranes to dry out; store in sodium metabisulfite preservative solution when not in service.

Theoretical assumptions & boundary condition limits

  • The transport and thermodynamic models apply within the specified calibrated operating window (0.5 to 3.0 bar).
  • Assumes ideal continuum flow and homogeneous phase mixing without unmodeled parasitic wall losses.
  • Continuous sensor readings reflect ordinary least-squares regression across successive observations.
  • High-temperature or extreme concentration excursions require non-ideal equation-of-state corrections.
