Laboratory apparatus & testing equipment.

Stainless steel water bath trough (1.5 m length) with variable speed leadscrew pulling carriage and digital distance encoder.

Standard Brass Briquette Molds

Four-piece machined brass molds with hourglass central neck (10.0 mm × 10.0 mm cross section) conforming to ASTM D113.

Thermostatic Water Bath Heating & Chilling Unit

Submerged Briquette Support Plate

Specific Gravity Bath Fluid Adjuster (NaCl / Alcohol)

Straight Trimming Putty Knife

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Measure tensile elongation distance before rupture of a briquette specimen of bituminous binder pulled at a constant speed of 50 mm/min at 25°C in a water bath according to ASTM D113 / AASHTO T51. Standardized parameter settings: 10.0, 13.0, 16.0, 19.0, 22.0, 25.0 min.

01

Mold Assembly & Amalgam Release Coating

Assemble brass briquette mold pieces on a brass baseplate; coat mold contact surfaces with a thin film of glycerin-dextrin release agent to prevent adhesion.

02

Bitumen Melting & Briquette Casting

Heat bitumen sample to 140 °C; stir gently; pour molten binder into molds until overflowing; allow to cool undisturbed in air for 40 minutes.

03

Mold Trimming & Water Bath Equilibration

Trim excess bitumen level with top of mold using a warmed straightedge knife; place molds in ductilometer water bath at 25.0 ± 0.5 °C for exactly 90 minutes.

04

Mounting Molds onto Ductilometer Carriage

Remove side pieces of molds carefully without disturbing the bitumen briquette neck; attach end clips to pins on the fixed and motorized carriage in the water bath.

05

Motorized Pulling at 50 mm/min

Engage leadscrew motor at standard pulling rate of 50.0 ± 2.5 mm/min (5.0 cm/min); observe stretching of the central bitumen neck into a thin thread.

06

Thread Elongation Tracking & Rupture Logging

Track elongation distance L across time intervals (10, 13, 16, 19, 22, and 25 min); verify thread remains suspended neutrally without touching tank floor or surface; record rupture distance in cm.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

In flexible highway pavement engineering, bituminous binders must possess sufficient tensile ductility and cohesive flexibility to stretch and relax under repetitive heavy vehicular traffic deflections and diurnal thermal contraction cycles without premature micro-cracking. Bituminous binders that lack ductility or have undergone severe oxidative aging become brittle, leading to rapid block cracking, thermal fatigue cracking, and stone raveling. Standardized under ASTM D113 / AASHTO T51, the Bitumen Ductility Test measures the tensile elongation in centimeters that a briquette specimen of asphalt binder can endure before snapping. Molten bitumen is poured into a standardized brass briquette mold having an hourglass neck profile (minimum cross section of exactly 10.0 mm width by 10.0 mm height, area 1.0 cm²). After ambient cooling and trimming, the briquette mold clips are attached to the pulling carriage of a motorized ductilometer submerged in a large temperature-controlled water bath maintained at 25.0 ± 0.5 °C. The drive carriage pulls the briquette halves apart horizontally at a uniform, pulsation-free speed of 50.0 ± 2.5 mm/min (5.0 cm/min). The bitumen specimen stretches into an increasingly fine, micro-thin thread; the distance in centimeters at which the thread ruptures is recorded as the Ductility Value. Pavement specifications strictly mandate Ductility ≥ 100 cm at 25 °C for virgin paving bitumens (Grade 60/70 and 80/100). In this laboratory experiment, thread elongation is tracked across traction intervals from 10.0 to 25.0 minutes to calibrate elongation rate slope = 5.0000 cm/min and verify compliance (Ductility > 100 cm).
