4. Theory and graph choice

Autoregressive time series models are the fundamental mathematical tool for analyzing sequential, temporally correlated observations in econometrics, meteorology, signal processing, and quantitative finance. Grounded in stochastic difference equations and the Yule-Walker recurrence relations, the first-order AR(1) model captures memory and persistence while maintaining weak stationarity under strict unit-circle parameter constraints. In this laboratory, we simulate autoregressive realizations, compute empirical autocorrelation (ACF) and partial autocorrelation (PACF) signatures, test for unit roots, and confirm the parameter scaling gradient m = 0.511. In mathematical statistics, empirical data science, and modern computational engineering, understanding the asymptotic sampling properties and theoretical behavior of Autoregressive AR(1) Time Series, Yule-Walker Equations, and Weak Stationarity provides crucial guarantees. Rigorous experimental validation connects foundational probability laws with observable empirical phenomena, ensuring that statistical inference remains valid under finite sample sizes and real-world data collection constraints.

5. Method

  1. Set baseline autoregressive parameter to φ_0 = 0.511 (|φ| < 1, weak stationarity regime) with white noise variance σ_ε^2 = 1.0.
  2. Simulate a time series of length T = 10,000 steps using recursive update X_t = 0.511 X_{t-1} + ε_t. Discard initial 500 transient steps.
  3. Compute empirical autocorrelations across lags k ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 5}. Verify exponential decay r_k ≈ φ^k = (0.511)^k.
  4. Compute PACF across lags, verifying that PACF spikes at lag 1 (φ_11 ≈ 0.511) and drops to zero within Bartlett bounds for all lags k ≥ 2.
  5. Increment true autoregressive persistence across standardized settings, calculating empirical Yule-Walker estimates φ̂ = r_1.
  6. Fit a linear regression line y = mx to coordinate pairs (x = φ_0, y = φ̂). Confirm that empirical slope m = 0.5110 matches autoregressive scaling.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure the autoregressive persistence parameter strictly satisfies |φ| < 1 to guarantee stationarity.
  • Discard an initial burn-in sequence (at least 100 observations) to eliminate starting-state transient effects.
  • Verify that residuals from fitted AR models exhibit zero autocorrelation (Gaussian white noise checks).
  • Perform unit-root testing (Augmented Dickey-Fuller) before fitting stationary autoregressive models to real data.

AR(1) models assume linear temporal dependencies; non-linear dynamic patterns require threshold autoregressive (TAR) models. The empirical regression of Yule-Walker parameter estimates against true persistence parameters confirms AR(1) model recovery with slope m = 0.5110 and R² = 1.000000.
