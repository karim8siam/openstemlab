FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Evaluate aggregate mechanical toughness against dynamic shock impact (BS 812-112) and resistance to crushing under progressive static compressive loading (BS 812-110 / IS 2386-4). Standardized parameter settings: 5.0, 8.0, 11.0, 14.0, 17.0, 20.0 blows.

01

Aggregate Sizing & Oven Drying

Sieve bulk aggregate to extract particles passing the 12.5 mm sieve and retained on the 10.0 mm sieve; dry in oven at 110 °C for 4 hours; cool to room temperature.

02

Cylindrical Cup Filling & Consolidation

Fill cylindrical steel measure in three equal layers; tamp each layer with 25 strokes of bullet-nosed tamping rod; strike off surplus with straightedge; record initial mass M_initial (approx. 350 g).

03

Apparatus Base Clamping

Transfer sample into the machine cup; ensure uniform bed thickness; tamp with 25 strokes; clamp cup firmly onto the heavy cast-iron anvil base.

04

Drop Hammer Adjustment & Dynamic Impacting

Adjust hammer free-fall height to exactly 380 ± 5 mm above aggregate surface; release hammer across target blow counts (5, 8, 11, 14, 17, and 20 blows) at 1-second intervals.

05

Sample Recovery & Sieving Over 2.36 mm Sieve

Carefully remove pulverized aggregate from cup into pan; sieve over 2.36 mm sieve for 5 minutes; brush cup interior clean into pan.

06

Fines Mass Weighing & AIV Computation

Weigh fraction passing 2.36 mm sieve (M_fines) and retained fraction (M_retained); verify total mass within 1.0 g of M_initial; calculate AIV (%) = (M_fines / M_initial) · 100.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

In highway pavement design, bridge deck overlays, and railway ballast engineering, mineral aggregates must resist dynamic shock impacts from high-speed vehicular traffic and wheel flat spots without shattering into structural powder. The Aggregate Impact Value (AIV) test (BS 812-112 / IS 2386-4) directly quantifies aggregate dynamic toughness. A standardized cylindrical steel measure (102 mm internal diameter, 50 mm depth) is filled with oven-dried aggregate passing the 12.5 mm sieve and retained on the 10.0 mm sieve, consolidated in three layers with 25 tamping rod strokes. The sample is transferred into a heavy cast-iron cup secured to a rigid anvil resting on a concrete floor. A 13.5 to 14.0 kg sliding steel hammer drops freely from a height of 380 ± 5 mm down vertical guide rails onto the aggregate bed. Under standard testing, exactly 15 blows delivered at an interval of not less than one second subject the aggregate particles to intense compressive shock waves. The crushed specimen is removed and sieved over a 2.36 mm (BS No. 8) sieve: AIV (%) = (M_fines / M_initial) · 100. Pavement specifications classify aggregates based on AIV: < 10% exceptionally tough, 10% to 20% strong for high-speed wearing courses, 20% to 30% acceptable for road base courses, and > 35% weak and unfit for pavement construction. In this laboratory investigation, fines generation is evaluated across 5 to 20 impact blows to calibrate dynamic crushing sensitivity slope = 1.6500 %/blow (yielding standard 15-blow AIV = 24.75%).
