1. Title and aim

Medical Ultrasound Transducer Acoustic Beam Attenuation & Spatial Profiling. Determine the acoustic attenuation coefficient α_u of a tissue-mimicking phantom in an automated ultrasound test tank across depths using a calibrated PVDF needle hydrophone.

4. Theory and graph choice

Diagnostic ultrasound imaging is among the most widely deployed, safe, and cost-effective clinical imaging modalities, offering real-time visualization of soft tissues, cardiac dynamics (echocardiography), fetal development, and vascular perfusion without ionizing radiation. The propagation of ultrasound waves through biological media is governed by acoustic wave equations. As the piezoelectric transducer launches high-frequency longitudinal pressure waves (typically 2 to 15 MHz) into soft tissue, the acoustic intensity I(z) decays exponentially with propagation depth z due to absorption (conversion of acoustic energy into viscous heat) and scattering (redirection of acoustic energy away from the beam by sub-wavelength cellular structures): I(z) = I_0 · exp(-2·α·z). In clinical acoustic engineering and decibel notation, acoustic signal attenuation A_db is expressed as A_db = 10 · log₁₀(I_0 / I) = 20 · log₁₀(P_0 / P) = α_u · z, where α_u is the acoustic attenuation coefficient in dB/cm. In soft tissues, acoustic attenuation scales approximately linearly with ultrasound frequency: α_u(f) = a_0 · f^n (where n ≈ 1.0 to 1.1, and a_0 ≈ 0.5 to 0.7 dB/(cm·MHz)). For an abdominal probe centered at 3.5 MHz, standard tissue phantoms exhibit an attenuation of approximately 1.85 dB/cm. In this laboratory investigation, an automated 3-axis ultrasound beam profiling tank equipped with a 0.5 mm PVDF needle hydrophone scans acoustic pressure fields through standardized tissue-mimicking phantoms to evaluate α_u.

A_db = α_u · z = 1.8500 · z (at 3.5 MHz ultrasound center frequency)

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Axial depth z (cm), and the dependent coordinate y represents Acoustic attenuation A_db (dB). The gradient m directly determines Acoustic Attenuation Coefficient α_u, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Fill test tank with degassed deionized water (dissolved O₂ < 4 ppm) and align transducer acoustic axis with PVDF needle hydrophone tip using pulse-echo reflections.
  2. Scan the hydrophone across orthogonal x-y planes to identify the maximum acoustic pressure axis, aligning coordinate z with the beam propagation path.
  3. Record baseline peak-to-peak acoustic pressure P_0 in pure degassed water at focal distance (z = 0) as reference unattenuated intensity.
  4. Position calibrated tissue-mimicking phantom blocks between transducer and hydrophone, setting initial tissue path length z = 2.0 cm.
  5. Record 100 averaged RF acoustic A-line waveforms, calculate root-mean-square acoustic pressure P(z), and compute decibel attenuation A_db = 20 · log₁₀(P_0 / P).
  6. Increment phantom thickness through 4.0, 6.0, 8.0, 10.0, and 12.0 cm, recording stabilized decibel attenuation A_db at each step.
  7. Plot decibel attenuation A_db versus axial depth z and extract acoustic attenuation coefficient α_u from the slope of the linear fit through the origin.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure water tank is thoroughly degassed; microscopic air bubbles act as acoustic reflectors that scatter the ultrasound beam.
  • Protect fragile PVDF needle hydrophone tip (diameter 0.5 mm); mechanical contact with tank walls causes permanent sensor destruction.
  • Verify acoustic alignment across x-y planes at each depth to ensure hydrophone remains centered in the main acoustic focal lobe.
  • Maintain constant water temperature (22.0 °C) to prevent sound speed variations from skewing spatial coordinates.

The linear decibel attenuation model applies to homogeneous media; human anatomy presents complex layered structures with refraction and reverberation.
