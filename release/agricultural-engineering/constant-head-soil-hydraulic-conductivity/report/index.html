1. Title and aim

Constant-Head Soil Saturated Hydraulic Conductivity (K_sat) Determination. Determine the saturated hydraulic conductivity (K_sat) of agricultural soil cores using a constant-head permeameter and verify Darcy's linear filtration law.

4. Theory and graph choice

Saturated hydraulic conductivity (K_sat) is the fundamental quantitative measure of a soil's ability to transmit water when all pore spaces are completely saturated under laminar flow conditions. It serves as the cornerstone parameter for agricultural tile drainage design (Hooghoudt's equation), irrigation scheduling, seepage loss calculations from farm ponds and earthen canals, and solute transport modeling. For coarse-textured soils and undisturbed agricultural topsoils possessing moderate to high permeability, K_sat is determined in accordance with ASTM D2434 and standard soil physics protocols using a constant-head permeameter. In accordance with Darcy's classical law, the specific volumetric discharge rate per unit cross-sectional area (the Darcy flux or apparent velocity v = Q / (A·t)) is directly proportional to the dimensionless hydraulic gradient i = ΔH / L (the hydraulic head loss ΔH across a soil column of length L). The constant of proportionality is K_sat, which depends on soil pore geometry, pore size distribution, fluid density, and dynamic viscosity. In this experiment, de-aired water is passed through undisturbed soil core samples across controlled hydraulic gradients to extract K_sat.

v = K_sat · i = 0.1800 · i

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents Hydraulic gradient i = ΔH / L (m/m), and the dependent axis y represents Darcy velocity v (cm/min). The gradient m directly determines Saturated Hydraulic Conductivity K_sat, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Trim the ends of the undisturbed soil core flush with the sample cylinder. Place the core in a vacuum saturator and slowly saturate from the bottom upward with de-aired water over 24 hours to eliminate entrapped air.
  2. Assemble the saturated core into the permeameter cell between fine mesh screens and saturated porous stones, tightening tie rods to seal against side-wall bypass leakage.
  3. Connect the constant-head reservoir to establish an initial hydraulic head loss ΔH = 5.0 cm across the 10.0 cm core (gradient i = 0.50 m/m).
  4. Allow water to flow until effluent discharge reaches steady state, then collect water in a graduated cylinder over exactly 5.0 minutes, logging collected volume Q.
  5. Increment hydraulic head gradient in four stages up to i = 2.5 m/m (ΔH = 9.0, 13.0, 17.0, 21.0, 25.0 cm), collecting timed effluent for each gradient.
  6. Calculate Darcy velocity v = Q / (A·Δt) (cm/min), plot v against hydraulic gradient i, and perform linear regression through the origin to determine K_sat.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Always saturate soil cores from bottom to top; top-down saturation traps air pockets in blind pores.
  • Verify zero side-wall leakage around the core perimeter before recording effluent measurements.
  • Record water temperature during each trial to apply dynamic viscosity temperature corrections.
  • Ensure flow velocities remain laminar; avoid excessive hydraulic gradients that cause soil particle piping or wash-out.

Rigid-wall cells do not permit confining radial stress, making them susceptible to edge effects in swelling clay soils. Evaluated using undisturbed sandy loam soil core (diameter 7.5 cm, length 10.0 cm, dry bulk density 1.45 g/cm³) using de-aired water at 20°C.
