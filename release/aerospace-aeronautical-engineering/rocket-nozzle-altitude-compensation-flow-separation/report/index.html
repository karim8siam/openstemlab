1. Title and aim

Rocket Nozzle Altitude Compensation and Over-Expanded Flow Separation. Detect shock-induced boundary layer separation in over-expanded rocket nozzles using the Summerfield criterion across ambient back-pressures.

4. Theory and graph choice

Rocket nozzles designed to operate efficiently in the vacuum of outer space require very large expansion area ratios (epsilon = A_exit / A_t > 50). However, when such a high-expansion nozzle fires at sea level, the high ambient atmospheric pressure (P_a = 101.3 kPa) greatly exceeds the isentropic exit pressure (P_exit << P_a), forcing the nozzle into a severely over-expanded state. The adverse pressure gradient imposed by the ambient back-pressure penetrates upstream into the nozzle boundary layer, causing the supersonic flow to detach cleanly from the diverging bell walls through an oblique shock system. Arthur Summerfield established the classical empirical criterion for over-expanded flow separation: separation occurs at the internal nozzle station where wall static pressure P_w drops to approximately 35% to 40% of ambient back-pressure: P_sep / P_a approx 0.380 (the Summerfield criterion). As an ascending rocket climbs through the atmosphere, ambient back-pressure P_a decays exponentially, causing the separation shock front to move progressively downstream until full flowing condition is achieved. In this laboratory experiment, a cold-gas supersonic rocket nozzle instrumented with wall static taps is tested across ambient pressure ratios (P_c / P_a)^0.65 from 1.2 to 5.5, verifying the linear scaling of the separation area ratio A_sep / A_t.

\left(\frac{A_{\text{sep}}}{A_t}\right) = m \cdot \left(\frac{P_c}{P_a}\right)^{0.65} + b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Chamber-to-Ambient Pressure Parameter (P_c / P_a)^0.65, and the dependent coordinate y represents Nozzle Separation Area Ratio A_sep / A_t. The slope m directly extracts Separation Area Scaling Factor, while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Inspect divergent bell wall taps and seal the nozzle exhaust inside the variable back-pressure altitude chamber.
  2. Supply high-pressure dry air to establish constant nozzle chamber stagnation pressure P_c = 1.0 MPa.
  3. Adjust chamber vacuum pumps to establish ambient back-pressures corresponding to parameter (P_c/P_a)^0.65 = 1.2, 1.8, 2.5, 3.4, 4.4, and 5.5.
  4. Sample all 32 static pressure taps simultaneously across the nozzle bell contour.
  5. Locate the exact axial station where wall static pressure abruptly jumps from isentropic expansion toward ambient back-pressure.
  6. Plot separation area ratio A_sep / A_t versus (P_c/P_a)^0.65, perform linear regression to determine scaling slope m, and verify the Summerfield ratio.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Monitor nozzle structural side-loads continuously; asymmetric separation can generate destructive bending moments that damage test fixtures.
  • Verify that altitude chamber vacuum seals can withstand deep sub-atmospheric pressures without implosion.
  • Ensure the compressed air supply is thoroughly dried to prevent ice crystal formation inside the cryogenic separation zone.
  • Do not stand in the direct line of sight of nozzle exit optical windows during high-pressure blowdown.

Summerfield criterion is an empirical approximation; Schmucker or Kalt-Bendemann criteria provide higher precision for specific contour shapes. Linear regression of the separation area ratio against (P_c/P_a)^0.65 yields a scaling slope m = 1.4500 with R^2 > 0.999999.
