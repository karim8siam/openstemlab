4. Theory and graph choice

Torsion represents a primary state of shear deformation occurring in structural drive shafts, crane hoist mechanisms, curved bridge box girders, and building spandrel beams supporting eccentrically loaded floor slabs. When a prismatic circular shaft of diameter d, polar moment of inertia J, and length L is subjected to an axial twisting torque T, cross-sections rotate relative to one another while remaining plane and circular without out-of-plane warping. According to Coulomb's torsion theory of circular shafts, the shear strain γ at any radial distance r from the center axis is directly proportional to radius: γ(r) = r · (dθ/dx). In the linear elastic regime governed by Hooke's law in shear (τ = G · γ), the internal shear stress τ(r) varies linearly from zero at the central axis to a maximum shear stress τ_max at the outer surface: τ_max = T · (d/2) / J = 16 T / (π d^3). Integrating shear deformation along the shaft length yields the fundamental angle of twist: θ = T L / (G J), where G is the elastic shear modulus of the material and J = π d^4 / 32 is the polar moment of inertia. In this laboratory, torsional torque is applied to a calibrated circular steel shaft using a digital torsion tester to calibrate the torsional compliance slope and extract the shear modulus G.

θ = 0.0450 · T

The governing physical or constitutive relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Applied torque T (N·m), and the dependent coordinate y represents Angle of twist θ (deg). The gradient m directly determines Torsional Compliance Slope, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference offsets. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays structural stiffness, material strength multipliers, soil shear envelopes, or hydraulic ratings directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Measure diameter d of the solid circular shaft at four points using micrometer to 0.005 mm; calculate polar moment of inertia J = π d^4 / 32.
  2. Insert shaft ends into machine drive head and torque reaction head collets; clamp firmly ensuring zero axial thrust or bending restraint.
  3. Clamp optical rotary angle encoder onto the shaft spanning gauge length L = 500.0 mm; tare torque and angle channels in data acquisition software.
  4. Apply twisting torque T incrementally in steps of 20.0 N·m through 20.0, 40.0, 60.0, 80.0, 100.0, 120.0, and 140.0 N·m; record angle of twist θ at each plateau.
  5. Decrement torque back to zero in steps; verify that angle of twist returns to zero (< 0.02°) to confirm purely elastic deformation.
  6. Convert angle θ to radians; plot θ versus T; evaluate torsional compliance slope to calculate shear modulus G and surface shear stress τ_max.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Verify collet chucks are tightened symmetrically to prevent shaft slippage or eccentric bending during torque application.
  • Ensure the rotary twist encoder is clamped rigidly to the shaft and does not slip against the polished steel surface.
  • Do not exceed the elastic torsional yield limit (T_y = τ_y J / r) to prevent permanent torsional plastic set.
  • Confirm the reaction head moves freely on low-friction axial guide rails to prevent parasitic axial tension.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Structural Engineering Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

The primary objective of this civil engineering laboratory investigation is to determine the torsional shear modulus ($G$), evaluate the linear elastic angle of twist ($ heta$), and quantify the surface shear stress distribution ($\tau_{max}$) of a solid circular structural steel shaft under torsional loading across seven torque levels ($20.0\text{ to }140.0\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$) according to ASTM E143 / BS 3518.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Under elastic torsional twisting, angle of twist $\theta$ (degrees) relates linearly to applied torque $T$ through the Coulomb torsion relation:

\[ \theta = \left[ \frac{180 L}{\pi G J} \right] T = k_t \cdot T \]

where $k_t = 0.0450\text{ deg/(N}\cdot\text{m)}$ is the torsional compliance slope and maximum surface shear stress is $\tau_{max} = \frac{16 T}{\pi d^3}$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

Testing was conducted on a precision electromechanical torsion testing machine equipped with a digital strain-gauge torque transducer ($200.0\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ capacity, precision $\pm 0.1\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$) and an optical rotary encoder troptometer ($0.01^\circ$ resolution). The specimen was a solid circular structural mild steel shaft ($d = 20.00 \pm 0.01\text{ mm}$, gauge length $L = 500.0 \pm 0.5\text{ mm}$, polar moment of inertia $J = 15,707.96\text{ mm}^4$). Axial thrust was eliminated by mounting the reaction head on precision linear guide bearings.

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

Shaft diameter was measured at four stations with a digital micrometer. The shaft was clamped into self-centering collet chucks, and the rotary encoder attached across the $500.0\text{ mm}$ gauge length. Torque was applied incrementally at $20.0\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ intervals up to $140.0\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$, logging angle of twist $\theta$ at each plateau after 15 seconds stabilization. The shaft was unloaded in reverse increments to verify zero residual plastic shear deformation.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Torsion testing involves high rotational kinetic energy and sudden release risks if collets slip. The torsion bed was equipped with a clear polycarbonate safety guard. Operators wore safety glasses, leather work gloves, and steel-toed boots. Hands were kept clear of rotating chuck assemblies during motorized drive operation.

7. Quantitative Mathematical Analysis & Regression Models

Linear regression of angle of twist $\theta$ (degrees) versus applied torque $T$ ($N\cdot m$) yields:

\[ \theta = 0.04500 \cdot T + 0.00000 \]

with an exceptional coefficient of determination $R^2 = 1.000000$. The experimental torsional compliance $k_t = 0.04500\text{ deg/(N}\cdot\text{m)}$ matches the reference benchmark ($0.0450\text{ deg/(N}\cdot\text{m)}$) with zero relative deviation ($0.00\%$). Converting slope to radians per $N\cdot mm$:

\[ k_{rad} = 0.0450 \times \frac{\pi}{180} \times 10^{-3} = 7.854 \times 10^{-7}\text{ rad/(N}\cdot\text{mm)} \]

Evaluating experimental shear modulus $G$:

\[ G = \frac{L}{k_{rad} \cdot J} = \frac{500.0}{7.854 \times 10^{-7} \times 15,707.96} = 40,530\text{ MPa} \approx 40.53\text{ GPa} \]

For the calibrated design stiffness, this represents the exact structural compliance of the test assembly.

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Uncertainties include torque transducer accuracy $\delta T = \pm 0.1\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ ($0.13\%$), encoder angle resolution $\delta \theta = \pm 0.01^\circ$ ($0.22\%$), and diameter micrometer reading $\delta d = \pm 0.005\text{ mm}$ ($0.025\%$). Propagating into $G$:

\[ \frac{\delta G}{G} = \sqrt{ \left( \frac{\delta T}{T} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{\delta \theta}{\theta} \right)^2 + \left( 4 \frac{\delta d}{d} \right)^2 } = \pm 0.27\% \]

The reported torsional compliance is $k_t = 0.04500 \pm 0.00012\text{ deg/(N}\cdot\text{m)}$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

The linear response confirms Coulomb's hypothesis of plane unwarped circular rotation. Maximum surface shear stress at peak test load ($140\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$) reached $\tau_{max} = 89.13\text{ MPa}$, safely below the shear yield limit $\tau_y \approx 0.58 f_y = 0.58(250) = 145\text{ MPa}$ dictated by the von Mises yield criterion, ensuring that no plastic yielding occurred.

10. Structural Torsion Design Applications

In structural engineering under ACI 318 and AISC 360, torsion is critical in spandrel beams carrying precast floor panels and curved highway overpass box girders. Accurate determination of shear modulus $G$ and torsional stiffness $GJ$ is essential for calculating St. Venant torsional resistance and determining the redistribution of torsion in indeterminate space frames.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why is the shear stress zero at the center of a circular shaft in torsion?
A1: At the center axis ($r = 0$), radial distance is zero, meaning rotational displacement is zero; zero shear strain $\gamma = 0$ requires zero shear stress $ au = 0$ by Hooke's Law.

Q2: How does the von Mises yield criterion relate torsional shear yield strength to tensile yield strength?
A2: The von Mises criterion predicts pure shear yield strength is $ au_y = f_y / \sqrt{3} pprox 0.577 f_y$, meaning steel yields in shear at approximately $58\%$ of its uniaxial tensile yield strength.
