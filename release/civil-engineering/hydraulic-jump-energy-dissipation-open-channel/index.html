A reason behind every step.

Investigate hydraulic jump formation, measure initial and sequent flow depths, compute head loss and energy dissipation efficiency, and verify the classical Bélanger momentum equation in a tilting flume. Standardized parameter settings: 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5, 4.0 ratio.

01

Flume Bed Leveling & Sluice Gate Calibration

Set flume horizontal; zero hook gauges at flume floor datum; open sluice gate to initial test aperture (e.g. 20 mm).

02

Supercritical Flow Generation

Start recirculation pump; adjust discharge Q to establish high-velocity supercritical flow beneath the sluice gate.

03

Jump Stabilization via Tailwater Control

Raise downstream tailwater weir until a stable, stationary hydraulic jump forms in the middle glass inspection section of the flume.

04

Initial Depth & Velocity Measurement

Position upstream hook gauge immediately upstream of jump roller; record supercritical flow depth y_1; log upstream velocity v_1 = Q / (B · y_1); compute Fr_1.

05

Sequent Depth Measurement

Position downstream hook gauge immediately downstream of the surface roller where water surface becomes smooth and subcritical; record sequent depth y_2.

06

Systematic Variation & Energy Loss Calculation

Adjust gate aperture and pump discharge across six systematic Froude conditions (predictor = 1.5 to 4.5); record y_1, y_2; calculate energy loss ΔE.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

A hydraulic jump is a rapid, highly turbulent open-channel flow phenomenon occurring when supercritical flow (Froude number Fr > 1) abruptly transitions to subcritical flow (Fr < 1). In hydraulic infrastructure—including spillway stilling basins, sluice gate chutes, and canal drop structures—hydraulic jumps are intentionally generated as primary energy dissipators to convert catastrophic kinetic energy into internal thermal turbulence, preventing severe downstream bed scour and riverbank erosion. Applying the one-dimensional momentum conservation equation across the control volume while neglecting boundary friction yields the classical Bélanger equation (1828) relating initial supercritical depth y_1 to subcritical sequent (conjugate) depth y_2: y_2 / y_1 = 0.5 · [√(1 + 8·Fr_1²) - 1], where upstream Froude number Fr_1 = v_1 / √(g·y_1). Specific energy drops dramatically across the jump: head loss ΔE = E_1 - E_2 = (y_2 - y_1)³ / (4·y_1·y_2), representing macro-turbulent roller energy dissipation. Depending on initial Froude number Fr_1, jumps are categorized as undular (1.0 < Fr_1 < 1.7), weak (1.7 < Fr_1 < 2.5), oscillating (2.5 < Fr_1 < 4.5), steady (4.5 < Fr_1 < 9.0), or strong/choppy (Fr_1 > 9.0). In this laboratory flume experiment, supercritical flow is discharged beneath an adjustable sluice gate, and a downstream tailwater weir forces hydraulic jump stabilization to verify conjugate depths and energy dissipation efficiency.
