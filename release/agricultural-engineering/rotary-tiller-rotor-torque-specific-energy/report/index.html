4. Theory and graph choice

Rotary tillers (rotavators) are active PTO-driven tillage implements widely utilized in seedbed preparation, paddy puddling, and intensive horticulture. Unlike passive draft implements that rely on tractor drawbar pull to slice soil, rotary tillers transmit mechanical engine power directly through the tractor PTO shaft and bevel gearbox to a transverse rotor shaft fitted with curved L-shaped or C-shaped blades. As the tractor advances at forward speed v_f while the rotor rotates with peripheral tip speed u, each blade executes a trochoidal trajectory through the soil, cutting curved slices (trowel bites) of length L_b = 2π·v_f / (z·ω). The forward velocity ratio λ = u / v_f dictates the degree of soil pulverization, tilling bite thickness, and energy consumption. As λ increases, the bite size decreases, producing finer soil tilth at the expense of higher rotor torque and specific tilling energy E_s (energy consumed per unit volume of tilled soil, kJ/m³). In this trial, an instrumented telemetry slip-ring PTO torquemeter measures shaft torque, angular speed, and specific energy across six regulated velocity ratios.

E_s = k_e · λ = 18.2000 · λ

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents Forward velocity ratio λ = u / v_f, and the dependent axis y represents Specific energy E_s (kJ/m³). The gradient m directly determines Specific Energy Slope k_e, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Install the in-line strain-gauge torque sensor onto the tractor 540 RPM PTO spline shaft, zeroing the static tare calibration before attaching the rotavator drive shaft.
  2. Set the rotavator side skid shoes to maintain a uniform 12.0 cm tilling depth, and adjust the rear trailing hood to standard spring pressure.
  3. Engage tractor PTO and adjust engine throttle to deliver exactly 540 RPM PTO speed (rotor shaft speed 220 RPM).
  4. Engage tractor transmission in Gear B-1 (high λ = 4.5, slow forward travel) and log torque, RPM, and speed over a 40-meter run.
  5. Progressively increase tractor forward travel speed across five additional transmission gears (decreasing λ toward 1.5) while keeping PTO RPM constant.
  6. Calculate tilled volume rate V_dot = width × depth × v_f, average shaft power P = T·ω, specific energy E_s = P / V_dot, and evaluate the slope k_e against λ.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Verify PTO safety slip clutch torque setting to ensure immediate disengagement upon striking underground obstructions.
  • Never open or inspect the rear trailing shield while the PTO drive is engaged.
  • Ensure constant tractor engine governor setting to avoid confounding torque drops with speed changes.
  • Check blade bolt torques before and after trials; loose blades cause severe dynamic rotor imbalance.

Linear specific energy model applies to standard down-cut rotation within normal moisture and velocity ratio limits. Evaluated at constant rotor speed of 220 RPM and working depth of 12 cm on sandy clay loam. Forward tractor speed is varied from 1.8 to 5.4 km/h to modulate λ.
