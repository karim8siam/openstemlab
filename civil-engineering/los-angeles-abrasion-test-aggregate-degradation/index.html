FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the Los Angeles (LA) Abrasion Value of coarse mineral aggregates subjected to combined mechanical impact, crushing, and abrasive grinding in the rotating steel drum according to ASTM C131 / AASHTO T96. Standardized parameter settings: 100.0, 180.0, 260.0, 340.0, 420.0, 500.0 revs.

01

Aggregate Washing, Sizing & Oven Drying

Wash crushed basalt aggregate to remove dust; sieve into Grading B fractions (2500 g passing 19.0 mm / retained 12.5 mm, and 2500 g passing 12.5 mm / retained 9.5 mm); oven dry at 110 °C for 16 hours to constant mass M_initial = 5000.0 g.

02

Drum Charging & Steel Sphere Loading

Clean interior of LA machine drum; inspect shelf for wear; place 5000.0 g aggregate charge and exactly 11 steel abrasive spheres into drum; seal dust-tight cover plate.

03

Automated Rotation at Controlled Speed

Set digital counter to target revolutions (100, 180, 260, 340, 420, and 500 revs); run machine at 31 rpm; observe sound of abrasive ball impacts.

04

Sample Discharge & Coarse Separation

Open drum cover; discharge entire mixture into catching pan; separate steel abrasive spheres using magnet; inspect aggregate breakdown.

05

Sieving Over 1.70 mm (No. 12) Sieve

Pass pulverized aggregate over 1.70 mm sieve; wash retained material over the sieve with running tap water until wash water runs clear.

06

Oven Drying Retained Coarse Fraction & Loss Calculation

Oven dry washed aggregate retained on 1.70 mm sieve at 110 °C; weigh dry retained mass M_retained; compute degraded fines mass M_fines = M_initial - M_retained; calculate LA Abrasion Loss percentage.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

In civil highway and pavement engineering, coarse mineral aggregates comprise 90% to 95% of the total mass of asphalt concrete mixtures and up to 80% of Portland cement concrete pavements. During pavement construction (stockpiling, pneumatic mixing, vibratory roller compaction) and throughout service life under heavy pneumatic commercial truck wheel traffic, coarse aggregates are subjected to intense mechanical crushing, edge grinding, and impact degradation. Standardized under ASTM C131 / AASHTO T96, the Los Angeles (LA) Abrasion Test evaluates aggregate mechanical toughness and abrasion resistance. A clean, oven-dried 5000 g aggregate sample of specified gradation (Grading A, B, C, or D) is charged into a hollow steel drum (711 mm internal diameter, 508 mm length) along with an abrasive charge of 6 to 12 cast-iron or steel spheres (approx. 46.8 mm diameter, 390 to 445 g each). As the drum rotates at 30 to 33 rpm, an internal radial shelf catches the aggregates and steel spheres, carrying them upward until they drop 700 mm onto the opposite drum wall, inflicting severe impact crushing combined with mutual abrasive friction. After 500 complete revolutions, the aggregate charge is removed, dry-sieved over a 1.70 mm (No. 12) sieve, and washed to determine the loss percentage: LA Loss (%) = [(M_initial - M_retained) / M_initial] · 100. Highway agency specifications restrict LA loss to ≤ 25% for high-speed wearing courses and ≤ 40% for base courses. In this laboratory experiment, degraded fines mass is monitored across revolution increments from 100 to 500 revs to calibrate degradation rate slope = 2.4500 g/rev (LA loss = 24.5%).
