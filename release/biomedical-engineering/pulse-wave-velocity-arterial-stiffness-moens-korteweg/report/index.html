1. Title and aim

Carotid-Femoral Pulse Wave Velocity & Moens-Korteweg Arterial Stiffness. Determine carotid-femoral pulse wave velocity (cfPWV) across varying mean arterial pressures (MAP) using dual applanation tonometry and evaluate pressure-dependent vascular stiffening.

An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical PWV-Pressure Sensitivity Slope k_p of 0.0450 (m/s)/mmHg (difference from theoretical reference = 0.0000%). 4. Theory and graph choice

In cardiovascular medicine and epidemiology, arterial stiffness is an independent predictor of coronary artery disease, stroke, renal failure, and all-cause cardiovascular mortality. The gold-standard non-invasive clinical metric of central aortic stiffness is carotid-femoral Pulse Wave Velocity (cfPWV). When the left ventricle ejects blood into the ascending aorta, it initiates a mechanical pressure pulse wave that travels down the arterial tree at a speed governed by the elastic properties of the vascular wall and blood density. According to the classical Moens-Korteweg equation and the Bramwell-Hill relationship: PWV = √[E · h / (2 · r · ρ)], where E is the incremental Young's modulus of the arterial wall, h is wall thickness, r is lumen radius, and ρ is blood density. Because the arterial wall is a non-linear viscoelastic biomaterial composed of distensible elastin fibers and stiff collagen fibrils, vascular stiffness increases with transmural distending pressure: at higher blood pressure, collagen fibers are progressively recruited, increasing apparent Young's modulus E. Consequently, empirical clinical data demonstrate that cfPWV scales linearly with mean arterial pressure (MAP): PWV = k_p · MAP + PWV_0, where k_p is the pressure sensitivity coefficient ((m/s)/mmHg). In this laboratory investigation, dual-channel applanation tonometry on an instrumented cardiovascular simulator tracks carotid and femoral pressure foot transit times across varying MAP to determine cfPWV and evaluate k_p.

PWV = k_p · MAP + PWV_0 = 0.0450 · MAP + 2.8000

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Mean arterial pressure MAP (mmHg), and the dependent coordinate y represents Carotid-femoral PWV (m/s). The gradient m directly determines PWV-Pressure Sensitivity Slope k_p, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Measure superficial surface distance from suprasternal notch to carotid site and from suprasternal notch to femoral site; calculate net aortic transit distance Δx = 0.450 m.
  2. Position tonometer probes over the right common carotid artery and right femoral artery, adjusting hold-down pressure until maximum signal amplitude is registered.
  3. Set circulatory simulator mean arterial pressure to 70.0 mmHg and verify stable heart rate (70 BPM) and cardiac output.
  4. Record 10 consecutive pulse wave pairs at 2000 Hz; execute intersecting tangent algorithm to detect foot transit time delay Δt, calculating PWV = Δx / Δt.
  5. Increment circulatory MAP through 80, 90, 100, 110, and 120 mmHg, capturing stabilized cfPWV over 10 cycles at each pressure step.
  6. Plot cfPWV versus MAP and determine the vascular pressure sensitivity slope k_p and baseline velocity PWV_0 via linear regression.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Accurately determine surface transit distance Δx; distance measurement error is the largest contributor to clinical PWV uncertainty.
  • Ensure consistent tonometer probe hold-down pressure to prevent artificial flattening or distortion of pulse wave upstrokes.
  • Verify that ECG R-wave or simultaneous dual-tonometer channels are perfectly synchronized without inter-channel clock skew.
  • Allow 3 minutes of hemodynamic stabilization after altering simulator MAP before logging pulse wave transit times.

The linear PWV-MAP relationship applies over physiological pressure ranges (70 to 120 mmHg); extreme pressures display exponential stiffening.
