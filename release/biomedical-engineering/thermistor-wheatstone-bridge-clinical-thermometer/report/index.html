1. Title and aim

Thermistor Wheatstone-Bridge Clinical Thermometer Calibration. Calibrate a negative temperature coefficient (NTC) bead thermistor in an operational Wheatstone bridge circuit across clinical core body temperatures (35.0 °C to 41.0 °C) and determine bridge sensitivity.

An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Bridge Thermal Sensitivity S_T of 18.5000 mV/°C (difference from theoretical reference = 0.0000%).   • NTC Medical Bead Thermistor Probe: Provides high-sensitivity thermal transduction with fast thermal time constant (< 0.5 s in liquid).
  • Precision Constant-Temperature Water Bath: Maintains calibration temperature stable to within ±0.01 °C with continuous magnetic stirring.
  • NIST-Traceable Reference Standard Thermometer: Monitors calibration water bath temperature with 0.005 °C absolute metrological accuracy.

  • Calibration fluid: Deionized water in circulating stirred bath

4. Theory and graph choice

In medical instrumentation and physiological patient monitoring, accurate measurement of core body temperature is vital for detecting hypothermia, fever, systemic infection, and post-surgical metabolic decompensation. Because human physiological core temperature is confined to a narrow clinical range (typically 35.0 °C to 41.0 °C), clinical electronic thermometers require transducers with exceptional sensitivity, rapid thermal response, and minimal self-heating errors. Negative temperature coefficient (NTC) semiconductor bead thermistors are the dominant sensor technology in esophageal, tympanic, and catheter probes due to their large temperature coefficient of resistance (approximately -4% / °C at 37 °C), described by the Steinhart-Hart and exponential resistance-temperature relation: R(T) = R_0 · exp[β · (1/T - 1/T_0)]. However, this intrinsic exponential non-linearity must be conditioned for linear analog-to-digital conversion. By placing the thermistor in a Wheatstone bridge with matched fixed precision metal-film resistors, the bridge output voltage V_br exhibits high linearity across the narrow ±3.0 °C clinical window around the bridge null point (balanced at 35.0 °C). In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented clinical thermistor probe immersed in a precision circulating water calibration bath is evaluated to quantify bridge output voltage, verify linearization, and evaluate thermal sensitivity S_T.

V_br = S_T · T + V_0 = 18.5000 · T - 647.5000

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Temperature T (°C), and the dependent coordinate y represents Bridge output voltage V_br (mV). The gradient m directly determines Bridge Thermal Sensitivity S_T, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Set the precision water bath to exactly 35.00 °C and trim the Wheatstone bridge balancing potentiometer until differential output voltage reads exactly 0.000 mV.
  2. Measure thermistor current under 5.00 V excitation; verify power dissipation is under 0.2 mW to ensure thermal self-heating elevation remains below 0.01 °C.
  3. Adjust bath setpoint to 36.20 °C and allow 10 minutes for the circulating bath and immersed probe to achieve thermal equilibrium.
  4. Record 10 successive bridge voltage samples over 60 seconds using the 6.5-digit DMM and compute the mean differential voltage V_br in millivolts.
  5. Increment the calibration bath temperature through 37.4, 38.6, 39.8, and 41.0 °C, repeating steady-state voltage logging at each step.
  6. Plot differential bridge output voltage V_br versus temperature T, computing the thermal sensitivity S_T and coefficient of determination R².

Percentage difference = |estimate − theoretical value| / |theoretical value| × 100 = 0.0000%.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Verify water bath circulation avoids localized thermal stratification around the submerged sensor probe.
  • Keep sensor excitation voltage low to avoid self-heating errors exceeding clinical tolerances.
  • Allow full thermal equilibration at each temperature increment before recording bridge telemetry.
  • Shield bridge circuitry and wiring inside an aluminum Faraday enclosure to suppress 60 Hz electromagnetic hum.

The linear sensitivity model applies across the clinical window (35.0 °C to 41.0 °C); wide temperature excursions require polynomial Steinhart-Hart corrections.
