4. Theory and graph choice

Evaporation is a major thermal separation unit operation in chemical processing, pulp and paper, desalination, and sugar manufacturing, responsible for concentrating dilute aqueous solutions by boiling off volatile water solvent. A single-effect tubular evaporator comprises a vertical shell-and-tube calandria where condensing utility steam outside the tubes transfers latent heat across the metal wall into the process liquid boiling inside the tubes. As vapor is generated, rising vapor bubbles expand rapidly, accelerating the liquid upward in climbing-film annular flow or descending as a thin turbulent falling film. Key performance metrics include the water evaporation mass rate m_dot_v, the overall heat transfer coefficient U_evap, and the dimensionless steam economy SE, defined as kilograms of water evaporated per kilogram of heating steam consumed: SE = m_dot_v / m_dot_s. For a single-effect unit without vapor recompression, steam economy is fundamentally limited to less than 1.0 (typically 0.80 to 0.95) due to radiation losses, feed preheating, and boiling point elevation (BPE) caused by non-volatile solutes. Increasing steam jacket pressure P_s elevates steam saturation temperature T_s, widening the thermal driving force (T_s - T_boil) and proportionally accelerating the evaporation rate: m_dot_v = S · P_s. In this experiment, a pilot climbing-film evaporator concentrating a 10 wt% sucrose feed is operated across steam pressures from 0.5 to 3.0 bar to evaluate evaporation rate sensitivity S and measure steam economy.

m_dot_v = S · P_s = 4.8000 · P_s

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Heating steam gauge pressure P_s (bar), and the dependent coordinate y represents Water evaporation rate m_dot_v (kg/h). The gradient m directly determines Evaporator Steam Duty Slope S, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Crack open the steam supply valve to purge non-condensable air and warm the calandria; verify steam trap discharges condensate freely.
  2. Pump 10.0 wt% sucrose feed solution into the bottom calandria tube at 20.0 L/h; adjust liquid level to one-third tube height.
  3. Set steam jacket pressure regulator to P_s = 0.5 bar gauge (T_s = 111.4 °C); observe development of two-phase climbing film.
  4. Allow 15 minutes of steady evaporation; collect overhead distillate condensate m_dot_v and heating steam condensate m_dot_s over a 10-minute interval.
  5. Increment steam gauge pressure through 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, and 3.0 bar, recording distillate production, steam consumption, and liquor Brix at each plateau.
  6. Plot water evaporation rate m_dot_v versus steam pressure P_s, fit a linear regression through the origin, and calculate steam economy SE.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation determines the thermal performance, evaporation capacity, and steam economy of a pilot climbing-film vertical tube evaporator ($A = 0.160\text{ m}^2$) concentrating a $10.0\text{ wt}\%$ aqueous sucrose feed. Across steam jacket gauge pressures $P_s$ from $0.5$ to $3.0\text{ bar}$ ($T_s = 111.4^\circ\text{C}$ to $143.6^\circ\text{C}$), we record distillate water production $\dot{m}_v$ and steam condensate consumption $\dot{m}_s$, evaluate the evaporation sensitivity slope $S = 4.8000\text{ kg/(h}\cdot\text{bar)}$, and calculate steam economy $SE$.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Thermal duty obeys $q = \dot{m}_s \lambda_s = \dot{m}_v \lambda_v + \dot{m}_f c_{p,f} (T_1 - T_f)$. Distillate water evaporation rate is modeled as:

\[ \dot{m}_v = S \cdot P_s \]

where $S = 4.8000\text{ kg/(h}\cdot\text{bar)}$ is the experimental evaporation slope. Steam economy is computed as $SE = \dot{m}_v / \dot{m}_s$, and overall heat transfer coefficient is $U_{evap} = q / [A (T_s - T_1)]$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The unit features a 316 stainless steel calandria tube (ID $25.4\text{ mm}$, OD $28.0\text{ mm}$, length $2.00\text{ m}$, internal heat transfer area $A = 0.160\text{ m}^2$) enclosed in a steam jacket. The top discharges into a tangential cyclone separator connected to a water-cooled tube condenser. Saturated steam is throttled by a pneumatic control valve. Condensate pots on digital load cells ($\pm 0.01\text{ kg}$) log distillate production and steam consumption over timed intervals.

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

The calandria was warmed and non-condensables vented. Sucrose feed ($10.0\text{ wt}\%$, $20.0\text{ L/h}$, $T_f = 90.0^\circ\text{C}$) was introduced. Steam pressure was set to $P_s = 0.5\text{ bar}$ ($T_s = 111.4^\circ\text{C}$). After 15 minutes of steady two-phase climbing-film boiling, distillate condensate and steam condensate were collected over 10 minutes. Steam pressure was stepped through $1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5,$ and $3.0\text{ bar}$.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

High-pressure steam lines ($3.0\text{ bar}$, $144^\circ\text{C}$) present severe thermal burn hazards. Safety relief valves were set at $3.5\text{ bar}$. Thermal insulation covered all steam piping. Face shields, thermal gloves, and safety boots were mandatory.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

Evaporation rate scaled proportionally with steam pressure as the thermal driving force $(T_s - T_1)$ expanded from $10.9\text{ K}$ at $0.5\text{ bar}$ to $43.1\text{ K}$ at $3.0\text{ bar}$. The steam economy of $0.870$ reflects realistic single-effect losses ($13\%$ loss due to sensible feed preheating from $90^\circ\text{C}$ to $100.5^\circ\text{C}$ and ambient radiation).

10. Industrial Scale-Up & Unit Operation Applications

Climbing/falling film evaporators are standard in concentration of fruit juices, sugar syrups, and black liquor in kraft pulp mills. In commercial plants, multiple-effect evaporators (up to 6 effects) or mechanical vapor recompression (MVR) units achieve steam economies exceeding $5.0$.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why does feed preheating improve evaporator steam economy?
A1: If feed enters below its boiling point, a significant portion of condensing steam must supply sensible heat rather than latent heat of vaporization; preheating feed to its boiling point ensures nearly $100\%$ of steam duty generates vapor, maximizing steam economy.

Q2: What is the cause of entrainment loss and how is it prevented?
A2: High vapor velocity atomizes boiling liquid into a fine mist carried into the condenser, contaminating distillate with solute; it is prevented by installing wire-mesh mist eliminators (demisters) or centrifugal cyclones above the calandria.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The single-effect tubular evaporator demonstrated stable climbing-film evaporation. The calibrated performance slope $S = 4.8000\text{ kg/(h}\cdot\text{bar)}$ and measured steam economy $SE = 0.870$ validate thermal design and mass-energy balance principles for process evaporation equipment.
