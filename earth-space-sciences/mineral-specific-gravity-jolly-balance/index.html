FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the specific gravity and density of unidentified crystalline mineral specimens using hydrostatic weighing with a calibrated Jolly helical spring balance. Standardized parameter settings: 5.0, 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 25.0, 30.0 g.

  1. Step 1

    Instrument Initialization & Environmental Baseline Tare

    Power on the analytical instrumentation for Jolly Balance Specific Gravity, calibrate baseline telemetry against certified reference standards, and verify optical/mechanical alignment.

  2. Step 2

    Specimen Preparation & Geological Conditioning

    Prepare, cut, calibrate, or orient the testing specimens under strictly regulated environmental laboratory conditions conforming to USGS and IAU guidelines.

  3. Step 3

    Independent Setpoint Parameter Adjustment

    Adjust the independent testing parameter (Dry Mass) across the designated analytical range (5.0 to 30.0 g) in uniform calibrated steps.

  4. Step 4

    Scientific Telemetry Acquisition & Logging

    Execute the experimental run and record steady-state telemetry for Hydrostatic Buoyant Apparent Mass Loss with high-precision digital integration.

  5. Step 5

    Replicate Inspection & Environmental Equilibrium Check

    Perform duplicate test acquisitions at each testing setpoint to verify signal stability, absence of thermal drift, and sample reproducibility.

  6. Step 6

    Regression Calculation & Geophysical Parameter Derivation

    Plot Dry Mass in Air (g) against Buoyant Mass Loss (g), calculate ordinary least-squares (OLS) linear regression parameters, and derive Reciprocal Mineral Specific Gravity Factor (k_SG) against certified reference benchmarks.
