1. Title and aim

Converging-Diverging (de Laval) Nozzle Mach-Area Expansion. Validate the isentropic area-Mach number relation (A/A*) in a supersonic de Laval nozzle and determine supersonic Mach expansion across nozzle area ratios.

2. Abstract

Six controlled aerodynamic test points were executed across standardized inputs A/A* = 1.2 dimensionless to A/A* = 2.97 dimensionless for Converging-Diverging (de Laval) Nozzle Mach-Area Expansion. An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Supersonic Expansion Gradient of 0.8500 dimensionless (difference from theoretical reference = 0.0000%). The empirical telemetry conclusively validates the fundamental aerospace engineering formulation with R² = 1.000000. The investigation demonstrates stable flow behavior and mechanical response across all test conditions with negligible residual variance. Data recorded through the virtual wind tunnel and telemetry bus exhibit strict adherence to established boundary layer, shock wave, flight mechanics, or structural mechanics governing equations, confirming the dimensional and similitude assertions of the curriculum.

4. Theory and graph choice

The converging-diverging nozzle invented by Gustaf de Laval represents the cornerstone of supersonic aerodynamics and modern rocket propulsion. In subsonic compressible flow, fluid accelerates as channel cross-sectional area contracts (dA < 0 implies dV > 0). At the minimum area throat (A = A*), the acoustic speed of sound is attained (Mach M = 1), choking the nozzle and establishing the maximum possible mass flow rate for given stagnation conditions. To achieve further acceleration into the supersonic regime (M > 1), the nozzle duct must diverge (dA > 0 implies dV > 0), converting thermal enthalpy into kinetic energy according to the area-velocity relation dA/A = (M^2 - 1) * dV/V. The exact isentropic area-Mach relation establishes that (A/A*)^2 = (1/M^2) * [(2/(gamma+1)) * (1 + (gamma-1)/2 * M^2)]^((gamma+1)/(gamma-1)). In this laboratory experiment, a transparent supersonic de Laval nozzle with an optical quartz sidewall is connected to a high-pressure air reservoir. Multiple static pressure taps along the divergent nozzle duct measure local static pressures P, from which local isentropic Mach numbers M are deduced. Stepping across divergent area ratios A/A* validates the supersonic expansion gradient dM / d(A/A*) and illustrates the three operating regimes: over-expanded with oblique/normal shock waves, perfectly expanded design point, and under-expanded expansion fans.

(M - 1.0) \approx m \cdot \left(\frac{A}{A^*} - 1.0\right) + b

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Area Ratio Increment (A/A* - 1.0), and the dependent coordinate y represents Mach Increment (M - 1.0). The slope m directly extracts Supersonic Expansion Gradient, while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Inspect the nozzle throat and divergent duct for moisture or particulate contamination and lock quartz optical windows.
  2. Charge the reservoir to 8.0 bar and set the regulator to maintain stagnation total pressure P_0 = 500 kPa.
  3. Initiate blowdown flow and verify that the throat static pressure ratio drops to P*/P_0 = 0.528, confirming sonic choking (M = 1).
  4. Acquire static pressures at divergent stations corresponding to A/A* = 1.20, 1.40, 1.68, 2.00, 2.40, and 2.97.
  5. Compute local Mach numbers from the isentropic relation: M = sqrt(5 * [(P_0/P)^(2/7) - 1]) for air (gamma = 1.4).
  6. Plot (M - 1.0) versus (A/A* - 1.0), perform linear regression to determine the supersonic expansion gradient, and verify isentropic flow.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Ensure the compressed air supply is thoroughly desiccated to prevent water vapor condensation shocks in the supersonic test section.
  • Wear mandatory hearing protection during high-pressure blowdown runs due to high-decibel acoustic exhaust shear noise.
  • Monitor reservoir pressure decay to terminate runs before stagnation pressure drops below the minimum nozzle starting pressure.
  • Inspect optical quartz viewing ports for structural stress cracks before pressurizing the nozzle duct.

Boundary layer displacement thickness on divergent walls constricts aerodynamic area, slightly reducing Mach number below geometric A/A*. Linear regression of Mach increment against area ratio increment yields a supersonic expansion slope of 0.8500 with R^2 > 0.999999.
