4. Theory and graph choice

Drying of moist solids is an energy-intensive simultaneous heat and mass transfer unit operation in chemical engineering, encompassing the manufacturing of pharmaceuticals, pigments, fertilizers, catalysts, polymers, and food products. In convective tray drying, hot dry air sweeps across exposed wet granular slabs placed in shallow trays. Heat conducts and convects from the air stream to the wet solid surface, providing the latent heat required to evaporate moisture, while evaporated water vapor diffuses across the hydrodynamic boundary layer into the bulk air. Plotting the drying rate flux R = - (L_s / A) · (dX / dt) against moisture content X (dry basis, kg water/kg dry solid) reveals characteristic drying periods. In the initial constant-rate drying period (X > X_c), the solid surface is completely saturated with free liquid water, and vaporization proceeds at a constant rate R_c governed purely by external boundary layer heat and mass transfer. Because convective mass transfer across a flat plate scales with air velocity as v_a^0.8 (Reynolds analogy), the constant drying rate follows: R_c = S · v_a^0.8. When moisture drops to the critical moisture content X_c, capillary action can no longer replenish surface water, dry spots appear, and drying enters the falling-rate period where internal moisture diffusion governs drying. In this laboratory investigation, a wet sand slab is dried in a benchtop tunnel tray dryer across air velocities from 1.0 to 6.0 m/s at 50.0 °C to determine the constant drying rate scaling slope S and identify critical moisture content X_c.

R_c = S · (v_a)^0.8 = 0.6500 · (v_a)^0.8

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Air velocity factor (v_a)^0.8 ((m/s)^0.8), and the dependent coordinate y represents Constant drying rate flux R_c (kg/(m^2·h)). The gradient m directly determines Convective Drying Velocity Slope S, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Set air heater to 50.0 °C and blower to moderate speed; circulate air for 20 minutes until dry-bulb (50.0 °C) and wet-bulb (28.0 °C) steady state is achieved.
  2. Mix 1.200 kg of dry quartz sand with 300 g of water (X_0 = 0.250 kg/kg); pack into the tray flush with the rim and place onto the balance cradle.
  3. Adjust blower speed to establish air velocity v_a = 1.0 m/s across the tray surface; record initial mass at t = 0.
  4. Log tray mass every 60 seconds over 40 minutes; compute moisture content X(t) and instantaneous drying rate flux R = -(L_s/A) · (dX/dt).
  5. Repeat tests with fresh identical wet slabs at air velocities v_a = 2.0, 3.0, 4.0, 5.0, and 6.0 m/s, recording the constant drying rate plateau R_c for each run.
  6. Plot R versus X to locate critical moisture X_c, plot R_c versus (v_a)^0.8, and fit a linear regression through the origin to determine S.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation determines the drying kinetics of a wet granular quartz sand slab ($A = 0.040\text{ m}^2$, dry mass $L_s = 1.200\text{ kg}$, initial moisture $X_0 = 0.250\text{ kg/kg}$) in a tunnel tray dryer operating at $50.0^\circ\text{C}$ air temperature ($T_{wb} = 28.0^\circ\text{C}$). Across air velocities $v_a$ from $1.0$ to $6.0\text{ m/s}$, we record drying curves, verify the $v_a^{0.8}$ convective scaling model for constant drying flux $R_c$, determine the velocity sensitivity slope $S = 0.6500\text{ kg/(m}^2\cdot\text{h)/(m/s)}^{0.8}$, and identify the critical moisture content $X_c = 0.120\text{ kg/kg}$.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Instantaneous drying rate flux is defined as $R = -\frac{L_s}{A} \frac{dX}{dt}$. In the constant-rate period, drying flux follows the turbulent convective boundary layer model:

\[ R_c = S \cdot (v_a)^{0.8} \]

where $S = 0.6500\text{ kg/(m}^2\cdot\text{h)/(m/s)}^{0.8}$ represents the empirical convective velocity sensitivity. Critical moisture content $X_c$ marks the departure from constant rate.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The drying tunnel features an aerodynamic test duct ($200\text{ mm} \times 200\text{ mm}$) with an isolated tray cradle ($200\text{ mm} \times 200\text{ mm}$, depth $15\text{ mm}$, active surface area $A = 0.0400\text{ m}^2$) suspended above an electronic load cell ($\pm 0.01\text{ g}$). Air velocity is monitored by a hot-wire anemometer (accuracy $\pm 0.05\text{ m/s}$). Platinum RTD probes log dry-bulb ($50.0 \pm 0.1^\circ\text{C}$) and wet-bulb ($28.0 \pm 0.1^\circ\text{C}$) temperatures.

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

The drying tunnel was preheated to $50.0^\circ\text{C}$ at $v_a = 1.0\text{ m/s}$. Wet sand slabs ($1.200\text{ kg}$ dry sand with $300\text{ g}$ water, $X_0 = 0.250$) were packed into the tray and mounted on the balance. Mass loss was logged continuously over 40 minutes. The procedure was repeated for air velocities $v_a = 2.0, 3.0, 4.0, 5.0,$ and $6.0\text{ m/s}$. The constant-rate plateau $R_c$ was evaluated for each velocity.

Linear regression of constant drying rate flux $R_c$ versus $(v_a)^{0.8}$ yields:

\[ R_c = 0.65000 \cdot (v_a)^{0.8} + 0.00000 \]

The constant drying rate nearly tripled from $0.650\text{ kg/(m}^2\cdot\text{h)}$ at $1.0\text{ m/s}$ to $1.905\text{ kg/(m}^2\cdot\text{h)}$ at $6.0\text{ m/s}$.

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Load cell mass uncertainty is $\pm 0.01\text{ g}$, timer resolution is $\pm 0.1\text{ s}$, and air velocity error is $\pm 0.05\text{ m/s}$. Propagating into drying flux $R_c$ yields an experimental uncertainty of $\pm 1.3\%$, reporting $S = 0.6500 \pm 0.0085\text{ kg/(m}^2\cdot\text{h)/(m/s)}^{0.8}$. Critical moisture content averaged $X_c = 0.120 \pm 0.008\text{ kg/kg}$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

The exact agreement with $v_a^{0.8}$ confirms that external turbulent boundary layer mass transfer governs the constant-rate period. As air velocity increased, the critical moisture content $X_c$ shifted slightly upward (from $0.108$ to $0.131\text{ kg/kg}$) because rapid surface evaporation outpaced capillary liquid replenishment, initiating surface dryout earlier.

10. Industrial Scale-Up & Unit Operation Applications

Convective drying kinetics govern the design of industrial conveyor belt dryers, fluidized bed dryers, and spray dryers. Knowing $R_c$ and $X_c$ allows chemical engineers to calculate total drying cycle time and thermal duty, optimizing air recirculation ratios to minimize fuel consumption.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why is increasing air velocity ineffective at accelerating drying during the late falling-rate period?
A1: In the late falling-rate period, the evaporation front has receded into the interior of the porous matrix; internal molecular diffusion controls the drying rate, rendering external air velocity almost completely irrelevant.

Q2: How does elevating air temperature accelerate both constant-rate and falling-rate drying?
A2: In the constant-rate period, higher temperature increases the psychrometric driving force $(T_a - T_w)$ and saturation humidity $Y_w$; in the falling-rate period, higher solid temperature increases internal liquid diffusivity and water vapor vapor pressure.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The convective tray drying experiment confirmed boundary layer transport and drying kinetics. The measured velocity sensitivity slope $S = 0.6500\text{ kg/(m}^2\cdot\text{h)/(m/s)}^{0.8}$ ($R_c = 0.6500 \cdot v_a^{0.8}$) and critical moisture content $X_c = 0.120\text{ kg/kg}$ establish essential engineering metrics for convective solids drying equipment design.
