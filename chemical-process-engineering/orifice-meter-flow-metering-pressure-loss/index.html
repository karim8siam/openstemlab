FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Calibrate a concentric sharp-edged orifice plate flowmeter in a closed-loop pipe network, measure differential pressure across flange taps vs volumetric flow rate, and evaluate discharge coefficient C_d and permanent pressure loss. Standardized parameter settings: 8.0, 16.0, 24.0, 32.0, 40.0, 48.0 L/min.

01

Zero Calibration & Line Bleeding

Isolate the DP transmitter manifold, open the equalizing valve to verify zero differential reading (0.00 kPa), and bleed air from pressure tapping impulse lines.

02

Initial Low-Flow Stabilization

Crack open the pump throttling valve until reference flow rate Q reads 8.0 L/min; allow flow and digital pressure readings to stabilize for 60 seconds.

03

Flange Differential Pressure Logging

Record differential pressure ΔP across flange taps in kilopascals, alongside reference flow rate and liquid temperature.

04

Flow Rate Increment Protocol

Advance flow rate in uniform increments through 16.0, 24.0, 32.0, 40.0, and 48.0 L/min, recording steady-state differential pressure at each step.

05

Permanent Head Loss Determination

Measure downstream static wall pressure at tap station 8D downstream of the plate to compute permanent unrecovered pressure loss ΔP_perm.

06

Calibration Model Regression

Calculate sqrt(ΔP) for each run, plot sqrt(ΔP) versus Q, and fit a linear regression through the origin to evaluate calibration sensitivity S and discharge coefficient C_d.

THEORY AND UNIT OPERATIONS CONTEXT

Process engineering background & physical principles.

In chemical process manufacturing, square-edged concentric orifice meters are the most prevalent differential pressure primary elements owing to their mechanical simplicity, rigorous standardization (ISO 5167 / ASME MFC-3M), and low manufacturing cost. An orifice plate consists of a thin metal disc with a precision sharp upstream edge installed perpendicularly across a conduit. As fluid approaches the restriction, streamlines converge beyond the physical orifice opening to form a minimum cross-sectional area termed the vena contracta, where flow velocity peaks and static wall pressure plummets to a minimum. Unlike the guided contour of a Venturi meter, the jet exiting the orifice expands abruptly into the downstream pipe, generating intense turbulent recirculating eddies that dissipate a substantial fraction of kinetic energy into irreversible viscous thermal friction. Consequently, the discharge coefficient C_d of a sharp-edged orifice plate is significantly lower (typically 0.60 to 0.62), and permanent pressure loss accounts for 60% to 75% of measured differential head. In this experiment, flange tapping pressure differences are monitored across an orifice plate to establish calibration sensitivity S, verify square-root scaling, and calculate discharge coefficient C_d.
