1. Title and aim

Surface Plasmon Resonance (SPR) Biosensor Angle Shift & Biomolecular Affinity. Quantify the resonance angle shift of a gold-film SPR immunosensor as a function of analyte protein concentration to determine association kinetics and Langmuir adsorption linearity.

4. Theory and graph choice

In modern pharmacology, biomarker discovery, and clinical immunology, Surface Plasmon Resonance (SPR) is the gold-standard label-free optical biosensing technology for measuring real-time macromolecular binding kinetics (antibody-antigen affinity, protein-protein interactions, and DNA hybridization). Under the Kretschmann prism-coupling optical configuration, p-polarized monochromatic laser light undergoes Total Internal Reflection (TIR) at the interface of a high-refractive-index glass prism coated with a 50 nm thin gold film. At a critical resonance angle θ_spr, photon energy couples into collective oscillations of free conduction-band electrons at the gold-dielectric interface, generating non-radiative surface plasmon polaritons that propagate along the metal surface. This resonance produces a sharp, localized intensity dip in the reflected light spectrum. The evanescent electromagnetic field decays exponentially into the sample solution (penetration depth ~200 nm), making the resonance angle exquisitely sensitive to minute refractive index changes in the immediate dielectric vicinity. When target antigen proteins bind specifically to immobilized antibodies on the sensor chip surface, the local mass density and refractive index increase, causing the SPR angle dip to shift by Δθ. In the sub-saturation Langmuir binding regime, angle shift Δθ is strictly linear with analyte concentration: Δθ = k_spr · C_ag. In this experiment, a functionalized anti-IgG gold sensor chip is tested across nano-molar antigen concentrations.

Δθ = k_spr · C_ag = 8.4000 · C_ag

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Antigen concentration C_ag (nM), and the dependent coordinate y represents Resonance angle shift Δθ (millidegrees). The gradient m directly determines SPR Refractive Sensitivity k_spr, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Dock the functionalized CM5 gold chip into the optical block and prime flow channels with HBS-EP running buffer at 30 μL/min until baseline drift is < 0.2 mdeg/min.
  2. Configure Channel 1 as non-specific reference (mock-immobilized) and Channel 2 as active antibody surface for inline differential subtraction.
  3. Inject 5.0 nM human IgG antigen for 180 seconds, allowing binding association to reach equilibrium plateau; log net resonance angle shift Δθ in millidegrees.
  4. Inject a 30-second pulse of 10 mM glycine-HCl (pH 2.0) to dissociate bound antigen and regenerate baseline antibody binding capacity.
  5. Sequentially inject antigen standards of 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 25.0, and 30.0 nM, executing regeneration and baseline re-referencing at each step.
  6. Plot steady-state angle shift Δθ against concentration C_ag, performing linear regression to evaluate SPR refractive sensitivity k_spr.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Degas all running buffers thoroughly; micro-bubbles in the flowcell create massive optical scattering spikes.
  • Maintain strictly constant flow rate (30 μL/min) to prevent mass-transport-limited binding artifacts.
  • Control optical deck temperature within ±0.01 °C; refractive index is highly sensitive to temperature.
  • Ensure regeneration pulses are brief (30 s) to avoid irreversible antibody denaturation.
