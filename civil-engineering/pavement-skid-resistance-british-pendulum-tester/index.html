FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine British Pendulum Number (BPN) and skid resistance of wet road surfaces and highway pavement wearing courses according to ASTM E303 / BS EN 13036-4. Standardized parameter settings: 20.0, 26.0, 32.0, 38.0, 44.0, 50.0 units.

01

Apparatus Leveling & Zero Free Swing Calibration

Set British Pendulum on level test floor; adjust three leveling foot screws until circular bubble is centered; raise pendulum arm to horizontal latch; release arm to swing freely in air; verify drag pointer registers exactly zero on the BPN scale.

02

Rubber Slider Conditioning & Inspection

Inspect rubber slider edge for rounded wear; condition by making 10 swings over dry 400-grit silicon carbide paper to establish uniform contact.

03

Sliding Contact Length Adjustment (126 mm)

Lower apparatus column using rack-and-pinion until the rubber slider lightly touches the surface; slide gauge between contact points; adjust height until contact path length is exactly 126 ± 1 mm.

04

Pavement Surface Flooding

Spray generous amount of clean water to completely flood the 126 mm contact zone, ensuring full hydrodynamic boundary lubrication.

05

Pendulum Swing Release & Pointer Reading

Latch pendulum arm horizontally; return drag pointer to zero stop; depress release trigger cleanly; catch arm on return backswing; record pointer position as BPN reading.

06

Five-Swing Sequence & Temperature Correction

Execute five consecutive swings, re-wetting the surface between swings; discard the first swing; average swings 2 through 5; record surface temperature; apply ASTM temperature correction.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

In civil transportation highway safety engineering, wet-weather skidding crashes account for thousands of vehicular fatalities annually. When rain falls on a roadway, water forms an intervening lubricating fluid film between pneumatic rubber tires and the pavement surface, dramatically reducing frictional traction and initiating hydroplaning. Pavement friction is governed by two geometric scales: (1) Macrotexture (wavelengths 0.5 to 50 mm): large voids between coarse aggregate particles that provide drainage escape channels for bulk water; and (2) Microtexture (wavelengths < 0.5 mm, sub-millimeter asperities): microscopic harshness on individual aggregate particle faces that penetrates the remaining thin water film to establish direct molecular contact with the tire rubber. Developed by the UK Road Research Laboratory and standardized under ASTM E303 / BS EN 13036-4, the British Pendulum Tester is the worldwide standard dynamic pendulum apparatus measuring low-speed pavement surface microtexture and wet skid resistance. A spring-loaded rubber slider (mounted on a counterbalanced pendulum arm) is released from a horizontal fixed position to swing freely across a wetted pavement surface. Over a strictly calibrated sliding contact path length of 124 to 127 mm, friction dissipates kinetic energy from the pendulum, reducing its upward overswing. A friction pointer registers the maximum swing height on a calibrated circular scale directly reading the British Pendulum Number (BPN, or PTV). A higher BPN indicates superior wet microtexture and skid resistance (BPN > 65 required for high-risk crash locations such as roundabouts and pedestrian crossings; BPN 45 to 55 for general highway running lanes; BPN < 40 considered polished and dangerously slick). In this laboratory experiment, BPN is evaluated across calibrated aggregate microtextures from T_x = 20.0 to 50.0 units to calibrate the friction sensitivity slope = 1.2500 BPN/unit (baseline polished surface BPN = 47.0 at T_x = 20.0).
