Agricultural and physical foundations of Paddy Rubber-Roll Husking.

Paddy rice milling converts raw harvested paddy (rough rice) into polished white rice for human consumption through two sequential mechanical unit operations: dehusking (removal of the fibrous protective outer hull to produce brown rice) and whitening/polishing (removal of the bran layer and germ). The rubber-roll husker is the global industry standard machine for paddy dehusking, offering superior hulling efficiency with minimal grain breakage compared to obsolete steel hullers or emery stone discs. The machine consists of two resilient vulcanized rubber rollers rotating in opposite directions at different peripheral velocities (typically a fast roller at 10 to 12 m/s and a slow roller at 8 to 9 m/s, yielding a differential velocity ratio of 1:1.20 to 1:1.30). As paddy kernels enter the narrow clearance between the rollers, the slower roller retards one side of the husk while the faster roller strips the opposite side, exerting a pure shearing force that peels off the loose hull without crushing the fragile starch endosperm. If the speed differential Δv = v_fast - v_slow is too low, unhusked paddy passes through, lowering efficiency. If Δv is too high, excessive friction generates heat, wears the expensive rubber rolls rapidly, and fractures kernels, depressing Head Rice Yield (HRY). In this laboratory, an instrumented pilot rubber-roll husker calibrates hulling metrics across varying differential speeds.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

How does a rubber-roll husker accomplish paddy dehusking?

Paddy is fed into the nip between two rubber rollers rotating in opposite directions at different speeds. The slow roll grips and holds one side of the husk while the faster roll shears the opposite side, creating a mechanical shear couple that peels off the hull without crushing the kernel.

Define Head Rice Yield (HRY) and explain its commercial significance.

Head Rice Yield is the percentage by weight of rough paddy rice that is recovered as whole unbroken milled kernels (kernels that are at least three-fourths of their original length). Broken rice sells at a 50% discount; thus, maximizing HRY is the primary economic objective in rice milling.

What is the typical speed ratio between the fast and slow rolls in a commercial rubber-roll husker?

The standard commercial speed ratio is approximately 1:1.20 to 1:1.30 (fast roller rotates 20% to 30% faster than the slow roller), providing optimal shearing force without excessive frictional heating.

Why do rubber rollers wear out, and why must their positions be periodically reversed?

The faster roller does more abrasive shearing work and wears down faster than the slow roller. Reversing the positions of the two rollers (swapping fast and slow drives) balances wear and extends total rubber roll service life.

What causes rice kernel breakage during dehusking?

Kernel breakage is caused by excessive roller pressure (clearance too tight), excessively high speed differential, pre-existing internal sun-cracks or drying stress fissures in the paddy, and thermal shock from overheated rubber rolls.

What is the function of the aspirator immediately following the rubber-roll husker?

The aspirator uses an upward airstream (velocity approx. 4 to 5 m/s) to lift and blow away the lightweight, bulky hulls (which constitute about 20% of paddy weight), leaving clean brown rice and unhusked paddy for subsequent separation.

Experimental precautions and agricultural field safety

  • Never operate rubber rolls without paddy feeding; contact between rotating rubber faces generates extreme friction that burns and melts the rubber within seconds.
  • Ensure paddy is free from stones, gravel, and metal by passing through a pre-cleaner destoner prior to husking.
  • Check roller parallelism across the entire width using feeler gauges; tapering clearance causes uneven hulling and localized roller grooving.
  • Maintain uniform paddy feed across the full 50 mm roll width to ensure uniform wear.

Theoretical assumptions & biological limits

  • Rubber rolls soften as operational temperature rises (> 60°C), slightly shifting the effective friction coefficient and hulling efficiency during prolonged continuous runs.
  • Requires paddy moisture strictly between 12.5% and 14.0%; wet paddy (> 15%) deforms without dehusking, while over-dry paddy (< 11%) shatters into brokens.
  • Evaluates single-pass dehusking; commercial mills recirculate unhusked paddy back through a secondary return husker.
  • Rubber durometer hardness degrades with storage age and UV exposure.
