Determination of Apparent and Partial Molar Volumes in Binary Ethanol-Water Mixtures via Precision Pycnometry

To determine the density of a series of binary ethanol-water mixtures using high-precision Gay-Lussac pycnometry at 25.0 °C, compute the apparent molar volume (\phi_V) of ethanol as a function of molality, and evaluate the limiting partial molar volume of ethanol at infinite dilution (\bar{V}_2^\infty) and excess thermodynamic properties via linearized extrapolation. Physical chemistry assumptions and metrological limits

Gay-Lussac pycnometry assumes that ambient room temperature does not diverge significantly from the bath setpoint (25.00 °C) during the brief weighing period, minimizing capillary expansion overflow. At higher ethanol concentrations (> 3 mol/kg or > 15 wt%), non-linear higher-order solute-solute clustering effects require quadratic terms in the molality expansion. The physical simulation calculates binary solution densities and apparent molar volumes at 25.00 °C governed by φ_V = (1/m) · [ (1000 + m·M₂)/ρ − 1000/ρ₀ ], where solvent is Millipore water (M₁ = 18.015 g/mol, ρ₀ = 0.997047 g/cm³) and solute is anhydrous ethanol (M₂ = 46.069 g/mol, pure molar volume V₂* = 58.68 cm³/mol). In the dilute regime (m = 0.50 to 3.00 mol/kg), non-electrolyte hydrophobic hydration dictates linear apparent volume expansion: φ_V(m) = φ_V° + b_V·m, with limiting partial molar volume at infinite dilution φ_V° = V̄₂^∞ = 54.20 cm³/mol and slope b_V = +0.250 cm³·kg·mol⁻². For a calibrated 25.000 cm³ Gay-Lussac pycnometer with dry tare mass m₀ = 18.4250 g, filled pycnometer masses span 43.2529 g (m = 0.50 mol/kg, ρ = 0.99312 g/cm³) down to 42.7912 g (m = 3.00 mol/kg, ρ = 0.97465 g/cm³). Ordinary least-squares linear regression extracts the intercept b = 54.20 cm³/mol with R² = 0.99998, validating limiting partial molar volume without bias and demonstrating volumetric contraction of 4.48 cm³/mol due to interstitial cavity accommodation.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

To determine the density of a series of binary ethanol-water mixtures using high-precision Gay-Lussac pycnometry at 25.0 °C, compute the apparent molar volume (\phi_V) of ethanol as a function of molality, and evaluate the limiting partial molar volume of ethanol at infinite dilution (\bar{V}_2^\infty) and excess thermodynamic properties via linearized extrapolation. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0 mol/kg.

01

Pycnometer Cleaning, Drying, and Tare Weighing

Thoroughly clean the 25 mL Gay-Lussac pycnometer with chromic acid substitute, rinse repeatedly with deionized water and acetone, dry with warm filtered nitrogen, and record the dry tare mass m₀ on the analytical balance to ±0.0001 g.

02

Pycnometer Volume Calibration with Pure Water

Fill pycnometer with freshly boiled, degassed deionized water, seat capillary stopper allowing excess to overflow, equilibrate in the 25.00 °C bath for 20 minutes, wipe dry, weigh (m_water), and calculate exact internal volume V_pyc = (m_water - m₀) / ρ₀.

03

Gravimetric Preparation of Ethanol-Water Standards

Prepare six binary solutions of exact ethanol molalities m = 0.50, 1.00, 1.50, 2.00, 2.50, and 3.00 mol/kg solvent by weighing degassed water into flasks followed by anhydrous ethanol, sealing promptly to prevent volatile evaporation.

04

Pycnometer Loading and Constant-Temperature Equilibration

Rinse pycnometer twice with 3 mL aliquots of the test solution, fill to the brim, insert capillary stopper firmly, and immerse in the 25.00 °C water bath for 20 minutes to reach thermal and volumetric equilibrium.

05

Meniscus Adjustment, Exterior Drying, and Analytical Weighing

Carefully remove pycnometer from bath, swiftly touch capillary apex with lint-free paper to bring meniscus flush with the ground rim without capillary draw, wipe exterior dry, and record mass m_soln on the analytical balance to ±0.0001 g.

06

Repetition, Density Computation, and Apparent Molar Volume Calculation

Repeat duplicate trials for each concentration, compute solution density ρ = (m_soln - m₀) / V_pyc, determine apparent molar volume φ_V = [M₂/ρ − 1000(ρ − ρ₀)/(m·ρ·ρ₀)], and perform linear regression to obtain limiting partial molar volume.
