1. Title and aim

Rainfall Simulator Surface Runoff & Sediment Yield (Universal Soil Loss Equation). Measure surface runoff volume and soil sediment detachment under controlled artificial rainfall and calibrate the soil erodibility factor (K) in the Universal Soil Loss Equation (USLE).

2. Abstract

Six controlled agricultural engineering observations were recorded across standardized settings Erosivity EI/100 = 1.0 index/100 to Erosivity EI/100 = 4.5 index/100 for Rainfall Simulator Surface Runoff & Sediment Yield (Universal Soil Loss Equation). An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Soil Erodibility Scaling Factor K_usle of 12.5000 kg/(ha·index) (percentage difference from theoretical benchmark = 0.0000%). The empirical telemetry conclusively validates the governing agricultural engineering formulation with R² = 1.000000. The investigation demonstrates stable physical response, mechanical equilibrium, and reliable operational performance across all test conditions with negligible residual variance. 3. Apparatus and fixed settings

  • Laboratory Oscillating Nozzle Rainfall Simulator: Applies realistic drop size distribution and terminal impact velocity across the flume.
  • Tilting Soil Erosion Flume (1.0 m × 2.0 m): Heavy-duty steel soil bed with porous drainage floor and adjustable slope from 0% to 20%.
  • Runoff & Sediment Collection Flume End-Trough: Captures all overland runoff water and detached soil sediment continuously.
  • Electronic Runoff Hydrograph Weighing Logger: Measures cumulative runoff mass at 5-second intervals to plot the hydrograph.
  • Suspended Sediment Centrifuge & Convection Oven: Separates suspended sediment from runoff aliquots and dries soil at 105°C.
  • Optical Disdrometer Raindrop Sensor: Measures droplet size spectrum and kinetic impact energy E with 0.1 mm precision.
  • Flume dimensions: 1.0 m × 2.0 m
  • Bed slope: 9.0% (standard USLE)
  • Soil: Bare tilled sandy clay loam

4. Theory and graph choice

Soil erosion by water degrades agricultural soil productivity, depletes topsoil organic matter, and causes severe downstream reservoir sedimentation and water pollution. The Universal Soil Loss Equation (USLE) and its revised form (RUSLE), developed by Wischmeier and Smith, is the international standard empirical model for estimating long-term average annual soil loss: A = R·K·LS·C·P, where A is soil loss, R is the rainfall-runoff erosivity factor, K is the soil erodibility factor, LS is the slope length-steepness topographic factor, C is the cover-management factor, and P is the support practice conservation factor. Rainfall erosivity R is quantified by the product of total storm kinetic energy E and maximum 30-minute rainfall intensity I_30 (the EI_30 index). High-energy raindrop impact detaches soil aggregates by splash detachment, while excess rainfall exceeding the infiltration capacity generates overland sheet and rill runoff that transports suspended sediment. In this laboratory investigation, an oscillating nozzle rainfall simulator applies controlled storm intensities across a standard 1.0 m × 2.0 m tilted soil erosion flume to quantify runoff hydrographs, sediment discharge, and soil erodibility K.

A = K_usle · (EI_30 / 100) = 12.5000 · (EI_30 / 100)

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents Rainfall erosivity index EI_30 / 100, and the dependent axis y represents Sediment yield A (kg/ha). The gradient m directly determines Soil Erodibility Scaling Factor K_usle, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Pack the erosion flume with sieved agricultural soil in 5 cm lifts to achieve uniform bulk density (1.30 g/cm³), setting flume tilt to exactly 9% standard slope.
  2. Pre-wet the soil bed with fine mist for 15 minutes to bring topsoil to consistent field capacity and eliminate antecedent moisture variability.
  3. Set oscillating nozzle frequency and water pressure to deliver rainfall intensity I = 35 mm/h (corresponding to EI_30 / 100 = 1.00).
  4. Engage rainfall for exactly 30 minutes, recording the time to runoff initiation and collecting 1-liter runoff-sediment samples every 5 minutes from the collection trough.
  5. Flocculate and centrifuge runoff samples, decant clear water, and dry sediment in the 105°C oven to compute total sediment yield A (kg/ha).
  6. Increment rainfall erosivity across five additional levels (EI_30 / 100 = 1.7, 2.4, 3.1, 3.8, 4.5), plot sediment yield A versus erosivity, and extract the erodibility slope K_usle.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Calibrate nozzle oscillation and pressure before test runs to ensure uniform spatial rainfall coverage across the entire soil bed.
  • Ensure runoff collection trough is completely cleared of settled sediment between consecutive storm trials.
  • Maintain flume side-wall seals tight; gaps along walls create artificial rills that skew sediment yield.
  • Oven-dry sediment samples for a full 24 hours at 105°C to ensure 100% moisture removal before gravimetric weighing.

USLE predicts average sheet and rill erosion; it does not model gully erosion, stream bank collapse, or mass landslides. Evaluated using bare tilled sandy clay loam soil packed in a 9% slope erosion flume (LS = 1.0, C = 1.0, P = 1.0) under simulated rainfall intensities from 35 to 110 mm/h.
