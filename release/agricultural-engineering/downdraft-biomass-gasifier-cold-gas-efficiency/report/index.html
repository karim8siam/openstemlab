4. Theory and graph choice

Agricultural residues such as corncobs, rice husks, wood chips, and crop stalks represent abundant lignocellulosic feedstocks for thermochemical bioenergy conversion. Biomass gasification converts solid organic residues into a combustible gaseous fuel mixture known as producer gas or synthesis gas (syngas), primarily comprising carbon monoxide (CO), hydrogen (H2), methane (CH4), carbon dioxide (CO2), and nitrogen (N2). In small to medium decentralized agricultural power systems, downdraft (co-current) throat gasifiers are preferred because tars and pyrolysis vapors pass through a high-temperature incandescent char bed (900 °C to 1100 °C) where they are cracked into non-condensable fuel gases. The primary operational control parameter governing gasifier performance is the equivalence ratio Φ, defined as the actual air-to-fuel ratio divided by the stoichiometric air-to-fuel ratio required for complete combustion. In autothermal gasification, increasing Φ supplies more oxygen, which accelerates exothermic partial oxidation and raises bed temperature, but simultaneously promotes complete combustion of valuable H2 and CO into CO2 and H2O, thereby diluting the gas higher heating value (HHV_g). In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented 20 kW_th downdraft gasifier fed with dried corncob chunks is operated across controlled equivalence ratios to analyze syngas composition, higher heating value, and cold gas conversion efficiency.

HHV_g = -k_Φ · Φ + HHV_0 = -12.5000 · Φ + 8.8000

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent axis x represents Equivalence ratio Φ (ratio), and the dependent axis y represents Syngas HHV (MJ/Nm³). The gradient m directly determines Gas Heating Equivalence Sensitivity k_Φ, while the intercept b accounts for zero-load offsets, baseline tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence failure. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays soil resistance gradients, flow exponents, drying kinetics, or power conversion efficiencies directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential soil shear failure, flow cavitation, or saturation limits.

5. Method

  1. Fill the reactor hopper with 25.0 kg of dried corncob chunks (size 25-50 mm, moisture 11.2%) and latch the airtight feed lid.
  2. Ignite the charcoal bed at the tuyeres with an LPG torch, drawing primary air via the suction blower until throat temperature exceeds 800 °C.
  3. Adjust blast air flow rate to establish the initial test equivalence ratio Φ = 0.20 based on measured biomass consumption rate.
  4. Operate the gasifier for 30 minutes at steady state, monitoring throat temperature (950-1050 °C) and syngas flare flame stability.
  5. Sample clean, conditioned syngas through the continuous infrared/thermal conductivity gas analyzer and log volumetric flow rate.
  6. Step the equivalence ratio through 0.24, 0.28, 0.32, 0.36, and 0.40, computing producer gas HHV = Σ (y_i · HHV_i) and fitting linear regression.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Verify airtight sealing of all gasifier flanges; producer gas contains deadly odorless carbon monoxide (CO).
  • Ensure continuous operation of the syngas flare to prevent unburned combustible gas accumulation.
  • Maintain biomass fuel moisture strictly below 15% to prevent excessive throat temperature suppression.
  • Equip the laboratory with calibrated toxic gas monitors (CO and H2S alarms) and positive room ventilation.
