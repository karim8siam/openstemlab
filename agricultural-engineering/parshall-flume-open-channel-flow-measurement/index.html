Agricultural assumptions and boundary condition limits

Flumes must be fabricated strictly to standardized ASTM/USBR dimensions; geometric scaling errors invalidate published discharge coefficients. Evaluated using a standard 6-inch (15.2 cm) throat width Parshall flume in free-flow regime across upstream heads from 0.05 m to 0.40 m.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Calibrate a standard Parshall flume in an open irrigation channel, determine the free-flow power-law discharge rating equation, and evaluate the critical submergence limit. Standardized parameter settings: -2.9957, -2.5257, -2.1203, -1.7148, -1.3093, -0.9163 ln(m).

01

Flume Leveling & Zero Setting

Level the Parshall flume longitudinally and laterally in the channel. Set the stilling well point gauges to exactly zero against the flat crest of the flume converging floor.

02

Free-Flow Calibration Setup

Lower the downstream tailwater gate completely to ensure free-fall discharge, ensuring the submergence ratio H_b / H_a remains well below 0.60.

03

Initial Low-Flow Stabilization

Regulate pump throttle to deliver initial low discharge producing an upstream head H_a = 0.05 m (ln H_a = -2.9957), allowing water levels in the stilling wells to stabilize.

04

Synchronous Discharge Logging

Record the upstream head H_a from the stilling well and the reference discharge Q from the magnetic flowmeter simultaneously over 3 minutes.

05

Head Range Stepping

Increment pump discharge in five uniform stages to achieve upstream heads of 0.08, 0.12, 0.18, 0.27, and 0.40 m, recording H_a and Q for each flow rate.

06

Power-Law Logarithmic Regression

Transform H_a and Q into natural logarithms, plot ln Q versus ln H_a, and perform linear regression to determine the exponent n and coefficient C.
