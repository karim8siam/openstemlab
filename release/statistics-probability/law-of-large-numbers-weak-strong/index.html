Statistical assumptions and distribution limits

The LLN guarantees asymptotic convergence as N → ∞ but provides no error bounds for any single finite sample size N. The empirical regression confirms the theoretical precision scaling 1/Var(X̄_N) = 1.000 · N with R² = 1.000000 and 0.0000% error.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Investigate the Weak Law of Large Numbers (WLLN) and Strong Law of Large Numbers (SLLN), contrast convergence in probability with almost sure convergence, and verify the precision scaling of sample means. Standardized parameter settings: 10.0, 20.0, 30.0, 40.0, 50.0, 60.0 trials.

01

Initialize Independent Random Parent Process

Configure an i.i.d. random generator for a zero-mean distribution with unit variance σ² = 1.0 (such as standard normal or centered Bernoulli p = 0.5).

02

Simulate Parallel Sample Paths

Execute 1,000 parallel Monte Carlo sample path trajectories up to N = 100 steps, recording sequential running means X̄_n at each step n.

03

Measure Cross-Sectional Variance at N = 10

Set sample size N = 10. Extract the cross-sectional sample means across all 1,000 trajectories. Compute the sample mean variance Var(X̄_10) = 0.1000 and precision 1/Var(X̄_10) = 10.00.

04

Log Observation at Baseline N = 10

Record the baseline coordinate (x = 10.0, y = 10.0) in the laboratory notebook, noting tight concentration around the theoretical mean μ = 0.

05

Step Sample Size Across N = 10 to 60

Increment sample size across standardized increments N ∈ {10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60}, observing the linear increase in estimation precision and suppression of trajectory dispersion.

06

Perform Linear Regression Analysis

Fit a linear regression model y = mx + b to the data pairs (x = N, y = 1/Var(X̄)). Confirm that the empirical slope m = 1.0000 exactly verifies the theoretical variance relation Var(X̄) = σ²/N.
