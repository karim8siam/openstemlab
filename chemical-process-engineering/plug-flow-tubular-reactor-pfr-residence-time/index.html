FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Operate an isothermal tubular plug-flow reactor (PFR) conducting liquid-phase saponification, evaluate reactant conversion as a function of space time τ, and verify the first-order / linearized PFR performance model ln(1/(1 - X_A)) = k·τ. Standardized parameter settings: 1.5, 3.0, 4.5, 6.0, 7.5, 9.0 min.

01

Optical Sensor Calibration

Zero the spectrophotometer with pure solvent and record absorbance baseline for 0% and 100% conversion standards.

02

Bath Temperature Stabilization

Set bath temperature to 25.00 °C; circulate water for 15 minutes to ensure the coiled tube is isothermal.

03

Initial Flow Rate Setpoint

Set feed pumps to combined flow rate v_0 = 0.800 L/min (reactor space time τ = 1.20 L / 0.800 L/min = 1.50 min).

04

Steady-State Effluent Logging

Allow 3 residence times (4.5 minutes) for steady state; record effluent absorbance to calculate fractional conversion X_A.

05

Space Time Stepping Protocol

Step combined flow rate down through 0.400, 0.267, 0.200, 0.160, and 0.133 L/min to establish space times τ = 3.0, 4.5, 6.0, 7.5, and 9.0 min.

06

PFR Kinetic Regression Analysis

Calculate ln[1 / (1 - X_A)] for each run, plot versus space time τ, and fit a linear regression through the origin to determine rate constant k.

THEORY AND UNIT OPERATIONS CONTEXT

Process engineering background & physical principles.

Plug-flow reactors (PFRs or tubular reactors) represent the idealized opposite mixing extreme to the continuous stirred-tank reactor (CSTR). In an ideal PFR, fluid elements travel through an elongated conduit in orderly plug flow with zero axial mixing or back-dispersion, while exhibiting perfect radial mixing across any cross-section. Consequently, every fluid element resides in the reactor for exactly the same duration, equal to the space time τ = V / v_0. Reactants enter at maximum concentration, and reaction rate is highest at the entrance, declining progressively along the tube axis as reactants are consumed. This makes tubular PFRs substantially more efficient than CSTRs for positive-order reactions, requiring less reactor volume to achieve high fractional conversion. For a first-order or pseudo-first-order liquid reaction: -r_A = k · C_A = k · C_A0 · (1 - X_A), integrating the differential PFR design equation dX_A / dτ = -r_A / C_A0 yields the celebrated exponential relation: 1 - X_A = exp(-k · τ) -> ln[1 / (1 - X_A)] = k · τ. In this laboratory investigation, a coiled stainless steel tubular PFR submerged in a constant-temperature water bath is operated across space times from 1.5 to 9.0 min to measure conversion X_A, verify plug-flow kinetics, and evaluate rate constant k = 0.1850 min^-1.
