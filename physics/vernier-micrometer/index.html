FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the fundamental circular geometric ratio π and investigate instrument zero errors and least count by precision measurement of cylinder diameters and circumferences using Vernier calipers and micrometer screw gauges. Suggested independent settings: 15.0, 20.0, 25.0, 30.0, 35.0 mm.

1

Determine the least count and zero error of the calipers

Close the caliper jaws gently and observe whether the zero mark of the sliding vernier coincides with the zero mark of the main scale; record any positive or negative zero error.

Why this step matters

A persistent zero offset shifts every dimensional measurement by an additive constant, introducing systematic intercept error.

2

Inspect the micrometer screw gauge zero error

Clean the anvil and spindle faces with lens paper, rotate the ratchet thimble until it clicks three times against the anvil, and record any baseline circular division offset.

Why this step matters

Microscopic dust or grit on the anvil face causes a positive zero error that artificially enlarges all diameter readings.

3

Measure cylinder diameter at orthogonal orientations

Position the cylinder between the external caliper jaws perpendicular to the main beam, tighten the slide clamp gently, and read the main scale and matching vernier division.

Why this step matters

Measuring across two orthogonal axes and at multiple axial positions verifies that the cylinder is truly circular without ovality or taper.

4

Wrap the precision ribbon around the cylinder perimeter

Wrap the flexible steel ribbon snugly around the cylinder circumference without twisting or stretching, ensuring the ribbon edges align flat against the metal surface.

Why this step matters

Ribbon skew, tilt, or slack causes an overestimation of the circumference, distorting the geometric ratio.

5

Read circumference at the alignment index

Read the main scale on the ribbon and the coincidence index with a magnifying loupe to record the boundary circumference C to the nearest 0.02 mm.

Why this step matters

Parallax in reading flexible scale graduations introduces random reading scatter in the dependent variable.

6

Repeat for the complete series of diameters

Execute the diameter and circumference measurements for all five test cylinders spanning 15.0 mm to 35.0 mm to assemble the regression dataset.

Why this step matters

Evaluating multiple independent diameters across a wide dynamic range provides high statistical leverage for the slope determination.
