Aerodynamic and physical foundations of Prandtl-Meyer Expansion Fan.

When a supersonic flow encounters a convex corner where the wall turns away from the stream by an angle theta, the flow must expand continuously to remain attached to the surface. In stark contrast to compression corners that generate discontinuous shock waves, supersonic expansion is an isentropic, reversible process that occurs through an infinite fan of diverging acoustic Mach waves centered at the corner apex—known as a Prandtl-Meyer centered expansion fan. Each individual Mach wave in the fan is inclined at the local Mach angle mu = arcsin(1/M) and turns the flow by an incremental angle dtheta = sqrt(M^2 - 1) * dV / V. Integrating this differential relation yields the celebrated Prandtl-Meyer function: nu(M) = sqrt((gamma+1)/(gamma-1)) * arctan(sqrt((gamma-1)/(gamma+1) * (M^2 - 1))) - arctan(sqrt(M^2 - 1)). The total turn angle theta equals the change in the Prandtl-Meyer function: theta = nu(M_2) - nu(M_1). Across the expansion fan, Mach number increases substantially, while static pressure, density, and temperature drop smoothly according to isentropic gas relations, maintaining constant total pressure (P_02 = P_01) and constant entropy. In this laboratory experiment, supersonic flow at initial Mach M_1 = 1.80 expands over a series of calibrated convex corners from theta = 3 to 18 degrees. Wall static pressure taps downstream of the corner record static pressure drops, from which downstream Mach numbers M_2 are calculated, validating the linear expansion rate dM / dtheta.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

Why is a supersonic expansion fan isentropic whereas a supersonic compression is a shock wave?

In an expansion fan, acoustic waves diverge away from each other because each successive wave enters a region of higher Mach number and lower sound speed. Since acoustic waves never coalesce or steepen, no discontinuities form, and the process remains perfectly isentropic with Delta s = 0 and constant total pressure P_0.

How are the leading and trailing Mach waves of an expansion fan oriented?

The leading Mach wave is inclined at the upstream Mach angle mu_1 = arcsin(1/M_1) relative to the upstream wall. The trailing Mach wave is inclined at the downstream Mach angle mu_2 = arcsin(1/M_2) relative to the downstream deflected wall.

What happens to temperature, density, and static pressure across an expansion fan?

As the supersonic flow accelerates to a higher Mach number (M_2 > M_1), internal thermal energy is converted into directed kinetic energy. Consequently, static temperature T, static density rho, and static pressure P all drop smoothly according to isentropic gas relations.

What is the physical significance of the Prandtl-Meyer angle nu(M)?

The Prandtl-Meyer angle nu(M) represents the total geometric angle through which a sonic stream (M = 1) must be expanded isentropically to reach a given supersonic Mach number M. The deflection angle between any two supersonic states is simply Delta theta = nu(M_2) - nu(M_1).

What is the theoretical maximum turning angle for air expanding into a complete vacuum?

As Mach number approaches infinity (vacuum condition P = 0), the Prandtl-Meyer function approaches its asymptotic limit: nu_max = (pi/2) * [sqrt((gamma+1)/(gamma-1)) - 1]. For air with gamma = 1.4, nu_max = 130.45 degrees.

How is a Prandtl-Meyer expansion fan utilized in the design of supersonic aerofoils?

Supersonic aerofoils (such as diamond or double-wedge sections) use sharp leading edges to generate attached oblique shocks for compression, followed by convex mid-chord corners where Prandtl-Meyer expansion fans lower upper-surface pressure, generating net aerodynamic lift with minimal wave drag.

Experimental precautions and wind tunnel safety

  • Ensure the corner apex is perfectly sharp; rounding the corner converts the centered fan into a distributed non-centered expansion.
  • Verify that downstream wall static taps are located outside the fan region where pressure has reached its uniform expanded value P_2.
  • Confirm that reflected expansion waves from the opposite wind tunnel ceiling do not intersect the measurement taps.
  • Maintain dry air supply to prevent moisture condensation caused by severe static temperature drops across large expansion turns.

Theoretical assumptions & flow limits

  • Convex corner curvature creates a localized boundary layer thinning and favorable pressure gradient that can laminarize turbulent flow.
  • Assumes two-dimensional flow across the channel width; sidewall boundary layers induce slight optical ray curvature.
  • Calorically perfect gas assumption assumes constant specific heat ratio gamma = 1.40 across the temperature drop.
  • Expansion cannot proceed beyond the point where static pressure drops to absolute vacuum (M_vacuum = 5.03 for air).
