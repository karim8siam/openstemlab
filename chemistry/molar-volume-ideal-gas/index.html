Molar Volume of an Ideal Gas: Eudiometric Acid-Metal Displacement of Hydrogen Gas

Quantitatively determine the molar volume of hydrogen gas at Standard Temperature and Pressure (STP: 273.15 K, 101.325 kPa) via the single displacement reaction between metallic magnesium and hydrochloric acid, applying Dalton's law of partial pressures for aqueous vapor pressure and liquid hydrostatic head corrections, and extracting the empirical molar volume via ordinary least-squares regression. Physical chemistry assumptions and metrological limits

The determination of ideal gas molar volume via eudiometric hydrogen displacement is subject to specific physical constraints: (1) Real Gas Non-Ideality: Hydrogen molecules exhibit finite molecular excluded volume (van der Waals b = 0.0266 L/mol), causing real hydrogen molar volume at STP to be 22.428 L/mol rather than the ideal 22.414 L/mol (a +0.062% offset). (2) Aqueous Gas Solubility: Hydrogen has a small but finite solubility in water (~1.9 mL/L at 20 °C), resulting in the dissolution of approximately 0.05 mL of H₂ in the aqueous column. (3) Meniscus Parallax and Glass Drainage: Capillary depression and water drainage films along the eudiometer wall contribute ±0.03 mL of random reading uncertainty. (4) Atmospheric Barometric Drift: Minor barometric pressure changes during an extended multi-trial session can introduce small baseline drifts unless monitored concurrently. The eudiometric gas model incorporates four coupled thermodynamic and hydrodynamic corrections: (1) Stoichiometric mole equivalence n(H₂) = m_Mg / 24.305 g/mol; (2) Dalton's law of partial pressures subtracting the temperature-dependent equilibrium saturation vapor pressure of liquid water P_H2O*(T) formulated via the Antoine equation log₁₀(P) = A - B / (T + C); (3) Hydrostatic head elimination by matching the internal and external water menisci inside the tall leveling jar (or applying the mercury-equivalent hydrostatic correction ΔP_head = Δh_water / 13.595 mmHg); and (4) Combined gas law reduction to Standard Temperature and Pressure (T_0 = 273.15 K, P_0 = 760.0 mmHg). The regression slope m_slope = V_m,STP / M_Mg relates directly to the molar volume of hydrogen gas, achieving sub-0.05% agreement with the theoretical value of 22.414 L/mol.

Class A Gas Measuring Eudiometer Tube

50.00 mL borosilicate glass eudiometer tube with permanently fused ceramic white graduations every 0.10 mL (Class A tolerance ±0.05 mL), closed and rounded at the top to trap all evolved hydrogen gas without bubble loss.

Tall-Form Leveling Water Cylinder

1000 mL heavy-walled borosilicate graduated leveling jar filled with deionized water, allowing the eudiometer to be submerged to match internal and external liquid menisci, completely neutralizing the hydrostatic column pressure.

Copper Wire Cage Assembly

High-purity spiraled copper wire (gauge 20) cage that mechanically secures the folded magnesium ribbon at the bottom orifice of the eudiometer, preventing buoyant magnesium fragments from floating into the calibrated tube before dissolution.

Precision Fortin Barometer & Digital Thermometer

Mercury Fortin barometer (vernier resolution ±0.1 mmHg) equipped with brass scale temperature correction, paired with a NIST-traceable digital immersion thermistor (resolution ±0.05 °C) to monitor ambient pressure and bath temperature.

One-Hole Neoprene Stopper & Acid Dispenser

Size 00 one-hole neoprene stopper with glass capillary insert to seal the eudiometer while allowing water displacement, and a calibrated 10 mL pipette for bottom-layering concentrated 6.0 M HCl.

Micro-Analytical Balance

Five-place electromagnetic force restoration microbalance with 0.01 mg (0.00001 g) readability and draft enclosure to determine exact masses of cleaned magnesium ribbon strips.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Quantitatively determine the molar volume of hydrogen gas at Standard Temperature and Pressure (STP: 273.15 K, 101.325 kPa) via the single displacement reaction between metallic magnesium and hydrochloric acid, applying Dalton's law of partial pressures for aqueous vapor pressure and liquid hydrostatic head corrections, and extracting the empirical molar volume via ordinary least-squares regression. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 25.0, 30.0, 35.0 mg.

01

Magnesium Ribbon Abrasion and Micro-Gravimetric Weighing

Thoroughly abrade high-purity magnesium ribbon strips with fine 600-grit silicon carbide emery cloth until a bright, silvery metallic luster is achieved, completely removing passivating magnesium oxide (MgO) and carbonate crusts; wipe with a lint-free tissue and weigh to ±0.01 mg on the analytical microbalance.

02

Copper Cage Enclosure and Reagent Layering

Fold the weighed magnesium strip into an accordion zig-zag and encase it tightly within a fine copper wire cage, leaving a 10 cm copper tail; introduce 10.0 mL of 6.0 M HCl into the bottom of the dry eudiometer tube, then slowly and carefully overlay deionized water down the tilted tube wall to fill it to the brim without mixing the dense acid layer.

03

Stopper Insertion and Tube Inversion

Insert the copper wire tail through the one-hole neoprene stopper so the magnesium cage is positioned approximately 3 cm into the tube mouth; insert the stopper firmly, seal the hole with a gloved finger, and invert the eudiometer into a 1000 mL beaker of deionized water before clamping it vertically to a ring stand.

04

Acid Diffusion, Active Displacement, and Thermal Equilibration

As the denser 6 M HCl diffuses downward under gravity through the water column, it reaches the magnesium ribbon, initiating a vigorous, effervescent displacement reaction: Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) -> MgCl2(aq) + H2(g); hydrogen gas ascends rapidly, displacing water downward through the stopper until the metal dissolves completely; allow 15 minutes for the eudiometer to cool to ambient bath temperature.

05

Meniscus Leveling and Hydrostatic Head Elimination

Unclamp the eudiometer and transfer it into the tall-form 1000 mL leveling cylinder; raise or lower the tube until the liquid level inside the eudiometer aligns precisely with the water level in the external cylinder, equalizing total internal gas pressure with atmospheric pressure (P_gas = P_atm); record the gas volume to the nearest 0.05 mL, and measure bath temperature and barometric pressure.

06

Vapor Pressure Subtraction, STP Reduction, and Molar Volume Regression

Subtract the equilibrium saturated water vapor pressure P_H2O*(T) from barometric pressure to isolate dry hydrogen pressure P_H2; convert measured volume to Standard Temperature and Pressure (STP: 273.15 K, 760.0 mmHg) using the combined gas law; repeat across six magnesium masses (10.0 to 35.0 mg), tabulate data, and apply ordinary least-squares regression of V_STP versus m_Mg to extract the molar volume V_m,STP.
