Bifilar suspension theory

Understand bifilar suspension: the equations, assumptions, supporting concepts and measurement method.

WorkbenchDetailed theoryMethodMy resultsExample report
THE WORKING RELATIONSHIP
T² = mL + b; I = mMga²/(4π²)

Keep the physical quantities, graph variables and units consistent.

01Inertia depends on the chosen axis

Rotational inertia weights each small mass element by the square of its perpendicular distance from an axis. This experiment uses the vertical axis through the rod centre, perpendicular to its length. For a uniform slender rod of length D, integration gives I = MD squared/12. With M = 0.500 kg and D = 0.500 m, the model value is 0.0104167 kg m squared. A finite-width rectangular bar instead has I = M(D squared + w squared)/12; ignoring width is justified only for a slender rod.

I = integral(r^2 dm); I_centre = MD^2/12
Would the same rod have the same inertia about one end?

No. Moving the parallel axis to an end adds M(D/2) squared by the parallel-axis theorem.

02Bifilar geometry and the factor of four

Here a is half the string separation, so the two strings are 2a = 0.200 m apart. Both upper attachments must be directly above their corresponding lower attachments at equilibrium. L is their common equilibrium length, not the rod length D. Using full separation in a formula written for half-separation gives an inertia four times too large.

d = 2a; a = 0.100 m; L = equilibrium string length
If the strings are 0.200 m apart, what a belongs in our equation?

a = 0.100 m. The symbol denotes half the separation.

03Why a twisted rod returns

A gentle twist moves the lower attachment points sideways and lifts the rod slightly. Its increased gravitational potential energy supplies the restoring tendency. For small theta, the lift is approximately a squared theta squared/(2L). Differentiating Mg times this lift gives a restoring torque -(Mga squared/L)theta. Thin flexible strings are assumed to supply negligible material torsional stiffness.

delta z approximately a^2 theta^2/(2L); torque approximately -(Mga^2/L)theta
Is gravity absent because the rod rotates approximately horizontally?

No. The small rise during a twist changes gravitational potential energy and creates the restoring torque.

04Period of the rotational oscillation

Combining the restoring torque with I times angular acceleration gives a simple harmonic oscillator. A larger inertia slows the oscillation, longer strings weaken the restoring torque, and greater attachment separation strengthens it. Keep twists small and avoid sideways swinging or tilting so that one rotational mode dominates. A timing interval must include 20 full returns to the same orientation and direction.

I theta'' + (Mga^2/L)theta = 0; T = 2pi sqrt(IL/(Mga^2))
What happens to period if L doubles while all other quantities remain fixed?

It increases by a factor of the square root of two.

05Recover inertia from a graph

Plot period squared vertically against string length horizontally. If T squared = mL + b, the gradient is 4pi squared I/(Mga squared). Thus I = mMga squared/(4pi squared); the gradient unit is seconds squared per metre and the resulting inertia unit is kg m squared. The ideal intercept is zero. Do not force it to zero before examining possible length offsets or effects omitted by the model.

T^2 = [4pi^2 I/(Mga^2)]L; I = mMga^2/(4pi^2); b_ideal = 0
Does the graph horizontal axis contain rod length or string length?

String length L. Rod length D stays fixed and is used only in the independent theoretical inertia calculation.

06Uncertainty, comparison and evidence

Because I depends on a squared, a small fractional separation error contributes twice that fractional error to I. For independent inputs, combine relative standard uncertainties from gradient, mass, g and twice the half-separation uncertainty in quadrature. A physical comparison with MD squared/12 must also include rod dimension and mass uncertainty. Perfect virtual agreement is expected because the data generator uses that rod formula; it is a consistency exercise rather than independent experimental confirmation.

[u(I)/I]^2 approximately [u(m)/m]^2 + [u(M)/M]^2 + [u(g)/g]^2 + [2u(a)/a]^2
Why measure string separation carefully?

The inferred inertia is proportional to its square, so a fractional separation error is doubled to first order.

Connect the equations to a measurement.

Equal, parallel strings are attached symmetrically about the centre of mass. The model uses the small-yaw relation T = 2π√[IL/(Mga²)]. It assumes negligible string mass, rod width, damping and support compliance. The simulated target inertia is Mℓ²/12. Angles above 5° cannot be recorded.
