1. Title and aim

Clark Polarographic Electrode Limiting Diffusion Current & Oxygen Tension. Measure the steady-state cathodic reduction limiting diffusion current of a membrane-covered Clark polarographic oxygen electrode across calibrated dissolved oxygen partial pressures.

4. Theory and graph choice

In critical care medicine, respiratory physiology, and arterial blood gas (ABG) clinical laboratories, continuous quantification of blood oxygen tension (PO2) is vital for managing patients with acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and during mechanical ventilation. Invented by Leland Clark in 1956, the Clark polarographic electrode revolutionized medical diagnostics by isolating the working cathode and reference anode behind an oxygen-permeable, electrically insulating polymer membrane (typically Teflon or polypropylene). When a polarizing potential of -0.65 to -0.75 V is applied to the platinum working micro-cathode relative to an Ag/AgCl reference anode immersed in KCl buffer, molecular oxygen diffusing through the membrane undergoes immediate four-electron electrochemical reduction at the platinum surface: O2 + 2 H2O + 4 e⁻ → 4 OH⁻. Because the reduction reaction is virtually instantaneous at this polarizing plateau voltage, surface oxygen concentration drops to zero, and the resulting steady-state cathodic current I_d is strictly governed by Fick's first law of diffusion: I_d = 4·F·(P_m·A / x_m) · P_O2 = k_O2 · P_O2, where F is Faraday's constant, P_m is membrane permeability, A is cathode area, x_m is membrane thickness, and P_O2 is oxygen partial pressure. In this experiment, a clinical Clark electrode cell is tested across calibrated gas-equilibrated saline solutions to establish linearity and determine oxygen sensitivity k_O2.

I_d = k_O2 · P_O2 = 1.6500 · P_O2

The governing physiological relationship was linearized into the standard regression coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Oxygen partial pressure P_O2 (kPa), and the dependent coordinate y represents Cathodic current I_d (nA). The gradient m directly determines Oxygen Diffusion Sensitivity k_O2, while the intercept b accounts for baseline null offsets, pre-stress tares, or reference thresholds. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear solver instability or convergence traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays sensor sensitivities, elastic moduli, hemodynamic resistances, or acoustic attenuation coefficients directly as linear slopes. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential sensor saturation, biological yield, or non-linear compliance.

5. Method

  1. Inspect the 12 μm Teflon membrane for micro-tears, fill electrolyte well with fresh 0.1 M KCl buffer, and apply -0.700 V cathodic polarizing potential.
  2. Perfuse the flowcell with zero-oxygen calibration fluid (deoxygenated with sodium sulfite) and record residual dark background current (I_0 < 0.1 nA).
  3. Perfuse saline tonometered to P_O2 = 2.0 kPa (15.0 mmHg) at 37.0 °C, establishing a constant perfusion velocity to prevent boundary layer depletion.
  4. Allow 45 seconds for diffusion boundary layer stabilization; record steady-state cathodic current I_d in nanoamperes.
  5. Increment tonometered oxygen partial pressure through 5.0, 8.0, 11.0, 14.0, and 17.0 kPa (up to physiological normoxia ~100-128 mmHg), logging I_d at each step.
  6. Plot reduction current I_d against oxygen partial pressure P_O2, performing linear regression through zero to calculate diffusion sensitivity k_O2.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Maintain constant fluid perfusion velocity over the membrane to eliminate stagnant boundary layer depletion.
  • Control flowcell temperature strictly at 37.00 ± 0.05 °C to prevent thermal solubility and diffusion artifacts.
  • Ensure zero air bubbles are trapped within the electrolyte cavity beneath the Teflon membrane.
  • Allow polarizing current to stabilize for at least 15 minutes prior to initial calibration recording.
