Vector addition and equilibrium of concurrent coplanar forces on a force table

Investigate the static mechanical equilibrium of concurrent coplanar forces using a precision circular force table, verify the parallelogram and polygon laws of vector addition by resolving forces into orthogonal Cartesian components, and determine the unknown magnitude of equal symmetric load forces using ordinary least-squares regression. Simulation assumptions and limits

The simulation assumes idealized frictionless pulleys, massless cords, an infinitely thin centering pin, and perfectly concurrent lines of action through the mathematical center. Simulated readings reflect symmetric applied loads of reference mass M_0 = 0.1500 kg (150.0 g) with slotted weight set resolution of 0.1 g.

Know your apparatus.

Circular 360° graduated force table

A precision-machined circular aluminum table graduated in single degrees from 0° to 360° along its outer raised rim, supported on a heavy cast-iron tripod stand fitted with three adjustable leveling foot-screws and a central removable centering pin.

Low-friction clamp-on pulleys

Three miniature ball-bearing pulleys equipped with knurled thumbscrew clamps that secure rigidly to the table rim, designed so the pulley groove plane aligns strictly with the table radius toward the center pin.

Central floating ring and radial cords

A lightweight aluminum ring of negligible mass connected to three non-stretch braided cords that radiate outward across the pulleys, maintaining concentric floating alignment around the centering pin during equilibrium.

Precision slotted brass weights and hangers

A set of standardized slotted brass weights (1.0 g to 100.0 g) and lightweight 50.0 g weight hangers, calibrated on an analytical laboratory balance (0.01 g resolution) to apply precise gravitational tensions.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Investigate the static mechanical equilibrium of concurrent coplanar forces using a precision circular force table, verify the parallelogram and polygon laws of vector addition by resolving forces into orthogonal Cartesian components, and determine the unknown magnitude of equal symmetric load forces using ordinary least-squares regression. Suggested independent settings: 20.0, 30.0, 40.0, 50.0, 60.0 °.

1

Level the circular force table

Place a circular bullseye spirit level directly onto the center of the force table disk. Adjust the three leveling screws on the tripod base until the spirit level bubble is centered within the target index circle. Rotate the level through 90° to verify that the table is horizontal in all directions.

Why this step matters

Any tilt in the table surface introduces an unmodeled gravitational component acting parallel to the plate, causing the central ring to slide downhill and biasing the force equilibrium measurements.

2

Mount the symmetric applied force pulleys

Clamp the first pulley at angle +θ (e.g. 20.0°) and the second pulley at symmetric angle 360° - θ (e.g. 340.0°). Thread two radial cords from the central ring over these pulleys and attach identical total masses M_0 = 150.0 g (including the weight hangers) to both cords.

Why this step matters

Symmetric angular placement forces the lateral orthogonal components to cancel exactly (+M_0 g sin θ - M_0 g sin θ = 0), aligning the net vector resultant R purely along the 0° Cartesian axis.

3

Position the equilibrant pulley at 180°

Clamp the third pulley at exactly 180.0° on the table rim, diametrically opposite the 0° line of action of the resultant force. Pass the third cord over this pulley and suspend an empty weight hanger (50.0 g tare).

Why this step matters

Static equilibrium demands an equilibrant vector E that is collinear, equal in magnitude, and oppositely directed to the resultant vector R (E = -R), which corresponds strictly to 180° on the circle.

4

Balance the central ring and achieve equilibrium

Carefully add slotted brass weights to the equilibrant hanger until the central ring floats freely and concentrically around the centering pin without contacting it. Tap the rim of the table gently with a pencil to eliminate static bearing friction in the pulley axles.

Why this step matters

Free concentric flotation of the ring without touching the centering pin proves that the net vector sum of the three concurrent tension forces equals zero (Σ F = 0).

5

Record equilibrant mass across varying angles

Record the total equilibrant mass m_E (weights plus hanger) to the nearest 0.1 g. Systematically alter the symmetric half-angle setting to θ = 30.0°, 40.0°, 50.0°, and 60.0°. At each setting, adjust the equilibrant mass m_E until the ring is perfectly centered, and record the new balance mass.

Why this step matters

Testing across five distinct angles spans a wide range of cosine values (0.500 to 0.940) and equilibrant masses (150 g to 282 g), providing robust statistical leverage for linear regression.

6

Compute trigonometric projection and perform regression

Calculate the independent variable x = cos(θ) for each setting. Convert m_E to kilograms (y = m_E / 1000). Plot y against x, fit an ordinary least-squares regression line, determine the slope S, and compute the estimated component mass M_0 = S / 2.

Why this step matters

Linear regression averages out random ring-centering reading errors and isolates any minor cord tare offsets into the intercept b, yielding an unbiased estimate of M_0 with rigorous confidence intervals.
