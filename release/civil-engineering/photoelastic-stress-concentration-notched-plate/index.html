FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the elastic stress concentration factor (K_t) and calibrate the photoelastic fringe constant (f_σ) around a circular cutout in a structural tension plate using transmission photoelasticity according to ASTM E1319. Standardized parameter settings: 5.0, 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 25.0, 30.0 MPa.

01

Polariscope Optical Alignment & Dark-Field Setup

Align polarizer, first quarter-wave plate, second quarter-wave plate, and analyzer along the optical axis; verify dark-field condition (complete extinction) under zero load.

02

Specimen Geometry & Residual Stress Inspection

Mount polycarbonate specimen in tensile grips; inspect under polariscope at zero load to verify zero residual machining fringes around the hole boundary.

03

Monochromatic Lighting Initialization

Energize 589 nm sodium light source; focus digital imaging camera onto the horizontal hole boundary equator.

04

Incremental Tensile Loading & Fringe Counting

Apply tensile load incrementally to produce nominal stresses σ_nom of 5.0, 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 25.0, and 30.0 MPa; count isochromatic fringe orders N emerging at the hole boundary.

05

Fractional Fringe Measurement using Compensator

At each load step, use the Babinet–Soleil compensator to measure exact fringe order N at the hole edge to within 0.02 fringes.

06

Stress Concentration Factor Evaluation

Plot fringe order N versus nominal stress σ_nom; determine response slope; calculate peak boundary stress and compute stress concentration factor K_t.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

In structural and bridge engineering, holes, cutouts, notches, and re-entrant corners (such as bolt holes, access manholes, and weld copes) disrupt the uniform flow of internal stress trajectories, concentrating stresses into localized peaks several times greater than the nominal average stress. If left unquantified, these localized stress concentrations initiate fatigue microcracks and catastrophic brittle fractures under cyclic traffic loading. Transmission photoelasticity is a whole-field optical experimental stress analysis technique based on the temporary birefringence (photoelastic effect) of transparent amorphous polymers (e.g. polycarbonate or epoxy). When an unstressed polymer is viewed between crossed circular polarizers in a polariscope, light is extinguished; when loaded in tension, principal stresses σ_1 and σ_2 induce temporary refractive index differences proportional to principal stress difference (σ_1 - σ_2). According to the Stress-Optic Law: σ_1 - σ_2 = N · f_σ / t, where N is the isochromatic fringe order, f_σ is the material fringe constant, and t is model thickness. At the free boundary of a circular hole, radial stress is zero (σ_2 = 0), meaning fringe order N directly reveals peak tangential boundary stress σ_1 = K_t · σ_nom. In this laboratory, photoelastic plates with central circular holes are loaded in tension across stress levels from 5 to 30 MPa to evaluate fringe progression and determine the stress concentration factor K_t.
