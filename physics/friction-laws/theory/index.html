Friction & normal force theory

Understand friction & normal force: the equations, assumptions, supporting concepts and measurement method.

THE WORKING RELATIONSHIP
F_f = μN + b

Keep the physical quantities, graph variables and units consistent.

01Amontons’ laws of friction and microscopic contact

Macroscopic friction is governed by two empirical laws formulated by Guillaume Amontons (1699) and confirmed by Charles-Augustin de Coulomb (1785): (1) The frictional force is directly proportional to the normal applied load; (2) The frictional force is independent of the apparent macroscopic contact area. Modern surface physics explains this through asperity contact: solid surfaces are rough on a microscopic scale, touching only at microscopic peaks (asperities). The true contact area A_true is proportional to the normal load N (A_true ≈ N / H, where H is material hardness). Because frictional resistance equals interfacial shear strength multiplied by true contact area (F_f = τ A_true = τ N / H), F_f scales linearly with N and remains independent of the apparent macroscopic area.

F_f = \mu N = \tau A_{\text{true}} = \tau \left(\frac{N}{H}\right)
Why does turning a rectangular friction block onto its narrower side not change the measured sliding friction force?

Although the apparent contact area is halved, the normal pressure is doubled, causing individual microscopic asperities to deform twice as much. The sum of all microscopic junction areas (the true contact area) remains exactly unchanged, keeping the frictional force invariant.

02Static versus kinetic friction and stiction transition

Static friction F_s resists the initiation of motion and adjusts to balance any applied lateral force up to a limiting maximum: F_s ≤ μ_s N. Once the applied force exceeds the limiting static friction, microscopic cold-welded junctions shear, the surfaces begin to slide, and friction drops to a lower, nearly constant kinetic value F_k = μ_k N. The kinetic coefficient μ_k is almost universally smaller than the static coefficient μ_s because moving asperities do not have sufficient dwell time to form full adhesive bonds with opposing valleys.

F_s \le \mu_s N, \quad F_k = \mu_k N \quad (\mu_k < \mu_s)
Why is the static coefficient of friction μ_s generally greater than the kinetic coefficient μ_k?

At rest, microscopic contact asperities have time to undergo plastic creep and form strong adhesive bonds across the interface. In motion, asperities ride over one another rapidly without settling, reducing the required shearing force.

03Linear regression model for friction force against normal load

When plotting kinetic friction force F_f on the vertical axis against normal load N on the horizontal axis, the data follow F_f = mN + b. The slope m represents the coefficient of kinetic friction μ. In an ideal dry contact system, the theoretical intercept b is zero. An experimental non-zero intercept b indicates an uncorrected horizontal dynamometer tare error, track misalignment, or adhesive surface tack.

F_f = \mu N + b \implies m = \frac{\sum (N_i - \bar{N})(F_{f,i} - \bar{F}_f)}{\sum (N_i - \bar{N})^2} = \mu
What does an unexpected positive y-intercept b signify in a friction experiment?

A positive intercept usually indicates an uncorrected zero error in the dynamometer, track friction in the pulling thread guide, or surface adhesion/cohesion that resists motion even under zero external load.

04Velocity dependence of kinetic friction

Coulomb’s third law states that kinetic friction is independent of sliding velocity over moderate speed ranges. At very low speeds, stick-slip relaxation oscillations can occur; at high speeds, frictional heating alters interfacial viscosity and softens surface asperities, leading to a modest decline in μ. In standard undergraduate experiments, keeping pulling speed uniform at approximately 0.05 m/s ensures the measurement remains squarely in the velocity-independent Coulomb regime.

\mu_k(v) \approx \text{constant} \quad (0.01\text{ m/s} < v < 0.2\text{ m/s})
What causes the stick-slip phenomenon often observed when pulling a slider with an elastic spring?

Stick-slip occurs when the static coefficient μ_s exceeds the kinetic coefficient μ_k. The spring stretches statically until force exceeds limiting friction, the block slips forward rapidly at lower kinetic friction, and then catches again when relative velocity reaches zero.

05Effect of pulling angle on normal force

If the traction force F is applied at an angle θ relative to the horizontal surface, the normal force is no longer simply the gravitational weight W = Mg. Resolving forces gives N = W - F sin(θ) for an upward pull, and the horizontal equilibrium becomes F cos(θ) = μ_k N = μ_k (W - F sin(θ)). Rearranging yields F = μ_k W / (cos(θ) + μ_k sin(θ)). To avoid this trigonometric coupling and measure F_f = μ_k W directly, the pulling line must be kept strictly horizontal (θ = 0).

F_{\text{pull}} = \frac{\mu_k M g}{\cos\theta + \mu_k \sin\theta} \to \mu_k M g \quad (\theta=0)
Why does pulling slightly upward reduce the force needed to move a heavy crate across a rough floor?

An upward pulling force provides a vertical lift component F sin(θ) that reduces the normal compressive reaction N between floor and crate, directly diminishing the frictional resistance μ N.

06Rolling friction versus sliding friction

Rolling friction occurs when a circular cylinder or sphere rolls over a surface without slipping. Rather than shearing interfacial junctions, rolling resistance arises from the viscoelastic hysteresis loss during the cyclic compression and relaxation of the contact materials: F_r = (c_r / R) N, where c_r is the coefficient of rolling resistance and R is the wheel radius. Because no surface shear occurs, rolling friction coefficients (typically 0.001 to 0.01) are orders of magnitude smaller than sliding friction coefficients (0.1 to 1.0).

F_{\text{rolling}} = \frac{c_r}{R} N \ll \mu_k N
Why did the invention of the wheel represent such a monumental mechanical breakthrough?

Wheels replace sliding shear friction across large contact areas with rolling deformation resistance, reducing the pulling traction required to move heavy loads by a factor of 50 to 100.

07Uncertainty budget in friction determinations

In a single pulling trial, the relative uncertainty in μ satisfies (u_μ / μ)² = (u_Ff / Ff)² + (u_N / N)². Normal force N is determined from calibrated masses with uncertainty below 0.1%. Friction force F_f measured on a spring balance has fluctuations of ±0.02 N due to microscopic local roughness variations. Multi-point linear regression averages out these local asperity fluctuations across multiple loads, yielding a slope standard error well below 0.5% of μ.

\frac{u_\mu}{\mu} = \sqrt{\left(\frac{u_{F_f}}{F_f}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{u_N}{N}\right)^2}
Why is ordinary least-squares regression superior to averaging individual quotients F_f / N?

Linear regression isolates any additive calibration tare error into the intercept b, preventing zero offsets from biasing the slope μ, while weighting larger, more reliable loads appropriately.
