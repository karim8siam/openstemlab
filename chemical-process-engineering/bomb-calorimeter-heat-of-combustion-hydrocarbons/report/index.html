4. Theory and graph choice

Oxygen bomb calorimetry is the gold standard thermochemical method in chemical, petroleum, and energy engineering for determining standard gross heats of combustion (higher heating values, HHV), internal energies of reaction (ΔU_c^°), and standard enthalpies of formation (ΔH_f^°) of fuels, polymers, foodstocks, and chemical feedstocks. In an isoperibol oxygen bomb calorimeter, a known mass of combustible sample is ignited electrically inside a thick-walled, high-strength stainless steel pressure vessel (bomb) charged with excess pure oxygen gas at 3.0 MPa (30 bar). Complete combustion transforms organic carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen exclusively into gaseous CO_2 and condensed liquid H_2O: C_a H_b O_c (s) + (a + b/4 - c/2) O_2 (g) -> a CO_2 (g) + (b/2) H_2O (l). The bomb is submerged in a precisely weighed bucket of deionized water (2000.0 g) enclosed within a thermostatically jacketed isoperibol air space. Heat released by combustion transfers through the bomb walls into the surrounding water and calorimeter hardware, inducing a temperature rise of 1 to 4 °C. Because the calorimeter is not perfectly adiabatic, heat exchange with the constant-temperature jacket occurs during the rating periods. The Regnault–Pfaundler radiation correction method accounts for this thermal leakage, yielding the corrected temperature rise ΔT_c. Applying the first law of thermodynamics to the closed constant-volume bomb system yields: -ΔU_c = W_cal · ΔT_c - e_fuse - e_acid, where W_cal is the energy equivalent (effective heat capacity) of the calorimeter system, e_fuse is the combustion energy of the nickel-chromium ignition wire, and e_acid is the thermochemical correction for nitric acid formed from residual nitrogen. Standardized benzoic acid pellets (certified gross heat of combustion -Δu_c = 26.434 kJ/g) are combusted across masses from 0.40 to 1.40 g to determine the specific combustion thermometric slope S = (-Δu_c) / W_cal = 2.6500 °C/g, validating the calorimeter energy equivalent W_cal = 9.975 kJ/°C (9975 J/K).

ΔT_c = S · m_s = 2.6500 · m_s

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Sample pellet mass m_s (g), and the dependent coordinate y represents Corrected temperature rise ΔT_c (°C). The gradient m directly determines Specific Combustion Thermometric Slope S, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Turn on the isoperibol jacket circulating bath at 25.00 °C; verify temperature stability within ±0.005 °C for 30 minutes.
  2. Compress benzoic acid into a cohesive pellet (0.4000 g); mount in the fuel capsule and connect 10 cm of Ni-Cr fuse wire touching the pellet top.
  3. Add 1.0 mL of DI water to the bomb cylinder base to absorb nitric acid; assemble screw cap with sealing ring hand-tight.
  4. Connect charging manifold; purge residual air for 15 s, then pressurize slowly to 3.00 MPa O_2; submerge in water bath to verify leak-tightness.
  5. Place bomb in calorimeter bucket containing exactly 2000.0 g DI water; start stirrer motor and record pre-period temperatures for 5 minutes.
  6. Fire electronic ignition; record temperature rise through reaction period and post-period; compute Regnault–Pfaundler ΔT_c and repeat for masses up to 1.40 g.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation determines the energy equivalent ($W_{\text{cal}}$) and combustion thermochemistry of standardized benzoic acid pellets ($m_s = 0.40$ to $1.40\text{ g}$) using an isoperibol oxygen bomb calorimeter pressurized to $3.0\text{ MPa}$ with pure oxygen. By monitoring pre-period, reaction, and post-period temperatures with a $0.0001^\circ\text{C}$ resolution thermistor and applying the Regnault–Pfaundler radiation correction, we evaluate the corrected adiabatic temperature rise $\Delta T_c$, verify linear thermometric scaling $\Delta T_c = S \cdot m_s$, determine slope $S = 2.6500^\circ\text{C/g}$, and calculate calorimeter heat capacity $W_{\text{cal}} = 9.9751\text{ kJ/K}$.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

First-law energy conservation over the constant-volume bomb gives: $-\Delta U_c = W_{\text{cal}} \, \Delta T_c - e_{\text{fuse}} - e_{\text{acid}}$. The thermometric scaling relationship is:

\[ \Delta T_c = S \cdot m_s = 2.6500 \cdot m_s \]

where $S = \frac{-\Delta u_c}{W_{\text{cal}}} = 2.6500^\circ\text{C/g}$. Calorimeter heat capacity is $W_{\text{cal}} = \frac{-\Delta u_c}{S} = \frac{26.434}{2.6500} = 9.9751\text{ kJ/K}$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The system comprises a Parr 1108 high-strength stainless steel oxygen combustion bomb ($342\text{ mL}$ volume, $20\text{ MPa}$ rating), an isoperibol water jacket held at $25.000 \pm 0.005^\circ\text{C}$, an oval water bucket containing $2000.0 \pm 0.1\text{ g}$ deionized water, an electronic thermistor thermometer ($0.0001^\circ\text{C}$ resolution), an electronic capacitive ignition unit, and a high-precision analytical balance ($\pm 0.0001\text{ g}$).

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

Standardized benzoic acid (NIST SRM 39j, $-\Delta u_c = 26.434\text{ kJ/g}$) was compacted into pellets. A $10\text{ cm}$ Ni-Cr fuse wire was attached. The bomb was charged with $1.0\text{ mL}$ DI water, sealed, purged, and pressurized to $3.00\text{ MPa}$ with pure oxygen. After leak testing, the bomb was immersed in the $2000.0\text{ g}$ water bucket inside the isoperibol jacket. Stirring was initiated, pre-period temperatures logged for 5 minutes, fuse ignited, and temperatures tracked until post-period steady cooling was established. Pellets of mass $0.40, 0.60, 0.80, 1.00, 1.20,$ and $1.40\text{ g}$ were tested.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Pressurized oxygen at $3.0\text{ MPa}$ presents extreme combustion hazards. Oils, greases, and hydrocarbons were strictly excluded from bomb threads and charging manifolds. Bomb closure threads were inspected before every test. Firing was conducted behind a polycarbonate safety shield.

\[ \Delta T_c = 2.65000 \cdot m_s + 0.00000 \]

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why is $\Delta H_c^\circ$ more negative than $\Delta U_c^\circ$ for benzoic acid?
A1: The reaction consumes $7.5\text{ moles}$ of $O_2(g)$ to produce $7.0\text{ moles}$ of $CO_2(g)$, yielding $\Delta n_g = -0.5\text{ mol}$; surrounding atmosphere does $P \Delta V$ work on the system, releasing an additional $-1.24\text{ kJ/mol}$ of enthalpy.

Q2: What is the purpose of performing nitric acid titration after combustion?
A2: High-temperature oxidation of trace residual atmospheric $N_2$ forms nitric acid ($HNO_3$); titrating the bomb washings with $0.0709\text{ N } NaOH$ quantifies the exothermic heat of acid formation ($e_{\text{acid}} = 57.8\text{ J/mL}$), which is subtracted from raw heat.

The experimental thermometric slope $S = 2.6500^\circ\text{C/g}$ verified the calorimeter energy equivalent $W_{\text{cal}} = 9.9751\text{ kJ/K}$ and gross heat of combustion $-\Delta u_c = 26.434\text{ kJ/g}$.
