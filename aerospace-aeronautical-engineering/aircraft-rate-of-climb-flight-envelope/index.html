Aeronautical assumptions and boundary condition limits

Small climb angle assumption (sin(gamma) approx gamma and cos(gamma) approx 1) assumes gamma < 15 degrees; higher angles require full trigonometry. Linear regression of rate of climb against specific excess power yields an exact transmission slope of 1.0000 with R^2 > 0.999999.

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine maximum rate of climb (R/C)_max, climb angle gamma, and structural load factor limit lines on the aircraft maneuvering V-n diagram. Standardized parameter settings: 3.0, 5.5, 8.0, 10.5, 13.0, 15.5 m/s.

01

Aircraft Baseline Configuration

Set simulated twin-turboprop aircraft weight to W = 35.0 kN and trim for sea-level steady level flight at V = 65 m/s.

02

Climb Power Initialization

Advance engine power lever to Maximum Continuous Thrust (MCT), recording available shaft power P_A.

03

Specific Excess Power Stepping

Modulate climb pitch angle to establish steady climb conditions corresponding to specific excess power P_excess / W = 3.0 to 15.5 m/s.

04

Rate of Climb Acquisition

Sample the calibrated variometer rate of climb dh/dt across 60 seconds of unaccelerated climb at each power setting.

05

V-n Maneuvering Boundary Check

Execute pull-up maneuvers across test airspeeds to map the positive stall boundary and corner speed V_A on the V-n diagram.

06

Climb Rate Linear Regression

Plot measured rate of climb R/C versus specific excess power P_excess / W, perform linear regression to verify unity slope m = 1.0000.
