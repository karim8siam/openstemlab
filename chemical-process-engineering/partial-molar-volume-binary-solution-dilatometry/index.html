FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the densities of ethanol-water mixtures across ethanol mole fractions x_EtOH from 0.05 to 0.30 at 25.00 °C using Gay-Lussac pycnometry and capillary dilatometry, evaluate the apparent molar volume phi_V, and extract the infinite-dilution partial molar volume and volumetric concentration slope. Standardized parameter settings: 0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.2, 0.25, 0.3 mol/mol.

01

Pycnometer Tare & Volume Calibration

Thoroughly clean, rinse with acetone, and vacuum-dry the pycnometer; record empty dry weight W_0; fill with degassed DI water at 25.000 °C to find calibrated volume V_pyc.

02

Binary Solution Preparation

Prepare binary ethanol-water solutions at mole fractions x_EtOH = 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25, and 0.30 by gravimetric dispensing on the analytical balance.

03

Thermal Equilibration in Constant Bath

Fill pycnometer with x_EtOH = 0.05 solution; immerse in 25.000 °C bath for 30 minutes; seat capillary stopper and wipe exterior dry.

04

Precision Density Determination

Weigh filled pycnometer W_1; compute solution density ρ = (W_1 - W_0) / V_pyc corrected for air buoyancy.

05

Apparent Molar Volume Calculation

Compute apparent molar volume phi_V = (M_2 / ρ) - [(ρ - ρ_1^*) / (m · ρ · ρ_1^*)] for each composition.

06

Composition Step & Infinite Dilution Regression

Repeat density measurements for remaining mixtures; plot phi_V versus x_EtOH to determine infinite-dilution volume phi_V0 and slope S.

THEORY AND UNIT OPERATIONS CONTEXT

Process engineering background & physical principles.

In chemical solution thermodynamics, extensive thermodynamic properties—including total volume V, enthalpy H, entropy S, and Gibbs free energy G—depend not only on temperature and pressure but also on mixture composition. For a non-ideal binary mixture, the total volume is not simply the sum of the pure component volumes due to differences in molecular packing, interstitial cavity filling, and intermolecular hydrogen bonding. The partial molar volume of component i, defined as V_bar_i = (∂V / ∂n_i)_{T, P, n_j≠i}, represents the change in total mixture volume per mole of component i added at constant temperature, pressure, and remaining component amounts. By Euler's theorem for homogeneous functions of degree 1, the total volume of a binary solution containing n_1 moles of solvent (water) and n_2 moles of solute (ethanol) is given by: V = n_1 · V_bar_1 + n_2 · V_bar_2. Dividing by total moles yields the mean molar volume: V_m = x_1 · V_bar_1 + x_2 · V_bar_2. In dilute and moderately concentrated solutions, experimental data are analyzed through the apparent molar volume phi_V: phi_V = (V - n_1 · V_1^*) / n_2 = (M_2 / ρ) - [(ρ - ρ_1^*) / (m · ρ · ρ_1^*)], where M_2 is solute molar mass, m is molality, and ρ and ρ_1^* are solution and pure solvent densities. In the dilute alcohol concentration regime (x_EtOH = 0.05 to 0.30), the apparent molar volume increases linearly with mole fraction according to the Redlich–Meyer and Mason relations: phi_V = phi_V0 + S · x_EtOH, where phi_V0 is the infinite-dilution partial molar volume of ethanol in water (representing an isolated ethanol molecule surrounded purely by a cage of hydrogen-bonded water molecules, often exhibiting hydrophobic hydration) and S is the volumetric interaction slope. In this laboratory investigation, precision Gay-Lussac pycnometers and capillary dilatometers are operated at 25.000 ± 0.005 °C to measure densities across x_EtOH from 0.05 to 0.30, evaluate phi_V, and determine the infinite-dilution volume phi_V0 = 54.2000 cm^3/mol and slope S = 14.5000 cm^3/mol.
