Aerodynamic and physical foundations of Oblique Shock Polar.

When a supersonic stream encounters a two-dimensional concave corner or a sharp compression wedge, the fluid streamlines cannot turn abruptly without an acoustic signal propagating upstream. Because disturbance waves in supersonic flow are confined behind the Mach cone, the required flow deflection is accomplished through an attached oblique shock wave inclined at an angle beta relative to the upstream flow vector. The component of upstream velocity perpendicular to the shock wave front, V_n1 = V_1 * sin(beta), is supersonic and undergoes normal shock compression, while the tangential component V_t1 = V_1 * cos(beta) remains completely unaffected across the discontinuity. Applying conservation of mass, momentum, and energy across the inclined shock yields the classical theta-beta-M relation: tan(theta) = 2 * cot(beta) * [(M_1^2 * sin^2(beta) - 1) / (M_1^2 * (gamma + cos(2*beta)) + 2)]. For a given upstream Mach number M_1, there exists a maximum deflection angle theta_max beyond which the shock wave detaches from the wedge tip and bows upstream. In this experiment, a supersonic wind tunnel test section operating at Mach M_1 = 2.50 houses a modular variable-deflection wedge. Calibrated optical Schlieren imaging directly measures the shock angle beta as wedge deflection angle theta increases from 5 to 17.5 degrees, validating the linear weak-shock deflection gradient dbeta / dtheta.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

How does an oblique shock wave turn supersonic flow physically?

The normal velocity component V_n1 decelerates across the shock to subsonic V_n2, while the tangential velocity component V_t remains completely unchanged. Because the velocity vector's normal component is shortened while its tangential component is preserved, the resultant velocity vector turns toward the shock front by angle theta.

What is the physical meaning of the Mach angle mu = arcsin(1/M_1)?

The Mach angle mu is the inclination angle of an infinitely weak sound wave (acoustic Mach wave) generated by a vanishingly small disturbance (theta approaches 0). As wedge deflection theta increases from zero, the oblique shock angle beta increases above the Mach angle mu.

What happens when the wedge deflection angle theta exceeds theta_max?

When theta exceeds theta_max, the oblique shock wave can no longer satisfy boundary conditions as an attached planar shock. It detaches from the wedge tip and moves upstream, forming a curved detached bow shock with a subsonic stagnation zone behind its central normal shock core.

What is the difference between the weak shock solution and the strong shock solution?

For every deflection angle theta < theta_max, the theta-beta-M equation yields two possible shock angles: a smaller angle beta (weak shock) where downstream flow is typically supersonic, and a larger angle beta (strong shock) where downstream flow is subsonic. In unconfined external flows, nature almost always establishes the weak shock solution.

Why do supersonic aircraft intakes use multi-shock ramps instead of a single normal shock?

A series of oblique shock waves decelerates supersonic flow progressively with dramatically lower total pressure loss than a single normal shock at high Mach numbers. Total pressure recovery across multiple weak oblique shocks is vastly superior, dramatically improving engine thrust and fuel efficiency.

How does the static pressure jump across an oblique shock compare to a normal shock?

An oblique shock produces a smaller static pressure jump than a normal shock at the same free-stream Mach number because only the normal component M_1 * sin(beta) governs the jump. The static pressure ratio is P_2 / P_1 = 1 + [2*gamma / (gamma+1)] * (M_1^2 * sin^2(beta) - 1).

Experimental precautions and wind tunnel safety

  • Ensure the wedge leading edge is razor-sharp; a blunt leading edge causes local shock detachment and a curved detached bow shock.
  • Verify that optical windows are high-purity schlieren-grade quartz free of internal mechanical stress birefringence.
  • Avoid operating near the shock detachment angle theta_max where shock unsteadiness can damage the wind tunnel model.
  • Confirm that reflected shocks from tunnel walls do not impinge upon the wedge surface within the measurement zone.

Theoretical assumptions & flow limits

  • Leading edge boundary layer displacement thickness slightly increases the effective deflection angle theta by ~0.3 degrees.
  • Wedge sidewall leakage can produce weak expansion waves that slightly curve the three-dimensional shock ends.
  • Assumes steady, two-dimensional, inviscid supersonic flow over a semi-infinite planar wedge.
  • Optical refraction through window boundary layers introduces a +/- 0.1 degree uncertainty in shock angle measurement.
