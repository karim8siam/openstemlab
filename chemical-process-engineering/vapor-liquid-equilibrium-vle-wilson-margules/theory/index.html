Chemical engineering transport & equilibrium foundations of VLE Thermodynamic Consistency.

In chemical engineering thermodynamics, accurate vapor-liquid equilibrium (VLE) data are indispensable for designing, simulating, and optimizing distillation columns, flash drums, and reactive separation units. Ideal solutions follow Raoult's law: P · y_i = x_i · P_i^sat, where total system pressure P and vapor mole fraction y_i are governed purely by liquid mole fraction x_i and pure-component saturation vapor pressure P_i^sat. However, real chemical mixtures—particularly polar, hydrogen-bonding mixtures such as ethanol and water—exhibit pronounced deviations from ideal solution behavior due to molecular size disparities and asymmetric intermolecular forces. These non-idealities are captured through liquid-phase activity coefficients γ_i: P · y_i · Φ_i = x_i · γ_i · P_i^sat · φ_i^sat · exp[V_i^L (P - P_i^sat) / (R T)], where Φ_i is the vapor fugacity coefficient and the exponential term is the Poynting correction factor. At atmospheric pressure (101.325 kPa), vapor-phase non-idealities and Poynting factors are negligible, reducing the equilibrium relation to the modified Raoult's law: P · y_i = x_i · γ_i · P_i^sat(T). The excess Gibbs free energy G^E represents the thermodynamic penalty or gain of mixing relative to an ideal solution: G^E / (R T) = Σ [x_i · ln(γ_i)]. Classical excess Gibbs energy models include the two-suffix Margules equation, the van Laar equation, and local composition formulations such as the Wilson, NRTL (Non-Random Two-Liquid), and UNIQUAC models. In the Margules formulation, the activity coefficient of the volatile solute is expressed as ln(γ_1) = A_12 · (1 - x_1)^2, which rearranges to the linear form ln(γ_1) / (1 - x_1) = A_12 · x_1. Furthermore, experimental VLE datasets must satisfy the Gibbs–Duhem thermodynamic consistency equation: Σ [x_i · d(ln γ_i)] = 0 at constant temperature and pressure. Area tests (such as the Redlich–Kister integral criterion) and point-to-point consistency tests verify whether measured equilibrium compositions are physically sound or distorted by experimental systematic errors. In this laboratory investigation, an all-glass recirculating Othmer VLE still is operated across ethanol liquid mole fractions from 0.10 to 0.60 at 101.325 kPa to determine equilibrium temperatures, vapor compositions, and the Margules interaction parameter A_12 = 0.7250.

Phase Equilibrium Thermodynamics & Chemical Potential Equality

Phase equilibrium in multicomponent chemical systems is governed by the second law of thermodynamics. For a closed system containing $C$ chemical species partitioned across vapor ($V$) and liquid ($L$) phases at uniform temperature $T$ and pressure $P$, thermodynamic equilibrium is established when the chemical potential of every species $i$ is identical in both coexisting phases:

\[ \mu_i^L(T, P, \mathbf{x}) = \mu_i^V(T, P, \mathbf{y}), \quad \forall i = 1, 2, \dots, C \]

Because absolute chemical potentials diverge logarithmically at infinite dilution, chemical engineers express phase equilibrium through fugacity $\hat{f}_i$, defined by G. N. Lewis as $d\mu_i = R T \, d(\ln \hat{f}_i)$ at constant temperature, with the ideal gas reference state $\lim_{P \to 0} (\hat{f}_i / (y_i P)) = 1$. The equilibrium condition is therefore stated as fugacity equality:

\[ \hat{f}_i^L(T, P, \mathbf{x}) = \hat{f}_i^V(T, P, \mathbf{y}) \]

Liquid-Phase Activity Coefficients & Modified Raoult's Law

In the vapor phase, the fugacity is related to total pressure and vapor composition by the vapor fugacity coefficient $\hat{\Phi}_i^V$:

\[ \hat{f}_i^V = y_i \, P \, \hat{\Phi}_i^V \]

In the liquid phase, fugacity is described relative to the pure liquid standard state $f_i^\circ = P_i^{sat} \, \phi_i^{sat} \, \exp\left[ \frac{V_i^L (P - P_i^{sat})}{R T} \right]$ by introducing the liquid-phase activity coefficient $\gamma_i$:

\[ \hat{f}_i^L = x_i \, \gamma_i \, P_i^{sat} \, \phi_i^{sat} \, \exp\left[ \frac{V_i^L (P - P_i^{sat})}{R T} \right] \]

At atmospheric pressure ($P = 101.325\text{ kPa}$), the vapor behaves as an ideal gas mixture ($\hat{\Phi}_i^V \approx 1$, $\phi_i^{sat} \approx 1$), and the Poynting correction factor $\exp[V_i^L(P - P_i^{sat})/(RT)] \approx 1.000$, simplifying equilibrium to the modified Raoult's law:

\[ y_i \, P = x_i \, \gamma_i \, P_i^{sat}(T) \implies \gamma_i = \frac{y_i \, P}{x_i \, P_i^{sat}(T)} \]

Saturation vapor pressure $P_i^{sat}(T)$ is computed from the three-parameter Antoine equation:

\[ \ln P_i^{sat} = A_i - \frac{B_i}{T + C_i} \]

Excess Gibbs Energy Models & Margules Parameter Formulation

The non-ideality of the liquid mixture is represented by the molar excess Gibbs free energy $G^E$:

\[ \frac{G^E}{R T} = \sum_{i=1}^C x_i \ln \gamma_i = x_1 \ln \gamma_1 + x_2 \ln \gamma_2 \]

The symmetric two-suffix Margules model represents excess Gibbs energy as a quadratic function of composition:

\[ \frac{G^E}{R T} = A_{12} \, x_1 \, x_2 = A_{12} \, x_1 \, (1 - x_1) \]

Differentiating $n_t G^E / (R T)$ with respect to mole numbers $n_1$ and $n_2$ yields the activity coefficients:

\[ \ln \gamma_1 = A_{12} \, x_2^2 = A_{12} \, (1 - x_1)^2 \] \[ \ln \gamma_2 = A_{12} \, x_1^2 \]

Dividing $\ln \gamma_1$ by $(1 - x_1)$ produces the linearized diagnostic expression:

\[ \frac{\ln \gamma_1}{1 - x_1} = A_{12} \, (1 - x_1) \quad \text{or directly against composition: } \frac{\ln \gamma_1}{1 - x_1} = A_{12} \, x_1 \]

Plotting the activity factor against liquid mole fraction provides a direct linear regression to extract the binary Margules interaction parameter $A_{12} = 0.7250$.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What is the difference between positive and negative deviations from Raoult's law?

Positive deviations (γ_i > 1) occur when unlike molecular interactions (A-B) are weaker and more repulsive than like interactions (A-A or B-B), promoting volatilization and often forming minimum-boiling azeotropes; negative deviations (γ_i < 1) indicate strong unlike attractions (e.g. hydrogen bonding between chloroform and acetone), reducing volatility and producing maximum-boiling azeotropes.

How does the Gibbs–Duhem equation enforce thermodynamic consistency on binary VLE data?

At constant T and P, the Gibbs–Duhem relation x_1 d(ln γ_1) + x_2 d(ln γ_2) = 0 requires that changes in the two activity coefficients are strictly coupled; an area or point test integrates this constraint across composition, where any significant non-zero net area reveals systematic experimental measurement error.

Why is an Othmer still preferred over a simple batch distillation flask for VLE measurements?

Simple distillation produces continuous fractional depletion and shifting liquid composition; an Othmer still recirculates both the condensed vapor and returning liquid, maintaining constant macroscopic liquid composition until true dynamic and phase equilibrium is reached.

What is an azeotrope and why cannot it be separated by conventional distillation?

An azeotrope is a thermodynamic state where the equilibrium vapor composition is identical to the liquid composition (y_i = x_i) and the relative volatility α_12 = 1.0; at this pinch point, boiling produces vapor of identical composition, preventing further separation by ordinary distillation.

How do the Wilson and Margules models differ in handling liquid-liquid immiscibility?

The Margules equation is capable of predicting both vapor-liquid and liquid-liquid phase splits; the classic Wilson equation cannot predict liquid-liquid immiscibility (phase separation) because its mathematical form always exhibits positive second derivatives of excess Gibbs energy.

How does barometric pressure variation affect measured VLE equilibrium temperatures?

Boiling temperatures vary directly with ambient barometric pressure according to the Clausius–Clapeyron equation; deviations of ±1 kPa from standard 101.325 kPa shift ethanol boiling point by ~0.35 °C, requiring continuous barometric pressure logging and Antoine temperature correction.

Experimental precautions and process safety

  • Ensure ethanol-water sampling syringes are pre-chilled to prevent flash evaporation of volatile ethanol during transfer to the refractometer.
  • Verify condenser coolant flow is established before turning on mantle heating to prevent vapor escape and flammability hazards.
  • Maintain steady boiling without rapid boil-up surges to avoid entraining liquid droplets into the vapor condensation chamber.
  • Confirm total recirculation equilibrium by ensuring boiling temperature remains strictly constant for at least 15 minutes before sampling.

Theoretical assumptions & boundary condition limits

  • The transport and thermodynamic models apply within the specified calibrated operating window (0.1 to 0.6 mol/mol).
  • Assumes ideal continuum flow and homogeneous phase mixing without unmodeled parasitic wall losses.
  • Continuous sensor readings reflect ordinary least-squares regression across successive observations.
  • High-temperature or extreme concentration excursions require non-ideal equation-of-state corrections.
