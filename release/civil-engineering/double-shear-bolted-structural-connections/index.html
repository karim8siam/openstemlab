FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

Determine the shear load-deformation response, double-shear capacity, and joint shear compliance of high-strength structural bolted steel connections according to ASTM A325 / AISC 360. Standardized parameter settings: 20.0, 40.0, 60.0, 80.0, 100.0, 120.0 kN.

01

Joint Plate & Bolt Dimensional Inspection

Measure bolt diameter d (16.0 mm), plate thicknesses, and hole diameters (18.0 mm standard hole) using digital callipers to 0.01 mm.

02

Joint Assembly & Torque Tightening

Assemble main plate between dual splice plates; insert bolt with hardened washers beneath bolt head and nut; tighten nut with torque wrench to standard pretension (100 N·m).

03

UTM Grip Installation & Alignment

Mount connection assembly into hydraulic wedge grips of the UTM; ensure strict axial alignment to eliminate out-of-plane bending.

04

LVDT Sensor Mounting & Zeroing

Attach dual LVDT displacement gauges across the main plate/splice plate interface to monitor relative shear displacement; tare electronic channels.

05

Monotonic Tensile Loading

Apply tensile load P at a steady rate of 5.0 kN/min; log joint shear deformation Δ at 20.0, 40.0, 60.0, 80.0, 100.0, and 120.0 kN.

06

Shear Stress & Compliance Calculation

Calculate average double shear stress τ = P / (2 A_b); plot Δ versus P to determine joint shear compliance slope.

THEORY AND STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

Civil engineering background & physical principles.

Bolted connections represent the primary joining technique in structural steel buildings, industrial truss bridges, and offshore platforms, enabling rapid on-site erection without specialized field welding. Structural bolts are subjected to shear in two primary connection configurations: single shear (lap joints) and double shear (butt joints with dual splice plates). Double shear connections are vastly superior because the symmetric dual splice plates eliminate eccentric bending moments and prying forces, doubling the effective shear resistance area. Under ASTM A325 / AISC 360, when a tensile load P is applied across a double-shear butt splice joined by a bolt of nominal diameter d, the load is resisted by two parallel shear failure planes intersecting the bolt shank. The average nominal shear stress on each shear plane is τ = P / (2 A_b) = P / [2 (π d^2 / 4)]. Prior to slip, friction between tightly clamped plate surfaces resists the load (slip-critical connection); once clamping friction is overcome, the bolt goes into direct mechanical bearing against hole walls, exhibiting an elastic joint shear deformation Δ proportional to applied load P. In this laboratory investigation, double-shear bolted joints are tested across load levels from 20 to 120 kN to evaluate joint shear compliance and determine shear performance.
