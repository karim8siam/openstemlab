Physical chemistry assumptions and metrological limits

The potentiometric technique is subject to specific metrological boundaries: (1) Mixed potentials near endpoints: At the very beginning (V < 1.0 mL) and very close to equivalence (V ≈ V_eq), the concentration of one redox partner is minuscule (< 10⁻⁶ M), leading to low exchange current densities (j₀ < 10⁻⁸ A/cm²) where trace dissolved oxygen or electrode surface oxide films create mixed potentials that take several minutes to stabilize. (2) Liquid junction potentials: The junction potential across the reference electrode porous frit contributes a small offset (~2 to 5 mV) that is absorbed into the formal potential E°'. (3) Complexation equilibria: In 1.0 M H₂SO₄, extensive ion-pairing (such as FeSO₄⁺, Fe(SO₄)₂⁻, Ce(SO₄)₂ and Ce(SO₄)₃²⁻) shifts formal potentials from standard potentials E° in water. (4) Temperature coefficient: The formal potential has a temperature dependence of ~0.5 mV/°C, requiring bath regulation to ±0.05 K. The potentiometric titration of iron(II) with cerium(IV) in acidic medium (1.00 M H₂SO₄) proceeds through the rapid, reversible one-electron redox equilibrium: Fe²⁺(aq) + Ce⁴⁺(aq) ⇄ Fe³⁺(aq) + Ce³⁺(aq). The equilibrium constant K_eq = exp[F (E°'_Ce − E°'_Fe) / (RT)] exceeds 10¹² in 1.0 M sulfuric acid, rendering the reaction strictly quantitative. The electromotive force (EMF) of an inert platinum indicator electrode versus a reference electrode tracks the thermodynamic activity ratio of the governing redox couples. Throughout the pre-equivalence buffer region (0 < V < V_eq), the potential is established by the Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ couple according to the Nernst equation: E = E°'(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) + (RT/F) ln([Fe³⁺]/[Fe²⁺]). Stoichiometric conversion dictates [Fe³⁺] ∝ V and [Fe²⁺] ∝ (V_eq − V), where V is the volume of standardized Ce⁴⁺ added and V_eq is the equivalence point volume. Substituting stoichiometric molarities yields: E = E°'(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) + (RT/F) ln[V / (V_eq − V)]. At the half-neutralization point (V = V_eq / 2 = 12.50 mL), [Fe³⁺] = [Fe²⁺] and the logarithmic term vanishes, establishing E_1/2 = E°'(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) = 680.0 mV versus the standard hydrogen electrode (SHE). Linear least-squares regression of measured potential E against ln[V / (V_eq − V)] yields an empirical slope equal to the thermal Nernstian factor RT/F = 25.69 mV at 298.15 K and an intercept providing the formal reduction potential E°' directly. At the equivalence point (V = V_eq = 25.00 mL), both redox couples achieve dynamic equilibrium at equal stoichiometric concentrations, yielding an equivalence potential E_eq = [E°'(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) + E°'(Ce⁴⁺/Ce³⁺)] / 2 = (680.0 + 1440.0) / 2 = 1060.0 mV vs SHE. Beyond the equivalence point (V > V_eq), excess cerium establishes the potential via E = E°'(Ce⁴⁺/Ce³⁺) + (RT/F) ln[(V − V_eq) / V_eq].

Precision Water-Jacketed Titration Beaker (150 mL)

Contains the 50.00 mL acidic reaction mixture surrounded by an outer liquid jacket connected to a circulating thermostatic bath maintained at 298.15 ± 0.05 K.

Bright Platinum Wire Redox Indicator Electrode

Provides an inert, electrochemically active metallic surface for rapid, unpolarized electron exchange with the dissolved Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ and Ce⁴⁺/Ce³⁺ redox couples.

Double-Junction Silver/Silver Chloride Reference Electrode (3.0 M KCl)

Furnishes a stable, reproducible half-cell potential (+210.0 mV vs SHE at 25 °C) equipped with an outer 1.0 M KNO₃ salt bridge to prevent chloride migration into sulfate media.

Class A Precision 50.00 mL Digital/Burette System

High-Impedance Digital pH/mV Electrometer / Data Logger

FOLLOW THE METHOD

A reason behind every step.

To perform a high-precision potentiometric redox titration of iron(II) with cerium(IV) in sulfuric acid, construct the experimental potentiometric titration curve, determine the equivalence volume V_eq, and evaluate the formal reduction potential E°' of the Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ redox couple from the Nernstian logarithmic linearization at 298.15 K. Suggested independent volumetric settings: 5.0, 8.0, 10.0, 12.5, 15.0, 20.0 mL.

01

Electrode Conditioning and Potentiometer Calibration

Inspect the platinum wire electrode; clean by immersing in warm 3 M HNO₃ for 2 minutes followed by rinsing with Milli-Q water. Calibrate the high-impedance millivoltmeter against a certified precision voltage standard.

02

Thermostatic Bath Setup and Analyte Delivery

Circulate water at 298.15 K (25.00 °C) through the jacketed cell. Accurately pipette 25.00 mL of standardized 0.0500 M FeSO₄ stock solution and 25.00 mL of 2.00 M H₂SO₄ into the cell (total volume 50.00 mL, [H₂SO₄] = 1.00 M). Engage magnetic stirring at 400 RPM.

03

Burette Preparation and Baseline Potential Acquisition

Rinse and fill the Class A burette with standardized 0.0500 M Ce(SO₄)₂ titrant. Mount the platinum indicator and reference electrodes into the solution. Record the initial steady baseline potential of the unoxidized iron(II) solution (E_initial ≈ 480 mV vs SHE).

04

Pre-Equivalence Titration and Logarithmic Data Logging

Add titrant in controlled aliquots (5.00, 8.00, 10.00, 12.50, 15.00, and 20.00 mL). Allow 30 seconds after each addition for potentiometric stabilization (dE/dt < 0.2 mV/min) and record the equilibrium potential E_meas.

05

Equivalence Point Passage and Post-Equivalence Profiling

Continue titrating dropwise across the equivalence zone (24.0 to 26.0 mL) to capture the steep inflection jump (ΔE > +400 mV), then record post-equivalence potentials up to 35.0 mL to establish the cerium plateau.

06

Nernstian Linearization and Formal Potential Evaluation

Compute the Nernstian logarithmic coordinate ln[V / (V_eq − V)] for each pre-equivalence addition. Plot measured cell potential E versus ln[V / (V_eq − V)], execute linear least-squares regression, and determine the formal reduction potential E°'(Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) from the Y-intercept.
