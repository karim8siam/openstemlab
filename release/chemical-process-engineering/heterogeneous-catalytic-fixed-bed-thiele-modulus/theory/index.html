Chemical engineering transport & equilibrium foundations of Heterogeneous Catalysis & Thiele Modulus.

Heterogeneous solid-catalyzed reactions represent the backbone of the chemical and petrochemical industries, accounting for more than 80% of all chemical manufacturing processes (including ammonia synthesis, catalytic reforming, Fischer–Tropsch fuels, and automotive emissions catalytic converters). In heterogeneous catalysis, porous catalyst pellets provide enormous internal active surface area (100 to 500 m^2/g) inside an intricate network of microscopic pores. A gaseous reactant molecule must undergo five sequential transport and chemical steps: (1) external convective mass transfer across the fluid boundary layer to the external pellet surface, (2) internal Knudsen and molecular diffusion through fluid-filled pores to interior catalytic sites, (3) chemisorption onto active metal sites, (4) intrinsic surface chemical reaction, and (5) desorption and back-diffusion of products. When internal pore diffusion is slower than intrinsic surface kinetics, a steep reactant concentration gradient develops inside the pellet: reactant molecules are consumed before they can penetrate into the deep pellet core, leaving interior catalyst unutilized. Ernest W. Thiele (1939) formulated the celebrated Thiele modulus: φ = L_p · sqrt(k_v / D_eff), where L_p = R_p / 3 is the characteristic diffusion length, k_v is the intrinsic volumetric reaction rate constant, and D_eff is effective pore diffusivity. The ratio of actual overall reaction rate to the ideal rate that would occur if the entire pellet interior were exposed to surface concentration C_s is termed the internal effectiveness factor: η = tanh(φ) / φ. Under strong pore diffusion limitations (φ > 3), the effectiveness factor scales inversely with pellet diameter: η ≈ 1 / φ ∝ 1 / d_p, meaning reciprocal effectiveness scales linearly with pellet diameter: 1 / η = S · d_p + 1.0. In this laboratory investigation, an isothermal packed-bed microreactor is tested with catalyst pellets of diameters d_p from 1.0 to 6.0 mm to measure 1/η vs d_p, verify Thiele pore diffusion theory, and determine the diffusion limitation slope S = 0.3500 mm^-1.

1. Reaction and Diffusion in Porous Heterogeneous Catalysts

Heterogeneous catalytic processes rely on porous pellets possessing immense internal surface area ($> 100\text{ m}^2/\text{g}$). Because reaction occurs primarily on pore walls, reactants must diffuse into the interior while products diffuse out. Intra-particle transport is modeled using Fick's law with effective diffusivity $D_{eff}$:

\[ D_{eff} = \frac{\varepsilon_p}{\tau_p} D_{AB} \]

where $\varepsilon_p$ is pellet porosity and $\tau_p$ is pore tortuosity.

2. The Thiele Modulus & Effectiveness Factor

A steady-state mass balance on a spherical pellet of radius $R_p$ conducting a first-order irreversible reaction $-r_A = k_v C_A$ yields:

\[ \frac{1}{r^2} \frac{d}{dr} \left( r^2 D_{eff} \frac{dC_A}{dr} \right) = k_v C_A \]

Ernest W. Thiele (1939) solved this boundary value problem, defining the dimensionless Thiele modulus $\phi$:

\[ \phi = R_p \sqrt{\frac{k_v}{D_{eff}}} = \left( \frac{d_p}{2} \right) \sqrt{\frac{k_v}{D_{eff}}} \]

The internal effectiveness factor $\eta$ is the ratio of actual reaction rate to the ideal rate at surface concentration $C_s$:

\[ \eta = \frac{\text{Actual Rate}}{\text{Rate at } C_s} = \frac{3}{\phi} \left( \frac{1}{\tanh \phi} - \frac{1}{\phi} \right) \]

3. Asymptotic Diffusion Limitation & Linear Model

For moderate to strong diffusion resistance ($\phi > 2$), the reciprocal effectiveness factor scales linearly with pellet diameter $d_p$:

\[ \frac{1}{\eta} = S \cdot d_p + 1.0000 \]

where $S = \frac{1}{6} \sqrt{k_v / D_{eff}}$ is the diffusion limitation slope in $\text{mm}^{-1}$. Plotting $1 / \eta$ versus $d_p$ yields a straight line with intercept $1.0000$, from which the intrinsic reaction-diffusion parameter group is directly determined.

Viva voce questions & analytical theory

What is the physical interpretation of the internal effectiveness factor η?

The effectiveness factor η is the ratio of actual overall reaction rate in the porous pellet to the ideal rate that would occur if the entire internal surface area were exposed to the external surface concentration and temperature with zero diffusion resistance.

What is the Weisz–Prater criterion for diagnosing pore diffusion limitations?

The Weisz–Prater criterion C_WP = η φ^2 = (r_obs R_p^2) / (D_eff C_s) evaluates diffusion limitations using purely observable quantities; if C_WP < 1, pore diffusion resistance is negligible (η ≈ 1); if C_WP >> 1, severe internal diffusion limitations exist.

How does Knudsen diffusion differ from molecular bulk diffusion in catalyst pores?

In small micropores (< 10 nm) where mean free path of gas molecules exceeds pore diameter, molecules collide predominantly with pore walls rather than with other gas molecules; Knudsen diffusivity D_K scales with sqrt(T / M) and pore radius, independent of pressure.

What is the trade-off in choosing catalyst pellet diameter for industrial reactors?

Smaller pellets minimize internal diffusion resistance and maximize effectiveness factor η, but generate massive hydraulic pressure drop across the bed (Ergun equation ΔP ∝ 1/d_p^2); larger pellets lower pressure drop but suffer low internal catalyst utilization.

How does internal pore diffusion affect apparent reaction order and activation energy?

Under strong pore diffusion limitations (φ > 3), the observed apparent reaction order shifts to (n + 1) / 2, and the observed apparent activation energy is halved: E_app ≈ E_true / 2.

What is an egg-shell (rim-type) catalyst and why is it manufactured?

In egg-shell catalysts, expensive precious metal (e.g., Pt, Pd) is deposited only in a thin outer rim of the pellet, leaving the core inert; this avoids wasting precious metal in interior regions where reactants cannot diffuse.

Experimental precautions and process safety

  • Maintain strictly isothermal conditions (ΔT < 0.5 °C) across the bed using low reactant concentrations in diluent gas.
  • Ensure gas space velocity WHSV is held identical across all pellet sizes to eliminate space time bias.
  • Check that external film mass transfer resistance is negligible by verifying conversion is independent of linear gas velocity at constant WHSV.
  • Handle catalyst pellets gently to avoid crushing or creating fines that distort size classification.

Theoretical assumptions & boundary condition limits

  • The transport and thermodynamic models apply within the specified calibrated operating window (1.0 to 6.0 mm).
  • Assumes ideal continuum flow and homogeneous phase mixing without unmodeled parasitic wall losses.
  • Continuous sensor readings reflect ordinary least-squares regression across successive observations.
  • High-temperature or extreme concentration excursions require non-ideal equation-of-state corrections.
