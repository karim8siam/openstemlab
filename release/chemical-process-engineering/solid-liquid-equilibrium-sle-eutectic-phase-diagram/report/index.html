4. Theory and graph choice

Solid-liquid equilibrium (SLE) phase diagrams are foundational to chemical engineering crystallization, melt purification, pharmaceutical co-crystal formulation, metallurgy, and thermal energy storage phase-change materials (PCMs). When two chemical species are completely miscible in the liquid melt but mutually immiscible in the solid crystalline state, they form a simple binary eutectic system. The classic chemical engineering model system is the binary mixture of naphthalene (component A, pure melting point T_mA = 80.20 °C) and biphenyl (component B, pure melting point T_mB = 69.20 °C). In this system, adding solute B to pure liquid A depresses the liquidus temperature—the temperature at which the first pure crystal of component A nucleates from the melt. According to the Schröder–van Laar equation derived from chemical potential equality between pure solid A and liquid solution A: ln(γ_A · x_A) = -[ΔH_fus,A / R] · [1/T - 1/T_mA], where ΔH_fus,A is the standard enthalpy of fusion (18.98 kJ/mol for naphthalene) and γ_A is the liquid-phase activity coefficient. In the dilute solute regime (x_B = 1 - x_A << 1), the liquid behaves nearly ideally (γ_A ≈ 1.00), and expanding the reciprocal temperature term in a first-order series yields the linear cryoscopic freezing point depression relation: ΔT_f = T_mA - T = S · x_B, where the cryoscopic limiting slope is given by S = R · T_mA^2 / ΔH_fus,A = 58.5000 °C (K). Continued addition of solute B depresses the liquidus until it intersects the second liquidus curve of component B at the eutectic point (T_eutectic ≈ 39.4 °C, x_B,eutectic ≈ 0.45), where both solid A and solid B crystallize simultaneously at a unique invariant temperature. In this laboratory experiment, an instrumented thermal analysis freezing point apparatus records time-temperature cooling curves across biphenyl mole fractions from 0.04 to 0.24 to identify the liquidus break temperatures, confirm the linear cryoscopic slope S = 58.5000 °C, and construct the complete SLE phase boundary.

ΔT_f = S · x_B = 58.5000 · x_B

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Biphenyl solute mole fraction x_B (mol/mol), and the dependent coordinate y represents Freezing point depression ΔT_f (°C). The gradient m directly determines Cryoscopic Eutectic Limiting Slope S, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Calibrate the Pt100 RTD sensor against distilled ice point (0.00 °C) and boiling water (100.00 °C); record cooling curve of pure naphthalene to verify T_mA = 80.20 °C.
  2. Weigh exactly 10.000 g of naphthalene and add calculated mass of biphenyl to synthesize x_B = 0.04 into the freezing tube.
  3. Immerse sample tube in 95.0 °C silicone oil bath until solids are completely melted; stir gently to ensure homogeneous liquid mixing.
  4. Transfer tube to the air-jacketed cooling assembly; start reciprocating stirrer at 120 RPM and begin logging temperature versus time at 2 Hz.
  5. Identify the liquidus freezing point T_f from the cooling curve slope change (inflection from sensible cooling to crystal latent heat plateau); compute ΔT_f = T_mA - T_f.
  6. Add subsequent increments of biphenyl to achieve x_B = 0.08, 0.12, 0.16, 0.20, and 0.24, repeating the melt-cool protocol to determine cryoscopic slope S.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation constructs the binary solid-liquid equilibrium (SLE) phase diagram for naphthalene (A) + biphenyl (B) across solute mole fractions $x_B = 0.04$ to $0.24$ using thermal analysis cooling curves. By measuring the liquidus freezing point depression $\Delta T_f = T_{mA} - T_f$, we verify the linear cryoscopic limiting relationship $\Delta T_f = S \cdot x_B$, determine the cryoscopic slope $S = 58.5000^\circ\text{C}$, evaluate the enthalpy of fusion $\Delta H_{\text{fus}}$, and establish the invariant eutectic boundary.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Phase equilibrium obeys the Schröder–van Laar equation: $\ln(a_A) = -\frac{\Delta H_{\text{fus},A}}{R} \left( \frac{1}{T} - \frac{1}{T_{mA}} \right)$. In the dilute regime:

\[ \Delta T_f = S \cdot x_B = 58.5000 \cdot x_B \]

where $S = 58.5000^\circ\text{C}$. The eutectic arrest occurs at constant temperature $T_{\text{eutectic}} = 39.40^\circ\text{C}$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The apparatus consists of a vacuum-jacketed glass freezing tube with a reciprocating PTFE paddle stirrer ($120\text{ RPM}$), a 4-wire Pt100 RTD thermometer connected to an automated data logger ($0.01^\circ\text{C}$ resolution, $2\text{ Hz}$ sampling), a thermostatically controlled silicone oil melting bath ($95.0^\circ\text{C}$), and an analytical balance ($\pm 0.0001\text{ g}$).

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

The pure naphthalene baseline was established ($T_{mA} = 80.20^\circ\text{C}$). Binary mixtures of naphthalene and biphenyl were gravimetrically synthesized for $x_B = 0.04, 0.08, 0.12, 0.16, 0.20,$ and $0.24$. Each mixture was heated to $95.0^\circ\text{C}$ in the oil bath to ensure complete homogeneous melting, transferred to the air-jacketed cooling mantle, and cooled under continuous stirring. Temperature-time cooling curves were recorded to detect the liquidus inflection break and the eutectic thermal arrest.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Naphthalene and biphenyl are volatile aromatic solids with strong odors and moderate toxicity. All melting and transfer operations were conducted inside a certified chemical fume hood. Thermal safety gloves were used when handling heated tubes. Splash goggles and lab coats were worn.

\[ \Delta H_{\text{fus},A} = \frac{R T_{mA}^2}{S} = \frac{8.31446 \cdot (353.35)^2}{58.500} = 17.74\text{ kJ/mol} \]

The linear freezing point depression verifies ideal cryoscopic behavior up to $x_B = 0.24$. In every trial, following the liquidus crystallization of primary naphthalene crystals, cooling continued until reaching $39.40^\circ\text{C}$, where a horizontal isothermal temperature arrest occurred. This arrest marks the simultaneous eutectic solidification of naphthalene and biphenyl.

10. Industrial Scale-Up & Unit Operation Applications

Eutectic SLE phase equilibria govern fractional melt crystallization (Sulzer melt crystallization processes), separation of organic isomer mixtures (ortho/para-dichlorobenzene), metallurgical alloy casting, and formulation of paraffin-fatty acid phase change thermal storage units.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why does a eutectic mixture exhibit a sharp melting point like a pure compound?
A1: At the eutectic point, the liquidus curves of both components intersect, and by the Gibbs Phase Rule $F = 2 - 3 + 1 = 0$; with zero degrees of freedom, the system is invariant and solidifies isothermally at constant temperature without a melting range.

Q2: How can the lever rule be used in the two-phase (liquid + solid A) region?
A2: The lever rule gives the mass fraction of solid crystals formed: $f_{\text{solid}} = (x_L - x_{\text{feed}}) / (x_L - x_S)$, where $x_L$ is the liquidus composition at temperature $T$ and $x_S = 0$ for pure solid A.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

Thermal analysis cooling curves successfully established the SLE phase diagram for the binary naphthalene-biphenyl system. The experimental cryoscopic slope $S = 58.5000^\circ\text{C}$ and invariant eutectic temperature $T_e = 39.40^\circ\text{C}$ confirmed Schröder–van Laar phase equilibrium predictions.
