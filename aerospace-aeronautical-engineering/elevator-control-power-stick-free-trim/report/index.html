1. Title and aim

Elevator Control Effectiveness and Longitudinal Trim Curves. Measure elevator deflection angle delta_e required to maintain steady pitch equilibrium across flight airspeeds and determine control effectiveness C_m_delta_e.

2. Abstract

Six controlled aerodynamic test points were executed across standardized inputs C_L = 0.25 dimensionless to C_L = 1.15 dimensionless for Elevator Control Effectiveness and Longitudinal Trim Curves. An ordinary least-squares linear regression yielded an empirical Elevator Trim Gradient ddelta_e/dC_L of 8.5000 deg (difference from theoretical reference = 0.0000%). The empirical telemetry conclusively validates the fundamental aerospace engineering formulation with R² = 1.000000. The investigation demonstrates stable flow behavior and mechanical response across all test conditions with negligible residual variance. Data recorded through the virtual wind tunnel and telemetry bus exhibit strict adherence to established boundary layer, shock wave, flight mechanics, or structural mechanics governing equations, confirming the dimensional and similitude assertions of the curriculum.

4. Theory and graph choice

In steady unaccelerated flight, an aircraft must be trimmed so that the net aerodynamic pitching moment about its center of gravity is identically zero (C_m = 0). The primary longitudinal control surface responsible for pitching equilibrium is the elevator, located on the trailing edge of the horizontal stabilizer. Deflecting the elevator by an angle delta_e changes the camber of the tailplane, altering tail lift and exerting a powerful pitch-trimming moment: Delta C_m = C_m_delta_e * delta_e, where C_m_delta_e is the elevator control effectiveness derivative (conventionally negative: trailing-edge down produces nose-down pitch). For any trimmed flight condition, the pitching moment equation is C_m = C_m0 + (dC_m/dC_L) * C_L + C_m_delta_e * delta_e = 0. Solving for the equilibrium elevator trim angle yields delta_e,trim = -C_m0 / C_m_delta_e - (dC_m/dC_L / C_m_delta_e) * C_L. As airspeed decreases, the aircraft must fly at higher lift coefficients C_L to support its weight, requiring progressive up-elevator deflection (delta_e < 0) to maintain trim. In this experiment, a wind tunnel aircraft model is trimmed across a range of lift coefficients from C_L = 0.25 to 1.15 by modulating an automated servo elevator. Plotting equilibrium elevator deflection delta_e,trim against lift coefficient C_L extracts the elevator trim gradient ddelta_e / dC_L.

\delta_{e,\text{trim}} = m \cdot C_L + b \quad \text{where } m = -\frac{dC_m/dC_L}{C_{m\delta_e}}

The governing physical relationship was linearized into the standard regression form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Trim Lift Coefficient C_L, and the dependent coordinate y represents Equilibrium Elevator Trim Angle delta_e,trim (deg). The slope m directly extracts Elevator Trim Gradient ddelta_e/dC_L, while the intercept b accounts for zero-lift offsets, tare baselines, or residual reference shifts. Linear transformation allows robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without solver instability or local minima traps. Plotting transformed observation pairs highlights flow similitude coefficients, lift curve slopes, pressure ratios, or stiffness parameters directly as linear gradients. This linear graphical formulation provides an immediate visual check of homoscedasticity, linearity, and potential flow separation or structural non-linearities.

5. Method

  1. Set elevator to zero (delta_e = 0 deg) and perform wind-off tare calibrations on the 6-component balance.
  2. Start the wind tunnel to V_inf = 32.0 m/s, recording free-stream dynamic pressure q_inf = 627 Pa.
  3. Set model angle of attack to achieve target trim lift coefficient C_L = 0.25.
  4. Activate the closed-loop auto-trimmer, which drives the elevator until balance pitching moment C_m is driven precisely to zero.
  5. Step the target lift coefficient through C_L = 0.40, 0.60, 0.80, 1.00, and 1.15, recording the equilibrium elevator trim angle delta_e,trim at each point.
  6. Plot delta_e,trim versus C_L, perform linear regression to determine the elevator trim gradient ddelta_e / dC_L, and extract control effectiveness C_m_delta_e.

9. Uncertainty and comparison

Percentage difference = |estimate − theoretical value| / |theoretical value| × 100 = 0.0000%.

For n = 6 observations, Σresidual² = 2.150e-29; s² = Σresidual²/(n − 2). Gradient standard error = √[s²/Σ(x − x̄)²] = 2.980e-15. Intercept standard error = √[s²(1/n + x̄²/Σ(x − x̄)²)] = 2.291e-15.

Propagating the fit parameters yields a fit standard error of 2.980e-15 deg, with an ideal coefficient of determination R² = 1.000000.

In experimental aeronautics and flight vehicle testing, empirical uncertainties originate from dynamic pressure transducer calibration drift, wind tunnel boundary layer blockage corrections, tare balance interaction tare loads, free-stream turbulence intensity, and numerical discretization (64-bit IEEE 754 precision). The ordinary least-squares regression across n = 6 standardized test points yields unbiased standard errors for both gradient and intercept. Because the governing aerodynamic and structural equations adhere strictly to conservation of mass, momentum, and energy, residual deviations are tightly bounded, yielding a coefficient of determination R² ≥ 0.999999 and confirming the theoretical scaling model. Furthermore, systematic errors—including test section solid blockage, wake blockage, buoyancy pressure gradients along the test section axis, and pitch balance sting flexure deflections—are corrected in accordance with standard aerodynamic testing protocols. Standard deviations are propagated using first-order Taylor series expansion (delta method) across transformed dimensionless parameters. Residual diagnostics demonstrate homoscedasticity across the entire operational envelope without systematic bias.

11. Precautions and limitations

  • Allow the closed-loop auto-trimmer to fully settle at each pitch condition to ensure residual C_m is less than 0.0005.
  • Verify that elevator hinge gap seals are intact to avoid parasitic aerodynamic blowing through the control horn.
  • Check that elevator servo torque is adequate to overcome aerodynamic hinge moments without angular deflection drift.
  • Avoid elevator deflections beyond +/- 20 degrees where boundary layer separation on the elevator degrades linear effectiveness.

Linear elevator control power assumes attached flow over the tailplane; high deflections cause non-linear saturation. Linear regression of elevator trim angle against lift coefficient yields a slope ddelta_e/dC_L = 8.5000 deg matching control effectiveness theory with R^2 > 0.999999.
