4. Theory and graph choice

In chemical engineering thermodynamics, accurate vapor-liquid equilibrium (VLE) data are indispensable for designing, simulating, and optimizing distillation columns, flash drums, and reactive separation units. Ideal solutions follow Raoult's law: P · y_i = x_i · P_i^sat, where total system pressure P and vapor mole fraction y_i are governed purely by liquid mole fraction x_i and pure-component saturation vapor pressure P_i^sat. However, real chemical mixtures—particularly polar, hydrogen-bonding mixtures such as ethanol and water—exhibit pronounced deviations from ideal solution behavior due to molecular size disparities and asymmetric intermolecular forces. These non-idealities are captured through liquid-phase activity coefficients γ_i: P · y_i · Φ_i = x_i · γ_i · P_i^sat · φ_i^sat · exp[V_i^L (P - P_i^sat) / (R T)], where Φ_i is the vapor fugacity coefficient and the exponential term is the Poynting correction factor. At atmospheric pressure (101.325 kPa), vapor-phase non-idealities and Poynting factors are negligible, reducing the equilibrium relation to the modified Raoult's law: P · y_i = x_i · γ_i · P_i^sat(T). The excess Gibbs free energy G^E represents the thermodynamic penalty or gain of mixing relative to an ideal solution: G^E / (R T) = Σ [x_i · ln(γ_i)]. Classical excess Gibbs energy models include the two-suffix Margules equation, the van Laar equation, and local composition formulations such as the Wilson, NRTL (Non-Random Two-Liquid), and UNIQUAC models. In the Margules formulation, the activity coefficient of the volatile solute is expressed as ln(γ_1) = A_12 · (1 - x_1)^2, which rearranges to the linear form ln(γ_1) / (1 - x_1) = A_12 · x_1. Furthermore, experimental VLE datasets must satisfy the Gibbs–Duhem thermodynamic consistency equation: Σ [x_i · d(ln γ_i)] = 0 at constant temperature and pressure. Area tests (such as the Redlich–Kister integral criterion) and point-to-point consistency tests verify whether measured equilibrium compositions are physically sound or distorted by experimental systematic errors. In this laboratory investigation, an all-glass recirculating Othmer VLE still is operated across ethanol liquid mole fractions from 0.10 to 0.60 at 101.325 kPa to determine equilibrium temperatures, vapor compositions, and the Margules interaction parameter A_12 = 0.7250.

ln(γ_1) / (1 - x_1) = A_12 · x_1 = 0.7250 · x_1

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Liquid mole fraction x_1 (mol/mol), and the dependent coordinate y represents Margules factor ln(γ_1) / (1 - x_1). The gradient m directly determines Margules Binary Interaction Parameter A_12, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Calibrate the digital Abbe refractometer with HPLC-grade deionized water (n_D = 1.3330 at 20.0 °C) and pure absolute ethanol (n_D = 1.3614); verify calibration curve.
  2. Charge the boiling vessel with 250 mL of prepared binary ethanol-water solution at x_1 = 0.10; start recirculating condenser coolant at 10.0 °C.
  3. Apply 85 W heating via the Variac until gentle, continuous boiling is established; operate in total recirculation mode for 45 minutes until equilibrium boiling temperature stabilizes within ±0.02 °C.
  4. Simultaneously withdraw 0.5 mL samples of equilibrium liquid and condensed vapor using pre-chilled microsyringes; measure refractive indices at 20.0 °C to find x_1 and y_1.
  5. Increment liquid mole fraction x_1 through 0.20, 0.30, 0.40, 0.50, and 0.60 by injecting calculated aliquots of absolute ethanol, repeating equilibrium logging at each step.
  6. Calculate saturation vapor pressures P_i^sat via Antoine equations, determine activity coefficients γ_1 and γ_2, evaluate ln(γ_1) / (1 - x_1), and perform linear regression to determine A_12.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This experiment investigates vapor-liquid equilibrium (VLE) in a binary ethanol (1) + water (2) system at atmospheric pressure ($101.325\text{ kPa}$) using an all-glass recirculating Othmer equilibrium still. By measuring equilibrium boiling temperatures and vapor/liquid mole fractions across $x_1 = 0.10$ to $0.60$ via precision Abbe refractometry, we calculate liquid-phase activity coefficients $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$, test thermodynamic consistency, and determine the binary Margules interaction parameter $A_{12} = 0.7250$.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Phase equilibrium under modified Raoult's law dictates:

\[ y_1 P = x_1 \gamma_1 P_1^{sat}(T), \quad y_2 P = x_2 \gamma_2 P_2^{sat}(T) \]

The two-suffix Margules excess Gibbs energy model provides:

\[ \ln \gamma_1 = A_{12} \cdot (1 - x_1)^2 \implies \frac{\ln \gamma_1}{1 - x_1} = S \cdot x_1 = 0.7250 \cdot x_1 \]

where $S = A_{12} = 0.7250\text{ dimensionless}$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The apparatus consists of an all-glass Othmer recirculating VLE still with vacuum-jacketed boiling chamber ($250\text{ mL}$), a precision 4-wire Pt100 RTD sensor ($\pm 0.01^\circ\text{C}$), a digital Abbe refractometer (ATAGO NAR-1T, $\pm 0.0002\, n_D$), a constant-temperature recirculating bath ($10.0^\circ\text{C}$), and an electronic digital barometer ($\pm 0.01\text{ kPa}$).

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

The refractometer was calibrated at $20.0^\circ\text{C}$ using deionized water ($n_D = 1.3330$) and pure ethanol ($n_D = 1.3614$). The still was charged with $200\text{ mL}$ of mixture at $x_1 = 0.10$. Heating was applied until steady total recirculation boiling was maintained. After 45 minutes of thermal equilibrium ($\Delta T < 0.02^\circ\text{C}$ over 15 min), liquid and condensed vapor samples were withdrawn and analyzed. The mixture was stepped through $x_1 = 0.20, 0.30, 0.40, 0.50,$ and $0.60$.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Ethanol vapors are flammable. Condenser cooling was interlocked with mantle power to prevent vapor release. Sampling was performed with pre-chilled airtight syringes in a well-ventilated fume hood. Chemical splash goggles, neoprene gloves, and flame-retardant lab coats were worn.

Refractometer index uncertainty is $\pm 0.0002$ ($u(x) = \pm 0.0012$), temperature sensor uncertainty is $\pm 0.01^\circ\text{C}$ ($u(P_1^{sat}) = \pm 0.12\text{ kPa}$), and pressure uncertainty is $\pm 0.01\text{ kPa}$. 9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

The positive Margules parameter ($A_{12} = 0.7250 > 0$) confirms positive deviations from Raoult's law ($\gamma_1 > 1.0$), reflecting endothermic mixing and disruption of strong water-water hydrogen-bonding networks upon ethanol addition. At low ethanol concentrations ($x_1 = 0.10$), the activity coefficient reaches $\gamma_1 = 2.91$, resulting in sharp vapor enrichment ($y_1 = 0.53$). As composition approaches the binary azeotrope ($x_1 \approx 0.89$), $\gamma_1$ asymptotically approaches $1.00$.

10. Industrial Scale-Up & Unit Operation Applications

VLE models and interaction parameters are fundamental for designing industrial bioethanol dehydration columns, pressure-swing distillation trains, extractive distillation using ethylene glycol, and molecular sieve adsorption units.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why does ethanol-water form an azeotrope while benzene-toluene does not?
A1: Ethanol and water have highly asymmetric polar and hydrogen-bonding forces, generating strong positive deviations from ideality that elevate vapor pressure until the VLE curves touch ($y=x$); benzene and toluene are similar nonpolar aromatics that form nearly ideal solutions.

Q2: How can the Redlich–Kister area test verify whether this experimental data set is thermodynamically consistent?
A2: By plotting $\ln(\gamma_1 / \gamma_2)$ against $x_1$ from 0 to 1, the integral must evaluate to zero within experimental tolerance ($\le 2\%$), proving consistency with the Gibbs–Duhem relation.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The Othmer VLE still successfully demonstrated non-ideal vapor-liquid equilibrium for ethanol-water at $101.325\text{ kPa}$. The experimental data confirmed modified Raoult's law and yielded the binary Margules parameter $A_{12} = 0.7250$, fully validating thermodynamic consistency.
