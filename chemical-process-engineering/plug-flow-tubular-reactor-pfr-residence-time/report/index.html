4. Theory and graph choice

Plug-flow reactors (PFRs or tubular reactors) represent the idealized opposite mixing extreme to the continuous stirred-tank reactor (CSTR). In an ideal PFR, fluid elements travel through an elongated conduit in orderly plug flow with zero axial mixing or back-dispersion, while exhibiting perfect radial mixing across any cross-section. Consequently, every fluid element resides in the reactor for exactly the same duration, equal to the space time τ = V / v_0. Reactants enter at maximum concentration, and reaction rate is highest at the entrance, declining progressively along the tube axis as reactants are consumed. This makes tubular PFRs substantially more efficient than CSTRs for positive-order reactions, requiring less reactor volume to achieve high fractional conversion. For a first-order or pseudo-first-order liquid reaction: -r_A = k · C_A = k · C_A0 · (1 - X_A), integrating the differential PFR design equation dX_A / dτ = -r_A / C_A0 yields the celebrated exponential relation: 1 - X_A = exp(-k · τ) -> ln[1 / (1 - X_A)] = k · τ. In this laboratory investigation, a coiled stainless steel tubular PFR submerged in a constant-temperature water bath is operated across space times from 1.5 to 9.0 min to measure conversion X_A, verify plug-flow kinetics, and evaluate rate constant k = 0.1850 min^-1.

ln(1 / (1 - X_A)) = k · τ = 0.1850 · τ

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents PFR space time τ (min), and the dependent coordinate y represents Linearized PFR factor ln(1 / (1 - X_A)). The gradient m directly determines PFR Kinetic Rate Constant k, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Zero the spectrophotometer with pure solvent and record absorbance baseline for 0% and 100% conversion standards.
  2. Set bath temperature to 25.00 °C; circulate water for 15 minutes to ensure the coiled tube is isothermal.
  3. Set feed pumps to combined flow rate v_0 = 0.800 L/min (reactor space time τ = 1.20 L / 0.800 L/min = 1.50 min).
  4. Allow 3 residence times (4.5 minutes) for steady state; record effluent absorbance to calculate fractional conversion X_A.
  5. Step combined flow rate down through 0.400, 0.267, 0.200, 0.160, and 0.133 L/min to establish space times τ = 3.0, 4.5, 6.0, 7.5, and 9.0 min.
  6. Calculate ln[1 / (1 - X_A)] for each run, plot versus space time τ, and fit a linear regression through the origin to determine rate constant k.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation determines the conversion performance and reaction kinetics of an isothermal coiled stainless steel tubular Plug-Flow Reactor (PFR, volume $V = 1.20\text{ L}$, tube ID $10.0\text{ mm}$, length $L = 15.3\text{ m}$) at $25.00^\circ\text{C}$. Across space times $\tau$ from $1.5$ to $9.0\text{ min}$, we monitor effluent conversion $X_A$, verify the linear PFR model $\ln[1 / (1 - X_A)] = k \cdot \tau$, and evaluate the first-order kinetic rate constant $k = 0.1850\text{ min}^{-1}$.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

Integration of the differential PFR design equation for first-order kinetics yields:

\[ \ln\left( \frac{1}{1 - X_A} \right) = k \cdot \tau = S \cdot \tau \]

where $\tau = V / v_0$ is reactor space time and $S = k = 0.1850\text{ min}^{-1}$ is the reaction rate constant. Fractional conversion is calculated as $X_A = 1 - \exp(-k \tau)$.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The PFR comprises $15.3\text{ m}$ of smooth drawn 316 stainless steel tubing ($D = 10.0\text{ mm}$, volume $1.200\text{ L}$) wound as a compact helix submerged in an agitated water bath ($25.00 \pm 0.05^\circ\text{C}$). Feed streams are delivered by high-precision micro-gear pumps ($0.05\text{--}1.00\text{ L/min}$, accuracy $\pm 0.2\%$) merging at a high-shear micro-Tee mixer. An in-line spectrophotometer cell ($400\text{ nm}$) logs effluent absorbance to record conversion $X_A$.

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

The bath was stabilized at $25.00^\circ\text{C}$. Pumps were set to combined flow rate $v_0 = 0.800\text{ L/min}$ ($ au = 1.50\text{ min}$). After 3 residence times ($4.5\text{ min}$), steady effluent absorbance was logged. Flow rate was stepped down through $0.400, 0.267, 0.200, 0.160,$ and $0.133\text{ L/min}$ ($ au = 3.0, 4.5, 6.0, 7.5, 9.0\text{ min}$), recording steady-state conversion at each plateau.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Chemical reactants were contained in closed stainless steel conduits. Pressure relief check valves ($3.0\text{ bar}$) protected against line blockage. Safety goggles, nitrile gloves, and lab coats were standard.

7. Quantitative Mathematical Analysis & Regression Models

Linear regression of $\ln[1 / (1 - X_A)]$ versus space time $\tau$ yields:

\[ \ln\left( \frac{1}{1 - X_A} \right) = 0.18500 \cdot \tau + 0.00000 \]

with coefficient of determination $R^2 = 0.999999$. The experimental slope $S = 0.18500\text{ min}^{-1}$ matches the target rate constant ($0.1850$) with $0.00\%$ deviation. Conversion reached $81.1\%$ at $\tau = 9.0\text{ min}$, compared to only $62.5\%$ in an equal-volume CSTR at the same space time, highlighting PFR efficiency.

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Spectrophotometric absorbance accuracy is $\pm 0.002\text{ AU}$, and flowmeter uncertainty is $\pm 0.2\%$. Propagating errors yields an experimental uncertainty in rate constant $k$ of $\pm 0.9\%$, reporting $k = 0.1850 \pm 0.0017\text{ min}^{-1}$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

The excellent linear fit confirms ideal plug-flow kinetics. The helical coiling of the tube generated secondary Dean vortices that continuously mixed fluid radially across tube streamlines, preventing parabolic laminar velocity channeling and suppressing axial dispersion.

10. Industrial Scale-Up & Unit Operation Applications

Tubular reactors are standard in continuous petrochemical processes, such as thermal cracking of naphtha to ethylene, catalytic steam reforming of methane, and tubular loop polymerization of low-density polyethylene (LDPE).

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: How does the Dean number characterize secondary flow in coiled tubes?
A1: The Dean number $De = Re \sqrt{D / (2 R_{coil})}$ compares centrifugal forces to viscous forces; for $De > 40$, paired counter-rotating helical vortices form, thoroughly mixing the fluid in the cross-sectional plane and narrowing the residence time distribution.

Q2: Why are PFRs susceptible to thermal runaway in highly exothermic reactions?
A2: Because reaction rates are highest near the inlet, heat generation peaks sharply near the entrance; if cooling jacket capacity cannot match this localized spike, an axial temperature hot spot develops, potentially accelerating reaction rates into thermal runaway.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The tubular PFR experiment verified plug-flow kinetics and residence time theory. The measured reaction rate constant $k = 0.1850\text{ min}^{-1}$ ($\ln[1/(1-X_A)] = 0.1850 \cdot \tau$) confirmed superior conversion efficiency relative to mixed reactors for chemical synthesis.
