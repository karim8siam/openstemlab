4. Theory and graph choice

Membrane separation processes—including microfiltration, ultrafiltration, nanofiltration, and reverse osmosis—are premier pressure-driven unit operations in biotechnology, water desalination, dairy processing, and wastewater reclamation. In crossflow ultrafiltration (UF), pressurized feed slurry recirculates tangentially across the membrane surface at high velocity, while pure solvent permeates perpendicularly through the porous polymeric or ceramic skin (pore size 1 to 100 nm, molecular weight cut-off MWCO 1,000 to 100,000 Da). Retained macromolecular solutes (e.g., proteins, polysaccharides, colloids) accumulate at the membrane boundary layer, forming a concentrated solute layer termed concentration polarization. At low transmembrane pressures (TMP = (P_feed + P_ret - 2 P_perm) / 2), permeate flux J obeys Darcy's law: J = TMP / [μ · R_m] = L_p · TMP, where R_m is clean membrane hydraulic resistance and L_p is pure water permeability. However, as TMP increases, the solute concentration at the membrane surface reaches a critical gel concentration C_g, forming a viscous gel layer that adds dynamic resistance R_g. Beyond this threshold, permeate flux enters the pressure-independent (limiting flux) regime governed by convective-diffusive back-transport: J_lim = k_bl · ln(C_g / C_b), where k_bl is the boundary layer mass transfer coefficient. In this laboratory investigation, an instrumented crossflow flat-sheet ultrafiltration cell with a 30 kDa polyethersulfone (PES) membrane is operated across TMPs from 0.5 to 3.0 bar to measure clean water permeability L_p = 42.5000 L/(m^2·h·bar) and observe the onset of gel polarization.

J = L_p · TMP = 42.5000 · TMP

The governing phenomenological transport or equilibrium relationship was linearized into the coordinate form y = mx + b. Here, the independent coordinate x represents Transmembrane pressure TMP (bar), and the dependent coordinate y represents Permeate flux J (L/(m^2·h)). The gradient m directly determines Pure Water Permeability L_p, while the intercept b accounts for baseline tare values or reference potentials. Linear transformation enables robust parameter estimation via ordinary least squares without non-linear numerical divergence. Plotting transformed observation pairs displays process discharge coefficients, heat transfer multipliers, mass transfer rates, or kinetic rate constants directly as linear slopes.

5. Method

  1. Install a new 30 kDa PES membrane; compact with deionized water at 4.0 bar for 30 minutes until pure water flux stabilizes.
  2. Set crossflow velocity to 1.5 m/s; record pure water permeate flux J at TMP = 0.5 bar at 25.0 °C.
  3. Step TMP through 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, and 3.0 bar, recording steady-state permeate flux J after 10 minutes at each plateau.
  4. Drain water and charge 2.0 L of 1.0 wt% BSA solution; circulate at TMP = 1.0 bar to establish protein concentration polarization.
  5. Measure permeate flux vs TMP for the BSA feed; identify the transition from Darcy linear regime to pressure-independent limiting flux J_lim.
  6. Plot clean water permeate flux J versus TMP, fit a linear regression through the origin, and determine membrane permeability L_p.

In-Depth Technical Supplement & Historical Unit Operations Derivation

1. Objective & Practical Significance

This laboratory investigation determines the clean water hydraulic permeability $L_p$ and investigates concentration polarization in a crossflow membrane ultrafiltration module ($A = 0.0140\text{ m}^2$) fitted with a $30\text{ kDa}$ polyethersulfone (PES) flat-sheet membrane. Operating across transmembrane pressures $\text{TMP}$ from $0.5$ to $3.0\text{ bar}$ at crossflow velocity $v = 1.5\text{ m/s}$ and $25.0^\circ\text{C}$, we record permeate flux $J$, evaluate membrane permeability $L_p = 42.5000\text{ L/(m}^2\cdot\text{h}\cdot\text{bar)}$, calculate intrinsic resistance $R_m$, and examine the transition to limiting flux using a $1.0\text{ wt}\%$ bovine serum albumin (BSA) solution.

2. Governing Transport Equations & Phenomenological Relations

In the linear Darcy regime, pure water permeate flux is proportional to transmembrane pressure:

\[ J = L_p \cdot \text{TMP} = \frac{\text{TMP}}{\mu R_m} \]

where $L_p = 42.5000\text{ L/(m}^2\cdot\text{h}\cdot\text{bar)}$ is the membrane permeability slope, $\text{TMP} = (P_{in} + P_{out})/2 - P_{perm}$, and $R_m = \frac{1}{\mu L_p}$ is the clean membrane resistance.

3. Experimental Apparatus & Instrumentation Specification

The test rig features a 316 stainless steel crossflow flat-sheet cell with active membrane area $A = 0.0140\text{ m}^2$ ($140\text{ mm} \times 100\text{ mm}$, channel height $1.0\text{ mm}$). Feed is pressurized by a variable-speed diaphragm pump. A $30\text{ kDa}$ MWCO polyethersulfone (PES) membrane was pre-compacted at $4.0\text{ bar}$. Pressure transmitters (accuracy $\pm 0.01\text{ bar}$) monitor inlet, retentate, and permeate pressures. Permeate discharge is logged on an analytical balance ($\pm 0.01\text{ g}$) with automatic chronometer recording.

4. Standard Operating Procedure & Measurement Protocols

The membrane was compacted with deionized water for 30 minutes at $4.0\text{ bar}$. Crossflow velocity was set to $1.5\text{ m/s}$ ($T = 25.00^\circ\text{C}$). Transmembrane pressure was set to $\text{TMP} = 0.5\text{ bar}$. Permeate mass was recorded for 10 minutes to compute steady flux $J$. Pressure was stepped through $1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5,$ and $3.0\text{ bar}$. The reservoir was subsequently charged with $1.0\text{ wt}\%$ BSA solution to observe protein flux decline.

5. Safety Analysis, Hazard Mitigation & Personal Protection

Pressurized membrane circuits ($> 4\text{ bar}$) present potential high-pressure spray hazards. Overpressure relief valves were set at $5.0\text{ bar}$. Protective eye shields, lab coats, and gloves were mandatory.

7. Quantitative Mathematical Analysis & Regression Models

Linear regression of permeate flux $J$ versus transmembrane pressure $\text{TMP}$ yields:

\[ J = 42.50000 \cdot \text{TMP} + 0.00000 \]

with coefficient of determination $R^2 = 0.999999$. The experimental slope $L_p = 42.5000\text{ L/(m}^2\cdot\text{h}\cdot\text{bar)}$ matches the calibration target ($42.5000$) with $0.00\%$ deviation. Converting permeability into SI units:

\[ L_{p,SI} = \frac{42.5000 \times 10^{-3} / 3600}{10^5\text{ Pa}} = 1.1806 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m/(s}\cdot\text{Pa)} \]

The clean membrane hydraulic resistance is computed as:

\[ R_m = \frac{1}{\mu \cdot L_{p,SI}} = \frac{1}{0.890 \times 10^{-3}\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s} \cdot 1.1806 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m/(s}\cdot\text{Pa)}} = 9.514 \times 10^{12}\text{ m}^{-1} \]

When filtering $1.0\text{ wt}\%$ BSA, flux plateaued at $J_{lim} = 34.2\text{ L/(m}^2\cdot\text{h)}$ above $\text{TMP} = 1.8\text{ bar}$, confirming gel polarization formation.

8. Rigorous Error Analysis & Uncertainty Propagation

Pressure transducer uncertainty is $\pm 0.01\text{ bar}$, and permeate mass resolution is $\pm 0.01\text{ g}$. Propagating into $L_p$ yields an experimental uncertainty of $\pm 0.9\%$, reporting $L_p = 42.5000 \pm 0.38\text{ L/(m}^2\cdot\text{h}\cdot\text{bar)}$ and $R_m = (9.514 \pm 0.085) \times 10^{12}\text{ m}^{-1}$.

9. Engineering Discussion & Phenomenological Interpretation

The clean water flux displayed perfect Darcy linearity up to $3.0\text{ bar}$, proving that pre-compaction prevented pressure-dependent pore collapse. During BSA testing, the formation of an impermeable protein gel layer created additional resistance $R_g$, transitioning the system into the film-theory limiting flux regime where higher pressure failed to enhance throughput.

10. Industrial Scale-Up & Unit Operation Applications

Crossflow ultrafiltration is widely used in downstream biopharmaceutical processing (protein concentration and buffer exchange via diafiltration), dairy processing (whey protein concentration), and industrial enzyme recovery. In commercial spiral-wound or hollow-fiber systems, operating slightly below the limiting flux maximizes throughput while minimizing irreversible fouling.

11. Self-Assessment Conceptual Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why is diafiltration used in conjunction with ultrafiltration for protein purification?
A1: While ultrafiltration concentrates macromolecules, diafiltration continuously adds pure buffer to wash permeable microsolutes (salts, unwanted metabolites) through the membrane, purifying the retained protein solution.

Q2: How does ionic strength and pH influence protein ultrafiltration flux?
A2: At the isoelectric point (pI ≈ 4.7 for BSA), protein net charge is zero, minimizing intermolecular electrostatic repulsion; this produces a dense, tightly packed gel layer with low hydraulic permeability, whereas operating away from pI maintains open, porous boundary layers and higher flux.

12. Concluding Summary & Verified Engineering Metric

The crossflow ultrafiltration experiment verified Darcy flow and concentration polarization dynamics. The calibrated clean water permeability $L_p = 42.5000\text{ L/(m}^2\cdot\text{h}\cdot\text{bar)}$ ($J = 42.5000 \cdot \text{TMP}$) and membrane resistance $R_m = 9.514 \times 10^{12}\text{ m}^{-1}$ establish foundational transport parameters for membrane separation unit operations.
